A movement pattern common in sport where the most proximal joint reaches its peak angular velocity first, then the middle joint, then the most distal joint, like hip, then knee, then ankle in a jump. This lets kinetic energy flow down the chain, so the distal joint can reach faster speeds than it could on its own.

It also means each joint works at a different point on the force-velocity curve. The proximal joint peaks at the slowest speed, so its contribution depends most on maximum strength, while the distal joint peaks fastest, so its contribution depends most on maximum speed. That's why the best training for a joint depends on where it sits in the sequence.

In vertical jumps, a longer delay between hip and knee extension, meaning a more proximal-to-distal strategy, went with higher jumps.
[Chiu 2014](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24476777/)
