The force a muscle fiber experiences, either while actively contracting against a load (active tension) or while being stretched (passive tension). Active tension in recruited fibers is the main stimulus for hypertrophy; passive tension, sensed through titin, stimulates sarcomerogenesis.

## Connections
**Feeds into**
- [[Muscle swelling]] — swelling doesn't add tension, so it can't drive growth
- [[Anabolic Androgenic Steroids]] — steroids add growth on top of tension rather than replacing it
- [[Increasing motor unit recruitment]] — fibers only grow if they're recruited and feel tension
- [[Age and adaptations]] — older muscle maintains on the same tension dose as young muscle
- [[Sarcomerogenesis]] — passive tension is the stimulus for adding sarcomeres
- [[Flexing and co-contraction training]] — isometric flexing gives activated fibers high tension, so only activation is needed
