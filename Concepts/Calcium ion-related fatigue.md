Peripheral fatigue caused by calcium ions accumulating inside muscle fibers. It covers excitation-contraction coupling failure (ECCF), reduced myofibrillar calcium sensitivity, and muscle damage. In these notes it underlies post-workout fatigue, the repeated bout effect, and shifts toward slower fiber types.

## Calcium accumulation during prolonged stimulation

In rat muscle stimulated once per second for 4 hours, calcium uptake rose 30% in the slow soleus and 154% in the fast extensor digitorum longus. Prolonged stimulation also caused leakage of lactate dehydrogenase, consistent with membrane damage. Calcium entering the fiber can activate protein breakdown and contribute to further membrane leakage.
[Gissel 2000](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11104058/)

## How calcium damages fibers

If the fiber membrane lets in more calcium than the cell can control, calcium overload can activate calpains that break down cell proteins, phospholipase A2 that weakens membranes, and more reactive oxygen species that cause lipid peroxidation. These can worsen the damage in a self-reinforcing process. Calcium overload can happen during exercise, trauma, prolonged ischemia, Duchenne muscular dystrophy and cachexia.
[Gissel 2005](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16533926/)
