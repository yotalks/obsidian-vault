> [!summary] Bottom line
> Sarcomerogenesis is the addition of sarcomeres in series, which lengthens muscle fibers. It's driven by passive tension from stretched sarcomeres (sarcomere length, not muscle length), can happen within days in animals, and also occurs in humans with eccentric training. It's separate from hypertrophy: it needs its own protein synthesis and satellite cells, can happen while fibers atrophy, and is lost within about two weeks once the stimulus stops.

## Animal models

Sarcomere addition has been seen in multiple animal models with the most known example being in immobilization studies. The limbs of animals are placed in plaster casts at long muscle lengths for several days or even a couple weeks. After, the number of sarcomeres are counted in the muscle fibers of the stretched limb and compared to the number of sarcomeres in the unstretched limb. These studies show increases in both muscle fascicle length and the number of sarcomeres in series, although this doesn't stop muscle loss from atrophy, which shows that myofibrillar addition and sarcomere addition are completely separate adaptations.
[Shah 2001](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11316490/)

We see that sarcomere addition happens very fast in comparison to muscle fiber hypertrophy. This study saw sarcomere addition in 4 days.
[Aoki 2009](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19705480/)

More conventional passive static stretching similarly creates increases in number of sarcomeres in series in animal models. Here, the stretching increases total muscle mass, since there is no immobilization-induced atrophy occurring.
[Coutinho 2006](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17118750/)

The static stretching literature shows that sarcomere addition only continues to happen when the maximum range of motion is increased progressively. This tells us the stimulus for adding sarcomeres is most likely sarcomere length, since each sarcomere that is added reduces the resting sarcomere length in the muscle fiber. Since sarcomere length is a key determinant of the passive mechanical tension that a sarcomere generates, it's very likely the magnitude of passive mechanical tension is therefore the stimulus for sarcomerogenesis.
[Cox 2000](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/12563903_Growth_induced_by_incremental_static_stretch_in_adult_rabbit_latissimus_dorsi_muscle)
→ [[Mechanical tension]]

Surgical limb lengthening (distraction) has been studied extensively in animal models and has been shown to produce the addition of sarcomeres. Importantly, this literature again shows that the addition of sarcomeres is dependent upon sarcomere length and not upon muscle length.
[Zöllner 2012](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3462200/)

## Eccentric training

Unlike in the previous animal models, eccentric training involves activated muscle fibers. There are still indications that sarcomere addition happens after this type of training in the same way as after implementation of previous interventions.
[Hinks 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11449626/)

It is apparent eccentric contractions also respond to sarcomere length. In this study, differences in muscle fiber lengthening between muscle regions affected the resulting sarcomerogenesis. In regions where the muscle fiber lengthening was greater, there was a larger amount of sarcomere addition.
[Butterfield 2006](https://scholars.uky.edu/en/publications/the-magnitude-of-muscle-strain-does-not-influence-serial-sarcomer)

Eccentric training has also been shown to stimulate increases in sarcomere number in humans, alongside increases in muscle fascicle length. In this study, the increase in fascicle length and sarcomere number varied between muscle regions, and distal region displayed greater increase in both fascicle length and sarcomere number, showing the two measurements do correspond relatively well to one another.
[Andrews 2025](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2095254624001534)

## Mechanisms

Since sarcomere addition involves the production of new contractile proteins, it is not surprising that it requires a robust increase in [[Glossary#MPS|MPS]] rates. This doesn't protect myofibrils from atrophy during immobilization, which tells us that the proteins are exclusively directed toward sarcomerogenesis in response to the sarcomere length stimulus, and that the MPS elevations don't have generic effects.
[Laurent 1978](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1186246/)

Given the robust increase in MPS rates, it's not surprising that sarcomere addition involves increased activation in the mTOR signaling pathway. This mTOR signaling doesn't protect myofibrils from atrophy during immobilization, which tells us that the mTOR signaling (like MPS rate elevations) doesn't have generic effects in muscles.
[Aoki 2006](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00441-005-0081-4)

Sarcomerogenesis appears to be more dependent upon the presence of satellite cells compared to muscle fiber hypertrophy. Depleting satellite cells greatly reduces the number of sarcomeres that can be added after detraining but doesn't similarly affect the number of myofibrils that can be added. The nature of the new sarcomeres as new structures may require more building blocks from the satellite cells than simply adding more myofibrils inside existing structures.
[Emara 2020 (commentary)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32118609/)
