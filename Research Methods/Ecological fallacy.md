> [!summary] Bottom line
> A trend across groups doesn't have to hold for the individuals in them, and it can even reverse. Countries that smoke more live longer, but smoking shortens individual lives. So country-level correlations can only suggest a hypothesis.

The ecological fallacy is when you look at two temporally concurrent variables and imply a causal relationship. What applies on a country average level may not apply on an individual level.
![[Pasted image 20261105025154.png]]
[Wikipedia: Ecological fallacy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ecological_fallacy)

In this graph on average as X increases, so does Y. But if you look at each cluster separately, as X increases Y decreases. A real life example is the relationship between smoking and longevity.
![[Pasted image 20261105025259.png]]

If you plot each country's smoking rate and lifespan  you'll see that the more people who smoke, the longer they live. This correlation breaks at an individual level.
