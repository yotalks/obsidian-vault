> [!summary] Bottom line
> Case-control studies start from disease and look back for exposure; cohort studies start from exposure and look for disease. Because a case-control study sets how many cases it includes, it can only report an [[Glossary#OR|odds ratio]], which approximates the [[Glossary#RR|relative risk]] only for rare diseases. Cohort studies can report both.

![[Case-control vs cohort study designs.png]]

## Case-control studies

The researcher starts with people who have the disease (cases) and people who don't (controls), then looks back at their exposures. Because the researcher decides how many cases and controls to include, the prevalence of disease in the study is set by design, so only an odds ratio (OR) can be calculated, never a relative risk (RR). Case-control studies are good for rare diseases, where the OR approximates the RR. For common diseases the OR and RR point the same direction but give different numbers.
[BU: Case-control studies](https://sphweb.bumc.bu.edu/otlt/MPH-Modules/EP/EP713_Case-Control/index.html)

## Cohort studies

The researcher starts with people grouped by exposure and compares how often they develop the disease. Both RR and OR can be calculated.
- **Prospective cohort:** the researcher identifies exposure now and follows people forward to see who develops the disease.
- **Retrospective cohort:** the researcher starts after the fact, using records of past exposure and finding out who has since developed the disease.

[BU: Cohort studies](https://sphweb.bumc.bu.edu/otlt/MPH-Modules/EP/EP713_CohortStudies/index.html)
