> [!summary] Bottom line
>Pangram is a reliable AI-text detector, there are low reported false-positive and false-negative rates. It has weaker performance on short or unusually repetitive text and its estimated AI-written percentage isn't exact. Interpreting a flag also requires distinguishing error rates from predictive values.

## Pangram

AI detectors need to be evaluated individually. Poor results from other detectors don't establish that Pangram is unreliable, and that its accuracy is an empirical question.
## Evidence cited in the article

- **Reported error rates:** the article cites a collaboration between Pangram and University of Maryland researchers reporting a **0.0041% false-positive rate** and **0.3396% false-negative rate**. [Cited paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.27183)
- **Other evaluations:** it also cites a University of Chicago working paper as supporting Pangram's accuracy. [Cited working paper](https://bfi.uchicago.edu/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/BFI_WP_2025-116.pdf)
- **Counterexample challenge:** a $500 challenge sought at least 750 consecutive words written before 2019 that Pangram classified as over 75% AI-written. The article reports an eventual counterexample involving an unusually repetitive poem.

## Limits

Short passages and unusually repetitive writing can produce errors. The percentage classified as AI-written shouldn't be read as the exact percentage actually generated by AI.

## Interpreting a flag

A false-positive rate is the chance of a flag given human-written text, not the chance that flagged text is human-written. Predictive values also depend on sensitivity and the prevalence of AI-written text in the population being assessed.
