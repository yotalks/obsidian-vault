> [!summary] Bottom line
> Hypocrisy arguments claim vegans violate their own values by buying crops, phones or clothes. Even if that were true, it wouldn't make eating animals okay. These arguments also usually fail on their own terms: they misstate what vegans value, assume an empirical claim nobody has shown, or confuse "something better exists" with "this makes things worse." Vegans shouldn't answer with intention or necessity, because those replies fail too.

## The structure

A hypocrisy argument always has the same form: if this empirical claim is true and you hold this value, you should act this way or you're a hypocrite. So there are only two premises to check:

- **The value:** Does the vegan actually hold it? Vegans aren't committed to opposing all death or all suffering, and there are cases where death is justified.
- **The empirical claim:** Has it actually been shown? The person claiming hypocrisy carries the burden of proof, so the vegan doesn't need to prove the opposite.

Hypocrisy wouldn't settle anything anyway. A smoker telling you not to smoke can still be right.

## Four common errors

**Better isn't the same as harmful.** Showing that some option is more ethical doesn't show that what you're doing makes things worse than before. Giving to a children's hospital doesn't lower utility just because the Against Malaria Foundation does more with the money. A view that you must always pick the best possible option leads to consequentialist slavery. → [[Positive and negative rights]]

**Agnostic about a part isn't agnostic about the whole.** Being unsure whether crop deaths raise or lower utility doesn't make someone unsure about veganism overall, any more than being unsure about one factor out of five stops the other four from pointing one way.

**Baking in the conclusion.** Asking "if the empirics turned out to favor X, would your values favor X?" is empty. The answer is trivially yes, and the only real question is whether the empirics do turn out that way. The same goes for saying that if your life causes net harm, you should end it.

**The differential has to be comparable.** Saying one crop kills less than another isn't enough. To show hypocrisy, the gap has to meet the same threshold that makes buying animal products wrong.

## Common arguments

### Crop deaths and lawn mowing

Crop deaths and mowing a lawn aren't clearly bad. Insects and small animals often live short lives and suffer a lot, so reducing how many exist on a piece of land might even reduce suffering. There's no evidence either way that a mown lawn holds more suffering than an unmown one, and uncertainty cuts both ways, so it can't be used as a reason to act in one direction.
→ [[Crop deaths]]
→ [[Wild animal suffering]]

Hunting a deer instead of eating crops has the same problem. It needs evidence that fewer animals die once clearing land and everything else is counted, and even then deaths aren't equal, since a deer is far more sentient than an insect.

### Electronics and clothing

For these to count as hypocrisy, buying the product has to lower utility or increase rights violations compared with not buying it, and by a comparable amount to animal agriculture. A bad situation existing somewhere doesn't show that boycotting it helps. Animal agriculture differs because buying less means fewer animals are bred and killed, while a boycott might only cost workers their jobs.

The evidence on whether factory jobs help workers is mixed. In a randomized trial of 947 job applicants in Ethiopia, factory job offers didn't improve employment or earnings over a year and led to serious health effects, though most people quit and no lasting harm was found at five years. So these jobs aren't clearly better than the alternatives, and that also means there's no clear case that boycotting them would help or harm.
[Blattman 2022](https://www.nber.org/papers/w25788)

### Animal ingredients in products

Some of these claims are just false. LCD screens don't appear to contain cholesterol. Liquid crystals were called cholesteric because they were first discovered in cholesterol derivatives, but most screens use synthetic nematic crystals.
[Wikipedia: Cholesteric liquid crystal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cholesteric_liquid_crystal)

Even real trace ingredients, like a few micrograms of lactose in a pill, send so little money to animal agriculture that they're unlikely to matter next to the benefit of the product.
→ [[Propofol]]
→ [[Organic is not vegan]]

### Films with animals

Seeing a film that shows someone riding a horse doesn't create demand for future horse use, unless the film's success depends on it.

## Bad vegan replies

**"I don't intend to kill them."** A meat-eater can say the same thing: they intend to eat a burger, and the animals are just in the way. Intention also doesn't make an outcome okay, since an accidental genocide would still be terrible.

**"Death isn't fundamentally required for crops."** It isn't fundamentally required for eggs or even meat either. And if crops were fertilized with the blood of murdered children, the murder not being strictly necessary wouldn't make that corn okay to buy.

## Factory farming

Sometimes the reply is that the speaker only eats humanely farmed animals. An estimated 99% of US farmed animals live on factory farms, including 74.9% of cows, 98.6% of pigs, 98.3% of egg-laying hens and over 99.9% of chickens raised for meat. So someone who rejects factory farming has already rejected nearly all of what's bought. Small farms aren't automatically acceptable either.
[Sentience Institute 2024](https://www.sentienceinstitute.org/us-factory-farming-estimates)
