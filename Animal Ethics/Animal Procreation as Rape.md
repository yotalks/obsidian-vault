> [!summary] Bottom line
> Whether animal sex is rape depends on a theory of rape. If informed consent is only required when at least one party is capable of it, sex between beings who can't consent but intend to breed needn't be rape. Some animals have sex voluntarily, so not all animal sex is rape, though there's no strict criterion for telling which is.

## The question

I'm unsure whether animal sex constitutes rape or not. This requires a theory of what constitutes rape. I'm unsure if the preconditions of informed consent would hold in the case of two mentally handicapped humans breeding while both in the intentional state of breeding with each other in order to not be rape.
→ [[Animal Rights]]

## Consent between beings who can't consent

In the context of 2 individuals incapable of informed consent, yet have intentional states to breed with each other, one doesn't need to hold the view that this is rape. One can hold the view that informed consent for both is only required when one of the two or more is capable of informed consent.

## Where I land

> [!note] My take
> I'd hold the view that not all forms of non-human animal sex is rape as some animals do voluntarily have sex with one another. I don't have a strict set of criteria for telling what is and isn't rape in non human animals, just some over and under determinations.
