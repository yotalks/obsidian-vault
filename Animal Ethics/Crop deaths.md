> [!summary] Bottom line
> There's no evidence cropland kills more animals than wildland. The studies claiming it ignore the land used by grass-fed beef, misread their sources and overstate mouse plagues by a factor of about 43; even their 7.3 billion figure is below the number of animals killed in US animal agriculture, and mouse poisoning protects pastures too.

## No evidence cropland kills more

We cannot claim to know that cropland kills more animals than wildland, because if we did know this, there would be evidence for it, but such evidence doesn't exist. So, it cannot be claimed that crop farming leads to more animal deaths.

## Problems with crop-death studies

Studies concluding a vegan diet will result in more deaths generally contain the following problems: They don't account for total land use of grass fed beef and they select areas that are outliers for crop deaths, such as regions of mouse plagues.
[AnimalVisuals: US slaughter rates](https://animalvisuals.org/p/slaughter)

This study is often cited as evidence that 7.3 billion animals are killed every year from plant agriculture. The estimates come from the work of Archer and Davis. Davis misread the sugarcane study he cites, as he ignored the longer growing time, so the annual mortality rate is only 38.5%, not 77% while Archer's analysis offers a similar example. It exaggerates the scope of mouse plagues.

## Mouse plagues are overstated

Archer writes that "each area of grain production in eastern Australia is subject on average to a mouse plague every 4 years". The sources Archer cites support this statement insofar as "each area" is understood to refer in large geographic regions such as states or parts of states every hectare of grain cropland in Australia. The difference here is between a quadrennial mouse plague happening somewhere in all of Victoria and such a plague happening on every last Victorian farm.

A more accurate picture is suggested by the Cooperative Research Centre for Pest Animal Control, which notes that each year between 100,000 and 500,000 ha of grain crops in Australia are subject to mouse plagues. Australia plants approximately 22 million hectares of grain crops annually (Australian Export Grains Innovation Centre 2016). These figures suggest that in an average year 2.3% of Australian grain cropland is hit by plague. When Archer's figure of 55 deaths per hectare of grain is recalculated to only apply to 2.3% of cropland the mortality rate for grain becomes 1.27 animal per hectare (error by a factor of 43).
[Fischer 2018](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10806-018-9733-8)

## Compared with animal agriculture

These numbers should also be compared to the animals killed on farms in the US. 
Cattle: 40 million per year
Pigs: 120 million per year
Broiler Chickens 9 billion per year

This alone puts animal agriculture above plant agriculture in the death count even if the 7.3 billion figure was correct.

## Mouse poisoning protects pastures too

The previous paper also notes that mouse plagues also damage grazing land. A primary reason farmers resort to poison is to avoid the extensive economic issues mouse plagues cause. Insofar as mouse plagues damage farms indiscriminately, it's wrong to argue that farmers poison mice to grow grain. The poisoning that occurs in Australia is done to protect all aspects of affected farming operations, including pastures. Archer's analysis is thus misleading in failing to associate mouse poisoning with animal agriculture, and this undermines his attempt to use mouse poison deaths as grounds to prefer pasture raised meat over plant foods.
