> [!summary] Bottom line
> The Kalam cosmological argument says the universe began to exist, so it has a cause. On the B theory of time, the universe can have a first moment the way a yardstick has a first inch, without coming into being, so the argument loses its force.

Craig's version of the Kalam argument presupposes an A view of time, where time flows from past to present to future. It seems it can't be sustained on the B view, where all events tenselessly coexist and are equally real.
[SEP: Cosmological argument](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/cosmological-argument/)

On the B theory, the universe is more like a yardstick. It has a starting point, and it may have an end point, but it exists throughout all of time. Asking what came before the first moment is like asking what comes before the start of the yardstick, which doesn't make sense.

The premise that things which begin to exist have a cause only clearly applies to things that come into existence. Having a first moment isn't the same as coming into existence, so it doesn't follow that the universe needs a cause.
