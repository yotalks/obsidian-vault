## Theories of truth

Whether something counts as true can depend on your theory of truth:

- **Correspondence:** true if it matches a state of affairs in the world.
- **Coherence:** true if it fits with a system of beliefs or axioms.
- **Pragmatist:** true if it's useful to believe.

[SEP: Truth](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/truth/)

Pragmatism can also be used as an axiom of its own. Whether to adopt that axiom is a separate question from what it means.

## Axioms and bootstrapping

Any attempt to justify a belief ends in a circle, an infinite regress or an assumed starting point. This is the Münchhausen trilemma.
[Wikipedia: Münchhausen trilemma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M%C3%BCnchhausen_trilemma)

Saying you're confident rather than certain doesn't get around this. If you're 90% confident in evolution and 90% confident in that confidence, multiplying gives 81%, and it keeps falling toward zero the further you ask. So at some level you have to assume something rather than know it.

That doesn't mean evidence is useless. Evolution is accepted because the fossil record, endogenous retroviruses and homologous genes fit base axioms like logic and mathematics. Doubting those axioms in a debate doesn't mean we can't convict murderers or accept evolution, since we can act on what follows from them.

Morality can work the same way. Bootstrap an axiom, like the goal of optimizing well-being and suffering, and logical relations follow from it. It's then a moral system, even for someone who doesn't claim the axiom is objectively true.

### Combining axioms

A system can hold more than one axiom. Threshold deontology says duties hold up to a point, and consequentialism takes over once the consequences become bad enough.
[SEP: Deontological ethics](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/ethics-deontological/)

This isn't inconsistent, because the switch point follows from the values you assign. If killing has a value of −10,000 and saving a life has a value of +1, you wouldn't kill one person to save 5 or 1,000, but you would to save 10,001. Those exact numbers are arbitrary, like any axiom, but the answers follow consistently from them, and they'd be the same for trait-equalized humans and animals.
