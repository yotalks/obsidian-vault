> [!summary] Bottom line
> One version of the appeal to nature says that if humans normally undergo a physiological process, it can't lead to a bad health outcome. Applied to aging, that would mean senescence doesn't result in death, which would make humans immortal. Since that's absurd, being a normal process doesn't make something harmless.

## The reductio

If someone commits to this variation of the appeal to nature fallacy, it's unclear why they wouldn't also have to affirm that humans are immortal, like Connor MacLeod in *Highlander*.

| Symbol | Meaning |
|---|---|
| P(x) | humans undergo (x) |
| Q(x,y) | (x) results in (y) |
| x | normal physiological process |
| y | negative health outcome |
| s | senescence |
| d | death |

- **P1:** If humans undergo a normal physiological process, then the normal physiological process does not result in a negative health outcome. (∀x∀y(Px→¬Qxy))
- **P2:** Humans undergo senescence. (Ps)
- **C:** Therefore, senescence does not result in death. (∴¬Qsd)

The argument is valid, and P2 is true, so the false conclusion means P1 is false.

Aging itself is a normal process that evolution favors even though it harms us late in life → [[Antagonistic pleiotropy]].
