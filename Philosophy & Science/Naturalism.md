> [!summary] Bottom line
> Naturalism says that what exists is what the natural sciences deal with, with no supernatural entities acting in the world. The evolutionary argument against naturalism doesn't work as a case for theism, since evolution would make belief in the supernatural just as suspect. And saying something has no natural explanation takes more than showing one proposed explanation fails.

## What naturalism is

Ontological naturalism is about what exists: everything in space and time is made up of physical things, and nothing supernatural makes a physical difference. Methodological naturalism is about how to investigate: philosophy and science both answer to observation, and intuitions are fallible.
[SEP: Naturalism](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/naturalism/)

It's hard to define precisely. "The natural world" is circular if it just means what naturalism says exists. Defining it as the sort of thing the natural sciences study depends on what counts as that sort of thing. → [[Fine Tuning]]

Moral naturalism is a separate claim about moral properties. → [[Metaethics]]

## The evolutionary argument against naturalism

Plantinga argues that if naturalism and evolution are both true, our cognitive faculties probably aren't reliable, because evolution selects for survival rather than true beliefs. So believing both would undercut itself.
[Wikipedia: Evolutionary argument against naturalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evolutionary_argument_against_naturalism)

But this cuts against the negation of naturalism at least as much. Most people who have ever lived believed in something beyond the natural world, so that's the belief evolution seems more likely to produce. If evolved beliefs aren't truth-tracking, that's more of a problem for supernatural belief than for naturalism. The argument may also beg the question against a pragmatist theory of truth. → [[Metaethics]]

## Saying there's no natural explanation

A miracle claim is usually a claim that no natural explanation is available. That's a strong claim, because it has to hold up against explanations nobody has tested yet. Two things tend to be missing:

- **Other parameters:** If someone calculates that an event would need a certain physical change, that only counts if the calculation includes everything that could matter. Showing that one mechanism can't explain it leaves the others.
- **Validation:** A method used to date or locate an old photo should be tested blind on similar photos, with someone else recording the true time and place. That covers sources of error nobody has thought of. Science requires this because methods with good mechanistic defenses often fail once they're validated. → [[Evidence hierarchy]]

Expert confidence also matters less than the evidence itself. A tissue sample that looks inconclusive to other pathologists doesn't become conclusive because it was presented confidently.
