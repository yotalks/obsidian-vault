> [!summary] Bottom line
> Being agnostic isn't the same as being a deist. An agnostic neither believes nor disbelieves in God, and can still live as if no god exists. Suffering doesn't disprove a deist god, because that god doesn't have to care about suffering.

An agnostic is someone who has considered whether there's a God and believes neither that it's true nor that it's false. Some people also call themselves agnostic atheists, usually meaning their belief that no God exists is reasonable but isn't certain knowledge.
[SEP: Atheism and agnosticism](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/atheism-agnosticism/)

Not being 100% certain doesn't mean treating the options as 50/50. You can be highly unconvinced of a god and live accordingly, the same way a jury can convict without certainty.

What can be asserted without evidence can be dismissed without evidence, so a lack of evidence for a deist god is a reason not to accept it, without needing proof that it doesn't exist.
[Wikipedia: Hitchens's razor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hitchens%27s_razor)

## Suffering and deism

The argument from suffering works against a God who is all-good. It doesn't work against a deist god, which only has to set things going and doesn't have to intervene. That god doesn't have to regard suffering as bad at all, so a world full of predation and disease doesn't count against it.
