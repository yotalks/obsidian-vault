> [!summary] Bottom line
> Restrictions do reduce abortions and can increase births (about 3% more births after Texas SB8), but the size of the effect depends on how restrictive they are.

## Abortion restrictions can reduce abortion rates and increase births

Robert Michael’s survey analysis found that pregnancies conceived after Roe v. Wade were more likely to end in abortion than those conceived before it. Abortion decisions were also associated with marital status, previous pregnancies, and educational or career opportunities. This historical association alone does not isolate legalization’s causal effect.
[Michael (Harvard working paper)](https://econpapers.repec.org/paper/harwpaper/9910.htm)

A study of 1,178 counties across 18 states found that highly restrictive abortion policies were associated with approximately 0.48 fewer recorded abortions per 1,000 women, roughly 17% of the sample’s median abortion rate. This supports restrictions affecting abortion access, although the study depended on state reporting data.
[Brown 2020](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamanetworkopen/fullarticle/2772683)

Texas’s SB8 abortion restriction was associated with an estimated 9,799 additional births between April and December 2022, about 3.4% above the number predicted without the policy. This supports the conclusion that restricted access led some pregnancies to continue to birth that otherwise would not have.
[Bell 2023](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jama/fullarticle/2806878)

## The effect of abortion restrictions depends on how restrictive they are

Levine and Staiger’s Eastern European analysis found that moderate restrictions were associated with approximately 25% fewer abortions and 10% fewer pregnancies, with little change in births. Severe restrictions were associated with 9–17% higher birth rates, although this finding lost statistical significance when country-specific trends were included. Fewer abortions therefore did not always translate into more births.
[Levine 2004](https://doi.org/10.1086/380475)

> [!question] Objections to prepare for
> - Restrictions may shift abortions across state lines or to self-managed abortion rather than preventing them.
> - Most of these studies are observational; do they isolate a causal effect?
> - Even if restrictions raise births, that doesn't settle whether they're justified.
