> Rebuttals to common pro-life claims: post-abortion syndrome isn't established, conception is a multi-hour process rather than a moment, not every fertilization yields an embryo, and pregnancy itself carries substantial maternal risk.

## Post-abortion syndrome

Post-abortion syndrome (PAS) isn't real. There are women who do feel genuine distress or regret after an abortion... as with many hard life choices. But calling it a syndrome gives it prevalence it doesn't have.
[Dadlez 2009](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/j.1467-8519.2009.01739.x?casa_token=1WRNrcY3LicAAAAA%3A9OVJsOw-sxHavkPLyDgcqnOm0Dpq_V166kgWPpaX1X-d18xXnvU79WZzIgXr3SsloElOaAUOlIY0-2Ig)

## Peri-implantation sex

For those who believe the right to life begins at conception, is it immoral to have sex during the peri-implantation period of a woman's menstrual cycle (there's evidence it reduces the chance of implantation of a zygote and thus increases chance of miscarriage)?

## Conception is a process

Fertilization is a multi-hour process, so conception is not a "moment". We have sperm entry, pronuclear migration, and the actual fusion of the two pronuclei happen over many hours. PLK-1 is needed for that pronuclear fusion part, which shows there are discrete molecular gates before a single unified genome even exists.
[Ma 2020](https://rupress.org/jcb/article/219/2/e202001048/133643/Membrane-fusion-drives-pronuclear-meeting-in-the)  
[NCBI Gene: PLK1](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/gene/5347)

## Unique genomes and conception

Some pro-life views say a human has value because it's a genetically distinct organism, and that value starts at conception. Those two claims don't line up cleanly. After fertilization the maternal and paternal chromosomes sit in separate pronuclei for about a day, until the pronuclear membranes break down.
[Virtual Human Embryo: Stage 1](https://virtualhumanembryo.lsuhsc.edu/demos/Stage1/Intro_pg/Intro.htm)

In mouse embryos they go on to sit on separate spindles, so they're still kept apart during the first division. Unification often fails, and some human embryos end up with whole cell lines carrying only one parent's genome.
[Reichmann 2018](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30002254/)
[Cavazza 2021](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33964210/)
[De Coster 2022](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13059-022-02763-2)

So if value requires one unified genome, it doesn't start at sperm entry. There are three ways to fix this:

1. Move the start of value to when the genomes unify.
2. Drop genetic uniqueness as the reason for value.
3. Say uniqueness only needs both sets to be in one cell. This is the best reply, since each gamete's chromosomes already differ from the parent's, and the cell's activity is coordinated from the start.

Saying "human life begins at conception" doesn't answer this, because the issue is when value starts and why. It only shifts things by about a day, and nobody can detect a pregnancy that early, so it doesn't change which abortions are at stake. It matters as a test of whether the reason given for value actually fits the conclusion.

## The biology of when life begins

In one survey, 96% of 5,577 biologists said a human's life begins at fertilization. About 60,000 were invited, so it's open to self-selection.
[Jacobs 2018](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=3211703)

Even taken at face value, that answers a biological question. It doesn't say why a being has moral value. Skin cells are also living and human, so everyone needs more than "it's a living human" to explain what they value.

## Hydatidiform moles

Not every fertilization yields an embryo. A hydatidiform mole is a fertilized product that develops into placental overgrowth with no viable embryo. Complete moles are usually androgenetic, while recurrent biparental moles have one maternal and one paternal set but fail to lay down proper maternal imprints. So they behave as all-paternal 
[Van den Veyver 2006](https://academic.oup.com/humupd/article/12/3/233/554310)

Mole cancer risk to mother exists but is easily treatable. Around 15% transformation to choriocarcinoma in complete moles (around 2-4% in Western countries), 0.5% in partial moles, and unknown in biparental. But over a 90% cure rate even if it happens, and around 100% if monitored and treated by stage II. 
[Goldstein 2012](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22244665/)

### Moles as a reductio

If being alive, genetically human and genetically distinct are what give something value, then a mole qualifies, even though it's just a growth with no embryo. Each way out adds something to the view:

- **It's not viable:** That adds viability as a requirement, and a pro-life view usually doesn't allow aborting other non-viable pregnancies, such as fatal conditions diagnosed early.
- **It threatens the mother:** Then the justification is the risk, so if the risk is removed it should be wrong to remove the mole. And if a mole's risk were lower than a normal pregnancy's, a woman would have to carry a tumor at that risk for no one's sake.
- **There's no fetal tissue:** That makes the value depend on developing a body, which is no longer the original criterion. If a mole grew a living hand, it would count.
- **Excluding moles by definition:** Excluding them by name is just an ad hoc patch, because you could change one molecule and need another exception.

### The threshold for the mother's life

An exception for the mother's life needs some level of risk, because every pregnancy carries some risk of death. Nobody needs to name the exact number, but the threshold has to run in one direction: if a 10% chance of death justifies abortion, 11% does too. So it can't be left entirely to a doctor's judgment, since the doctor is estimating the risk, and the moral question is how much is enough.

## Maternal risks of pregnancy

Pregnancy itself can carry a large maternal risk. Gestational diabetes 2-9%, pre-eclampsia 2-8%, eclampsia 1.4%, HELLP 0.5-0.9%, VTE around 2/1000 deliveries, placental abruption 0.4-3.8% by region. Importantly, 31% of women report long-term health problems persisting past six months postpartum.
[Haram 2009](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/labs/pmc/articles/PMC2654858) (HELLP)  
[Downes 2017](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/labs/pmc/articles/PMC5683164/) (abruption)  
[Borders 2006](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1016/j.jmwh.2005.10.014) (long-term problems)

Multiple-gestation pregnancies increase maternal risk, with a dose-response from twins to triplets and quadruplets.
[Luke 2008](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18177828/)  
[Wen 2004](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15295375/)
