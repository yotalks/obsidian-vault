> [!summary] Bottom line
> LH, FSH, estrogen and progesterone coordinate ovulation and prepare the uterine lining; implantation only succeeds during a narrow window of receptivity.

Hormones coordinate egg release and preparation of the uterine lining for implantation.

[[Glossary#LH|LH]] stimulates theca cells to produce androgens, which granulosa cells convert into estrogen using aromatase under FSH stimulation. Estrogen helps rebuild the uterine lining, and sustained high estradiol triggers the LH surge that causes ovulation. The remaining follicle becomes the corpus luteum, which produces progesterone to prepare the lining for implantation. Without pregnancy, the corpus luteum regresses, hormone levels fall, and menstruation begins.
[Endotext: menstrual cycle](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK279054/)

Progesterone can suppress the hormonal signals that trigger ovulation. In this rat study, administering progesterone blocked the LH surge and slowed LH pulses through progesterone receptors in different regions of the hypothalamus. This provides evidence about hormonal control of ovulation, but does not directly test emergency contraception or abortion in humans.
[He 2017](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-017-08805-1)

## Implantation

Successful implantation requires coordinated interactions between an embryo and a receptive uterine lining during a limited window. This review explains implantation biology.
[Cha 2012](https://www.nature.com/articles/nm.3012)

In 52 donor-oocyte cycles, pregnancies occurred following embryo transfers on cycle days 17–19, but none followed transfers on day 16 or days 20–24. This supports the importance of timing and uterine receptivity in that treatment setting.
[Navot 1991](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/1986951/)
