> [!summary] Bottom line
> Electrical brain activity has been recorded in early fetuses, and some researchers think pain before 24 weeks can't be ruled out, which may justify proportionate precautions under uncertainty.

## Fetal brain activity

Borkowski and Bernstine recorded non-reflex arc EEG activity from two surgically removed specimens, estimated at 43–45 and 77 days of developmental age. The recordings occurred outside the uterus as the specimens were dying, and the authors interpreted some bursts as terminal electrical discharges. 
[Borkowski 1955](https://doi.org/10.1212/WNL.5.5.362)

Okamoto and Kirikae reported electrical activity in specimens from the third through seventh fetal months, recorded directly from the brain after delivery. They also observed changes after sedatives and stimulants.
[Okamoto 1951](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12980171/)

## Some authors argue that fetal pain may be possible earlier than traditionally assumed

Derbyshire and Bockmann argue that basic pain experience might not require a mature cortex or reflective awareness, and that pain before 24 weeks cannot confidently be ruled out. They discuss possible pain from around 12 weeks. 
[Derbyshire 2020](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31937669/)

## Uncertainty about sentience can justify proportionate precautions

Birch and Browning argue for proportionate welfare precautions when credible neurological indicators suggest possible sentience in neural organoids.
[Birch 2020](https://doi.org/10.1080/15265161.2020.1845858)
