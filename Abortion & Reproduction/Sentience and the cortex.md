> [!summary] Bottom line
> Having a developing cortex doesn't by itself show consciousness, and evidence from decorticate animals and children born without most of a cortex suggests basic awareness may not need one.

## Cortical development

Early cortical development does not by itself establish consciousness.

Meyer and colleagues describe early neurons and organization of the developing human neocortex. This supports the presence of developing brain structures.
[Meyer 2000](https://www.jneurosci.org/content/20/5/1858)

Teleost fish have forebrain structures organized into cell groups rather than a mammalian six-layered neocortex. The authors describe similarities in neural connections that could support comparable functions despite different anatomy. 
[Ito 2009](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC2657732/)

## Consciousness without a cortex

Some evidence challenges the claim that consciousness always requires a cerebral cortex.

Merker’s review argues that upper brainstem structures may support basic conscious experience, while the cortex elaborates its contents. He draws on animal experiments and observations of children with extensive cortical absence. 
[Merker 2007](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17475053/)

Rats whose neocortex was extensively removed shortly after birth still solicited play, chased, and wrestled. Some aspects changed: pinning frequency fell by approximately half when both partners were decorticated. This shows that substantial play behavior can persist without an intact neocortex, but behavior alone does not establish subjective experience.
[Panksepp 1994](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7972392/)

Shewmon and colleagues described four children aged 5–17 with total or near-total cortical absence who displayed behaviors interpreted as awareness, including social interaction, preferences, and associative learning. These cases challenge automatic assumptions of unconsciousness, but do not establish that cortical remnants played no role or that fetuses have equivalent capacities.
[Shewmon 1999](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1469-8749.1999.tb00621.x)
