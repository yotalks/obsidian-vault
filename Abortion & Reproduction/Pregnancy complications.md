> [!summary] Bottom line
> Cesareans can save lives but raise readmission and future placental risks. Preeclampsia and ectopic pregnancy can require early delivery or surgery, though some stable cases can be managed with monitoring or medication.

## Cesarean delivery

Cesarean delivery can be lifesaving but carries immediate and future pregnancy risks.

Cesarean delivery involves abdominal and uterine incisions and can cause hemorrhage, infection, and internal scar tissue called adhesions. The abdominal muscles are usually separated rather than cut.
[StatPearls: cesarean delivery](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK546707/)

Among women without documented prenatal risks, planned first cesareans were associated with more hospital readmissions within 30 days than planned vaginal births: 1.92% versus 0.75%. Wound complications and infections were leading reasons. Initial hospital stays averaged 4.3 versus 2.4 days. This was an observational comparison.
[Declercq 2007](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17329519/)

A previous cesarean was associated with higher risks of placenta previa, where the placenta covers the cervical opening, and placental abruption, where it separates prematurely. In the next pregnancy, previa occurred in 0.63% versus 0.38%, and abruption in 0.95% versus 0.74%, compared with previous vaginal delivery.
[Getahun 2006](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16582111/)

At one hospital, cesarean rates increased from 26% to 36.5% between 2003 and 2009. Concerning fetal heart patterns and stalled labor accounted for half the increase in first cesareans. These indications involve clinical judgment; the findings do not establish that those operations were unnecessary.
[Barber 2011](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21646928/)

A 2018 FIGO position paper argues that scheduling convenience and financial incentives contribute to unnecessary cesareans. It highlights examples where changing reimbursement reduced cesarean rates, supporting payment reform as one approach to reducing overuse. This is a policy argument drawing on other studies.
[Visser 2018](https://www.thelancet.com/journals/lancet/article/PIIS0140-6736(18)32113-5/abstract)

## Preeclampsia

For preeclampsia with severe features before 34 weeks, carefully monitored waiting may be considered when maternal and fetal conditions are stable. Deterioration can require delivery regardless of gestational age. Preeclampsia does not automatically require a cesarean; delivery method depends on obstetric circumstances.
[ACOG 2020](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32443079/)
[ACOG: preeclampsia](https://www.acog.org/womens-health/experts-and-stories/the-latest/7-things-to-know-about-preeclampsia)

## Ectopic pregnancy

Methotrexate interferes with DNA synthesis and cell division, allowing some ectopic pregnancies to be treated without surgery. Its effectiveness depends on patient selection, and repeat doses or surgery may still be necessary.
[Skubisz 2012](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3302016/)

Interstitial pregnancies implant in the portion of the fallopian tube passing through the uterine muscle and can cause severe bleeding if they rupture. Monitoring or medication may be appropriate for selected stable patients, while rupture or instability requires surgery. The review emphasizes that evidence comparing treatments is limited.
[Brincat 2019](https://doi.org/10.1186/s10397-018-1054-4)

In this observational study, 99 of 229 initially asymptomatic patients with suspected ectopic pregnancy experienced spontaneous resolution during monitoring, while 130 eventually required surgery. Higher or rising [[Glossary#hCG|hCG]] levels were associated with needing surgery. The authors emphasize that falling hCG does not eliminate rupture risk, so expectant management requires careful patient selection and close follow-up.
[Rodrigues 2012](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10397-012-0736-6)
