> [!summary] Bottom line
> Americans live about 3.5 years less than people in comparable rich countries. Most of the gap comes from deaths in early and middle adulthood (drug overdoses, firearms, car accidents and homicide) plus circulatory disease and mental/nervous-system diseases, not from cancer care, where the US actually does slightly better.

## The gap

The US spends far more per person on healthcare than any other country, yet has lower life expectancy than its peers. Why it spends so much is covered in [[US healthcare spending]].
![[Life expectancy vs health spending by country.png]]
Chart data: Our World in Data.

In 2018, life expectancy for American men and women was 5.18 and 5.82 years lower than the world leaders, and 3.60 and 3.48 years lower than the average of 17 other high-income countries. Americans aged 25–29 die at nearly 3 times the rate of their peers in those countries.
[Ho 2022](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35188201/)

## Which causes of death drive it

Injuries (drug overdose, firearm-related deaths, motor vehicle accidents and homicide), circulatory diseases, and mental/nervous-system diseases including Alzheimer's account for 86% of American men's shortfall and 67% of women's.

Each bar shows how many years each cause of death adds to the US shortfall compared with that country (A: men, B: women). Bars below zero are causes where the US does better.
![[US life expectancy shortfall by cause and country.webp]]

Against the average of the comparison countries in 2016, the biggest contributors were:
- Men (3.51 years total): circulatory diseases 1.13, drug overdose 0.62, firearm-related 0.54, motor vehicle accidents 0.34
- Women (3.56 years total): circulatory diseases 0.99, respiratory diseases 0.41, Alzheimer's 0.39, drug overdose 0.35, mental/nervous-system 0.34

The US does slightly better on suicide (−0.10 years for men, −0.05 for women), screenable/treatable cancers (−0.10 and −0.06) and lung cancer in men (−0.04).
![[US life expectancy shortfall by cause table.png]]
[Ho 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9154274/)

## Why it's getting worse

The US is falling further behind because death rates at prime adult ages are getting worse and its old-age mortality advantage is eroding.
[Ho 2022](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35188201/)
