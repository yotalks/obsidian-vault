> [!summary] Bottom line
> Several childhood conditions the MAHA report presents as surging have risen mostly on paper. Autism symptoms in the population have stayed flat while diagnoses climbed, as criteria broadened and milder cases were diagnosed; ADHD criteria were loosened in DSM-5; and antibody testing tripled recorded celiac disease without changing the rate of classic cases. Childhood cancer incidence rose modestly, partly from better detection, while cancer deaths fell by more than half.

## Autism

The report says autism affects 1 in 31 children by age 8, up from less than 1 in 10,000 in 1960, and rates of ADHD and other neurodevelopmental disorders are also rising.

Autism rates are largely a function of definitional changes, awareness, and better diagnostic tools and screening. There's no good evidence of a real increase in autism over time.

In California, prevalence by birth year starts climbing around the DSM-III (1980), DSM-III-R (1987) and DSM-IV (1994) revisions.
![[California autism prevalence by birth year.jpg]]
Chart data: [Nevison 2018](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29974300/) (whose authors themselves argue the rise is largely real).

The definition has broadened a lot since it was first described:
- Kanner (1943) emphasized two essential features: severe problems with social interaction and connectedness from the start of life, and an insistence on sameness, including stereotyped movements like body rocking and hand flapping.
- DSM-III (1980) required onset before 30 months, a pervasive lack of responsiveness to other people, and gross deficits in language development.
- DSM-III-R (1987) broadened autism to include children who are socially impaired but not pervasively unresponsive to others.
- DSM-5 (2013) reduced the symptoms to two domains (social communication, and restricted/repetitive behaviors), dropped language delay as a diagnostic symptom, and added over- or under-reactivity to sensory input.

[Wing 2002](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12216059/)

DSM-5 itself didn't add cases: compared with DSM-IV-TR it reduced autism diagnoses by about 21%.
[Kulage 2020](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30852784/)

The clearest evidence comes from Sweden. Among children born 1993–2002, the share with the autism symptom phenotype, measured the same way every year, stayed flat at about 1%, while registered autism diagnoses rose steadily. The authors conclude that administrative changes, not a real change in how often autism occurs, drove the increase.
![[Autism symptoms vs diagnoses in Sweden.jpg]]
[Lundström 2015](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25922345/)

Over the same period, children diagnosed with autism had fewer and fewer symptoms: their average symptom score fell 30% across birth cohorts, and by 50% from 2004 to 2014 among those diagnosed at ages 7–12. Fewer symptoms are now needed for a diagnosis.
[Arvidsson 2018](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29377119/)

## ADHD

DSM-5 loosened the ADHD criteria compared with DSM-IV: adults (17 and older) need 5 symptoms instead of 6, symptoms must start before age 12 instead of 7, impairment at onset is no longer required, evidence of symptoms (not impairment) in two settings is enough, and autism no longer rules out an ADHD diagnosis.
![[ADHD criteria DSM-IV vs DSM-5.jpg]]
Broader criteria are part of why ADHD prevalence has risen, alongside greater awareness, particularly in women and minorities.
[Abdelnour 2022](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36337990/)

## Developmental disabilities

From 2009–2011 to 2015–2017, parent-reported diagnoses rose for any developmental disability (16.2% to 17.8%), ADHD (8.5% to 9.5%), autism (1.1% to 2.5%) and intellectual disability (0.9% to 1.2%), while other developmental delays fell (4.7% to 4.1%). The authors suggest the changes may be related to better awareness and access to healthcare.
[Zablotsky 2019](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31558576/)

From 2019 to 2021, any developmental disability rose from 7.40% to 8.56% of children aged 3–17, while autism (2.79%, 3.49%, 3.05%) and intellectual disability (1.70%, 2.20%, 1.65%) went up and back down.
[CDC NCHS Data Brief 473](https://www.cdc.gov/nchs/data/databriefs/db473.pdf)

## Childhood cancer

The report says childhood cancer incidence has risen over 40% since 1975.

Incidence rose from 14.23 to 18.89 cases per 100,000 children between 1975–1979 and 2010–2019, and the authors note this may partly come from better diagnosis and more complete registration. Over the same period, the age-adjusted death rate fell from 4.9 to 2.3 per 100,000, a drop of more than 50%, and 10-year survival rose from 58.8% to 82.7%. The authors acknowledge that 45 years of changes in diagnosis, treatment and record-keeping limit how comparable the data are.
[Sultan 2025](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39752445/)

## Celiac disease

The rise in celiac disease is mostly artifactual. When antibody blood testing arrived, recorded childhood celiac disease more than tripled (from 2.0 to 7.3 cases per 100,000 children), the median age at diagnosis rose from 2 to 9, and classic presentations fell from 67% to 19% of cases, while the rate of classic celiac disease didn't change significantly. Testing found cases that would previously have gone undiagnosed.
[McGowan 2009](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19948628/)

In Olmsted County, Minnesota, celiac incidence rose from 11.1 to 17.3 per 100,000 person-years between 2000 and 2010, leveling off after 2004, while the share of new cases with the classic symptoms of diarrhea and weight loss fell.
![[Celiac classic presentation trend.jpg]]
[Ludvigsson 2013](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23511460/)
