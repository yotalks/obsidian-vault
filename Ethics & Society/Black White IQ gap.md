> [!summary] Bottom line
> Environmental adjustments, adoption studies and genetic selection tests suggest most of the Black–White IQ gap is environmental; high heritability within groups says nothing about differences between groups.

## Environmental adjustment studies

They assert after adjusting for a variety of environmental factors, the difference in gap of IQ was almost all explained and race barely played a role.
[Cottrell 2015](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25867168/)

Follow up study on the model with wider dataset found that it was less successful at predicting gap, though still found most of the gap was from environmental factors, though race did become statistically significant in this study at around 14%.
[Illinois IDEALS follow-up study](https://www.ideals.illinois.edu/items/105257)

## Adoption and mixed-parentage studies

In this review of transracial adoption studies, they found that most of the gaps in these studies are artifacts with four main methodological problems, the adoptees grew up in unusually nurturing, high [[Glossary#SES|SES]] homes which can boost scores, the Flynn effect, differential dropout between waves of longitudinal studies (lowest scoring whites in Minnesota study), and selective use of the data we have. When accounted for, the East Asian advantage mostly goes away, and the Black-White gap do as well. 
[Thomas 2016](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6526420/pdf/jintelligence-05-00001.pdf)
![[Transracial adoption IQ studies table.png|348]]

The Eyferth study compared IQ scores of white children and Black-white children raised by single German mothers in post-WWII Germany and found no significant difference in IQ scores between groups.
[Wikipedia: Eyferth study](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eyferth_study)

Replicates the findings from the Eyferth Germany study in Japan and found no gap between children of servicemen who had white fathers vs. black fathers. The authors also leaned hereditarian, yet still said the outcomes aligned more with an environmentalist model. Has many of the same issues as the Eyferth study such as small samples, unrepresentative selection, etc.
[Kirkegaard 2019](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/332634952_The_Intelligence_of_Biracial_Children_of_US_Servicemen_in_Northeast_Asia_Results_from_Japan)

## Genetic evidence

"Tests for selection using polygenic scores failed to find evidence of natural selection when the less biased within-family [[Glossary#GWAS|GWAS]] effect sizes were used. Tests for selection using Fst values did not find evidence of natural selection. Expected mean difference in IQ was substantially smaller than postulated by hereditarians, even under unrealistic assumptions that overestimate genetic contribution.  Given these results, hereditarian claims are not supported in the least. Cognitive performance does not appear to have been under diversifying selection in Europeans and Africans. In the absence of diversifying selection, the best case estimate for genetic contributions to group differences in cognitive performance is substantially smaller than hereditarians claim and is consistent with genetic differences contributing little to the Black-White gap."
[Bird 2021](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33529393/)

 In just the last couple years, GWAS has identified hundreds of such SNPs which together explain around 11% of the variance in IQ 
[Cofnas 2019](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/09515089.2019.1697803#abstract)

## Heritability within vs. between groups

Twin studies give an estimate of how much explanatory power the genes are in relation to non-gene explanations. The question being asked is different, it's with respect to the racial IQ difference. Something can be very hereditable (like height), yet still be true that the racial IQ gap is largely explained by environmental factors (like if a group of people were severely malnourished and were shorter than average mainly due to that). You can grant that IQ is largely hereditable, but it still be true that majority of the racial IQ gap is largely explained by environmental factors. 

Lewontin's seed/garden thought experiment takes a genetically varied seed, splits it randomly, and grows one half in rich soil and one half in poor soil. Within each pot, height differences is basically all genetic, but the difference between pots is purely environmental. So high within-group heritability doesn't tell you anything about between-group differences.

> [!note] My take
> I lean for the global racial IQ gap being mostly (over 50%) explained by environment. The gap is much wider worldwide (like double the gap in the USA) despite similar genetics, seems explainable by environmental conditions known to impact IQ, such as malnutrition, lack of education, high childhood mortality from infections, etc.

> [!note] My take
> Even if the hereditarian hypothesis turned out to be true, I don't see how it wouldn't just turn into a prescription about giving more special aid to these minorities, just like we do when we offer special education to mentally handicapped people. They can't do anything about their decreased abilities, so we should try to help them with extra attention that they require to flourish. I doubt the people pushing for this would be on board with more tax money going to extra schooling for minorities, however.

## Iodine

An example of a nutritional deficiency that can have a major effect on IQ is iodine. Just this one deficiency can cause a 10-15 IQ point difference as it's critical in brain development. The world's greatest single cause of preventable brain damage and mental retardation.
[Delange 2001](https://pmj.bmj.com/content/77/906/217)

> [!question] Objections to prepare for
> - Environmental variables like SES may themselves be partly genetic, so adjusting for them could over-correct.
> - Adoption and Eyferth-style studies have small, selected samples (the note already flags this).
> - Polygenic scores are built mostly on European samples and transfer poorly, which limits conclusions either way.
