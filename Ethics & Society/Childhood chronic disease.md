> [!summary] Bottom line
> The 2025 MAHA report says over 40% of US children have a chronic condition, but its own cited source gives 37.5%. Chronic conditions really are rising (from about 23% to 30% of 5–17-year-olds between 1999 and 2018), and 77% of 17–24-year-olds can't join the military without a waiver. Not every rising condition is equally real, though: eczema and food allergies mostly reflect real increases, while the rise in celiac disease is mostly an artifact.

## The claim: over 40% of children

The report says over 40% of the roughly 73 million US children (ages 0–17) have at least one chronic condition "according to the CDC", such as asthma, allergies, obesity, autoimmune diseases or behavioral disorders.
![[Childhood chronic disease claim and military eligibility.png]]

It doesn't link a CDC source for this. The source it does link, the National Survey of Children's Health, gives a lower figure. Weighting each age group by its population:
- 0–5 years: 21.8% of 23,590,173 = 5,142,658
- 6–11 years: 43.3% of 24,824,113 = 10,748,841
- 12–17 years: 46.6% of 25,018,853 = 11,658,786

That's 27,550,285 of 73,433,139 children, or 37.5%, which matches the survey's own figure for all 0–17-year-olds. The difference isn't large, but it shows a lack of rigor in how the report cites.
[NSCH: at least one condition, by age](https://nschdata.org/browse/survey/results?q=7344&g=714&a=11802&r=1)
[NSCH: at least one condition, ages 0–17](https://nschdata.org/browse/survey/results?q=7625)

## The rise is real

The share of 5–17-year-olds with a chronic condition or functional limitation rose from 22.6% in 1999/2000 to 30.2% in 2017/2018, about 130,000 more children each year, with asthma and mental/behavioral conditions among the biggest contributors. This measure doesn't account for obesity.
[Wisk 2025](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40058728/)

## Military eligibility

The report says over 75% of Americans aged 17–24 are ineligible for military service, "primarily" because of obesity, poor fitness or mental health.

The 2020 Qualified Military Available study found 77% are disqualified without a waiver and 23% qualify, down from 29% in earlier estimates. Most of the disqualified (44% of all youth) fail for more than one reason. Among those disqualified for a single reason, the most common are overweight (11%), drug abuse (8%) and medical/physical conditions (7%), with mental health at 4%. The biggest increases from 2013 to 2020 were in mental health and overweight disqualifications. Only 12% are both eligible and not in college.

## Mental health

The report's claims, not yet checked: teen depression rates nearly doubled from 2009 to 2019, and more than 1 in 4 teenage girls had a major depressive episode in 2022.

## Allergies and autoimmune conditions

The report's claims: over 1 in 4 children have allergies; eczema and skin allergies rose from 7.4% to 12.7% of children (1997–1999 to 2016–2018); food allergies rose 88% from 1997 to 2018; celiac disease has increased 5-fold since the 1980s; and inflammatory bowel disease rose 25% over the last decade.
![[Childhood food allergy prevalence 1997-2018.png]]
Chart data: NCHS, National Health Interview Survey.

Some of these increases are mostly real and others are mostly artifacts:
- Eczema and skin allergies: mostly real. Hospitalizations for eczema have risen over two decades, though this study looked at adults.
[Edigin 2022](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34252469/)
- Food allergies: mostly real, partly artifactual.
- Celiac disease: mostly artifactual.

## The US compared with peer nations

Chronic, non-communicable diseases are the leading cause of death worldwide and make up 8 of the 10 top causes of death in the US. They're shaped by medical and non-medical factors, like access to healthcare, physical inactivity, poor nutrition, tobacco and alcohol. See also [[US life expectancy]].
