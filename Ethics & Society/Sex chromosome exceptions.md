> [!summary] Bottom line
> Chromosomes don't always line up with the sex a person develops as. Women with mostly 46,XY cells have menstruated and given birth, a 46,XX man developed a normal male body without an [[Glossary#SRY|SRY]] gene, and a hermaphrodite with 96% 46,XY cells carried pregnancies. A chromosomal definition of sex has to say how to classify people like these.

## Women with XY cells

A woman with a 46,XY karyotype developed as a normal woman. She went through puberty on her own, menstruated regularly, had two unassisted pregnancies and gave birth to a 46,XY daughter with complete gonadal dysgenesis. Her blood cells were 46,XY, her skin cells were [[Glossary#Mosaicism|mosaic]] (80% 46,XY and 20% 45,X), and her ovary was 92.9% 46,XY, 5.9% 45,X, 0.6% 46,XX and 0.6% 47,XXY. Several other relatives across four generations had ambiguous genitals or infertility, in a pattern suggesting X-linked inheritance.
[Dumic 2008](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2190741/)

A 30-year-old woman with normal ovarian function, who had been pregnant at 17, had a 46,XY karyotype in her blood: 99% of blood cells had one X and one Y, and 1% had two Xs. Her ovarian, muscle and skin cells were 46,XX. She was a [[Glossary#Chimerism|chimera]], most likely from blood exchanged with her twin brother, who died shortly after birth.
[Sudik 2001](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11139536/)

A 22-year-old 46,XY woman with complete androgen insensitivity had persistent Müllerian structures, the tissue that normally forms the uterus, cervix and fallopian tubes. Her undeveloped gonads had germ cell tumors and were removed.
[Chen 1999](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10428170/)

## Men with XX cells

A 28-year-old man with a normal male body had a 46,XX karyotype, found while investigating infertility (he produced no sperm). No SRY gene was detected in his DNA. He was the sixth reported SRY-negative XX male with a normal male phenotype.
[Abusheikha 2001](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11278224/)

## Hermaphrodites

A true hermaphrodite with a mosaic karyotype that was 96% 46,XY had an ovotestis and two pregnancies. It was the 11th reported case of fertility in a true hermaphrodite, the second in a mosaic one, and the only pregnancy reported with a male-predominant 96% 46,XY karyotype.
[Schoenhaus 2008](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18394621/)
