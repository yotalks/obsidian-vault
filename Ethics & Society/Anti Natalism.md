If anti-natalists follow their own logic of having the goal to maximally reduce rights violations, then we should sterilize all sentient life in the universe. It would require many more generations of humans to get the technology for that, so we should therefore continue procreating and adopt pragmatic natalism until we achieve the technology and sterilize all sentient life.

## Why nuking all life wouldn't work

Trying this with nuclear weapons would likely backfire. If any life survives, such as in the deep sea, it inherits a world of empty niches and doesn't have to evolve from scratch, since animals with central nervous systems already exist. So the suffering comes back, this time on top of a radiation background.
→ [[Wild animal suffering]]

It has to reach 100% of life with neurons to work. Earth's worst known mass extinction, the end-Permian, killed about 81% of marine species and 70% of land vertebrate species, and life recovered.
[Wikipedia: Permian-Triassic extinction event](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Permian%E2%80%93Triassic_extinction_event)

Releasing carbon dioxide to acidify the oceans isn't an easy route either, because the oceans are so large. Between 1950 and 2026, human emissions lowered average surface ocean pH from about 8.15 to 8.05, and seawater is still alkaline. So a claim that it could kill everything needs actual numbers for how much carbon dioxide would be released and what pH that would give.
[Wikipedia: Ocean acidification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ocean_acidification)

## The asymmetry

Benatar argues that coming into existence is always a serious harm, because of an asymmetry. The absence of pain is good even if no one experiences that good, but the absence of pleasure isn't bad unless someone is deprived of it.
[IEP: Anti-natalism](https://iep.utm.edu/anti-natalism/)

Pointing to a lifeless place like Mars doesn't establish this. There's no pain on Mars, but there's no well-being either, so calling that good assumes the asymmetry instead of showing it. If you defined negativity as a lack of well-being, you'd conclude Mars is full of negativity.

Nor can you compare a whole life of suffering with a single pleasure. Asking whether one orgasm justifies a death is as misleading as asking whether one bad day cancels out a good life. What matters is net well-being across a life.

There's no state of pure happiness, but there's no state of pure torture either, since a person could almost always suffer more. So existing can't mean maximal suffering, and any suffering being greater than the zero of not existing doesn't make it the most possible.
