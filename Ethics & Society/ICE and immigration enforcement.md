## How many people could be deported

The US unauthorized immigrant population reached a record of about 14 million in 2023, 4.1% of the population and 27% of all immigrants. That estimate includes visa overstayers and some asylum seekers, not just people who entered illegally. Claims of 100 million or more are far off.
[Pew 2025](https://www.pewresearch.org/race-and-ethnicity/2025/08/21/u-s-unauthorized-immigrant-population-reached-a-record-14-million-in-2023/)

Mass deportation and mass denaturalization are different policies. Deportation removes noncitizens, and whether mass deportation is justified depends on who is being deported. Denaturalization strips citizenship from people who were naturalized, so doing it en masse would target citizens.

## Minneapolis shooting, January 2026

On January 7, 2026, at the start of a federal immigration operation in Minneapolis, an ICE officer shot and killed a 37-year-old woman in her car. As one agent tried to open the door, the car pulled forward, and another agent fired three shots toward the driver's window. The SUV then crashed into parked cars.

DHS said the woman had "weaponized" her vehicle to try to run over officers, and Homeland Security Secretary Kristi Noem called it domestic terrorism. Minneapolis Mayor Jacob Frey called the shooting reckless and unnecessary. Bystander videos show the moments before and after, and accounts of what they show differ on whether the agent was in the car's path when he fired.

### Operating without local cooperation

One proposal is that where local authorities are fully uncooperative with federal immigration enforcement, ICE should have adequate federal backup in place before conducting operations, until the non-cooperation is resolved.

## Iranian deportations and asylum confidentiality

The US ran deportation flights to Iran in September 2025, December 2025 and January 2026, and some of those deported were asylum seekers.

Federal regulations generally prohibit disclosing information that would reveal or imply that someone applied for asylum, because it can endanger the applicant or their family. A lawsuit filed in July 2026 by the Iranian American Legal Defense Fund and Public Citizen alleges that ICE shared confidential information from Iranian detainees' asylum and immigration files with Iranian officials, in monthly meetings and by mail, and had detainees meet an Iranian official who knew details of their asylum cases. The claim of illegality is about the information sharing, not the removals themselves. DHS called the allegations false.

## Medicaid and undocumented immigrants

Under 42 U.S.C. § 1396b(v), federal Medicaid funding for noncitizens without qualifying immigration status, including undocumented immigrants, is limited to emergency medical care. Federal funds can't pay for their non-emergency care.

California used a fixed proxy percentage, set in the early 2000s and never reassessed, to separate emergency from non-emergency costs in its managed care payments for these noncitizens. HHS's Inspector General found the proxy was 8.49 percentage points too low, so from October 2018 to June 2019 the state claimed $52.7 million in federal funds for prohibited non-emergency services. OIG recommended a refund.
[HHS OIG 2024](https://oig.hhs.gov/reports/all/2024/california-improperly-claimed-527-million-in-federal-medicaid-reimbursement-for-capitation-payments-made-on-behalf-of-noncitizens-with-unsatisfactory-immigration-status/)

Several states use their own funds to extend Medicaid-like coverage to undocumented immigrants. Critics argue this still draws on federal money indirectly, because money is fungible: state dollars spent on that coverage free up other funds within programs the federal government also pays for.
