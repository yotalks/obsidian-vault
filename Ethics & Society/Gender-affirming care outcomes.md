> [!summary] Bottom line
> Newer and larger studies than the 2016 Medicare memo link gender-affirming surgery to less psychological distress and suicidal ideation, but they're observational, and the main Swedish registry study had to walk back its surgery claim after reanalysis. Quality-of-life meta-analyses are weak partly because they pool outcomes surgery isn't meant to change. For puberty blockers, high persistence rates may reflect who gets blockers (the most severe cases) rather than the blockers themselves, and the animal data on memory comes from sheep.

## Surgery and mental health

Good studies of whether transition helps need three things:
- A control group.
- Controls who are trans people who want the treatment but haven't had it. Trans people who don't want it may already have had their dysphoria eased by earlier care, so we wouldn't expect more treatment to help them.
- Past-year outcomes, not lifetime ones, because lifetime suicide attempts include the years before transition.

Two often-cited studies fall short of these:
- A Swedish cohort of all 324 people who had sex reassignment in 1973–2003 found higher mortality (2.8 times), suicide (19.1 times), suicide attempts and psychiatric hospitalization than the general population. But the controls were the general population, not trans people who wanted reassignment and didn't get it.
[Dhejne 2011](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21364939/)
- 41% of trans and gender non-conforming respondents to a US national survey reported a lifetime suicide attempt, compared with 4.6% of the US population. But lifetime attempts include attempts before transition.
[Haas 2014](https://williamsinstitute.law.ucla.edu/wp-content/uploads/Trans-GNC-Suicide-Attempts-Jan-2014.pdf)

The 2016 Medicare memo didn't include a 2013 Ontario study (433 trans people) that meets these criteria. Among people who had medically transitioned, 23% had past-year suicidal ideation and 1% attempted suicide, compared with 55% and 27% among people who planned to transition but hadn't started. Strong parental support was also linked to less ideation (19% vs 44%) and fewer attempts (2% vs 15%).
![[Suicidality by transition status (Bauer 2013).jpg]]
[Bauer 2013](https://doi.org/10.7202/1017478ar)

In the 2015 US Transgender Survey (27,715 respondents), people who'd had gender-affirming surgery at least 2 years earlier had lower [[Glossary#OR|odds]] of past-month severe psychological distress (0.58), past-year smoking (0.65) and past-year suicidal ideation (0.56) than people who wanted surgery but hadn't had it, after adjusting for demographics and other gender-affirming care. There was a dose response: compared with people who'd had none of the surgeries they wanted, those who'd had all of them had lower odds of distress (0.47), ideation (0.44) and suicide attempts (0.44) than those who'd had only some (0.70, 0.72 and 0.70). It's a cross-sectional survey of people reached through community outreach, not a random sample.
![[Mental health with and without surgery (Almazan 2021).jpg]]
[Almazan 2021](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33909023/)

A Swedish total-population study originally reported that more time since surgery was linked to less mental health treatment. After letters questioned its methods, the authors reanalyzed the data comparing people with gender incongruence who'd had surgery with those who hadn't, and found no advantage of surgery for mood or anxiety care visits, prescriptions, or hospitalization after suicide attempts. The journal said the original conclusion was too strong.
[Bränström 2020](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31581798/)
[Correction 2020](https://doi.org/10.1176/appi.ajp.2020.1778correction)

If a procedure reduced suicide in a population by 50%, it would be worth doing even if part of the effect were placebo, as with cosmetic procedures that reduce depression.

## Quality of life

A network meta-analysis of 7 studies (420 people) found low-quality evidence that gender-affirming surgery likely improves quality of life, with the best results in trans men who had chest surgery. Heterogeneity was significant.
[Passos 2020](https://doi.org/10.1007/s13178-019-00394-0)

Problems with it:
- It doesn't report an overall effect size clearly, and it has no hard outcomes like suicidal ideation or attempts.
- It pools different quality-of-life questionnaires that may not measure the same construct.
- Some of the pooled domains, like body pain, physical health, spirituality or breast satisfaction, aren't what surgery is meant to change. The purpose of surgery is to relieve dysphoria, so including irrelevant domains may dilute the effect. A better analysis would pick out the relevant domains and redo it.
- Critics say only trans men's chest surgery was significant, but it was just the only procedure significant on all domains; other procedures were significant on some.

## Puberty blockers

In the first 70 Dutch adolescents given puberty blockers, behavioral and emotional problems and depressive symptoms decreased and general functioning improved, while anxiety, anger, gender dysphoria and body satisfaction didn't change. None stopped blockers, and all went on to cross-sex hormones.
[de Vries 2011](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20646177/)

That everyone continued doesn't show blockers lock in a trans identity. Blockers are typically prescribed for severe dysphoria, and the intensity of childhood dysphoria predicts whether it persists, so comparisons need to adjust for how severe the dysphoria was.
[Steensma 2013](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23702447/)
→ [[Gender dysphoria in the DSM-5]]

The UK's Gender Identity Development Service cited estimates that 2–27% of young children with gender dysphoria persist after puberty, and reported outcomes for its first 44 young adolescents on blockers.
[Carmichael 2016](https://web.archive.org/web/20191208040029/http://wpath2016.conferencespot.org/62620-wpathv2-1.3138789/t001-1.3140111/f009a-1.3140266/0706-000523-1.3140268)
[Carmichael 2016 talk](https://av-media.vu.nl/mediasite/Play/581e58c338984dafb455c72c56c0bfa31d?catalog=2d1908914e3f4936a4fa2e9766ae0d0d21)

Among transgender adults who'd wanted puberty blockers as teens, those who got them had lower odds of lifetime suicidal ideation (0.3) than those who didn't, after adjusting for demographics and family support.
[Turban 2020](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31974216/)

In UK adolescents with gender dysphoria, psychosocial functioning improved after 6 months of psychological support, and improved more after 12 months of blockers.
[Costa 2015](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26556015/)

In rams, blocking puberty with a GnRH agonist reduced long-term spatial memory (1.5 times slower in a maze), and this persisted after treatment stopped.
[Hough 2017](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27987429/)

For comparison, human simple reaction time slows by only about 0.55 ms per year of age between 18 and 65.
[Woods 2015](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25859198/)

## Publication bias

Randomized trials with null findings got similar citations, attention and views to trials that supported their hypotheses.
[Murray 2019](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31087013/)
