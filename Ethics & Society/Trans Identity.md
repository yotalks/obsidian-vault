> [!summary] Bottom line
> Trans debates mostly come down to how we define man and woman. On a definition based on weighted gamete-associated traits, a trans adult with enough of those traits is that gender. And being transgender isn't a mental illness in itself, because it's logically possible for a trans person not to have one.

## Defining man and woman

All trans debates pretty much boil down to semantics about what constitutes a man or woman. Say we define a man as someone who possesses a sufficiently greater sum of weighted traits that more closely associate with small gametes, and a woman as someone who possesses a sufficiently greater sum of weighted traits that more closely associate with large gametes, and they are over the age of 18. Then a trans person who possesses those characteristics is that gender.

## Is being trans a mental illness?

Transgenderism is the condition of having a gender that doesn't match the body you were born with. The dictionary notes the word is often used by people who see this as a bad thing.
[Cambridge Dictionary](https://dictionary.cambridge.org/us/dictionary/english/transgenderism)

Nothing in that definition requires distress or impairment, so being trans can't be a mental illness by definition:
- **P1:** If transgenderism is a mental illness, then it's not logically possible for a transgender person to not have a mental illness. (P ⇒ ¬Q)
- **P2:** It's logically possible for a transgender person to not have a mental illness. (Q)
- **C:** So transgenderism is not a mental illness. (∴ ¬P)
