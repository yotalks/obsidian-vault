> [!summary] Bottom line
> Children develop a sense of their gender in stages, and most have a stable gender identity by about age 4. The David Reimer case, where a boy raised as a girl never identified as one, is often cited as evidence that gender identity isn't simply taught.

## Milestones

Gender identity develops in stages:
- **Around age 2:** children become aware of the physical differences between boys and girls.
- **Before age 3:** most can label themselves as a boy or a girl.
- **By age 4:** most have a stable sense of their gender identity.

Children learn gender role behavior over the same period, and cross-gender play is a normal part of development whatever their later gender identity.
[AAP: Gender identity development in children](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/ages-stages/gradeschool/Pages/Gender-Identity-and-Gender-Confusion-In-Children.aspx)

## David Reimer

John Money, the psychologist who introduced the terms gender identity, gender role and sexual orientation, believed gender identity was largely learned. After David Reimer's penis was destroyed in a botched circumcision as a baby, Money oversaw his reassignment and upbringing as a girl, with his identical twin Brian as a comparison. David never identified as a girl, and transitioned back to living as a male as a teenager.

Money also made the twins rehearse sexual acts with each other during their visits, and on at least one occasion photographed them. Brian died of an antidepressant overdose in 2002, and David killed himself in 2004. Their deaths can't be attributed to the reassignment alone, given the abuse.
[Wikipedia: David Reimer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Reimer)
