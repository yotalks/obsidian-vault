> [!summary] Bottom line
> In English-speaking countries, about 2 in 5 people say they'd use the pronoun that matches a trans person's lived gender, about 1 in 5 would use "they", and about a quarter don't know. Only 12–22% would use the pronoun of the person's birth sex, and Americans were the most likely to.

## How people say they'd refer to trans people

In a 2018 Ipsos survey of Great Britain, Australia, Canada and the US, people were asked what pronoun they'd use for someone born one sex who dresses and lives as the other. The question was only asked in English-speaking countries, because English pronouns are gendered.
- **Trans women:** 39–44% would say "she", 17–23% "they", 12–22% "he", and 22–25% didn't know.
- **Trans men:** 38–43% would say "he", 18–24% "they", 13–21% "she", and 23–25% didn't know.
- Americans were the most likely to use the birth-sex pronoun (22% for trans women, 21% for trans men), compared with 12–14% in the other three countries.
