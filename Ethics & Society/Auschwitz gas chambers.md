> [!summary] Bottom line
> Chemical tests of the Auschwitz gas chamber ruins, including tests by people trying to disprove the gassings, find more cyanide there than in comparison rooms. The Kraków forensic institute found cyanide in all five crematoria and none in living quarters. Delousing chambers have far more because killing lice takes much higher concentrations for much longer than killing people. And the crematoria alone could have burned roughly 0.9–1.1 million bodies, before counting open-air burning pits.

## Cyanide residues

Both the homicidal gas chambers and the delousing chambers used Zyklon B (hydrogen cyanide). The delousing chambers have much more cyanide residue, often visible as blue staining (Prussian blue). That's expected: killing lice needs far higher concentrations for far longer than killing people, and nothing shows the gas chambers' conditions would have produced Prussian blue. Some tests, like Leuchter's, also ground whole chunks of brick even though cyanide only penetrates about 10 micrometers, diluting the samples by unknown amounts.
[Wikipedia: Leuchter report](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leuchter_report)

Comparing gas chamber samples with samples from rooms that weren't gas chambers or delousing chambers, in mg of cyanide per kg:

| Study | Gas chambers | Other rooms | Delousing chambers |
|---|---|---|---|
| Leuchter 1988 | 1.14 (24 samples) | 0.93 (12) | 1,050 (1) |
| Rudolf 1993 | 4.83 (3) | 0.90 (9) | |
| Ball | 0.57 (3) | 0.07 (1) | |
| Markiewicz 1994 | 0.08 (36) | 0 (8) | 0.22 (18) |
| Mattogno | 1.24 (2) | 0 (2) | |

- **Leuchter:** gas chamber samples averaged slightly higher, but the difference wasn't significant (p ≈ 0.7). 15 of 24 gas chamber samples and 7 of 12 others were below detection. Treating those as half the detection limit instead of zero doesn't change this.
- **Rudolf:** gas chamber samples averaged over 5 times higher (p = 0.015 with a standard t-test). With only 3 gas chamber samples and very unequal spread, a test that doesn't assume equal variances gives p ≈ 0.2, so it's suggestive rather than conclusive.
- **Markiewicz:** the Institute of Forensic Research in Kraków used a method that excludes iron cyanides like Prussian blue, so its numbers aren't comparable with the others. Cyanide was detectable in 29 of 36 gas chamber samples and in none of the 8 samples from living quarters, which were likely fumigated only once, during the 1942 typhus epidemic, before the Birkenau crematoria existed.
[Markiewicz 1994](https://phdn.org/archives/holocaust-history.org/auschwitz/chemistry/iffr/report.shtml)
[Wikipedia: Rudolf Report](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rudolf_Report)

## Cremation capacity

Estimating how many bodies the five Auschwitz crematoria could burn, from their number of muffles (oven chambers), approximate operating periods, and assumed downtime:

| Crematorium | Muffles | Operating | Days in use | Muffle-hours |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| I | 6 | Jun 1940 – Jun 1943 | 582 | 83,808 |
| II | 15 | Mar 1943 – Oct 1944 | 330 | 118,800 |
| III | 15 | Jun 1943 – Oct 1944 | 238 | 85,680 |
| IV | 8 | Mar 1943 – Oct 1944 | 336 | 64,512 |
| V | 8 | Apr 1943 – Jan 1945 | 391 | 75,072 |
| **Total** | | | | **427,872** |

Days in use subtract assumed downtime of 500 days for Crematorium I and 250 days for each of the others. At 2 bodies per muffle per hour that's about 856,000 bodies, and at 2.5 about 1.07 million. That's a conservative rate: the official daily capacities (340 for Crematorium I, 1,440 each for II and III, 768 each for IV and V) work out to about 2.4 bodies per muffle per hour for Crematorium I and 4 for the others.

Of about 1.1 million people murdered at Auschwitz, around 960,000 were Jews. The crematoria weren't the only method: during the 1944 deportation of Hungarian Jews, bodies were also burned in open-air pits.
