> [!summary] Bottom line
> Animals deserve rights like humans' (adjusted for their traits) unless someone can name a trait that justifies treating them differently. Every trait commonly offered (species, intelligence, language, soul, culture, law, taste) fails, because some humans lack it too, or it would license treating humans the same way.

## The argument

> [!note] My take
> I view animal rights as the logical extension of trait-adjusted human rights. If one wants to deny animals be given these rights and say that humans are above the moral threshold that protects them from exploitation and being killed for food, raped for milk, etc, they need to name the trait that accounts for the differential normative evaluation. Traits named usually lead to insane positions or just contradicting oneself.

## Common rebuttals and responses

Each rebuttal proposes a trait; each response imagines a human without that trait.

### Biology and species

**"The trait that makes killing animals ok is that they lack the cluster of genetics that humans have that distinguishes homo sapiens sapiens as a species."**
If a genetics test were done on your mother and it turned out they lack those genetics, would it be ok to rape, kill, and eat her?

**"The trait is that humans have the taxonomic classification of homo sapiens sapiens and animals do not."**
If we restricted the criteria used to categorize homo sapiens to exclude some populations like black people for example, would it be ok to kill and eat them?

**"The trait that makes it ok is whether I can recognize them as human."**
If we took your mom's brain and consciousness and put it in a cow, would it be ok to kill and eat her?

### Mental and social capacities

**"The trait is that humans have human level intelligence and animals don't"**
If we found humans with the same level of intelligence as an animal, would it be ok to kill and eat them?

**"The trait that makes the difference is that humans have the capacity to use and/or understand language, and animals don't."**
If we found humans with the same linguistic capabilities as animals, would it be ok to kill and eat them?

**"The trait that makes it ok is that humans can enter into social contracts where animals cannot."**
If we found humans with the same ability to enter into social contracts as animals, would it be ok to kill and eat them?

**"The trait that makes it ok is that humans have moral agency and animals don't."**
If we found humans with the same capacity for moral agency as animals, would it be ok to kill and eat them?

### Religion

**"The trait that makes it ok is that humans have a soul and animals don't."**
If we had a soul scanner that could detect whether a being had a soul, and it turned out you don't have a soul, would it be ok to kill and eat you?

**"The difference is that humans were made in the image of God and animals weren't"**
If we had an image of God scanner that could detect whether or not a being was made in the image of God, and it turned out all jews were not made in the image of God, would it be ok to commit another holocaust on them?

**"The trait that makes the difference is that God's word, as articulated in the Bible, allows us to consume animals."**
If the bible allowed raping kids, would it be ok to rape kids?

### Culture, law and power

**"The trait that makes the difference is that our culture accepts the consumption of animals, whereas our culture doesn't accept the consumption of humans."**
If our culture developed a tolerance for the consumption of humans, would it be OK to farm, kill, and eat humans?

**"The trait that makes the difference is that animal consumptions is traditional for humans, whereas human consumption isn't."**
If there was a population with a cultural tradition for cannibalism, would it then be ok to farm, kill, and eat humans?

**"The trait that makes the difference is that it is illegal to kill and eat humans, but isn't for animals."**
If cannibalism was made legal, would it then be ok to farm, rape, kill, and eat humans?

**"The trait that makes the difference is that humans have dominion over animals, but animals do not have dominion over humans."**
If xenomorphs came down and had dominion over humans, would it be ok for them to farm, kill, and eat us?

### Usefulness

**"The trait that makes the difference is that animals are rich in nutrients, but humans aren't."**
If it turned out that human meat was equally as rich in nutrients as the meat of animals, would it be ok to farm, kill, and eat humans?

**"The trait that makes the difference is that the meat of animals is currently instrumental to sustaining human civilization, but the meat of humans isn't."**
If we found a civilization of humans who were sustaining themselves on human meat in the same way we sustain ourselves on animal meat, is it ok for them to farm, kill, and eat those humans?

**"The trait that makes the difference is that animals are tasty, but humans aren't"**
If it turned out that human meat was just as tasty as animal meat, would it be ok to farm, kill, and eat humans?

> [!question] Objections to prepare for
> - It isn't one trait but a cluster of them (intelligence, language, moral agency together); can a cluster do work no single trait can?
> - Humans who lack a trait still belong to a kind whose normal members have it (Beckwith's 'kind of being' view in [[Abortion Ethics]]); does moral status come from your kind rather than your individual traits?
> - Some people bite the bullet and accept the conclusion for the hypothetical human; is the argument persuasive against someone consistent but callous?
> - Relational views ground duties in relationships and communities, not traits; does trait-equalization even engage them?
