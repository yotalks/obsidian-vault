> [!summary] Bottom line
> Across meta-analyses of cohorts and randomized trials, replacing saturated fat with polyunsaturated fat lowers cardiovascular events and deaths; in trials the reduction is around 30%, similar to statins.

There are many systematic reviews and meta analyses including RCTs or prospective cohort studies or both that show saturated fat intake is linked with negative health outcomes particularly with respect to morbidities like [[Glossary#CVD|CVD]] events, or mortality like CVD deaths.

## Replacing saturated fat with polyunsaturated fat

During a 4-10 year follow up, 5249 coronary events and 2155 coronary deaths occurred among 344,696 people. For a 5% lower energy intake from [[Glossary#SFA|SFAs]] and a concomitant higher energy intake from [[Glossary#PUFA|PUFAs]], there was a significant inverse association between PUFAs and risk of coronary events.
[Jakobsen 2009](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pubmed/19211817)

Cardiovascular mortality was reduced by 9% and cardiovascular events by 16%, which was attenuated in a sensitivity analysis that excluded a trial using fatty fish. Trials with at least two years' follow up provided stronger evidence of protection from cardiovascular events.
[Hooper 2001](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC30550/)

Replacing saturated fats with mixed omega 6 and omega 3 fatty acid PUFA diets lowered the risk of non-fatal heart attack and death from CVD by 22%. The 13% increased risk when omega 6 fatty acids alone were substituted were not statistically significant.
[Ramsden 2010](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pubmed/21118617)

A 5% of energy increment in [[Glossary#LA|LA]] intake replacing energy from saturated fat intake was associated with a 9% lower risk of [[Glossary#CHD|CHD]] events.
[Farvid 2014](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC4334131/)

Looking at over 13,614 participants with 1,042 CHD events, the average rated PUFA consumption was 5% energy in controls. The overall pooled risk reduction was 19% corresponding to a 10% reduced CHD risk for each 5% energy of increased PUFA, without evidence for statistical heterogeneity. Meta regression identified study duration as an independent determinant of risk reduction, with studies of longer duration showing greater benefits.
[Mozaffarian 2010](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pubmed/20351774/)

There was found to be convincing evidence that partial replacement of SFA with PUFA decreases risk of CVD, especially in men.
[Schwab 2014](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC4095759/)

This updated review suggested that reducing saturated fat by reducing and/or modifying dietary fat reduced risk of CVD events by 14%
[Hooper 2016](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pubmed/27224282)

RCTs that lowered intake of dietary SFA and replaced it with PUFA vegetable oil reduced CVD by around 30%, similar to the reductions achieved by statins.
[Sacks 2017](https://www.ahajournals.org/doi/10.1161/CIR.0000000000000510)

Replacement of SFA with unsaturated fat lowers CHD events and replacement with LA lowers CHD deaths as well.
[Clifton 2017](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0939475317302375)

Meta analysis of prospective cohort studies found a significant association between SFA intake and CHD mortality.
[Mazidi 2020](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0261561420301461)
