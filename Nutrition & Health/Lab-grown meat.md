> [!summary] Bottom line
> Lab-grown meat is real animal meat grown from animal cells in bioreactors instead of from a slaughtered animal. It's probably the most effective way to end factory farming, because it can make animal agriculture obsolete rather than relying on persuading billions of people to give up meat. Made without fetal bovine serum, it requires no animal agriculture and is compatible with veganism. It's already been approved for sale in Singapore and the US, but it's still expensive and small-scale, and some places have banned it.

## What it is

Lab-grown (cultured or cultivated) meat is meat tissue grown from animal cells in a bioreactor, so it's actual meat rather than a plant-based imitation.

Early methods grew the cells in fetal bovine serum, which is taken from calf fetuses at slaughter. Serum-free media now exist. Researchers at the cultured meat company Mosa Meat developed a chemically defined medium that let cow muscle stem cells mature into muscle fibres without serum. It was as effective as the standard serum-based method and worked for building 3D muscle tissue.
[Messmer 2022](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37118488/)

## Why it matters for animals

Most meat eaters won't give up meat for plant foods, and the most effective way to end animal agriculture is to make it obsolete. Lab meat that matches meat's taste and texture without slaughter does that, which is why supporting it is likely more impactful than most other animal advocacy. Ethical questions about an industry tend to get clearer once what it provides is no longer needed.

Lab meat made without fetal bovine serum is compatible with veganism. It creates no demand for animal agriculture beyond what buying plants does, so buying it can even help it take hold in the market. For the same reason, vegans who oppose lab meat may be working against the end of factory farming.

Factory farming also breeds disease. Animals are packed together in large numbers, pathogens emerge, and whole flocks are then killed. Meat grown in controlled bioreactors avoids this, so safety concerns about lab meat don't favor farmed meat.

## Feasibility and regulation

Lab meat is a technology about 20–30 years old competing with one about 10,000 years old, so its current cost and small scale aren't surprising. Early cars faced many of the same feasibility objections.

It's already been sold:
- **Singapore** approved the first commercial sale in December 2020, cultured chicken from Eat Just.
- **The US** cleared cultivated chicken from Upside Foods and Good Meat in June 2023, and cultivated salmon from Wildtype in May 2025.
- **Bans:** Italy's government moved to ban it in 2023, and Florida and Alabama have banned its manufacture and sale.
