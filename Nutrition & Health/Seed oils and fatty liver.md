> [!summary] Bottom line
> The idea that linoleic acid causes fatty liver rests on oxidized linoleic acid metabolites and on liver damage in tube-fed infants given soybean oil emulsions. That liver damage seems to come from the oil's plant sterols, not linoleic acid. In controlled human trials, swapping saturated fat for omega-6 PUFA lowered liver fat, and when people overate, saturated fat built up far more liver and visceral fat than PUFA did. Total calorie intake looks more important than fat type.

## The hypothesis

A review proposed a central role for omega-6 PUFA ([[Glossary#LA|LA]]) in [[Glossary#NAFLD|NAFLD]]: oxidative stress in the liver oxidizes LA into metabolites that may mark, or add to, liver damage.
[Santoro 2013](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26405460/)

## Intravenous lipid emulsions

The most direct evidence cited comes from parenteral nutrition, feeding through a vein. Infants on long-term parenteral nutrition with soybean oil emulsions often develop cholestasis, a liver disease where bile flow is impaired. Fish oil emulsions have been used to treat it.
[Fell 2015](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26374182/)

Soybean emulsions also carry plant sterols (phytosterols) from the soy oil and soy lecithin. Affected infants have high blood phytosterols, the association with cholestasis is striking, and in piglets phytosterols alone reduced bile flow. So phytosterols are the leading explanation.
[Clayton 1998](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9437703/)

In a mouse model of parenteral-nutrition liver disease, the soybean oil solution caused liver damage, while fish oil or fat-free solutions didn't. Stigmasterol, a phytosterol from the soy oil, was enough on its own to promote cholestasis and liver injury.
[El Kasmi 2013](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24107776/)

Infusing too much too fast is a separate problem, called fat overload syndrome:
- A 2-year-old developed it after an accidental rapid infusion of SMOFlipid, a mixed soybean, MCT, olive and fish oil emulsion. Earlier reports all involved soybean emulsions. [Hojsak 2014](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23520135/)
- Six infants accidentally given rapid infusions of a fish oil emulsion showed no signs of it. [Gura 2010](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20702846/)

Human trials comparing emulsions:
- In 19 neonates randomized to fish oil or soybean oil emulsion, cholestasis was too rare to compare and the trial stopped early. The fish oil emulsion was well tolerated, with no difference in growth, infections or development. [Nehra 2014](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23770843/)
- In 73 adults on 4 weeks of parenteral nutrition, the mixed SMOFlipid emulsion gave lower liver enzymes and bilirubin than a pure soybean emulsion, though both stayed within the normal range, and fewer serious adverse events (2 vs 8). Inflammatory markers didn't change in either group. [Klek 2013](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22796064/)

These are infusions straight into the blood in sick patients, a very different exposure from eating seed oils.

## Diet trials

In a single-arm, unblinded trial, 20 obese youths with fatty liver ate a low omega-6 to omega-3 ratio (4:1) diet for 12 weeks without losing weight. Liver fat fell by 26%, ALT by 34%, and insulin sensitivity improved, and oxidized LA metabolites fell.
[Van Name 2020](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32652034/)

But this wasn't a test of cutting LA. The ratio came from adding omega-3 (fish, nuts, and a cooking oil with twice as much omega-3 as omega-6), and plasma LA didn't change over the trial. There was no control group.
[Van Name 2020 full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7467848/)

In a 10-week trial, 67 abdominally obese adults were randomized to a diet high in omega-6 PUFA or one high in [[Glossary#SFA|saturated fat]] (mainly butter), with calories and macronutrients unchanged. Liver fat was lower on the PUFA diet (by 16% on MRI and 34% on spectroscopy). PCSK9 and some inflammation-related markers were lower too, and among people who stuck to the diet, LDL, triglycerides and insulin were lower. The high omega-6 diet showed no signs of increased inflammation or oxidative stress.
[Bjermo 2012](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22492369/)

When 39 young, normal-weight adults were overfed muffins made with either sunflower oil (PUFA) or palm oil (saturated fat) for 7 weeks, both groups gained similar weight. But saturated fat raised liver fat much more and doubled the increase in visceral fat, while PUFA gave nearly 3 times the gain in lean tissue, so less of the gained weight was fat.
[Rosqvist 2014](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24550191/)

## Observational studies

- In 162 people with type 2 diabetes, higher red blood cell PUFA (total PUFA, DHA, and the omega-6s arachidonic acid and DGLA) was linked to less liver fat. LA itself wasn't among the significant results. [Petit 2012](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22209679/)
- In 36 women, those with NAFLD ate more calories, fat, saturated fat and PUFA. After adjustment, liver fat was linked to total calories, total fat and saturated fat. [Cheng 2016](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27618908/)
- In 55 NAFLD patients and 88 healthy controls, the clearest difference was that patients ate more calories. Relative fat, carbohydrate and fructose intake didn't differ, and cutting calories after dietary advice improved liver enzymes. [Wehmeyer 2016](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27281105/)

So the observational data link liver fat to saturated fat and excess calories, not to PUFA. There's no clear evidence that LA uniquely causes fatty liver.
