> [!summary] Bottom line
> For heart disease, the evidence on omega-3 supplements is mixed. The largest meta-analysis found small reductions of about 3–8% in heart attack, coronary heart disease and cardiovascular death, but most other meta-analyses, including Cochrane's, found little or no effect on mortality or cardiovascular events, and larger, longer trials tend to show smaller benefits. Stroke risk doesn't change. In pregnancy, the largest trial found DHA didn't reduce postpartum depression or improve children's cognition, but it cut early preterm births and low birth weight. Vegans have low blood EPA and DHA, but similar to omnivores who eat little fish, and a small dose of algal oil raises it.

## Cardiovascular disease

A meta-analysis of 13 randomized trials (127,477 people, about 5 years on average) pooled marine omega-3 supplements, mostly around 850 mg a day of [[Glossary#EPA|EPA]] and DHA. Leaving out REDUCE-IT, which used 4 g a day of pure EPA, supplements lowered the risk of:
- heart attack ([[Glossary#RR|RR]] 0.92, 95% CI 0.86–0.99)
- [[Glossary#CHD|CHD]] death (RR 0.92, 0.86–0.98)
- total CHD (RR 0.95, 0.91–0.99)
- [[Glossary#CVD|CVD]] death (RR 0.93, 0.88–0.99)
- total CVD (RR 0.97, 0.94–0.99)

Stroke risk didn't change (RR 1.05, 0.98–1.14). Including REDUCE-IT made every benefit larger, but the trials then disagreed more with each other. Risk fell further at higher doses for total CVD and major vascular events, but the authors call that analysis highly exploratory, since nearly all the trials used the same dose.
[Hu 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6806028/)

One author has received research support from the California Walnut Commission and honoraria from supplement companies (Metagenics, Standard Process), outside this work. One of the included trials, VITAL, was led by another author and used fish oil donated by its manufacturers (Pronova BioPharma and BASF).

The effects are small per person. The authors argue they could still prevent many events because heart disease is so common. Whether the benefit holds for primary prevention is the open question → [[Omega 6 3 ratio]]

### Other meta-analyses

Most other meta-analyses of randomized trials found no clear benefit:
- **Rizos 2012** (20 trials, 68,680 people): no significant effect on all-cause mortality (RR 0.96, 0.91–1.02), cardiac death (0.91, 0.85–0.98), sudden death, heart attack or stroke, using a significance threshold of P < .0063 to adjust for multiple comparisons. Overall, omega-3 PUFA supplementation was not associated with a lower risk of all-cause mortality, cardiac death, sudden death, myocardial infarction, or stroke based on relative and absolute measures of association.
[Rizos 2012](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22968891/)
- **Hooper 2006** (48 trials, 36,913 people, plus 41 cohorts): long chain and shorter chain omega 3 fats do not have a clear effect on total mortality (RR 0.87, 0.73–1.03), combined cardiovascular events (0.95, 0.82–1.12), or cancer. The few trials at low risk of bias showed no effect.
[Hooper 2006](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16565093/)
- **Kotwal 2012** (20 trials, 63,030 people): omega 3s had some protection on vascular deaths (RR 0.86, 0.75–0.99), but no effect on all cause mortality, composite cardiovascular events or coronary events. Side effects, mostly gastrointestinal, were 18% more common.
[Kotwal 2012](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23110790/)

The most extensive systematic assessment of effects of omega 3 fats on cardiovascular health to date found moderate to high quality evidence that increasing EPA and DHA has little to no effect on mortality or cardiovascular health (mainly supplement trials). Previous suggestions of benefits seem to come from trials with high risk of bias. Low quality evidence suggests ALA may slightly reduce CVD event risk, CHD, and arrhythmia.
[Abdelhamid 2018 (Cochrane)](https://www.cochrane.org/CD003177/VASC_omega-3-intake-cardiovascular-disease)

This meta-analysis showed omega 3s had no significant association with fatal or nonfatal CHD or any major vascular events. It shows no support for such supplements for healthy people.
[Aung 2018](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29387889/)

Omega 3s weren't associated with significantly decreased mortality or restenosis prevention, though the probability of some benefit remains high, But in meta-regressions, there was an over 90 percent probability that larger studies and those with longer follow up were associated with smaller benefits.
[Filion 2010](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20525225/)

Looking at over 15,000 patients with diabetes but no evidence of ASCVD given 1g capsules of omega 3 or placebo, there were no significant difference in risk of serious vascular events.
[ASCEND 2018](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30146932/)

Insufficient evidence exists to suggest a benefit for omega 3 PUFA supplements in adults with peripheral arterial disease WRT cardiovascular events and serious clinical outcomes.
[Enns 2014](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24885361/)

The exception is people who already have heart disease taking high doses. In 11 trials of at least 1 g a day for at least a year (15,348 people), all-cause mortality and stroke didn't change, but cardiac death (RR 0.68, 0.56–0.83), sudden death (0.67) and heart attack (0.75) fell.
[Casula 2013](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23958480/)

## Pregnancy

A review of omega-3 across the lifespan covers several pregnancy trials.
[Swanson 2012](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3262608/)

In the largest, 2,399 pregnant women took 800 mg a day of DHA or vegetable oil capsules from mid-pregnancy to birth. DHA didn't reduce postpartum depressive symptoms (9.7% vs 11.2%, not significant), and children's cognitive and language scores at 18 months didn't differ. According to the review, there were fewer early preterm births (before 34 weeks: 1.1% vs 2.3%) and fewer low birth weight babies.
[Makrides 2010](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20959577/)

Smaller trials point to less allergy and asthma:
- In 533 women given fish oil (2.7 g a day of omega-3), olive oil or nothing from about week 30, the children of the fish oil group had 63% lower asthma risk over 16 years and 87% lower allergic asthma risk than the olive oil group. Only 19 children were diagnosed with asthma. [Olsen 2008](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18614738/)
- In 145 women from allergic families, fish oil from week 25 through breastfeeding lowered food allergy and allergic eczema in their babies at 1 year. [Furuhjelm 2009](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19489765/)

Small trials also found better infant problem-solving and hand-eye coordination with DHA, but the much larger trial above found no cognitive benefit.

Breast milk from vegans had a lot more unsaturated fat and total omega 3 fats and lower saturated fats, trans fats, and omega 6 to omega 3 ratios than vegetarians and omnivores. DHA concentrations in breast milk were low regardless, and were reflective of seafood intake and supplement use.
[Perrin 2019](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30051170/)


## Depression and anxiety

Omega 3s likely has little to no effect in preventing depression or anxiety symptoms.
[Deane 2021](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31647041/)

## Muscle in older adults

The results of several studies show dietary supplementation with fish oil omega 3 stimulates MPS and improves muscle mass and function in sedentary older adults and augments the training induced increase in strength in older adults. The exact mechanisms are unknown.
[Gray 2018](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29232264/)

## Vegans and omega-3 status

The differences between circulating omega 3s, EPA and DHA, between vegans, vegetarians, omnivores, fish eaters, etc aren't substantial.
[Welch 2010](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20861171/)

Vegans have low baseline omega 3 levels, but not lower than a group of omnivores who are also taking omega 3 supplements.
[Sarter 2015](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24679552/)

In that study, 165 long-term vegans had an average omega-3 index (red blood cell EPA + DHA) of 3.7%. Compared with 78 US soldiers eating Army rations, measured the same way, male vegans had higher EPA (0.56% vs 0.40%) and similar DHA (2.28% vs 2.61%) and omega-3 index (3.48% vs 3.47%). Among 46 vegans with an index below 4%, 4 months of an algal supplement with 254 mg of DHA plus EPA a day raised the index from 3.1% to 4.8%, and 87% got above 4%. One author works for OmegaQuant, which sells omega-3 index tests, and the supplement was made by DSM Nutritional Products.

Bioengineered plants can be a future alternative for omega 3 due to shortage of fish in ocean.
[Amjad Khan 2017](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5339522/)

## Fish oil vs krill oil

Similar plasma and RBC levels of EPA and DHA were achieved across all fish oil and krill oil products when matched for dose, EPA, and DHA concentrations, indicating similar bioavailability.
[Yurko-Mauro 2015](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26328782/)
