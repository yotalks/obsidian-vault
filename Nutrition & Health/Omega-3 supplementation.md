> [!summary] Bottom line
> In the largest meta-analysis of randomized trials, marine omega-3 supplements lowered the risk of heart attack, coronary heart disease and cardiovascular death by about 3–8%, but didn't change stroke risk. Benefits seemed to grow with dose. In pregnancy, the largest trial found DHA didn't reduce postpartum depression or improve children's cognition, but it cut early preterm births and low birth weight. Smaller trials suggest less allergy and asthma in children.

## Cardiovascular disease

A meta-analysis of 13 randomized trials (127,477 people, about 5 years on average) pooled marine omega-3 supplements, mostly around 850 mg a day of [[Glossary#EPA|EPA]] and DHA. Leaving out REDUCE-IT, which used 4 g a day of pure EPA, supplements lowered the risk of:
- heart attack ([[Glossary#RR|RR]] 0.92, 95% CI 0.86–0.99)
- [[Glossary#CHD|CHD]] death (RR 0.92, 0.86–0.98)
- total CHD (RR 0.95, 0.91–0.99)
- [[Glossary#CVD|CVD]] death (RR 0.93, 0.88–0.99)
- total CVD (RR 0.97, 0.94–0.99)

Stroke risk didn't change (RR 1.05, 0.98–1.14). Including REDUCE-IT made every benefit larger, but the trials then disagreed more with each other. Risk fell further at higher doses for total CVD and major vascular events, but the authors call that analysis highly exploratory, since nearly all the trials used the same dose.
[Hu 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6806028/)

One author has received research support from the California Walnut Commission and honoraria from supplement companies (Metagenics, Standard Process), outside this work. One of the included trials, VITAL, was led by another author and used fish oil donated by its manufacturers (Pronova BioPharma and BASF).

The effects are small per person. The authors argue they could still prevent many events because heart disease is so common. Whether the benefit holds for primary prevention is the open question → [[Omega 6 3 ratio]]

## Pregnancy

A review of omega-3 across the lifespan covers several pregnancy trials.
[Swanson 2012](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3262608/)

In the largest, 2,399 pregnant women took 800 mg a day of DHA or vegetable oil capsules from mid-pregnancy to birth. DHA didn't reduce postpartum depressive symptoms (9.7% vs 11.2%, not significant), and children's cognitive and language scores at 18 months didn't differ. According to the review, there were fewer early preterm births (before 34 weeks: 1.1% vs 2.3%) and fewer low birth weight babies.
[Makrides 2010](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20959577/)

Smaller trials point to less allergy and asthma:
- In 533 women given fish oil (2.7 g a day of omega-3), olive oil or nothing from about week 30, the children of the fish oil group had 63% lower asthma risk over 16 years and 87% lower allergic asthma risk than the olive oil group. Only 19 children were diagnosed with asthma. [Olsen 2008](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18614738/)
- In 145 women from allergic families, fish oil from week 25 through breastfeeding lowered food allergy and allergic eczema in their babies at 1 year. [Furuhjelm 2009](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19489765/)

Small trials also found better infant problem-solving and hand-eye coordination with DHA, but the much larger trial above found no cognitive benefit.
