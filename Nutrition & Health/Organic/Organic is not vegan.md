> [!summary] Bottom line
> Organic corn fertilized with manure sends more money to animal agriculture than conventional corn, more than drinking a cup of milk a day.

The estimated total nitrogen requirement for an acre of corn crop is 300lbs of Nitrogen.

Without using any manure or nitrogen fertilizer, this requirement is reduced to 117lbs nitrogen, because the soil contains some nitrogen at baseline, both organic N and inorganic N, legume crops from crop rotations, and nitrates from irrigation sources.

[Meisinger 2008](https://acsess.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.2134/agronmonogr49.c14) 

156 lbs of nitrogen is obtained from about 51.9 tons of manure (solid dairy manure) 

(updated manure wholesale price) Manure ranges from $14 to $24 per ton. 

[Drovers: manure value](https://www.drovers.com/article/what-economic-value-cattle-manure)

Let's use $19 as an average.

53.3 tons manure for 19 dollars/ton manure = $986.1

15,000,000 calories / 986.1 dollars = 15211.4calories/dollar

Assuming you only lived of this corn, you would be generating :

1 dollar to animal AG every 7.6 days

Daily demand: $0.1315789/day

Daily demand for conventional: $0.004997/day

Difference: $0.12658/day

Lifetime difference: $3465 to animal AG

How does this compare to Milk?

[USDA milk prices](https://www.ams.usda.gov/mnreports/dymadvancedprices.pdf)

Class 1 base price $16.99 per hundredweight 

Convert to daily demand: ((16.99/112lbs)*8.6lbs/gal)/16cup/gal = $0.0815

Therefore, the daily difference in demand  shows that an organic diet compared of corn to a conventional diet comprised of corn generates more demand to animal AG than drinking 1 cup of milk per day.

> [!question] Objections to prepare for
> - Organic farms can build fertility with green manure, compost or legumes instead of animal manure.
> - Manure is a by-product; buying it may barely change how many animals are raised.
> - Synthetic nitrogen fertilizer has its own environmental costs.
