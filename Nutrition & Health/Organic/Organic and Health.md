> [!summary] Bottom line
> Organic food shows no nutritional or health advantage, may carry more pathogens and antibiotic-resistance genes, and combined cohort data associate organic consumption with slightly more cancers, not fewer.

## Food safety

Higher concentrations of E. Coli in organic produce than conventional produce
[Mukherjee 2004](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pubmed/15151224)

Use of commercial organic fertilizer increases the abundance of antibiotic resistance genes and antibiotics in soil
[Zhou 2016](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11356-016-7854-z)

"we found that dairy cow manure amendment enhanced the proliferation of resident antibiotic-resistant bacteria and genes encoding β-lactamases in soil even though the cows from which the manure was derived had not been treated with antibiotics"
[Udikovic-Kolic 2014](http://www.pnas.org/content/111/42/15202)

Fusarium infection was significantly different between farming systems in maize (20% vs 35% in conventional and organic, respectively), while in 2010 the incidence was significantly higher than in 2011 (43% vs 25%)
[Crop Protection 2015](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0261219415000757)

## Nutrition and health

"On the basis of a systematic review of studies of satisfactory quality, there is no evidence of a difference in nutrient quality between organically and conventionally produced foodstuffs."
[Dangour 2009](https://academic.oup.com/ajcn/article/90/3/680/4597089)

"From a systematic review of the currently available published literature, evidence is lacking for nutrition-related health effects that result from the consumption of organically produced foodstuffs."
[Dangour 2010](https://academic.oup.com/ajcn/article/92/1/203/4597310)

"Consumers may also choose to buy organic food because they believe that it is safer and more nutritious than other food. However, the balance of current scientific evidence does not support this view."
[Times Online: organic food](https://web.archive.org/web/20100331234955/http://extras.timesonline.co.uk/organicfood2.pdf)

"current evidence does not support any meaningful nutritional benefits or deficits from eating organic compared with conventionally grown foods, and there are no well-powered human studies that directly demonstrate health benefits or disease protection as a result of consuming an organic diet"
[Forman 2012](http://pediatrics.aappublications.org/content/130/5/e1406)

## Pesticides

"Two novel methods were combined to estimate a realistic worst case hazard index for pesticide residues in the Danish population. The methods were able to refine previous estimates especially with regard to below LOQ (left censored) data.

The highest Hazard Index (HI) calculated for the complete pesticide usage analyzed in this study was for the population group Child aged 4–6. Our estimated HI for this group was 44% of ADI. Due to the many conservative (cautious) assumptions made, the real life figure was probably much lower. This finding adds to the existing body of evidence showing that pesticides are very unlikely to pose a risk to the health of Danish consumers through chronic dietary exposure of residues.

The hazard index for pesticides for a Danish adult was on level with the hazard quotient for alcohol for a person consuming the equivalent of 1 glass of wine every seventh year."
[Larsson 2018](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0278691517306877?via%3Dihub)

## Cancer

When you combine the data of the two largest studies done on the topic, you find that consumers of organic are actually more likely to get cancer than non-organic consumers.   

53769 total cancers * 0.03 Increase percentage of getting cancer while on organic = 1613 additional cancers associated with organic.
[Bradbury 2014](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC4007233/)

935 cancers total (adding up the individual cancers listed) * 0.25 increase percentage of getting cancer while eating conventional = 234 additional cancers associated with conventional.
[Baudry 2018](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamainternalmedicine/fullarticle/2707948) 

 If one invokes statistical significance, here's the data from everything that was statistically significant  18691 breast cancers * 0.09 increased chance of getting breast cancer from organic = 1682.19 additional cancers form organic  2128 Non Hodgkin lymphomas * 0.21 increased chance of getting NHL from conventional = 446.88 additional cancers from conventional   1682.19 - 446.88 =1235.31 additional cancers associated from organic  Pairing that up with logans organic paper of  234 additional cancers associated with conventional. you get 1235.31-234=1001.31 additional cancers associated with organic. So no matter how you slice the data, when you combine all the available outcome based data you end up getting more cancers associated with organic. If his standard is to only add things that are statistically significant, you get 1001 extra cases of cancer to be expected by organic association  If his standard is to add everything regardless of statistical significance, you get 1369 extra cases of cancer to be expected by organic association So organic would be associated with either be an extra 1001 or 1369 cases of cancer per 692,026 people (combining the number of people in both studies) per 7-9 years (follow up times in both studies)

## Copper

Organic farming uses more copper sulfate per hectare than conventional.
[Kuhne 2017](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/321974582_The_Use_of_Copper_Pesticides_in_Germany_and_the_Search_for_Minimization_and_Replacement_Strategies)

> [!question] Objections to prepare for
> - Combining cancer estimates from two cohorts with different designs and exposure definitions may not be valid.
> - Lower pesticide residues on organic produce might still matter for some groups, even below safety limits.
> - The E. coli and Fusarium findings may be dated or context-specific.
