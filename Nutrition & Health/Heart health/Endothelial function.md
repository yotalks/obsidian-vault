> [!summary] Bottom line
> Endothelial function is how well blood vessels widen, usually measured as flow-mediated dilation in the arm. A single high-fat meal lowers it for at least 4 hours, by about 1 percentage point on average. Low values predict heart disease, but several treatments that improve it failed to prevent heart disease in randomized trials, so it can't stand in for hard outcomes. A temporary drop after a meal shows a real change in the vessel, not that the meal causes heart disease.

## What the endothelium does

The endothelium lines the blood vessels. It weighs about 1.5 kg and covers about 600 m². It controls vessel tone, clotting, how white blood cells and platelets stick to the vessel wall, and permeability. Short-term widening comes mostly from nitric oxide, prostacyclin and hyperpolarizing factor.

Endothelial dysfunction is an early feature of atherosclerosis, and it's thought to be one reason so many different risk factors lead to the same disease. Nearly every risk factor for atherosclerosis also impairs it, including age, smoking, high cholesterol, low HDL, high blood pressure, diabetes and obesity.
[Vogel 1999](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6656162/)

## How it's measured

The endothelium senses shear from increased blood flow and widens the vessel in response. In brachial artery ultrasound, a cuff blocks blood flow to the arm for 5 minutes, and the artery's change in diameter is measured about a minute after release. That's flow-mediated dilation (FMD), and it mostly depends on nitric oxide. Without nitric oxide synthesis, the extra blood flow still happens, but the vessel doesn't widen.

FMD in the arm correlates with flow-dependent dilation in the coronary arteries at about 0.8, and with their response to acetylcholine at about 0.4.
[Vogel 1999](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6656162/)

## Does it predict heart disease?

Across 14 cohorts and 5,547 people, each 1% higher FMD was linked to 13% fewer cardiovascular events after adjusting for risk factors (RR 0.87, 95% CI 0.83–0.91).
[Inaba 2010](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20339920/)

That's a prediction, not proof that raising FMD prevents events. Many of the treatments shown to improve endothelial function by 1999 later had randomized trials with hard outcomes:

| Treatment | Outcome in randomized trials |
| --- | --- |
| Vitamins C and E | No effect on major cardiovascular events ([Sesso 2008](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18997197/), [Heart Protection Study 2002](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12114037/)) |
| Lowering homocysteine | No effect on the main cardiovascular outcome (RR 0.95, 95% CI 0.84–1.07) ([Lonn 2006](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16531613/)) |
| Estrogen | No effect on coronary heart disease (HR 0.91, 95% CI 0.75–1.12), more strokes (HR 1.39) ([Anderson 2004](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15082697/)) |
| L-arginine after a heart attack | No improvement in vascular stiffness, and 6 deaths vs 0, so the trial was stopped ([Schulman 2006](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16391217/)) |

Cholesterol lowering and quitting smoking also improve endothelial function, and those do prevent heart disease, so this doesn't make it meaningless. It just can't settle whether something helps on its own.

## High-fat meals

In 10 healthy people, a 900-calorie meal with 50 g of fat cut FMD from 21% to 11% at 2 hours and 10% at 4 hours, while a 900-calorie meal with no fat changed nothing. Triglycerides rose from 94 to 147 mg/dL.
[Vogel 1997](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9036757/)

In 20 healthy people, the same high-fat meal cut FMD from 20% to 8% at 4 hours, and the drop tracked the rise in triglycerides (r = −0.54). Taking 1 g of vitamin C and 800 IU of vitamin E beforehand blocked it.
[Plotnick 1997](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9388088/)

The average effect is much smaller than those early studies suggest. Across 90 studies, a high-fat meal lowered FMD by 1.02 percentage points at 2 hours, 1.04 at 3 hours and 1.19 at 4 hours, with very high heterogeneity. The drop was larger in younger, healthy-weight people than in older, heavier, at-risk people, and it grew with the percentage of fat in the meal.
[Fewkes 2022](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35665799/)

### Which fats

In 10 healthy people given 900-calorie meals with 50 g of fat, olive oil cut FMD by 31%, from 14.3% to 9.9%. Canola oil, salmon, and olive oil with either vitamins C and E or with vinegar and salad didn't significantly reduce it. All five meals raised triglycerides.
[Vogel 2000](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11079642/)

This is one small study, and a drop in FMD doesn't tell us about long-term outcomes. Mediterranean diets rich in olive oil do improve FMD over the long term.

### Triglycerides

Raising triglycerides directly has a similar effect. In 7 young, healthy men, infusing a fat emulsion for an hour cut flow-induced dilation from 7.1% to 1.6%. Dilation in response to nitroglycerin fell too, from 20.5% to 11.5%, so this wasn't only an endothelial effect.
[Lundman 1997](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9396413/)

## Cholesterol lowering

In 7 healthy middle-aged men with cholesterol levels considered desirable, simvastatin 10 mg raised FMD from 5.0% to 10.5% after 2 weeks and 15.7% after 12 weeks as cholesterol fell from 200 to 153 mg/dL. Both returned to baseline 12 weeks after stopping.
[Vogel 1996](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8540454/)
