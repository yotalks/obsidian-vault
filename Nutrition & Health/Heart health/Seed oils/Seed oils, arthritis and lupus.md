> [!summary] Bottom line
> Seed oils are claimed to cause rheumatoid arthritis (RA) and lupus (SLE), but the evidence doesn't support it. In RA, a high omega-6 to omega-3 ratio is linked to more pain, but that tracks low omega-3, not high omega-6. Trials of oils rich in linoleic acid found no harm and sometimes small benefits. The lupus data are small and mostly cross-sectional, and point to antioxidants and omega-3 more than to linoleic acid. Mendelian randomization links genetically higher linoleic acid to lower risk of both diseases.

## Rheumatoid arthritis

### Omega-6, omega-3 and pain

In 591 early RA patients on methotrexate, a higher omega-6 to omega-3 ratio was linked to more unacceptable pain ([[Glossary#OR|OR]] 1.70) and refractory pain (OR 2.33) after 3 months. Omega-3 intake was linked to less of both (OR 0.57 and 0.47), while omega-6 alone wasn't linked to any pain measure. None of them were linked to inflammatory pain or inflammation markers.
[Lourdudoss 2018](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28371257/)

So the ratio's link to pain seems to come from too little omega-3 rather than too much omega-6. That fits the wider evidence on omega-3:
- A review of human and animal studies found that diets high in omega-3 may protect against developing RA, and that supplements may help manage it. [Navarini 2017](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29017507/)
- A meta-analysis of randomized trials found marine oil supplements modestly reduced pain in RA ([[Glossary#SMD|SMD]] −0.21, 22 trials). [Senftleber 2017](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28067815/)

### Trials of LA-rich oils

Several oils high in [[Glossary#LA|LA]] have been tested in RA:
- **Blackcurrant seed oil** is 33–47% LA, along with gamma-linolenic acid (GLA) and alpha-linolenic acid. [Šavikin 2013](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23341215/)
  - In a 24-week placebo-controlled trial in RA, it reduced signs and symptoms of disease activity, but overall clinical response wasn't better than placebo. [Leventhal 1994](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8081671/)
  - When RA patients and healthy volunteers took blackcurrant seed oil, with sunflower oil as the control, morning stiffness improved in the RA patients on blackcurrant oil, and their monocytes produced markedly different amounts of inflammatory cytokines. These changes were reported for blackcurrant oil, and nothing harmful was reported for sunflower oil, which is mostly LA. [Watson 1993](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8252313/)
- **Evening primrose oil** is mostly LA, with some GLA.
  - In 40 RA patients over 6 months, 6 g/day of evening primrose oil reduced morning stiffness at 3 months, while 6 g/day of olive oil (the placebo) reduced pain and the articular index, a count of tender joints, at 6 months. [Brzeski 1991](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/1913008/)
  - In 49 RA patients over 12 months, evening primrose oil (540 mg/day of GLA), alone or with fish oil, gave a subjective improvement compared with placebo and let patients cut their NSAID use. They relapsed when switched to placebo, and there was no sign of disease modification. [Belch 1988](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/2833184/)

These trials were small and short, and the benefits may come from GLA rather than LA. Either way, none of them showed LA-rich oils worsening RA or inflammation.

### Does omega-6 block fish oil?

In 50 RA patients whose diets were naturally low in omega-6 (under 10 g/day), fish oil improved clinical measures over 15 weeks compared with olive and corn oil capsules. The authors suggest fish oil works when omega-6 intake is low, but everyone in the trial ate a low omega-6 diet, so it can't show whether a higher omega-6 intake would blunt the effect.
[Volker 2000](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11036827/)

## Osteoarthritis

Some studies cited on this topic are about osteoarthritis, which isn't autoimmune:
- In 230 middle-aged adults without knee osteoarthritis, higher saturated fat intake was linked to new bone marrow lesions in the knee over 2 years (OR 2.56 per SD), while PUFA and MUFA intake weren't. [Wang 2009](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19426478/)
- In 100 people with knee osteoarthritis, 30 mL a day of argan oil for 8 weeks reduced pain and improved function compared with no treatment. Argan oil is about 36% LA and 43% oleic acid. [Essouiri 2017](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28699499/) [Khallouki 2003](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12548113/)

## Lupus

Very little is known about how diet affects lupus, and most of the data are cross-sectional or case-control.

### Cross-sectional studies

- In a pilot study of 30 lupus patients and 20 controls, serum LA was higher in patients and correlated with antinuclear antibody titre and corticosteroid dose. There was no diet data, no follow-up and no adjustment, and the authors call the analysis exploratory. [Gorczyca 2022](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34169789/)
- In 41 lupus patients and 41 controls, plasma free LA was lower in patients, as were several saturated fats (stearic and others), while myristic, palmitoleic and oleic acid were higher. [Shin 2017](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30830319/)
- In 456 lupus patients, each unit increase in the omega-6 to omega-3 ratio was linked to higher self-reported lupus activity, and more omega-3 was linked to less activity and better sleep. [Charoenwoodhipong 2020](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31074595/)
- In 111 lupus patients followed for 2 years, higher dietary LA (C18:2) was linked to lower odds of needing the same or a higher glucocorticoid dose, a proxy for active disease (OR about 0.3). [Lourdudoss 2016](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26848399/)
- In 91 lupus patients with poor-quality diets, no nutrient or antioxidant intake was linked to CRP. [Pocovi-Gerardino 2019](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30282476/)

### Oxidative stress and antioxidants

Lupus involves uncontrolled oxidative stress, including lipid peroxidation. [Shah 2014](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24636579/)

Lupus patients had lower antioxidant enzyme activity, higher malondialdehyde (a lipid peroxidation marker), and lower intake of vitamin A and beta-carotene than matched controls. [Bae 2002](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12426662/)

In 36 lupus patients, those given vitamin E had lower anti-DNA antibody titres, but no difference in a marker of oxidative DNA damage, so the authors think it worked through something other than its antioxidant effect. [Maeshima 2007](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17143589/)

### Prospective studies

In 216 Japanese women with inactive lupus followed for 4 years, higher vitamin C intake was linked to lower risk of active disease, even after correcting for [[Multiple comparisons|multiple comparisons]], and fiber showed a similar trend. Separately, the 7 patients who had vascular events (heart disease, stroke or clots) ate more vegetable fat at baseline than those who didn't, a result from very few cases.
[Minami 2003](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12672194/)

In the Nurses' Health Studies, neither a Western dietary pattern (HR 1.35, 95% CI 0.77–2.35) nor a prudent one was linked to developing lupus, and neither were four diet quality scores. Higher nut and legume intake was linked to lower risk (HR 0.59).
[Tedeschi 2020](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31718449/)
[Barbhaiya 2021](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32936999/)

## Mendelian randomization

A [[Glossary#MR|Mendelian randomization]] study found genetically higher plasma LA was linked to lower risk of RA and lupus. A later letter questioned the choice of genetic instruments, and the authors replied that a sensitivity analysis using 167 variants gave a similar estimate.
[Zhao 2019](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30409829/)
