> [!summary] Bottom line
> Linoleic acid, PUFA and vegetable fat aren't linked to higher risk of Alzheimer's disease, cognitive decline or dementia in prospective cohorts. The one study showing a non-significant rise didn't adjust for diet, and older data may reflect trans fat in seed oil products. Pooled across all outcomes, vegetable fat is linked to lower dementia risk, consistent with earlier meta-analyses.

Seed oils have also been touted for causing cognitive decline with things like dementia and Alzheimer's. 

## Alzheimer's disease

Looking at data on Alzheimer's in aggregate, results across all exposures were null, but there weren't many studies per subgroup, when subgrouping was removed, the results were still not statistically significant. There is also pretty high heterogeneity between included studies. Like the Ronnemaa study had a non significant increase in Alzheimer's risk with increasing [[Glossary#LA|LA]] intake, but Morris saw a statistically significant decrease in Alzheimer's risk with increasing seed oil intake.
The adjustment model by Ronnemaa had no adjustments for dietary covariates, so it's unclear if that's an independent effect of LA, as LA may just be a correlate for a different undisclosed dietary exposure that is actually increasing risk. But Morris saw a statistically significant effect of seed oils and did use some adjustment for diet quality.
In the fully adjusted model were adjustments for other dietary fatty acids. Among these findings, only two were statistically significant. The first is a statistically significant decrease in the risk of Alzheimer's with vegetable fats after an adjustment for other fatty acids. The other is a statistically significant increase in the risk of Alzheimer's with [[Glossary#TFA|TFA]], after an adjustment for other fatty acids.
Given the datasets that were investigated in these 2 studies were mainly using data that was collected in the 90s before the TFA ban, so it's possible the increase in risk with LA is because of TFA in many seed oil products.

## Cognitive decline and dementia

We also see null findings across all investigated exposures WRT dementia. The only statistically significant study was Beydoun which saw a 23% reduction in risk of cognitive decline. This study also had a robust multivariate adjustment model.
Okereke saw a non significant increase in cognitive decline with increasing [[Glossary#PUFA|PUFA]] intake and had a pretty good adjustment model and even included TFA as a covariate and adjusted for micronutrients and comorbidities. But the cohort had half the follow up time and used different ways of measuring cognitive function. Beydoun used in person interviewers to assess cognitive function while Okereke used phone interviews to assess cognitive impairment.

## All outcomes together

Results were slightly different when Alzheimer's, cognitive decline, and general dementia were al considered together. When they are all aggregated, vegetable fat was associated with a statistically significant 58% reduction in total dementia. Though more than 2/3 of the weight was derived from a single study.

## Overall

All in all, it doesn't seem like LA, PUFA, or vegetable fat is associated with increased risk of Alzheimer's disease, cognitive decline, or dementia in humans. This is consistent with previous meta analyses looking at these relationships. When all endpoints are pooled together, vegetable fat seems to be associated with reduced risk of total dementia.
[Cao 2019](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31062836/)
[Ruan 2018](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29701155/)
