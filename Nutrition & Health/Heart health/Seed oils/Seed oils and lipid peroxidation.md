> [!summary] Bottom line
> Polyunsaturated fats oxidize easily in isolation, which is the basis for claims that they're toxic. But in diet trials, high-PUFA diets didn't worsen markers of lipid peroxidation or oxidative DNA damage compared with low-PUFA diets, and trends favored them, so the mechanism doesn't pan out in humans.

## The mechanism

A lot of people also claim that [[Glossary#PUFA|PUFA]] in seed oils are toxic to humans especially in larger amounts with the primary mechanism being lipid peroxidation. PUFA are unique among fatty acids in that they have multiple double-bands. The carbons between these double-bonds are uniquely vulnerable to oxidation, which destroys the PUFA and creates a lot of toxic byproducts. So we know that they're fragile and break apart easily in isolation when exposed to different reactants like oxygen, but these mechanisms do not mean clinical relevance.

## Markers in diet trials

All in all, the only significant finding was that PUFA lowered 8-oxadG compared to baseline, but results were null compared to control. So both high and low PUFA diets lowered this marker of lipid peroxidation. This is interesting because it is a marker of DNA damage so its implications extend far beyond oxidative stress. If we just look at trends, we see that the results would overwhelmingly favor high PUFA diets over low PUFA diets, especially for MDA, which saw non-statistically significant decreases for high PUFA diets compared to baseline.

All in all, the mechanism of the toxic, in vivo effects of PUFA on lipid peroxidation in humans don't pan out and it doesn't seem like PUFA negatively affects markers of lipid peroxidation and may improve some of them.
