> [!summary] Bottom line
> Chocolate doesn't raise LDL despite its saturated fat, and may lower it: cocoa solids lower LDL through polyphenols and fiber, and cocoa butter's main fat, stearic acid, mostly converts to LDL-lowering monounsaturated fats.

Chocolate, despite being higher in saturated fat, doesn't seem to increase [[Glossary#LDL|LDL]]. In fact, they seem to lower LDL in humans.
[Tokede 2011](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21559039/)

## Cocoa solids

This study investigated the effects of dark chocolate on LDL and blood pressure compared to white chocolate and found that dark chocolate lowered LDL by 7% and improved blood pressure which tells us there's aspects in the ground cocoa itself that have these properties.
[Grassi 2008](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18716168/)

This study did a similar thing but used low-flavanol vs. high-flavanol cocoa drinks. For both groups, the within group differences in LDL were non-significant, but the between group treatment effect was statistically significant. 
[Engler 2004](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15190043/)

This tells us that ground cocoa is one of the components in chocolate that has these beneficial effects.

## Cocoa butter and stearic acid

Now with cocoa butter, the primary saturated fat is stearic acid, which hasn't really been shown to increase LDL in controlled human feeding studies.
[Mensink 2016 (WHO)](https://apps.who.int/iris/handle/10665/246104)

This study tried to find an explanation and it seems to be that much of stearic acid seems to easily convert to oleic acid and palmitoleic acid, which are LDL lowering monounsaturated fats, with a small minority of it converting to LDL raising palmitic acid.
[Rodríguez-Morató 2020](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32998517/)

Stearic acid seems to also preferentially esterify to cholesterol to form cholesteryl esters, which is a similar mechanism to the one responsible for the LDL lowering effects of polyunsaturated fat.
It also found that feeding stearic acid reduced LDL by 13%, but their LDL was quite high to begin with so for people with more normal levels, the effects likely won't be significant.
[Rodríguez-Morató 2020](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32998517/)

## Overall

All in all, cocoa itself seems to lower LDL via polyphenols and fiber and not raise LDL due to the unique properties of stearic acid.
