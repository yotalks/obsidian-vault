> [!summary] Bottom line
> HDL's link to lower heart disease comes from observational studies like Framingham. In Mendelian randomization the link disappears and risk tracks ApoB instead, so HDL itself likely plays little causal role in causing or preventing heart disease.

The association between elevated [[Glossary#HDL|HDL]] and lower [[Glossary#CVD|CVD]] goes back to the Framingham Heart Study. They saw HDL seemed to have predictive power WRT [[Glossary#CHD|CHD]]. Having low HDL seemed to dramatically increase risk of CVD and CHD, with the association much stronger for CHD.
[Wilson 1988](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/3196218/)

There have since been a number of investigations into HDL's role in the prevention of CVD, using much more rigorous methodology like in the form of [[Glossary#MR|MR]] studies.

When we investigate the relationships between HDL and CVD in MR, we see the association completely goes away.
[Richardson 2020](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32203549/)
[Karjalainen 2020](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32113648/)

The results seem to indicate that the risk actually tracks [[Glossary#ApoB|ApoB]] more than anything, so HDL in and of itself, likely has very little independent causal role in the development of, or protection against, CVD.
→ [[LDL and heart disease]]

## Genetic evidence

Variants that change HDL without changing other lipids give the cleanest test.

Carriers of the LIPG Asn396Ser variant had 0.14 mmol/L higher HDL, which should have cut myocardial infarction by 13% (OR 0.87, 95% CI 0.84–0.91). Across 20 studies with 20,913 cases and 95,407 controls, it made no difference (OR 0.99, 0.88–1.11). A score of 14 variants that only affect HDL also found nothing (OR 0.93 per SD, 0.68–1.26), compared with 0.62 (0.58–0.66) observationally. As a check that the method works, a score of 13 LDL variants did find an effect (OR 2.13, 1.69–2.69).
[Voight 2012](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22607825/)

The LCAT S208T variant tested this in the other direction. It lowered HDL by 13% (0.21 mmol/L) without raising myocardial infarction in about 54,500 people. A 50% drop in HDL from the variant gave an odds ratio of 0.49 (0.11–2.16), while the same drop observationally meant a hazard ratio of 2.11 (1.70–2.62).
[Haase 2012](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22090275/)

When tested alone in the larger analysis, HDL (OR 0.80, 0.75–0.86) and its main protein ApoA-I (OR 0.83, 0.77–0.89) did look protective. Both fell to about null after accounting for ApoB.
[Richardson 2020](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32203549/)

For ApoA-I specifically, higher levels went with less coronary artery disease observationally (HR 0.81, 0.75–0.88), but a variant in the APOA1 gene showed no protection in 122,733 cases (OR 1.13, 0.98–1.30). A variant in ABCA1 gave similar results.
[Karjalainen 2020](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32113648/)

## Raising HDL doesn't help

HDL looks like a marker of risk rather than a maker of it. Drugs that raise HDL have repeatedly failed:

- **Torcetrapib** raised HDL by 72% and lowered LDL by 25%, but more people died on it than on a statin alone (93 vs 59), and the trial was stopped early. Why it caused harm isn't fully known.
[Barter 2007](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17984165/)
- **Niacin** didn't add any benefit to statin therapy in people with low HDL.
[AIM-HIGH 2011](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22085343/)
- **Niacin with laropiprant** didn't reduce major vascular events.
[HPS2-THRIVE 2014](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25014686/)
- **ApoA-I infusions** of CSL112 didn't reduce heart attack, stroke or cardiovascular death at 90 days in 18,219 patients after a heart attack (HR 0.93, 0.81–1.05). Two earlier infusion products, MDCO-216 and CER-001, were dropped.
[Gibson 2024](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38587254/)
[Karjalainen 2020](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32113648/)

Anacetrapib is the exception that fits. It raised HDL by 104% and slightly cut major coronary events (10.8% vs 11.8%, rate ratio 0.91), but it also lowered non-HDL cholesterol by 18%, and the benefit tracked the fall in ApoB rather than the rise in HDL.
[REVEAL 2017](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28847206/)
[Karjalainen 2020](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32113648/)

This is a case where a good mechanism didn't hold up. HDL particles carry cholesterol out of the bloodstream, so raising them seems like it should help. One possible explanation is that what matters is how well HDL functions rather than how much there is, but the trial results stand either way. → [[Evidence hierarchy]]

So when exercise raises HDL, that's a sign of good health rather than the reason exercise helps.
