> [!summary] Bottom line
> HDL's link to lower heart disease comes from observational studies like Framingham. In Mendelian randomization the link disappears and risk tracks ApoB instead, so HDL itself likely plays little causal role in causing or preventing heart disease.

The association between elevated [[Glossary#HDL|HDL]] and lower [[Glossary#CVD|CVD]] goes back to the Framingham Heart Study. They saw HDL seemed to have predictive power WRT [[Glossary#CHD|CHD]]. Having low HDL seemed to dramatically increase risk of CVD and CHD, with the association much stronger for CHD.
[Wilson 1988](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/3196218/)

There have since been a number of investigations into HDL's role in the prevention of CVD, using much more rigorous methodology like in the form of [[Glossary#MR|MR]] studies.

When we investigate the relationships between HDL and CVD in MR, we see the association completely goes away.
[Richardson 2020](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32203549/)
[Karjalainen 2020](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32113648/)

The results seem to indicate that the risk actually tracks [[Glossary#ApoB|ApoB]] more than anything, so HDL in and of itself, likely has very little independent causal role in the development of, or protection against, CVD.
