> [!summary] Bottom line
> Atherosclerosis isn't limited to herbivores. It has been induced in normal cats and dogs and described in other carnivores, so an animal's natural diet doesn't make it immune.

It's been claimed that atherosclerosis only occurs in herbivores and not carnivores.

This is false. Atherosclerosis has been induced in carnivores (ie: domestic cat) that were otherwise normal and did not have their thyroids removed, and were not given any thyroid ablative medication.
[Ginzinger 1997](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9389784/)
[Ginzinger 1999](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10092984/)

Atherosclerosis has also been described in other carnivores as well, including the spotted hyena.
[Stout 1969](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2013639/)

It's been observed in dogs. Severe lesions, including blocked coronary and cerebral arteries, were produced in seven of eight dogs without thyroid suppression, using a diet with 5% cholesterol and 16% hydrogenated coconut oil for 12 to 14 months. These diets are far from normal, so they show carnivores can get the disease, not how likely it is on an ordinary diet.
[McCullagh 1976](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/1263442/)

And numerous other animals.
[Iowa State thesis](https://lib.dr.iastate.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=5280&context=rtd)
