> [!summary] Bottom line
> Many things linked to heart disease in observational studies lose that link when genes are used to test for cause. ApoB holds up as the main lipid cause. HDL, ApoA-I, CRP and homocysteine don't hold up. Testosterone and estradiol show no protective effect, though those studies are small and mostly measured risk factors. Higher iron may mean less carotid plaque but more blood clots. CRP not being causal doesn't clear inflammation, since other inflammatory pathways do hold up.

Comparing what observational studies and genes show for the same exposure, per 1 SD higher:

| Exposure   | Observational       | Genetic             | Source                                                        |
| ---------- | ------------------- | ------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **LDL-C**  | OR 1.54 (1.45–1.63) | OR 2.13 (1.69–2.69) | [Voight 2012](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22607825/)      |
| **HDL-C**  | OR 0.62 (0.58–0.66) | OR 0.93 (0.68–1.26) | [Voight 2012](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22607825/)      |
| **ApoA-I** | HR 0.81 (0.75–0.88) | OR 1.13 (0.98–1.30) | [Karjalainen 2020](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32113648/) |
| **CRP**    | RR 1.33 (1.23–1.43) | RR 1.00 (0.90–1.13) | [CCGC 2011](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21325005/)        |

## ApoB

Testing lipids one at a time, LDL-C (OR 1.66, 95% CI 1.49–1.86), triglycerides (OR 1.34, 1.25–1.44) and ApoB (OR 1.73, 1.56–1.91) all raised coronary heart disease risk. Testing them together, only ApoB kept a robust effect (OR 1.92, 1.31–2.81). LDL-C reversed and lost significance (OR 0.85, 0.57–1.27), and triglycerides fell to OR 1.12 (1.02–1.23). This used UK Biobank for the variants and 60,801 cases from CARDIoGRAMplusC4D.
![[Richardson 2020 lipids and CHD.webp]]
[Richardson 2020](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32203549/)

This doesn't mean LDL is harmless and ApoB is something separate. Each atherogenic particle carries one ApoB, so this points to the number of particles. ApoB is also what gets those particles trapped in the artery wall, so it's why lipoproteins are atherogenic rather than a confounder of them. These traits are so correlated that fully separating them is hard. → [[LDL and heart disease]]

## HDL and ApoA-I

HDL and ApoA-I, its main protein, both look protective in observational studies, but variants that raise them don't lower risk. → [[HDL]]

## CRP

In 47 studies with 194,418 people and 46,557 coronary cases, genetically raised CRP had no effect on coronary heart disease (RR 1.00, 0.90–1.13), while circulating CRP was linked to it (RR 1.33, 1.23–1.43, P = 0.001 for the difference). The variants raised CRP by up to 30% per allele and weren't related to other risk factors, or to fibrinogen, interleukin-6 or white cell count.
![[CCGC 2011 CRP and CHD.webp]]
[CCGC 2011](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21325005/)

A much smaller study of 3,802 people found the same pattern. hs-CRP of 1.08 mg/L or more predicted coronary heart disease (HR 1.69, 1.11–2.60), but the genetic analysis found no causal link (P = 0.78). With only 98 cases, it can't rule much out alone.
[Zhuang 2019](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31694563/)

So CRP is a marker. That doesn't mean inflammation plays no part:

- **Genetics:** A variant that reduces interleukin-6 receptor signaling lowered CRP and coronary heart disease (OR 0.95 per allele, 0.93–0.97) in 25,458 cases and 100,740 controls. [IL6R MR Consortium 2012](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22421340/)
- **Trial:** In 10,061 people with a previous heart attack and CRP of 2 mg/L or more, canakinumab 150 mg lowered CRP without lowering lipids and cut cardiovascular events (HR 0.85, 0.74–0.98). Fatal infections went up and all-cause mortality didn't change. Novartis funded the trial. [Ridker 2017](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28845751/)

## Homocysteine

Homocysteine rises with age and methionine intake, and falls with folate, B6 and B12. Smoking, drinking, coffee and being overweight also raise it.

Nine variants linked to homocysteine weren't related to coronary heart disease (OR 1.015, 0.923–1.106; 184,305 people) or acute myocardial infarction (OR 1.037, 0.932–1.142; 181,875 people). Weighted median and MR-Egger analyses agreed.
[Miao 2021](https://doi.org/10.1177/2047487319894679)

Trials agree for heart attacks. Across 15 trials with 71,422 people, lowering homocysteine didn't change myocardial infarction (RR 1.02, 0.95–1.10) or death, though stroke was slightly lower (RR 0.90, 0.82–0.99). The genetic study didn't test stroke.
[Martí-Carvajal 2017](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28816346/)
→ [[Fish and health]]

## Testosterone

Low testosterone is linked to worse risk factors and higher mortality, but this doesn't look causal. In 1,882 German men aged 20–79, with 277 deaths over 10 years, testosterone was tied to many cardiometabolic risk factors and to mortality in standard analyses, and to none of them using two genetic variants.
![[Haring 2013 testosterone and mortality.webp]]
[Haring 2013](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23258625/)

The genetic estimates here have very wide intervals, so this shows a lack of evidence for an effect more than evidence of no effect.

If anything, higher testosterone points the wrong way for lipids. In 4,212 older Chinese men, each 1 nmol/L of genetically predicted testosterone meant slightly higher LDL-C (0.02 mmol/L, 0.01–0.04) and lower HDL-C (−0.01 mmol/L, −0.02 to −0.001), with no effect on blood pressure or glucose.
[Zhao 2014](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24302542/)

Pooling those two studies gave the same answer: LDL-C 0.019 mmol/L (0.005–0.034) and HDL-C −0.01 mmol/L (−0.019 to −0.001), with no clear effect on blood pressure or glucose. The HDL result fits what happens to boys at puberty.
[Zhao 2014](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24302542/)

Treatment has been tested directly. In 5,246 men with hypogonadism and existing or high risk of cardiovascular disease, testosterone gel was noninferior to placebo for major cardiac events over about 33 months (7.0% vs 7.3%, HR 0.96, 0.78–1.17), with more atrial fibrillation, acute kidney injury and pulmonary embolism. So it wasn't worse for heart events, but it didn't prevent them either. AbbVie was among the funders.
[Lincoff 2023](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37326322/)

## Estradiol

In 3,092 older Southern Chinese women, genetically predicted 17β-estradiol wasn't associated with blood pressure, lipids, glucose, body size or Framingham score (−0.01, −1.34 to 1.31), and results were similar in women without reported cardiovascular disease. The authors conclude lifetime estrogen exposure doesn't seem to protect through these risk factors.
[Au Yeung 2016](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26907540/)

This looked at risk factors rather than heart disease events, and the genetic score came from 236 young women. The intervals are wide, like −11.8 to 4.78 mmHg for systolic blood pressure, so a larger study with outcomes is needed.

## Iron

Higher genetically determined iron status raised the risk of venous thromboembolism and lowered the risk of carotid plaque, with no effect on carotid intima-media thickness. Odds ratios per 1 SD:

| Marker | Venous thromboembolism | Carotid plaque |
|---|---|---|
| **Serum iron** | 1.37 (1.14–1.66) | 0.85 (0.73–0.99) |
| **Transferrin saturation** | 1.25 (1.09–1.43) | 0.89 (0.80–1.00) |
| **Ferritin** | 1.92 (1.28–2.88) | 0.72 (0.51–1.01) |
| **Transferrin** (higher means lower iron) | 0.76 (0.63–0.92) | 1.15 (0.97–1.35) |

[Gill 2019](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31310728/)

The clot result is clearer than the plaque result, which only just reached significance for two of the four markers. This is about iron status in the body and doesn't test heme iron from food. → [[Red meat]]

## How far this goes

- **Power:** A null result only rules out effects bigger than its interval. That's strong for CRP and weak for testosterone and estradiol.
- **Single variants:** Some of these use one or two variants, so a null result may only apply to that pathway.
- **Lifelong and small:** Variants show the effect of a small lifelong difference, which isn't the same as a drug or a deficiency.
