> [!summary] Bottom line
> Processed meat is consistently linked to higher mortality, especially from heart disease and cancer. The evidence for unprocessed red meat is weaker and less consistent: some large cohorts link it to higher mortality, but a meta-analysis and a measurement-error-corrected European cohort didn't. Red meat is also linked to stroke and breast cancer, and replacing it with fish, poultry, nuts, legumes or whole grains is linked to lower risk.

## All-cause mortality

In a meta-analysis of 9 cohort studies, processed meat (relative risk 1.23) and total red meat (1.29) were linked to higher all-cause mortality comparing the highest with the lowest intakes, rising non-linearly with dose. Unprocessed red meat on its own wasn't significantly linked (1.10, 95% CI 0.98–1.22).
![[Processed and total red meat mortality dose-response (Larsson 2014).jpg]]
[Larsson 2014](https://academic.oup.com/aje/article/179/3/282/103471)

In two large US cohorts (about 24,000 deaths), each extra daily serving was linked to higher total mortality: 13% for unprocessed red meat and 20% for processed red meat, with similar increases for cardiovascular and cancer deaths. Replacing one daily serving of red meat with fish, poultry, nuts, legumes, low-fat dairy or whole grains was linked to 7–19% lower mortality.
[Pan 2012](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamainternalmedicine/fullarticle/1134845)

In the European EPIC cohort (about 26,000 deaths), very high red meat intake (160 g a day or more) was linked to 14% higher all-cause mortality, and processed meat to 44% higher. After correcting for measurement error, only processed meat stayed significant (18% higher per 50 g a day), mainly from cardiovascular disease but also cancer. Poultry wasn't linked to mortality.
[Rohrmann 2013](https://bmcmedicine.biomedcentral.com/articles/10.1186/1741-7015-11-63)

In Finnish men, a higher ratio of animal to plant protein (hazard ratio 1.23) and higher meat intake (1.23) were linked to higher mortality, while fish, eggs, dairy and plant protein sources weren't.
[Virtanen 2019](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/m/pubmed/30968137/)

A review of large US and European cohorts and meta-analyses concluded that more red meat, and especially processed meat, is linked to higher total mortality, cardiovascular disease, colorectal cancer and type 2 diabetes, even after adjusting for known confounders. The link hasn't always been seen for unprocessed red meat, and isn't seen for white meat.
[Battaglia Richi 2015](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/m/pubmed/26780279/)

## Stroke

In a meta-analysis of 7 cohorts (over 2 million people), red meat was linked to higher stroke risk: 14% for total red meat, 17% for processed red meat and 13% for fresh red meat. Risk rose significantly above about 50 g a day of total red meat, 70 g a day of fresh red meat, and any processed meat. No type of meat was linked to hemorrhagic stroke.
[Yang 2016](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/m/pubmed/26935118/)

## Breast cancer

Among 88,803 women followed for 20 years, those with the highest red meat intake in early adulthood had a 22% higher risk of breast cancer than those with the lowest (95% CI 1.06–1.40). Replacing a daily serving of red meat with legumes was linked to 15% lower risk, and with poultry to 17% lower.
[Farvid 2014](https://www.bmj.com/content/348/bmj.g3437)

## Trials

Most clinical trials of red meat and heart health are funded by the red meat industry, and industry-linked trials reach more favorable conclusions; see [[Corporate capture of health research#Red meat research|Corporate capture of health research]].

In a meta-analysis of 36 trials (1,803 people), red meat didn't change LDL-C compared with all other diets combined, but the comparison diet mattered. Compared with legumes, soy and nuts, red meat lowered LDL-C 0.20 mmol/L (8 mg/dL) less. Compared with fish, red meat lowered LDL-C more, and compared with refined carbohydrates it lowered triglycerides more.
[Guasch-Ferré 2019](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30958719/)
