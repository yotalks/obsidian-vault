> [!summary] Bottom line
> People with ADHD have about 71% higher all-cause mortality, with higher risk of death from suicide, injuries and natural causes. Among people with ADHD, taking ADHD medication is linked to about 25% lower all-cause mortality. These are observational findings, and the medication result rests on only 3 studies, so it doesn't show medication causes the lower risk.

## ADHD and causes of death

A systematic review and meta-analysis of 19 cohort and case-control studies, covering about 15.9 million people, found ADHD was associated with increased mortality across multiple causes:
- **All-cause mortality:** [[Glossary#HR|HR]] 1.71 (95% CI 1.31–2.24)
- **Suicide:** HR 1.89 (1.21–2.95)
- **Unintentional or intentional injury:** HR 1.55 (1.22–2.00)
- **Natural causes:** HR 1.27 (1.16–1.38)

Heterogeneity between studies was very high for all-cause mortality (I² = 98%), suicide (93%) and injury (87%), but negligible for natural causes (under 1%). The studies came from different countries with different diagnostic criteria and health care systems. The Egger test found no evidence of publication bias.

## ADHD medication

ADHD medication use was associated with decreased all-cause mortality risk in people with ADHD (HR 0.75, 95% CI 0.64–0.87), with moderate heterogeneity (I² = 55%). Medication is also linked to lower risk of injury, mental health conditions, suicidal behavior and substance use disorders, which could explain part of it.

This result comes from only 3 studies, and treated and untreated people may differ in health care access, socioeconomic status, illness severity, treatment adherence and psychiatric comorbidity. That's confounding by indication, so the association isn't evidence that medication itself lowers mortality.

The review was funded by the Medical Research Foundation, and one author works for the charity ADHD UK.
[Corrigan 2026](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42895743/)
