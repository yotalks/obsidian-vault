> [!summary] Bottom line
> Low-carb diets help with weight and blood sugar in the short term, but the advantage over low-fat diets is modest and mostly fades by a year or two. In adolescents they didn't improve insulin resistance more than low-fat diets, which undercuts the MAHA report's claim that they work by fixing insulin resistance. And very-low-carb diets, especially ones high in saturated fat, raise LDL.

## The report's claim

The report says some of the most compelling diet evidence comes from RCTs of reduced-carbohydrate diets that reverse obesity, type 2 diabetes, fatty liver disease (MASLD) and heart disease risk factors like hypertension. It says both cutting UPFs and cutting carbs work by addressing the root cause of these diseases, insulin resistance, and that low-carb diets have been studied in several two-year trials while UPF trials have lasted only two weeks.

## Weight and blood sugar

In adolescents with overweight or obesity, low-carb diets led to more weight loss than low-fat diets (2.8 kg) and better triglycerides and HDL, but there was no difference in insulin resistance (HOMA-IR), percent body fat, total cholesterol or LDL.
[Zhang 2024](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39145286/)

In people with type 2 diabetes, ketogenic diets lowered fasting blood glucose by 1.29 mmol/L, HbA1c by 1.07 points and weight by 8.7 kg. These are before-and-after changes, not comparisons with another diet.
[Yuan 2020](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33257645/)

Compared directly with low-fat diets in type 2 diabetes, low-carb diets lowered HbA1c by 0.41% at 3 months and reduced weight, BMI, fasting insulin and triglycerides in the short to intermediate term, and people needed somewhat less diabetes medication later on. Otherwise the two diets were about equally effective, with no difference in lean mass.

[Apekey 2022](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36297075/)

## Cholesterol

Across 37 trials, low-carb diets caused weight loss and lowered diastolic blood pressure but raised total cholesterol and HDL. Very-low-carb diets (under 30% of energy from carbs) raised LDL more than moderate ones, and replacing carbs mainly with saturated fat raised total cholesterol, LDL and HDL.
[Fechner 2020](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32252374/)
