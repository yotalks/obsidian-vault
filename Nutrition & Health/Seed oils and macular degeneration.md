> [!summary] Bottom line
> The idea that seed oils cause age-related macular degeneration (AMD) rests on ecological data. Prospective cohorts are mixed and mostly null, especially the best-adjusted ones, where linoleic acid looks like a marker of diets low in omega-3 and carotenoids. Mendelian randomization links genetically higher plasma linoleic acid to lower AMD risk.

## Ecological data

Seed oils have been suggested as being the primary culprits for age related macular degeneration. The data used is purely ecological, though.
[Knobbe 2017](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29150284/)

## Prospective cohorts

There are quite a bit of prospective cohort studies investigating this, however. There was significant heterogeneity across all investigated exposures, and no single exposure reached statistical significance.

Among studies with the longest follow up time, largest cohort size, best adjustment models, and widest exposure controls, the results tended to be null. Like the Chang study adjusted for lutein, zeaxanthin, and sources of omega 3 which are inversely associated with late AMD. Their results were null for every exposure.
[Ma 2012](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21899805/)
[Chong 2008](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18541848/)

The strongest study was the Christen study. Their analysis had 3 different adjustment models that help us better ascertain the relationship between AMD and seed oils. Their analysis showed that [[Glossary#LA|LA]] was associated with AMD only before adjustment and for AMD risk factors, and that the association was likely from low omega 3s.
Basically seed oils were a correlate for poor diet quality overall. The seed oils themselves weren't independently increasing risk, but they tend to associate with dietary patterns that lack characteristics that decrease risk. The diets high in seed oils tend to be low in carotenoids and omega 3, for example. After adjusting for such confounders, the association goes away.

## Mendelian randomization

There is also [[Glossary#MR|MR]] data that  looked at the relationship between elevated LA biomarkers and AMD, in this study with over 8631 people across nations, they used 3 different statistical tests across 2 subgroups looking at different combinations of gene variants. In the aggregate, genetically elevated plasma LA was consistently associated with reduced risk of AMD, regardless of the test or gene variant subgroup. Tissue [[Glossary#AA|AA]] was positively associated with AMD.
[Wang 2021](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33982092/)

## Overall

Overall, prospective cohort studies looking at the relationship between LA and AMD show a null association. More robust MR research, however, shows higher plasma LA may protect against AMD. So the evidence points towards increasing seed oil intake maybe having benefits.
