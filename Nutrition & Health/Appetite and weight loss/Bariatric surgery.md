> [!summary] Bottom line
> Bariatric surgery produces much larger and more durable weight loss than dieting, with about half of excess weight still gone 10 or more years later. In people with type 2 diabetes it's linked to much lower mortality, though most of that evidence isn't randomized. Diets do work to a degree, but average maintained losses are only a few kilograms after 5 years.

## How weight loss is measured

Surgical studies often report percent excess weight loss (%EWL), the share of weight above a healthy weight that was lost, instead of percent total weight loss (%TWL). This matters because %EWL makes the same loss look larger, and whether someone counts as regaining weight depends on the definition used.
[Grover 2019](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31256357/)

## Weight loss after surgery

In 22,094 patients, mean excess weight loss was 61.2% (95% CI 58.1–64.4%). Operative mortality within 30 days was 0.1% for purely restrictive procedures, 0.5% for gastric bypass and 1.1% for biliopancreatic diversion or duodenal switch.
[Buchwald 2004](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15479938/)

Results last. In reports with at least 10 years of follow-up, mean excess weight loss was:

| Procedure | %EWL at 10+ years |
| --- | --- |
| Biliopancreatic diversion ± duodenal switch | 74.1% |
| Sleeve gastrectomy | 58.3% |
| Gastric bypass | 56.7% |
| Adjustable gastric band | 45.9% |

Reoperations were common in all groups, the sleeve figure comes from only 2 reports, and the authors reported industry grants.
[O'Brien 2019](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30293134/)

In 1,574 patients at one center, 93% reached at least 50% EWL by 1–2 years, and 61.8% kept that through 10 years. For 20% TWL, the figures were 97% and 70.3%.
[Grover 2019](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31256357/)

## Health outcomes

Diabetes resolved completely in 76.8% of patients and hypertension in 61.7%.
[Buchwald 2004](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15479938/)

In people with type 2 diabetes, metabolic surgery was linked to lower mortality (OR 0.34, 95% CI 0.25–0.46) and fewer macrovascular complications (OR 0.38, 95% CI 0.22–0.67) than medical treatment. That's from 6 randomized trials and 13 nonrandomized studies, so healthier patients getting surgery could explain part of it.
[Billeter 2019](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31201113/)

## Diets for comparison

Across 29 long-term US studies, the average person kept off more than 3 kg 5 years after a structured weight-loss program. People on very-low-energy diets kept off 7.1 kg, or 29% of what they'd lost, and those on standard reduced-calorie diets kept off 2.0 kg, or 17%. Follow-up at 4–5 years was incomplete, at 55.4% and 79.7%.
[Anderson 2001](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11684524/)

In a randomized trial of 160 overweight or obese adults, one-year weight loss was similar on all four diets tested, assuming no change in those who dropped out: 2.1 kg on Atkins, 3.2 kg on Zone, 3.0 kg on Weight Watchers and 3.3 kg on Ornish. About half to two-thirds finished. Adherence predicted weight loss (r = 0.60), but diet type didn't (r = 0.07).
[Dansinger 2005](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15632335/)
