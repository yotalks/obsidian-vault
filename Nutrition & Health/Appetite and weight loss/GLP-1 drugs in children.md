## Children and height

In a phase 3 trial (STEP Young), 167 children aged 6 to under 12 with obesity were randomized 2:1 to weekly semaglutide or placebo for 104 weeks, followed by a 7-week washout, with lifestyle counseling in both groups:
- **BMI.** At week 68, BMI fell 13.5% with semaglutide and rose 2.7% with placebo. At week 104, it was −9.4% vs +8.4%.
- **Below the obesity threshold.** 38% of children on semaglutide ended up below the 95th percentile for BMI, vs 2% on placebo.
- **Growth.** Children on placebo grew taller faster. The investigators saw no effects on puberty progression, pubertal development or bone mineral density.
- **Side effects.** Adverse events occurred in 96% vs 93%, mostly GI, and GI events led one child on semaglutide to stop.
[Jastreboff 2026](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43037562/) · [Healio 2026](https://www.healio.com/news/endocrinology/20261116/wegovy-leads-to-substantial-bmi-decrease-for-children-aged-6-to-11-years-with-obesity)

### Obesity speeds childhood growth and slows pubertal growth

Slower growth is what we'd expect when childhood obesity is treated, because obesity shifts growth earlier rather than adding to final height:
- **Faster early growth.** In 8,629 German children (37,493 measurements), children with obesity were already slightly taller at birth and grew up to 1.2 cm/year faster afterward, alongside higher IGF-1, insulin and leptin. At 6 to 8 years they were up to 7.6 cm taller than normal-weight peers. [Kempf 2021](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34386750/)
- **Slower pubertal growth.** In the same cohorts, children with obesity caught down during puberty. Pubertal growth velocity was up to 25% lower, with IGF-1 down 17%, testosterone down 62% in boys and estradiol down 37% in girls. [Kempf 2021](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34386750/)
- **Same adult height.** In 1,901 Swedish children, overweight or obese children grew faster before puberty, started puberty about 3 months earlier, and gained 2.3 cm (girls) and 3.1 cm (boys) less during puberty. Adult height wasn't related to childhood BMI. [Holmgren 2017](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27861464/)
- **Earlier, smaller growth spurt.** 100 obese prepubertal children followed every 6 months entered puberty earlier than 55 normal-weight controls and had an earlier, lower peak height velocity (boys 7.7 vs 9.3 cm/year, girls 7.6 vs 8.3). Their height SDS declined steadily, while the controls' didn't change. [De Leonibus 2014](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23713062/)
- **Dose-response.** In 68 children with moderate to extreme obesity, the higher the peak BMI in childhood, the smaller the pubertal height gain. [Holmgren 2021](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33173182/)

### Weight loss in childhood and final height

Children who lost weight in non-drug programs ended up at normal heights for their parents:
- **10-year follow-up.** Obese children aged 6 to 12 given family-based behavioral treatment were, 10 years later, on average 1.8 cm taller than their same-sex parent (r = .78), with no height differences between groups. [Epstein 1990](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/2232019/)
- **Height percentile.** In 158 obese children aged 6 to 12, the height percentile at entry (71.6) was well above their same-sex parent's (52.0). After 10 years and 22.7 cm of growth, it fell to 57.8, and they were 2.2 cm taller than their same-sex parent. Change in percent overweight didn't predict change in height, and obese and nonobese children didn't differ in growth at 10 years. [Epstein 1993](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8213679/)
- **Predicted adult height.** In 50 obese children aged 6 to 13, a 12-month family program cut BMI by about 5%. Height SDS fell and bone maturation slowed, but predicted adult height didn't change, which the authors read as reversing earlier early maturation. [Bermudez de la Vega 2007](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17882344/)

So the slower growth seen with semaglutide fits obesity treatment pulling childhood growth back toward normal. Final height would only end up lower if GLP-1 drugs had a direct effect on growth, or if children regained the weight and then had the smaller pubertal growth spurt on top of the slower early growth. Neither has been ruled out, so follow-up into adulthood is still needed.
