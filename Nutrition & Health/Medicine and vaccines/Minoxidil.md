> [!summary] Bottom line
> Minoxidil works as a preventive for pattern hair loss without touching DHT. Instead of blocking DHT or the conversion of testosterone to DHT, it offsets DHT's downstream effects on the follicle, partly by stimulating growth, and the hair is still kept from miniaturizing. Topical minoxidil's benefit peaks around a year and is still above baseline after 5 years, and oral minoxidil works about as well as topical in trials.

## How it prevents miniaturization

Minoxidil's only real use for pattern hair loss is as a preventive. Used consistently, it prevents DHT-driven miniaturization over the long term. It just doesn't do it by blocking DHT or the conversion of testosterone to DHT, the way finasteride does. It works on the downstream effects of the DHT pathway instead, and stimulating growth is one way it does that.

From the follicle's point of view, it matters little whether miniaturization is prevented by stopping testosterone converting to DHT, blocking DHT, or growth factors offsetting DHT. Either way, the hair is kept from miniaturizing.

## Mechanism

How minoxidil grows hair still isn't well understood. In animals, it shortens the resting (telogen) phase so follicles enter the growth (anagen) phase sooner, and it may also lengthen anagen and enlarge follicles. Its blood-pressure effect comes from its metabolite, minoxidil sulphate, opening potassium (KATP) channels, and the same may explain its effect on hair, though that hasn't been proven. In cell cultures it stimulates cell growth, vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF) and prostaglandins, but it's unclear how those apply to real follicles.
[Messenger 2004](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/14996087/)

## Long-term effect

In 31 men using topical minoxidil for 4.5 to 5 years, regrowth tended to peak at about a year and then slowly declined, but they still had more non-vellus hairs than at baseline after 5 years.
[Olsen 1990](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/2180995/)

## Oral vs topical

A meta-analysis of 4 trials (279 people, 24–39 weeks) found oral and topical minoxidil gave similar improvements in hair density and hair diameter. Oral minoxidil caused unwanted hair growth elsewhere (hypertrichosis) about twice as often, with no significant difference in low blood pressure.
[Sobral 2025](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39425514/)

Low-dose oral minoxidil isn't FDA-approved for hair loss. It's usually started at 1–5 mg a day for men and 0.5–1 mg for women, up to about 5 mg, and its effect seems to depend on dose. The main side effects are hypertrichosis and cardiovascular symptoms, with rare fluid around the heart (pericardial effusion).
[Gupta 2023](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38376087/)

The evidence for oral minoxidil across types of hair loss is still low quality, with few randomized trials.
[do Nascimento 2020](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33376283/)
