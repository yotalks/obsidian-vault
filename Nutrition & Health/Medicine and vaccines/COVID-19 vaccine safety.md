> [!summary] Bottom line
> Claims that COVID-19 vaccines caused a wave of deaths don't fit the data. US excess deaths were rising for months before vaccination began and fell as it spread, VAERS accepts unverified reports, and apparent spikes in "ill-defined" deaths are a reporting lag that happens every year. Vaccines can cause rare myocarditis, but so can COVID-19, and early studies found no effect on ovarian function or IVF outcomes.

## Excess deaths and the vaccine rollout

US excess mortality (deaths compared with the 2015–2019 average) was already rising months before vaccination started in mid-December 2020. It peaked at about 50% above normal in early January 2021, shortly after the rollout began, and then fell steeply to near normal by spring 2021 as vaccination spread. A rise in deaths after vaccination started doesn't show the vaccines caused it when the rise started well before them.

## VAERS

The Vaccine Adverse Event Reporting System (VAERS) accepts reports from anyone, and they aren't routinely verified, so it can flag possible signals but can't show that a vaccine caused anything. To demonstrate this, one physician filed a report that a flu vaccine had turned him into the Incredible Hulk. The report was accepted, and VAERS only deleted it after contacting him and getting his permission.
[Laidler: Chelation and autism](https://web.archive.org/web/20060423090641/http://www.neurodiversity.com/weblog/article/14/chelation-autism)

Self-reported data like VAERS can't override randomized placebo-controlled trials, which compare adverse events directly against a placebo group → [[COVID-19 vaccine trials]].

## Delayed death coding (R00–R99)

The ICD codes R00–R99 cover "symptoms, signs and abnormal clinical and laboratory findings, not elsewhere classified": deaths whose cause hasn't been determined yet. Recent deaths often sit in this category while the cause is investigated, and are reclassified once death certificates are completed. So in any single CDC data release, the most recent weeks always show a spike in R00–R99 deaths.

Plotted from one release, R00–R99 deaths seem to climb steeply from late 2020 into 2021, as if they rose with vaccination:
![[CDC R00-R99 deaths single release.png]]

But comparing successive releases shows the spike moving forward with each one. The weeks that looked high in the December 2020 release had dropped back to normal by the June 2021 release, as those deaths were assigned their real causes. The spike is an artifact of the reporting lag, not a real rise in deaths.

## Myocarditis

Myocarditis and pericarditis can follow both COVID-19 vaccination and COVID-19 itself, so they don't set the vaccine apart from the infection it prevents. COVID-19 myocarditis is thought to come from direct viral injury combined with the immune response.
[Siripanthong 2020](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32387246/)

In a case series of 7 healthy adolescent boys who developed myocarditis or myopericarditis within 4 days of their second Pfizer dose, all had elevated troponin and MRI findings typical of myocarditis, and all recovered rapidly.
[Marshall 2021](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34088762/)
[ACC: COVID-19 as a possible cause of myocarditis and pericarditis](https://www.acc.org/latest-in-cardiology/articles/2021/02/05/19/37/covid-19-as-a-possible-cause-of-myocarditis-and-pericarditis)

## Fertility

In 47 women who had IVF (ICSI) cycles both before and after the Pfizer vaccine, with each woman as her own control, the number of eggs retrieved, mature eggs, fertilization rate, embryo quality and clinical pregnancies didn't differ before and after vaccination.
[Safrai 2021](https://www.medrxiv.org/content/10.1101/2021.05.30.21258079v1)

In women undergoing egg retrieval, antibodies from both infection (n = 9) and the Pfizer vaccine (n = 9) reached the follicular fluid, but neither changed any measure of follicle function compared with 14 uninfected, unvaccinated controls.
[Bentov 2021](https://www.medrxiv.org/content/10.1101/2021.04.09.21255195v1.full.pdf)

Both were small preprints at the time.
