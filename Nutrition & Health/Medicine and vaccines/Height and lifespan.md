> [!summary] Bottom line
> Being taller raises cancer risk by about 10% per 9–10 cm, which fits taller people having more cells, and raises atrial fibrillation risk, but it lowers coronary artery disease risk. Growth hormone treatment in children adds height roughly in proportion. In children treated for isolated growth hormone deficiency or idiopathic short stature, long-term mortality wasn't higher than in the general population. Acromegaly, where growth hormone is pathologically high, raises cancer risk only slightly overall.

## Growth hormone treatment

In 200 Syrian children aged 4–9 treated with growth hormone, height rose over 4 years by 2.06 standard deviations in growth hormone deficiency, 1.48 in idiopathic short stature, 1.21 in children born small for gestational age, and 0.82 in Turner syndrome. Most of the gain came in the first year. It was a retrospective study without an untreated group.
[Al-Hawasli 2025](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41316111/)

### Body proportions

In a randomized, double-blind dose trial of 79 short children born small for gestational age, 6 years of growth hormone at either dose moved all body measurements toward normal. Sitting height grew a bit more than total height and reached the normal range, while hands, feet and pelvis became smaller relative to height. The authors found no negative effect on body proportions.
[Sas 2000](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11155088/)

In 37 children on growth hormone, kidneys were smaller than in 48 healthy controls at first assessment. Over 3 years, in the 23 who were followed up, kidney length and volume grew at a similar rate to height, and kidney function improved.
[Ece 2014](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24456095/)

## Height and cancer

Taller people have more cells, so if each cell carries some chance of becoming cancerous, taller people should get more cancer. Across 4 large cancer surveillance datasets covering 23 cancer categories, the predictions of this cell-number model held up, with overall cancer risk about 10% higher per 10 cm of height ([[Glossary#HR|HR]] about 1.1). Melanoma was more strongly linked to height than cell number alone predicts.
[Nunney 2018](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30355711/)

The genetic evidence agrees. In about 310,000 UK Biobank participants, each 9 cm of genetically predicted height raised the odds of being diagnosed with any cancer ([[Glossary#OR|OR]] 1.10, 95% CI 1.07–1.13) and of dying from cancer (OR 1.09, 1.02–1.16). The effect was larger in women.
[Ong 2018](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29581483/)

In 367,561 UK Biobank participants, each standard deviation of genetically predicted height (about 6.5 cm) raised overall cancer risk (OR 1.09, 1.05–1.12):

| Cancer | OR per 6.5 cm (95% CI) |
|---|---|
| **Biliary tract** | 1.45 (1.14–1.84) |
| **Ovarian** | 1.21 (1.05–1.40) |
| **Kidney** | 1.19 (1.02–1.38) |
| **Breast** | 1.12 (1.06–1.19) |
| **Colorectal** | 1.09 (1.01–1.19) |

The ovarian (1.37) and breast (1.10) results replicated in large international consortia.
[Vithayathil 2021](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34324486/)

These are [[Glossary#MR|Mendelian randomization]] studies, so they test lifelong, genetically set differences in height. Height added by growth hormone in childhood may not carry the same risk. → [[Mendelian randomization]]

## Height and heart disease

Height cuts both ways for the heart. In 65,066 people with coronary artery disease and 128,383 controls, each 6.5 cm less of genetically predicted height raised coronary artery disease risk by 13.5% (95% CI 5.4–22.1%), and the tallest quarter had 26% lower odds than the shortest (OR 0.74, 0.68–0.84). LDL cholesterol and triglycerides, which were less favorable in shorter people, explained about 30% of the link.
[Nelson 2015](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25853659/)

Taller people get more atrial fibrillation. In 65,446 cases and 522,744 controls, each 6.5 cm of genetically predicted height raised the odds by 34% (OR 1.34, 1.29–1.40), and this held after accounting for usual cardiovascular risk factors and, in a separate biobank, left atrial size.
[Levin 2020](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33031386/)

In the Million Veteran Program, genetically predicted height was linked to 127 conditions in 222,300 White veterans, largely independent of BMI. It confirmed the links with high blood pressure, high cholesterol, coronary heart disease and atrial fibrillation, and found new ones with venous circulation disorders and peripheral neuropathy.
[Raghavan 2022](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35653334/)

## Growth hormone excess

Acromegaly is an adult condition where the body makes too much growth hormone, which raises IGF-1. In 30 people with acromegaly, colon lining cells divided faster than in controls (a labeling index of 10.0% vs 5.7%), and only IGF-1 independently predicted this.
[Cats 1996](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8564965/)

In 529 Danish people with acromegaly, overall cancer incidence wasn't clearly higher than in the general population ([[Glossary#SIR|SIR]] 1.1, 95% CI 0.9–1.4), and cancer deaths weren't raised, though all-cause mortality was (SMR 1.3, 1.1–1.6). A meta-analysis of 23 studies with 9,677 patients found:

| Cancer | SIR (95% CI) |
|---|---|
| **Thyroid** | 9.2 (4.2–19.9) |
| **Colorectal** | 2.6 (1.7–4.0) |
| **Gastric** | 2.0 (1.4–2.9) |
| **Breast** | 1.6 (1.1–2.3) |
| **Overall** | 1.5 (1.2–1.8) |

Single-center studies found much higher overall risk (3.2) than population-based ones (1.4), which the authors put down mostly to selection and surveillance bias.
[Dal 2018](https://academic.oup.com/jcem/article/103/6/2182/4951496)

## Long-term safety of childhood treatment

In 24,232 children treated with growth hormone in 8 European countries and followed for over 400,000 patient-years, all-cause mortality in those treated for isolated growth hormone deficiency or idiopathic short stature was no higher than in the general population ([[Glossary#SMR|SMR]] 1.1, 95% CI 0.9–1.3). It was higher in children born small for gestational age (1.5, 1.1–1.9), mostly because of the French subgroup, and much higher in children with serious underlying conditions. Mortality wasn't linked to daily or cumulative dose in any group.
[Sävendahl 2020](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32707116/)

In Sweden, 3,408 people treated with growth hormone as children were compared with 50,036 matched controls over a median 19.8 years. Neoplasms overall were more common (HR 1.28, 1.12–1.46), mostly in men, but malignant tumors weren't (HR 0.91, 0.66–1.26).
[Tidblad 2024](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38505755/)
