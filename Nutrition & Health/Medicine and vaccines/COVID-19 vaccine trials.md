> [!summary] Bottom line
> The trials reviewed here found strong protection against symptomatic and severe COVID-19, with efficacy varying by vaccine and follow-up period. Side effects were mostly short-lived, and serious adverse events were similar between vaccine and control groups. Detailed results and follow-up findings are organized by vaccine below.

## Pfizer–BioNTech (BNT162b2)

### Choosing the candidate

The phase 1/2/3 protocol tested two candidates built on the same modified mRNA platform: BNT162b1, which encodes the spike protein's receptor-binding domain, and BNT162b2, which encodes the full-length spike, stabilized in its prefusion shape.
[Protocol C4591001](https://cdn.pfizer.com/pfizercom/2020-11/C4591001_Clinical_Protocol_Nov2020.pdf)

In the US phase 1 trial, 195 adults aged 18–55 or 65–85 were split into 13 groups of 15, with 12 getting vaccine and 3 placebo in each group. Both candidates produced similar neutralizing antibody levels, as high as or higher than in people who had recovered from COVID-19. BNT162b2 caused fewer and milder systemic reactions, especially in older adults. After the second 30 µg dose in 18–55-year-olds, fever occurred in 75% with BNT162b1 but 17% with BNT162b2. So BNT162b2 at 30 µg went forward to phase 2/3. Pain at the injection site was the most common local reaction with both.
[Walsh 2020](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33053279/)

### How success was defined

Efficacy was measured as 100 × (1 − the ratio of COVID-19 rates in the vaccine and placebo groups). The protocol planned 164 cases, enough for 90% power if the true efficacy was 60%. Success meant at least a 98.6% probability that efficacy was above 30%, under a pessimistic prior centred on 30% efficacy. Interim analyses after 32, 62, 92 and 120 cases needed a 99.5% probability to stop early for efficacy.
[Protocol C4591001](https://cdn.pfizer.com/pfizercom/2020-11/C4591001_Clinical_Protocol_Nov2020.pdf)

### Phase 3 results

43,548 people aged 16 and over were randomized, and 43,448 got two doses 21 days apart of vaccine (21,720) or placebo (21,728). From 7 days after the second dose, there were 8 COVID-19 cases with vaccine and 162 with placebo: 95% efficacy (95% credible interval 90.3–97.6). The vaccine group's risk was about one twentieth of the placebo group's. Efficacy was 90–100% across age, sex, race, ethnicity, body weight and coexisting conditions. Of 10 severe cases after the first dose, 9 were in the placebo group.
[Polack 2020](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33301246/)

Protection started before the second dose. The two groups' case curves split about 12 days after the first dose, and efficacy between the first and second doses was 52%.

**Side effects** in the 7 days after the second dose, vaccine vs placebo:

| | 16–55 years | Over 55 |
|---|---|---|
| Injection-site pain | 78% vs 12% | 66% vs 8% |
| Fatigue | 59% vs 23% | 51% vs 17% |
| Headache | 52% vs 24% | 39% vs 14% |
| Muscle pain | 37% vs 8% | 29% vs 5% |
| Chills | 35% vs 4% | 23% vs 3% |
| Fever | 16% vs 0% | 11% vs 0% |

They were mostly mild or moderate and short-lived, and less common in older adults. Serious adverse events were rare and similar in both groups. Two vaccine recipients died (arteriosclerosis, cardiac arrest) and four placebo recipients died (two from unknown causes, a hemorrhagic stroke and a heart attack). None of the deaths was considered related to the vaccine or placebo.

### Six-month follow-up

Through 6 months, efficacy was 91.3% (95% CI 89.0–93.2), declining gradually, and 96.7% against severe disease. It was 86–100% across countries and groups. In South Africa, where the beta variant dominated, it was 100% (95% CI 53.5–100). Few participants withdrew because of adverse events.
[Thomas 2021](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34525277/)

All three Pfizer papers were funded by BioNTech and Pfizer.

## Moderna (mRNA-1273)

30,415 US adults at high risk of COVID-19 or its complications were randomized to two 100 µg doses 28 days apart or placebo. At the end of the blinded phase, median follow-up was 5.3 months. Efficacy was 93.2% (95% CI 91.0–94.8), with 55 cases on vaccine and 744 on placebo. Against severe disease it was 98.2% (2 vs 106 cases), and against asymptomatic infection 63.0%. Efficacy was consistent across ethnic, racial and age groups and in people with coexisting conditions.
[El Sahly 2021](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34551225/)

Unsolicited adverse events over the whole blinded phase were similar with vaccine and placebo:

| | mRNA-1273 (n = 15,184) | Placebo (n = 15,162) |
|---|---|---|
| Any | 41.6% | 43.0% |
| Serious | 1.8% | 1.9% |
| Fatal | 17 | 16 |
| Severe | 3.0% | 3.2% |
| Serious and judged related to vaccination | 12 | 4 |
| Severe and judged related to vaccination | 0.6% | 0.2% |

No deaths were judged related to vaccination. The trial was funded by the US government (BARDA and NIAID).
[El Sahly 2021 supplementary appendix](https://www.nejm.org/doi/suppl/10.1056/NEJMoa2113017/suppl_file/nejmoa2113017_appendix.pdf)

## Oxford–AstraZeneca (ChAdOx1 nCoV-19)

ChAdOx1 nCoV-19 is a viral vector vaccine, not an mRNA one. It uses a harmless chimpanzee adenovirus to carry the spike gene. Four blinded randomized trials in the UK, Brazil and South Africa compared it with a meningococcal vaccine (MenACWY) or saline.

In the pooled interim analysis (11,636 participants), efficacy from 14 days after the second dose was 70.4% (30 cases among 5,807 vaccinated vs 101 among 5,829 controls). It was 62.1% with two standard doses and 90.0% in a UK subgroup whose first dose was accidentally halved, though that subgroup was small (3 vs 30 cases). From 21 days after the first dose, all 10 COVID-19 hospitalizations, including 2 severe cases and 1 death, were in the control group. Serious adverse events were similar (84 events with vaccine, 91 with control).
[Voysey 2021](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33306989/)

In the later primary analysis (17,178 participants), efficacy was 66.7% (95% CI 57.4–74.0), with 84 vs 248 cases and no hospitalizations in the vaccine group after the first 3 weeks, versus 15 in the control group. A single standard dose gave 76.0% efficacy from day 22 to day 90. Two doses gave more protection with a longer gap between them: 81.3% at 12 weeks or more, versus 55.1% at under 6 weeks. Serious adverse events occurred in 0.9% of the vaccine group and 1.1% of controls.
[Voysey 2021b](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33617777/)

The trials were funded by UK Research and Innovation, the NIHR, CEPI, the Gates Foundation, other foundations, and AstraZeneca.

## Johnson & Johnson and Delta-era findings

The single-dose Johnson & Johnson (Janssen) vaccine prevented about 56% of moderate to severe COVID-19 and 73% of severe disease. Against the Delta variant, protection against infection fell, more for Pfizer than Moderna, while protection against severe disease stayed high.
