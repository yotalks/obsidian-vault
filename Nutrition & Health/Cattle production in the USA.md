US beef cattle start on pasture in the cow-calf phase (about 6–10 months), often spend 2–6 months more grazing as stockers, and then differ in the finishing phase. Grass-finished cattle keep grazing for about 6–10 months and are harvested at 20–26 months. Grain-finished cattle spend about 4–6 months in a feedlot and are harvested at 14–22 months.
![[Grass vs grain finished cattle timeline.png]]
[Beef Research: Grass versus grain-finished](https://www.beefresearch.org/resources/beef-sustainability/fact-sheets/grass-versus-grain-finished)

So grain-finished cattle spend most of their lives on grass: between about 57% (8 of 14 months) and 81% (18 of 22 months) of their time, with a middle estimate of about 72%.
