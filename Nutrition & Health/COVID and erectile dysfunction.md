> [!summary] Bottom line
> COVID-19 is linked to a higher risk of erectile dysfunction (ED), and the link is plausibly causal: the virus has been found in the penile tissue of men who developed ED after COVID-19, and in animals it infects and damages the male genital tract. Vaccination prevented the damage in hamsters. The human data are still observational and imprecise, and some of the link may come from anxiety. It matters because ED is linked to higher mortality and self-harm.

## Why ED matters

ED is associated with a higher risk of death. In a 15-year study of 1,709 men aged 40–70, ED was linked to 26% higher all-cause mortality and 43% higher cardiovascular mortality, even after adjusting for BMI, alcohol, physical activity, smoking, self-rated health, heart disease, [[Glossary#HTN|hypertension]] and [[Glossary#DM|diabetes]]. The authors attribute it mainly to cardiovascular deaths, so it could be residual confounding from [[Glossary#CVD|CVD]], but a higher risk of suicide may also play a role.
[Araujo 2009](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19538544/)

ED is also associated with self-harm. In Australian cohorts, men with erectile problems had higher rates of self-harm: 1.9 times among those aged 45 and over, and 2.8 times (95% CI 1.0–7.7) among those 65 and over. Neither study linked ED to suicide itself.
[Erlangsen 2020](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32812087/)
[Erlangsen 2021](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33166417/)

Ecological data links access to ED treatment with suicide. After sildenafil (Viagra) came off patent in Sweden in 2013 and its price fell, there were 65 fewer suicides than expected among men aged 50–59.
[Catalano 2021](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33796979/)

## Human data

In an Italian online survey, ED was more common in men who'd had COVID-19 than in matched men who hadn't (28% vs 9.3%). The [[Glossary#OR|odds ratio]] was 5.66 after adjusting for psychological status, age and BMI, but the study was small (25 COVID-positive men) and the confidence interval was wide (1.50–24.01).
[Sansone 2021](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33742540/)

In a US patient registry, men with COVID-19 had 3.3 times the odds of ED. The odds ratio stayed between 1.6 and 4.8 when adjusting for age, smoking, race, respiratory disease, obesity, circulatory disease or diabetes, but each was adjusted for one at a time, not together.
[Katz 2021](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34846719/)

In men aged 20–40, erectile function scores and testosterone were lower after COVID-19 than before. But this only compares the same men before and after, with no control group to show what would have happened without COVID-19.
[Karkin 2021](https://doi.org/10.22541/au.161647831.14283015/v1)

A meta-analysis of 8 studies found men who'd had COVID-19 had 2.64 times the [[Glossary#RR|risk]] of ED, though the confidence interval barely excluded no effect (1.01–6.88). Anxiety was a risk factor for ED in COVID-19 patients.
[Zhang 2023](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36307637/)

US sales of ED medications rose 67% from February to December 2020, driven by tadalafil, and sales of the daily 5 mg dose doubled. Sales of other urology drugs didn't change. Surveys mostly found people had less sex during the pandemic, though the authors read the sales as more sex for at least some men.
[Hernandez 2021](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34170453/)

## Mechanistic data

Penile tissue from two men who developed severe ED after COVID-19 still contained the virus long after the initial infection: PCR found viral RNA, and electron microscopy showed viral particles near the blood vessel lining. Their tissue also showed signs of endothelial dysfunction (less eNOS) compared with two men with ED who'd never had COVID-19.
[Kresch 2021](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33988001/)

In hamsters, SARS-CoV-2 caused an acute drop in sperm count and testosterone, testicular inflammation and cell death, and testicular shrinkage that lasted up to 120 days. Flu virus didn't do this. Delta and Omicron caused similar damage, and vaccinated hamsters showed no testicular damage.
[Li 2022](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35178548/)

In rhesus macaques, a tracer that binds the virus's spike protein lit up the male genital tract in all three animals, and infected cells were found in testicular and penile tissue. One animal had severe testicular damage two weeks after infection. This is a preprint.
[Madden 2022](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2022.02.25.481974v1)

> [!note] My take
> Given the data, it's concerning and very understudied, and I think it's enough to include in a risk-benefit analysis of COVID-19. We need more robust data on whether and how much vaccination reduces the risk, whether any treatments do, whether it's self-limiting or permanent, and whether the risk is higher, lower or the same with Omicron.
