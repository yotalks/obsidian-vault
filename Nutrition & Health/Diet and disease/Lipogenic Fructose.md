> [!summary] Bottom line
> Overfeeding fructose raises liver fat more than glucose does, but on a weight-maintaining diet the rise is temporary. The main driver of NAFLD is excess weight from a calorie surplus, and losing weight improves it whatever the carb intake.

## The fructose hypothesis

It's been proposed that the main cause of [[Glossary#NAFLD|NAFLD]] in the population is an overconsumption of dietary fructose.

## Overfeeding vs. weight maintenance

When subjects are overfed 1000 calories consisting of 50% glucose and 50% fructose, there is a marked increase in liver fat (around 27%). This is because fructose stimulates de novo lipogenesis to a greater degree than glucose. The effect reduces insulin sensitivity and definitely contributes to visceral fat.
[Sevastianova 2012](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22952180/)

When we are maintaining our weight on a diet equally rich in fructose, however, it even shows dietary fructose uniquely produces a 25% increase in liver fat on average. But since it's a eucaloric diet, these effects are usually temporary. Fasting insulin, triglycerides, and [[Glossary#DNL|DNL]] didn't differ significantly between diet periods. This means that fructose did increase postprandial liver fat. But it was burned off the next day, as evidenced by the subjects maintaining their weight. So the temporary increase in liver fat doesn't matter.
[Schwarz 2015](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4454806/)

## Excess weight drives NAFLD

The main driver of NAFLD is excess weight, which happens because of a caloric surplus.
[Lean 2018](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29221645/)

When NAFLD subjects lose weight, their NAFLD improves or goes away, regardless of whether they had high carbs or not.
[Kirk 2009](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2677125/)

## Where I land

> [!note] My take
> But what begs the question is that in the overfeeding study where they saw fructose consumption only increases liver fat by 27%, where did the baseline liver fat come from, if not fructose? Dietary fat, as it's the most lipogenic energy substrate to the liver. So to maximally reduce liver fat, one would likely have to restrict dietary fat over all other macros.
