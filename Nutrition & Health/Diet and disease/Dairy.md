> [!summary] Bottom line
> In cohort studies, more dairy is linked to slightly higher prostate cancer risk, apparently not because of its calcium or fat. In young children, drinking a lot of cow's milk is linked to iron deficiency, and infants given cow's milk instead of follow-on formula had nearly 4 times the risk of iron deficiency anemia. Genetic data show milk doesn't lower the risk of diabetes or obesity. Cheese made with vegetable oil instead of milk fat lowers LDL cholesterol. An ethics analysis argues milk causes more animal suffering and more cattle deaths per calorie than beef.

## Prostate cancer

In a meta-analysis of 32 cohort studies, higher intakes of these were linked to slightly higher total prostate cancer risk:
- total dairy ([[Glossary#RR|RR]] 1.07 per 400 g a day)
- milk (1.03 per 200 g a day)
- low-fat milk (1.06 per 200 g a day)
- cheese (1.09 per 50 g a day)
- dietary calcium (1.05 per 400 mg a day)

Calcium from dairy was linked to higher risk, but calcium from other foods or supplements wasn't. Supplemental calcium was linked to fatal prostate cancer. Because low-fat milk carried risk and nondairy calcium didn't, the authors think something in dairy other than fat or calcium is responsible.
[Aune 2015](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25527754/)

## Iron deficiency in young children

In 400 healthy Dutch children aged 6 months to 3 years, 18.8% were iron deficient and 8.5% had iron deficiency anemia. Drinking a lot of cow's milk was linked to more iron deficiency, while formula and going to day care were linked to less.
[Uijterschout 2014](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24145621/)

A systematic review of cow's milk in children up to age 3 found that infants drinking cow's milk had 3.8 times the risk of iron deficiency anemia of infants on follow-on formula (RR 3.76, 95% CI 2.73–5.19, 4 studies). The age cow's milk was introduced wasn't linked to type 1 diabetes, and no other harms turned up.
[Griebler 2016](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25989719/)

A review explains why. Cow's milk has little iron, and what it has is poorly absorbed. Its calcium and protein interfere with absorbing iron from other foods. It's also linked to small intestinal bleeding in young children, and introducing it early or in place of breast milk can cause digestive and allergic problems.
[Oliveira 2005](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16247536/)

## Diabetes and weight

In 97,811 Danes, drinking any milk wasn't linked to lower type 2 diabetes risk (HR 1.10, 95% CI 0.98–1.24) and was linked to slightly more overweight (OR 1.06). Using [[Glossary#MR|Mendelian randomization]], people with the lactase persistence gene drank more milk (a median of 5 vs 3 glasses a week) but had no lower diabetes risk ([[Glossary#OR|OR]] 0.96, 0.86–1.08) and slightly more overweight (OR 1.06, 1.00–1.12).
[Bergholdt 2015](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26156736/)

## Cheese and LDL

[[Glossary#SFA|SFA]] rich cheese doesn't seem to increase LDL-C but [[Glossary#PUFA|PUFA]] enriched cheeses lowers LDL-C. The ratio of PUFA to SFA still affects LDL-C, even with cheese.
[Davis 1993](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8363165/)
[Karvonen 2002](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12428175/)

## Animal welfare

An ethical analysis argues that milk is a bigger problem than meat. Meat can be produced with little suffering, but milk always involves considerable suffering over a dairy cow's life and the lives of her calves. Milk production also causes more cattle deaths per calorie than meat production. The author concludes that replacing calories from meat with calories from dairy makes no sense if animal welfare is the goal.
[Kolbe 2018](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10806-018-9740-9)
