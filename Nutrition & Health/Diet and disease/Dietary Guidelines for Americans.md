> [!summary] Bottom line
> The MAHA report says the Dietary Guidelines are reductionist and industry-influenced, and should focus on minimizing ultra-processed foods instead of limiting nutrients like saturated fat and sodium. But "ultra-processed" doesn't map onto healthiness: a menu following the guidelines got 91% of its calories from ultra-processed foods and still scored 86 out of 100 for diet quality.

## The report's claims

These are the report's claims:
- Children see 15 food ads a day, over 90% for products high in fat, sugar or sodium, which has been linked to more cravings for sugary drinks and other unhealthy foods.
- The Dietary Guidelines for Americans (DGA) shape SNAP, the National School Lunch Program, food standards for the military, prisons and veterans' care, nutrition labels and industry practices.
- They recommend whole foods and limiting added sugar, saturated fat and sodium, but are written in technical language, which may help explain why fewer than 10% of Americans follow them fully.
- They're "reductionist", advising people to reduce saturated fat or limit sodium instead of minimizing ultra-processed foods.
- They've been influenced by industry: the 1992 Food Pyramid was shaped by sugar-industry research, put carbohydrates at the base and didn't distinguish refined from whole grains; a 2015 recommendation to cut processed meat was dropped after meat-industry pushback; and 95% of the 2020 advisory committee had industry ties.
- Federal crop insurance has mostly covered wheat, corn and soybeans: specialty crops were 17% of the portfolio in 2017, and fruit, vegetable, nut and organic support was 0.1% of the 2018 Farm Bill, while over 80% of Farm Bill spending goes to SNAP. SNAP served 42 million people a month at a cost of $113 billion in 2023, and 1 in 5 children receive it.

## The food pyramid

The withdrawn 1991 "Eating Right Pyramid" and the 1992 "Food Guide Pyramid" are nearly identical. Both put grains at the base, and the 1992 version tells people to use fats, oils and sweets "sparingly", which sits oddly with the claim that it was shaped by the sugar industry.

## Can a healthy diet be ultra-processed?

The report wants the guidelines to focus on minimizing ultra-processed foods (see [[Ultra-processed food and additives]]). But researchers built a 7-day menu that follows the 2020 guidelines' healthy dietary pattern with 91% of calories from foods NOVA classifies as ultra-processed. It scored 86 out of 100 on the Healthy Eating Index, losing points mainly for excess sodium and too few whole grains, and provided enough of every nutrient except vitamin D, vitamin E and choline. Some of the authors have food-industry affiliations.
