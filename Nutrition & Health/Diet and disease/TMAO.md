> [!summary] Bottom line
> Fish raises TMAO far more than eggs or meat, and Mendelian randomization finds no causal link between TMAO and heart disease. Choline supplements can raise fasting TMAO and platelet reactivity, eggs and phosphatidylcholine don't seem to, and neither result has been tied to disease outcomes. Vegans and vegetarians have lower fasting TMAO than omnivores, but among vegetarians it didn't depend on how strictly they avoided animal foods. In the main cohort, only the top quarter of TMAO stayed linked to heart attacks, strokes and deaths after adjustment.

Researchers fed participants dozens of different foods and measured how much TMAO showed up in their urine. Seafood was by far the biggest source. Choline and carnitine rich foods like eggs and meat produced comparatively small amounts. Fish is widely considered a heart healthy food, yet raises TMAO the most which makes it harder to argue TMAO itself is dangerous.
[Zhang 1999](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0278691599000289?via%3Dihub#TBL2)

Of 46 foods tested, only fish and other seafood significantly raised urinary TMA and TMAO. Fruits, vegetables, cereals, dairy and meat had no measurable effect. Halibut raised it the most, followed by cod, herring and haddock, while beef was no different from the control meal.
![[Urinary TMA and TMAO by food.webp]]
[Zhang 1999](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10456680/)

The strongest research we have on the relationship between TMAO and heart disease risk with the Mendelian randomization data, there is no causal link between TMAO in the blood and heart disease risk.
[Jia 2019](https://diabetes.diabetesjournals.org/content/68/9/1747)

Genetically predicted TMAO wasn't linked to coronary artery disease, heart attack, stroke, atrial fibrillation, type 2 diabetes or chronic kidney disease. In the other direction, type 2 diabetes and chronic kidney disease were causally linked to higher TMAO. Both also raise heart disease risk, so they could explain some of the link seen in cohorts.
[Jia 2019](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31167879/)

## Cohort studies

In 4,007 patients having elective coronary angiography, those in the top TMAO quartile (above 6.18 µM) had more deaths, heart attacks and strokes over 3 years than those in the bottom quartile (HR 2.54, 95% CI 1.96–3.28). After adjusting for risk factors, kidney function and other factors, this fell to 1.49 (1.10–2.03). This group was already at high risk, since 42% had a previous heart attack, so it may not apply to healthy people.
[Tang 2013](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23614584/)

Hazard ratios for each TMAO quartile compared with the lowest (below 2.43 µM):

| Quartile | TMAO | Unadjusted | Adjusted for risk factors and CRP | Also adjusted for kidney function, medications and more |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **2** | 2.43–3.66 µM | 1.24 (0.93–1.66) | 1.14 (0.86–1.53) | 1.08 (0.79–1.48) |
| **3** | 3.67–6.18 µM | 1.53 (1.16–2.02) | 1.29 (0.98–1.71) | 1.15 (0.85–1.56) |
| **4** | Above 6.18 µM | 2.54 (1.96–3.28) | 1.88 (1.44–2.44) | 1.49 (1.10–2.03) |

Only the top quartile stayed significant after adjustment. It has no upper limit, so a few very high values could drive it. The models didn't adjust for socioeconomic status.
[Tang 2013 full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3701945/)

## Diet and fasting TMAO

Fasting plasma TMAO was lower in 26 vegans and vegetarians than in 51 omnivores (P < 0.05). Most omnivores were below the 6.18 µM top-quartile cutoff from the cohort above: the omnivores' 75th percentile was about 4 µM, though their 90th percentile was about 7 µM.
![[Plasma TMAO vegans vs omnivores.png]]

Omnivores also made more TMAO than vegans or vegetarians from L-carnitine, which is abundant in red meat, after eating an 8-ounce sirloin steak (about 180 mg of carnitine) or labeled carnitine. Broad-spectrum antibiotics almost stopped TMAO from forming, and it returned once the gut bacteria recovered. In 2,595 patients having cardiac evaluation, higher plasma carnitine predicted heart disease and heart attacks, strokes and deaths only in those who also had high TMAO. In mice, carnitine supplements raised TMAO and atherosclerosis, but not when their gut bacteria were suppressed.
[Koeth 2013](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23563705/)

Among older vegetarians, TMAO didn't depend on how strictly they avoided animal foods: 38 vegans and 67 lacto-ovo-vegetarians both averaged 3.7 µM. Among lacto-ovo-vegetarians, men had higher TMAO than women. In 66 younger vegans tested twice 3 months apart, TMAO barely changed on average (−0.3 µM, 95% CI −0.7 to 0.1) and was fairly stable within each person, so a change of more than 80% on retesting likely reflects more than normal variation.
[Obeid 2017](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27562778/)

## Fish protein in mice

Male apoE null mice, which have no apolipoprotein E and develop atherosclerosis easily, were fed a Western diet with 20% protein from turbot, casein or soy for 16 weeks (12 per group). The fish protein group had twice the plaque area of the other groups, more calcification in its plaques, and much higher serum TMAO:

| Protein | Calcified share of plaque | Serum TMAO |
|---|---|---|
| **Fish (turbot)** | 7.57% | 7.03 µM |
| **Casein** | 2.86% | 0.92 µM |
| **Soy** | 3.46% | 1.32 µM |

[Yazdekhasti 2016](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26502377/)

Humans almost never lack apoE entirely. The closest human condition, familial dysbetalipoproteinemia, comes mostly from having two copies of the apoE2 variant, which clears cholesterol-carrying remnants poorly. Only about 15% of people with that genotype develop it, and it affects about 0.12–0.40% of people.
[Koopal 2017](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28098593/)

## Limits of the genetic evidence

Few genetic variants strongly affect TMAO. One later study took its variants from a metabolomics study of 2,076 people, and had to use a relaxed significance threshold (P < 5 × 10⁻⁵). A null result from data like this only rules out effects larger than its confidence interval.
[Wang 2022](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35935641/)

Mendelian randomization also tests a small lifelong difference, not a large rise from a supplement. → [[Mendelian randomization and atherosclerosis]]

## Choline supplements and eggs

In 18 healthy adults taking about 450 mg of choline a day as choline bisulfate for 2 months, with no control group, fasting TMAO rose well above the top cohort quartile. Platelet aggregation rose by about 10–14 percentage points, and low-dose aspirin blunted both changes. Neither measure is a disease outcome.

| Group | Baseline TMAO | At 2 months |
|---|---|---|
| **Vegans and vegetarians (8)** | 2.6 µM | 28.9 µM |
| **Omnivores (10)** | 2.5 µM | 36.8 µM |

Two of the authors hold patents and may receive royalties related to cardiovascular diagnostics.
[Zhu 2017](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28438808/)

The form of choline seems to matter. Over 4 weeks in healthy young adults with normal kidney function, median TMAO changed as follows:

| Group | Total choline | TMAO before and after | Platelet reactivity |
|---|---|---|---|
| **4 eggs (18)** | 467 mg | 2.0 → 2.3 µM | No significant change |
| **Choline bitartrate (20)** | 411 mg | 1.9 → 11.1 µM | Increased |
| **Eggs and choline bitartrate (16)** | About 880 mg | 2.3 → 12.3 µM | Increased |
| **Egg whites and choline bitartrate (18)** | About 410 mg | 2.6 → 28.1 µM | Increased |
| **Phosphatidylcholine capsules (10)** | 410 mg | 2.8 → 3.4 µM | No significant change |

The groups were small, and the egg group wasn't compared with a no-egg control. Most of the choline in eggs is phosphatidylcholine, which may be less available to gut microbes than free choline. Hazen, the same senior author, holds related patents and may receive royalties.
[Wilcox 2021](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33872583/)
