Why fiber is good for you

It's widely accepted that a potential benefit of fiber is through it's digestion into short chain fatty acids by our gut bacteria. These SCFAs may have an independent effect on inflammatory markers in the body. One recent trial shows a potentially important anti-inflammatory effect by supplementing one of these principle SCFAs, butyrate. They found those that supplemented butyrate for 4 weeks had reduced monocyte activation in response to oxidized LDL after butyrate supplementation.
https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6349871/

A meta analysis from 2012 looked at the relationship between dietary fiber and a number of endpoints related to intestinal motility and defecation in humans and while it was underpowered, there was sufficient data to elucidate the relationship between fiber supplementation and constipation. Universally, higher fiber intakes trended increasing stool frequency compared to control.
https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC3544045/

This study has been cited to claim that reducing fiber intake improved symptoms and consuming no fiber eliminated symptoms of constipation. Firstly, this isn't very generalizable, they had idiopathic constipation, so it's not clear this will apply to the general population at all. They also didn't even assess fiber intake, it was merely assumed based off of what they instructed the participants to do which is naturally going to be a very poor measurement.
[Ho 2012](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22969234/)

We also see fiber consistently reduce bowel transit time and improve symptoms of constipation.
[de Vries 2016](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26950143/)
[van der Schoot 2022](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35816465/)


The cholesterol lowering effects of fiber are also well-known. Increasing fiber in diet reduces ApoB, in addition to LDL-C. 
https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32203549/

ApoB is the most powerful predictor for lipid mediated CVD risk.
https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30694319/

It's also suggested that the degree that fiber improves blood lipid parameters is a function of its viscosity. Fiber types that produce higher viscosity gels in the gut tend to improve blood lipids more than fiber types that produce lower viscosity gels in the gut. There is one group of fiber subtypes unique to oats that have been studied a ton called β-glucans. RCTs looking at the relationship between that and blood lipids consistently show favorable effects.
https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25411276/

All in all, these help explain the benefits of fiber for CVD risk reduction.

Fiber also seems to benefit glycemic regulation in both diabetic and non diabetic people. Both HbA1C and fasting blood glucose was significantly lowered with increasing fiber intakes. There was significant heterogeneity between the included studies investigating the effects of fiber on those outcomes but it was discovered by authors it was due to differences in population age and follow-up time.
https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24180564/

It's also plausible fiber may have an effect on weight loss independent of conscious calorie restriction. This is because high-viscosity soluble fiber binds not only to cholesterol, but binds to macronutrients too which can reduce calories absorbed.
https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/647304/

A meta-analysis of RCTs tried to quantify the effect of fiber on energy balance in humans. They altogether found that heterogeneity in their results that were largely explained by population subtype they were investigating. Both overweight and diabetic subjects lost an average of 0.45kg of bodyweight compared to control with increasing fiber intake. They also saw fiber accounted for an average loss of 0.85% of total bodyweight as body fat.
https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31897475/

All in all, fiber may reduce inflammation, increase intestinal motility and stool frequency, reducing constipation, improve blood lipids, improve glycemic control, and aid in weight loss.
