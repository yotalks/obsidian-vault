> [!summary] Bottom line
> Fish raises TMAO far more than eggs or meat, and Mendelian randomization finds no causal link between TMAO and heart disease.

Researchers fed participants dozens of different foods and measured how much TMAO showed up in their urine. Seafood was by far the biggest source. Choline and carnitine rich foods like eggs and meat produced comparatively small amounts. Fish is widely considered a heart healthy food, yet raises TMAO the most which makes it harder to argue TMAO itself is dangerous.
[Zhang 1999](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0278691599000289?via%3Dihub#TBL2)

The strongest research we have on the relationship between TMAO and heart disease risk with the Mendelian randomization data, there is no causal link between TMAO in the blood and heart disease risk.
[Jia 2019](https://diabetes.diabetesjournals.org/content/68/9/1747)

> [!question] Objections to prepare for
> - Fish's other benefits (like omega-3s) could offset harm from TMAO, so fish doesn't clear TMAO.
> - Mendelian randomization depends on assumptions (no pleiotropy, strong instruments) that can fail.
