> [!summary] Bottom line
> Lab-grown meat is real animal meat grown from animal cells in bioreactors instead of from a slaughtered animal. It's probably the most effective way to end factory farming, because it can make animal agriculture obsolete rather than relying on persuading billions of people to give up meat. Made without fetal bovine serum, it requires no animal agriculture and is compatible with veganism. It's already been approved for sale in Singapore and the US, but it's still expensive and small-scale, and some places have banned it. The common objections, like cancer, contamination and environmental impact, don't favor farmed meat, which also drives pandemic risk and antibiotic resistance.

## What it is

Lab-grown (cultured or cultivated) meat is meat tissue grown from animal cells in a bioreactor, so it's actual meat rather than a plant-based imitation.

Early methods grew the cells in fetal bovine serum, which is taken from calf fetuses at slaughter. Serum-free media now exist. Researchers at the cultured meat company Mosa Meat developed a chemically defined medium that let cow muscle stem cells mature into muscle fibres without serum. It was as effective as the standard serum-based method and worked for building 3D muscle tissue.
[Messmer 2022](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37118488/)

## Why it matters for animals

Most meat eaters won't give up meat for plant foods, and the most effective way to end animal agriculture is to make it obsolete. Lab meat that matches meat's taste and texture without slaughter does that, which is why supporting it is likely more impactful than most other animal advocacy. Ethical questions about an industry tend to get clearer once what it provides is no longer needed.

Lab meat made without fetal bovine serum is compatible with veganism. It creates no demand for animal agriculture beyond what buying plants does, so buying it can even help it take hold in the market. For the same reason, vegans who oppose lab meat may be working against the end of factory farming.

Factory farming also breeds disease. Animals are packed together in large numbers, pathogens emerge, and whole flocks are then killed. Meat grown in controlled bioreactors avoids this, so safety concerns about lab meat don't favor farmed meat.

## Public health

**Pandemics:** More than 6 in 10 known infectious diseases in people can spread from animals, and 3 in 4 new or emerging ones come from animals.
[CDC: Zoonotic diseases](https://www.cdc.gov/one-health/about/about-zoonotic-diseases.html)

**Antibiotic resistance:** Animals consume about three times as many antimicrobials as humans, which has enabled large-scale animal protein production. Between 2000 and 2018 in low- and middle-income countries, the share of antimicrobials with resistance above 50% rose from 0.15 to 0.41 in chickens and from 0.13 to 0.34 in pigs.
[Van Boeckel 2019](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31604207/)

In 2019, bacterial antimicrobial resistance was associated with an estimated 4.95 million deaths and directly caused 1.27 million.
[Antimicrobial Resistance Collaborators 2022](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35065702/)

Meat grown without an animal doesn't need antibiotics to keep animals alive and growing in crowded conditions.
[Saied 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10389608/)

## Objections

### Safety

**Cancer:** Lab meat is made from real animal cells. Some cell lines are immortalized so they keep dividing, but while all cancers are immortalized, not all immortalized cells are cancer. No study has shown lab meat causes cancer.
[Full Fact 2024](https://fullfact.org/health/lab-grown-meat-turbo-cancer/)

**Contamination:** Growing meat in a closed bioreactor avoids the gut pathogens that come with slaughter and processing. Contamination still happens during production: in an industry survey of 11 companies, an average of 11.2% of batches failed from microbial contamination, mostly traced to staff, equipment and the production environment. Rates were higher in larger facilities, and the authors expect them to fall as the industry matures.
[Powell 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11681928/)

### Disgust

Conventional meat has its own problems on this front. Consumer Reports found bacteria indicating fecal contamination (enterococcus or non-toxin-producing E. coli) in all 458 pounds of ground beef it tested.
[Mother Jones 2015](https://www.motherjones.com/food/2015/08/poop-ground-beef-superbugs-antibiotic-resistant/)

### Environmental impact

The study behind headlines saying lab meat is worse for the climate than beef estimated 246–1,508 kg CO2e per kg when growth media are purified to pharmaceutical grade, 4 to 25 times the median for retail beef (about 60 kg). That came from multiplying the footprint of the growth media by 20, a factor taken from a 2010 study of fine chemical production. Without purification, its lowest estimate was 19.2 kg. It's a preprint, and the industry doesn't plan to use pharmaceutical-grade inputs.
[Risner 2023](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2023.04.21.537778v1.full)
[GFI letter on the study](https://gfi.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/Letter-to-UC-Davis-CM-LCAs.pdf)

A life cycle assessment using data from more than 15 companies modeled commercial production in 2030:
- **Conventional energy:** lower carbon footprint and lower overall environmental impact than beef, but higher overall impact than chicken, pork and plant-based meat.
- **Sustainable energy:** lower overall environmental impact than any conventional meat. It matches global average chicken and pork on carbon footprint once more than 30% of its energy is sustainable.
- **Plant protein:** still 2.5 to 4 times lower in carbon footprint than lab meat.

[CE Delft 2021](https://gfieurope.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/04/CE_Delft_190107_LCA_of_cultivated_meat_Def.pdf)

### Cost

Lab meat is still more expensive than conventional meat, but costs have fallen, and growing meat without raising and feeding a whole animal could make it cheaper in the long run. When it will reach price parity is uncertain.
[GFI: Reducing the price of alternative proteins](https://gfi.org/reducing-the-price-of-alternative-proteins/)

## Feasibility and regulation

Lab meat is a technology about 20–30 years old competing with one about 10,000 years old, so its current cost and small scale aren't surprising. Early cars faced many of the same feasibility objections.

It's already been sold:
- **Singapore** approved the first commercial sale in December 2020, cultured chicken from Eat Just.
- **The US** cleared cultivated chicken from Upside Foods and Good Meat in June 2023, and cultivated salmon from Wildtype in May 2025.
- **Bans:** Italy's government moved to ban it in 2023, and Florida and Alabama have banned its manufacture and sale.
