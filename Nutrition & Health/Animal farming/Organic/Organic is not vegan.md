The estimated total nitrogen requirement for an acre of corn crop is 300lbs of Nitrogen.

Without using any manure or nitrogen fertilizer, this requirement is reduced to 117lbs nitrogen, because the soil contains some nitrogen at baseline, both organic N and inorganic N, legume crops from crop rotations, and nitrates from irrigation sources.

## Why organic relies on animal inputs

USDA organic rules prohibit most synthetic substances and sewage sludge, so organic farms can't use the main nitrogen sources conventional farms can. That leaves animal inputs like manure, blood meal and bone meal, or veganic methods, and veganic farming is a small part of the sector. The manure doesn't have to come from organic animals either. → [[Organic and Health]]
[7 CFR 205.105](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/7/205.105)
[7 CFR 205.203](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/7/205.203)

Conventional farms use manure too, but they can use synthetic fertilizer instead. Less than a fifth of the manure nitrogen produced worldwide gets applied to cropland.
[Zhang 2017](https://essd.copernicus.org/articles/9/667/2017/)

## Byproducts are still commodities

Manure being a waste product doesn't change this. Anything sold for a profit is a commodity, the same way meat is a byproduct for a dairy farm. Even giving manure away for free helps the industry, because it would otherwise have to pay to dispose of it.

Buying organic also isn't the same as trace animal products in a laptop, because here there's an easy alternative. The comparison is a laptop with a 20% chance of funding the torture of trait-equalized humans and one with a 95% chance. Nobody would pick the second over a small difference in quality, and it not being your intention wouldn't change that.

So the benefits of organic would have to be so great that you'd accept them if the animals were trait-equalized humans. → [[Animal Rights]]

## Buying from companies that also sell animal products

The issue is what a purchase creates demand for, not who gets the money. Buying a bottle of water at McDonald's isn't wrong, because McDonald's will respond by selling more water, not by selling more hamburgers. The same goes for any company that sells plant products and also owns animal agriculture. Companies follow the demand for the product you actually bought.
