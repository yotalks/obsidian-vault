> [!summary] Bottom line
> GM crops have raised yields and lowered pesticide use on average, and long-term animal feeding studies haven't found health hazards. Ethically, the case against them doesn't compare with organic produce grown with manure, which sends money to animal agriculture every time you buy it. If you have the option, non-GMO conventional avoids both issues.

## Yields and pesticides

A meta-analysis of 147 studies of GM soybean, maize and cotton found that GM crops reduced chemical pesticide use by 37%, raised yields by 22% and raised farmer profits by 68%. Yield gains and pesticide reductions were larger for insect-resistant crops than herbicide-tolerant ones, and several of the original studies didn't report sample sizes or variance. The authors reported no competing interests.
[Klümper 2014](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25365303/)

## Health

A review of 12 long-term and 12 multigenerational animal feeding studies, up to 2 years and 5 generations, found no sign of health hazards. The small differences that appeared were within normal variation.
[Snell 2012](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22155268/)

## Terminator seeds

Seeds engineered not to reproduce, known as genetic use restriction technology, have never been sold commercially anywhere.
[Wikipedia: Genetic use restriction technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genetic_use_restriction_technology)

## Ethics compared with organic

Animal testing of GM crops is a real cost, but it isn't comparable in scale with an industry that confines and slaughters animals continuously. So even choosing only between GM and manure-grown organic, the harm from GM would have to be enormous and quantified to outweigh paying into that system.
→ [[Organic is not vegan]]

Most conventional produce isn't GM anyway, so this choice rarely comes up.

A company being badly behaved is also a separate issue from which product does more harm. Buying from a company doesn't fund everything it does, as long as the product itself doesn't create demand for the bad part.
