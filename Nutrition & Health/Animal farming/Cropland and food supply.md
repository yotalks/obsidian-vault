> [!summary] Bottom line
> Feeding crops to animals loses most of their calories. Using all cropland calories for direct human food could raise the food supply by up to about 70%, enough for about 4 billion more people, and plant replacements for US animal foods could feed 350 million more. These are upper bounds that leave out grazing land and assume people change what they eat. They show the land is there, not that hunger would end.

## Calories per hectare

Crops differ a lot in how much food they produce per hectare per day. These are averages from developing countries in 1981–83, so they show the rough ranking and not today's yields.

| Crop | Energy (1,000 kcal/ha/day) | Protein (kg/ha/day) |
|---|---|---|
| **Sweet potato** | 70 | 1.0 |
| **Potato** | 54 | 1.5 |
| **Rice** | 49 | 0.9 |
| **Yam** | 47 | 1.0 |
| **Wheat** | 40 | 1.6 |
| **Groundnut** | 36 | 1.7 |
| **Cassava** | 27 | 0.1 |
| **Lentil** | 23 | 1.6 |

[FAO: Roots, tubers, plantains and bananas in human nutrition](https://www.fao.org/4/t0207e/T0207E04.htm)

These figures are per day of growth, so they favor fast crops and ignore how many crops a field can grow in a year. Calories aren't everything either: lentils are last for energy and near the top for protein.

## Crops for feed

Of the calories produced by 41 major crops around the year 2000, 55% went directly to human food, 36% to animal feed and 9% to industrial uses like biofuels. Only about 12% of feed calories came back as meat, dairy and eggs.

Growing food only for direct human consumption could raise available calories by up to about 70%, which could feed about 4 billion more people, compared with the 2–3 billion expected from population growth. Even small shifts away from feed and biofuels could add a lot.
[Cassidy 2013](https://iopscience.iop.org/article/10.1088/1748-9326/8/3/034015/meta)

This is an upper bound. It only covers cropland, leaves out grazing, crop residues and food waste, and treats plant and animal protein as equal.

## Plant replacements

Another way to frame this is as a food loss: the food you give up by eating a land-intensive item instead of a nutritionally similar plant one. Plant replacements chosen to use the least cropland give twofold (eggs) to 20-fold (beef) more nutritionally similar food per unit of cropland.

| US food | Opportunity food loss |
|---|---|
| **Beef** | 96% |
| **Pork** | 90% |
| **Dairy** | 75% |
| **Poultry** | 50% |
| **Eggs** | 40% |

Retail-to-consumer losses are about 30% by comparison. Replacing all animal foods in the US diet could feed 350 million more people, which is more than eliminating all supply chain food waste.
[Shepon 2018](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29581251/)

These replacements are chosen to minimize cropland, so they aren't what people would actually switch to, and pasture isn't counted. That's why this has to be read with the problems of moving cattle onto grass. → [[Grass fed beef]]

## Limits

A model of removing animals from US agriculture found about 23% more food, mostly grain, and more of most nutrients, but more nutrients fell short of requirements. Critics disputed its assumptions in PNAS, so it doesn't settle what a planned plant-based food system could do.
[White 2017](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1707322114)

More calories also wouldn't automatically reach hungry people. These numbers show what the land could produce, and getting food to people is a separate problem.
