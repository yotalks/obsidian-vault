> [!summary] Bottom line
> Healthiness is comparative and depends on context. Every food provides nutrients or energy, but whether it's healthier depends on what it replaces: one food is healthier than another if swapping it in delays illness or disease. So the same food can be healthier in one setting and less healthy in another.

## Definition

A food is considered healthier than another in a specific context if it increases the time before illness or disease sets in when replacing the other food. On a desert island, since Pepsi can extend the time before starvation compared to leafy greens, it is considered healthier than leafy greens in that context.

Like any definition, this is a convention for how to use the word rather than a claim that can be true or false → [[Definitions]].

## Is all food health-promoting?

Given that food is, by its very definition, material that provides essential nutrients and energy to sustain bodily functions and growth, it follows that anything classified as food must be inherently health-promoting by its very nature.
Food is commonly defined as any substance consumed by an organism for nutritional support, usually containing essential nutrients like carbohydrates, fats, proteins, vitamins or minerals.
