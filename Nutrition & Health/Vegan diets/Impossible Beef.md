> [!summary] Bottom line
> Criticisms of the Impossible Burger mostly appeal to nature or whole-food purity. It's mainly soy protein and oils, and compared with beef it has a better polyunsaturated-to-saturated fat ratio, fiber, no cholesterol, and more vitamins and minerals per calorie. It's likely better for health than beef, though its saturated fat from coconut oil keeps it from being ideal.

## Ingredients

Criticisms of Impossible beef mostly appeal to nature, whole-food purism and emotion. Looking at the ingredients, it's mainly soy protein and oils.
![[Impossible Burger ingredients.png]]

In a meta-analysis of cohort studies, more plant protein was linked to lower all-cause and cardiovascular mortality, with each extra 3% of calories from plant protein linked to 5% lower all-cause mortality. Animal protein wasn't significantly linked to cardiovascular or cancer mortality, and the authors suggest replacing animal protein with plant protein could be associated with longevity.
[Naghshi 2020](https://www.bmj.com/content/370/bmj.m2412)

## Blood lipids

The Impossible Burger is high in saturated fat from coconut oil, which isn't ideal for LDL. Whether coconut oil is worse than beef fat is unclear: in one trial beef fat produced significantly lower LDL than coconut oil, while in a network meta-analysis both lowered LDL similarly compared with butter.
[Reiser 1985](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/4025191/)
[Schwingshackl 2018](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30006369/)
→ [[LDL and heart disease]]

But the Impossible Burger also has sunflower oil, which improves the polyunsaturated-to-saturated fat (P:S) ratio, and a higher P:S ratio improves blood lipids.
[Keys 1965](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25286466/)

With its better P:S ratio, fiber and lack of dietary cholesterol, the Impossible Burger is likely better than beef for LDL. Soy also lowers lipids compared with red meat: in postmenopausal women, replacing red meat with soy nuts lowered LDL by about 10% compared with the control diet.
[Azadbakht 2007](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17344494/)

## Nutrients

Comparing a 240-calorie serving of Impossible Burger with fatty beef brisket, the Impossible Burger has more protein and more of most vitamins and minerals.
![[Impossible Burger vs brisket nutrition.png]]

It also contains leghemoglobin, a plant analogue of the hemoglobin in meat, which carries heme and gives it a meaty flavor. Iron bioavailability is likely similar to beef's. The only data on leghemoglobin's allergy risk are from sequence comparisons and digestion tests, which suggest it poses very little risk to humans.
[Jin 2018](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28921896/)
