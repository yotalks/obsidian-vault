> [!summary] Bottom line
> The glute max has two regions. The upper region has good leverage for hip abduction when the hip is flexed, so the seated hip abduction machine trains it best, possibly leaning forward. The lower region has good leverage for hip extension when the hip is extended, so glute bridges and hip thrusts train it best, with the bridge likely better since it involves the quads less.

## Regions and functions

The glute max has distinct upper and lower regions.
[Stern 1972](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/4624653/)

It contributes to hip extension, abduction and external rotation, since hip muscles act in more than one plane at once.
[Song 2020](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7737424/)

External rotation is hard to train long term, though. There are no gym machines for it and it doesn't suit free weights, and the most common exercise, the band-loaded clamshell, isn't ideal for progressive overload.

## Upper region

The upper region has good leverage for hip abduction when the hip is flexed. In isometric hip abduction, upper glute max activity rose as hip flexion increased, reaching about 86% of maximum at 80° of flexion, the highest angle tested.
[Fujisawa 2014](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC3944285/)
→ [[Neuromechanical matching]]

So the seated hip abduction machine trains it well. The data don't go beyond 80° of hip flexion, but extrapolating suggests that leaning forward on the machine may increase the upper glute max's contribution further.

## Lower region

The lower region has good leverage for hip extension when the hip is extended. The glute max's hip extension moment arm is longest with the hip straight (about 79 mm) and shrinks as the hip flexes.
[Németh 1985](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/3988782/)

So glute bridges and hip thrusts, which are hardest with the hip extended, train it well. The barbell hip thrust activated both the upper and lower glute max far more than the back squat, with similar quad activity.
[Contreras 2015](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26214739/)

Glute bridges are likely better than hip thrusts, because they involve less knee extension torque and so less quad involvement, leaving more neural drive for the glute max. This hasn't been tested directly.
