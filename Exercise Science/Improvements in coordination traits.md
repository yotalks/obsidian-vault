> [!summary] Bottom line
> Coordination improves fast but is highly specific to the movement, velocity and contraction mode practiced, unlike gains in motor unit recruitment, which transfer broadly.

Improvements in coordination are an integral adaptation that contributes to strength and speed gains. Improvements in coordination show unique features that differ from many other adaptations, including other neural adaptations like increasing ability to recruit motor units.

Increases in coordination are very fast. They can happen after just one or two practice sessions. Compared to other strength gain mechanisms that are much slower.
[Green 2014](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24552367/)

The strength gains from improvements in coordination are very specific to the movement pattern that is used for the practice session. But there can be transfers to other movements as long as the trained and untrained movements are super similar.
[McGuire 2014](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4255822/)

Comparatively, there are no improvements in strength as a result of coordination increases when testing strength in a very different movement, like looking at difference of leg extension after a short time of training squats.
[[Ansdell 2020]]

In this respect, increases in coordination are completely different from increases in the ability to recruit motor units. Increases in voluntary activation are not even close to specific to the movement pattern or even joint used when training.
[[Ema 2018]]

Strength gains that come from improvements in coordination are specific to the velocity that is used when practicing. Strength gains are greater after fast movement practice when they're tested at fast speeds (rather than slow speeds) ,while strength gains are greater after slow movement practice when they're tested at slow speeds (compared to fast speeds).
[[Coburn 2006]]

Again increases in coordination are completely different than improvements in motor unit recruitment. Increases in voluntary activation   is no way near specific to movement speed that is used when practicing (although increases in motor unit firing rate is much more specific).
[[Behrens 2016]]

Improvements in coordination are also contraction mode specific used when practicing. The coordination of eccentric contractions improves much more after eccentric-only training compared to normal strength training.
[Mueller 2009](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19543908/)

Again completely different to increases in ability to recruit motor units, eccentric overload training increases voluntary activation during isometric contractions, even though no isometric contractions were done when training.
[Walker 2016](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27199764/)

Improvements in coordination seem to happen a lot slower after fast movement training involving low forces than slow movement  training with high forces. Why is unclear, but it tells us that greater time may be required practicing fast movements to get a certain level of coordination.
[Oliveira 2010](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20706733/)

There are also signs that improvements in coordination happen more slowly after eccentric training than concentric or normal strength training. This is most likely because eccentric contractions involve greater force and activation variability at baseline before training, and they seem to be more difficult for the [[Glossary#CNS|CNS]] to control.
[Purtsi 2012](https://journals.humankinetics.com/view/journals/japa/20/3/article-p345.pdf)

Data shows that eccentric contractions create higher level of effort for same amount of force output, telling us that they involve a cognitive load associated with the difficulty of coordinating the movement pattern itself.
[[Kan 2019]]
