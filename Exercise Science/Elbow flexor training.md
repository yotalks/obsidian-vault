> [!summary] Bottom line
> The biceps and brachialis have their best leverage with the elbow straighter, and a separate supination exercise may train the biceps' supinator role better than twisting during curls. The brachioradialis has its best leverage with the elbow more bent and the forearm pronated, so pronated-grip or EZ-bar curls against bands train it best.

## Leverage changes with elbow and forearm position

The elbow flexors are four muscles: the long and short heads of the biceps, the brachialis and the brachioradialis. Their moment arms vary by at least 30% across the elbow's range of motion. The biceps' flexion moment arm peaks with the elbow straighter and is larger with the forearm supinated, while its supination moment arm shrinks as the elbow straightens.
[Murray 1995](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7775488/)
→ [[Neuromechanical matching]]

## Biceps and brachialis

The biceps (both heads) and brachialis have better leverage than the brachioradialis for elbow flexion when the elbow is extended.

The biceps is also a forearm supinator, with some motor units dedicated to that job. Both heads were activated most when flexing and supinating at the same time, more than either task alone.
[Pérot 1996](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20719658/)

So starting each curl with the palm facing in (neutral) and turning it up (supinating) as you lift may involve the biceps more. A separate forearm supination exercise would work better, however.

## Brachioradialis

The brachioradialis has better leverage than the biceps and brachialis for elbow flexion when the elbow is more bent and the forearm is pronated (palms down). So pronated-grip or EZ-bar curls against elastic bands train it well.
