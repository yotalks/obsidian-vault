> [!summary] Bottom line
> ATP stays stable during aerobic, anaerobic and strength exercise. Fatigue mechanisms slow ATP use before it can run out, so performance drops come from fatigue (largely in the CNS), not from running out of energy.

## ATP levels do not change with exercise. 

We see ATP levels do not change with aerobic exercise. The reduction in exercise performance we have over time is largely by means of [[Glossary#CNS|CNS]] fatigue.
[Williams 2013](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1155/2013/371235)

We see ATP levels also do not change with anaerobic exercise, despite it depleting muscle glycogen well.
[Ball-Burnett 1991](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1180046/)

We see strength training exercise does not change ATP levels either when they did repeated isometric contractions with and without [[Glossary#BFR|BFR]].
[Lanza 2006](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16945975/)

Muscle fibers need a certain amount of energy supply in ATP to function. We see with ischemia data comparing different durations of being in a hypoxic state, substantial loss of ATP leads to the muscle fibers dying.
[Lindsay 1990](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/074152149090360M)

ATP levels not changing during exercise is able to happen because fatigue mechanisms stop or slow down the biological processes that use ATP.
