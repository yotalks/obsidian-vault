> [!summary] Bottom line
> Untrained people can't recruit all their high-threshold motor units, especially in large muscles. Recruitment improves once a movement is well coordinated and you can reach very high recruitment, which heavy or explosive efforts do better than light loads to failure.

In order to cause hypertrophy, we need to have high levels of motor unit recruitment. So it is important to consider what the levels of motor unit recruitment are in untrained lifters and how we can increase the number of high threshold motor units that can be recruited.

## Motor unit recruitment is lower in untrained people

Untrained individuals usually have a pretty limited capacity to recruit motor units. So many of their high-threshold motor units are not recruited even when they exert a maximal effort. Even so, this does vary between muscles. Larger muscles tend to display lower levels o motor unit recruitment than the smaller muscles (measured by interpolated twitch method for voluntary activation).
[Behm 2002](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11870692/)
[HAL preprint (voluntary activation)](https://hal.science/hal-04081468v1/document)
[Gilfeather 2020](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31775118/)
[Cheng 2010](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19768762/)
[Bech 2017](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28735103/)

Most well-trained individuals tend to have higher levels of motor unit recruitment, though it is still pretty unlikely that they can recruit every single muscle fiber in the larger muscles. This shows us it is possible to increase number of motor units we can recruit at maximal efforts.
[Balshaw 2019](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30387185/)

## Increases in motor unit recruitment happen after strength training

Strength training programs can increase our ability to recruit motor units by increaseing the size of the motor unit action potentials.
[[Sterczala 2020]]

After starting a strength training program, we don't immediately increase our ability to recruit motor units. Strength training programs that only last 4 weeks don't lead to any transferable strength gains from the squat to the leg extension, which tells us that there were no increases in motor unit recruitment. Rather, the neural adaptations must have been increases in coordination. It seems likely that we need to get coordination increases before we can increase motor unit recruitment.
[[Ansdell 2020]]
→ [[Improvements in coordination traits]]

When exercises are perceived to be more complex, they involve a higher level of perceived effort than when they are less complex, even when motor unit recruitment is similar. This means we cannot hit our current maximal levels of motor unit recruitment until we have achieved a relatively high degree of coordination in the exercise we are doing. This tells us that the stimulus for increasing motor unit recruitment is getting a very high level of motor unit recruitment. This obviously means we can't get it till we have obtained a sufficiently high degree of coordination on the movement.
[[Kan 2019]]

Increases in motor unit recruitment do not seem to be velocity0--specific. Vertical jumping training without external load increases voluntary activation during isometric contractions. This means that we can get an increase in motor unit recruitment at any movement velocity by training at any other movement velocity. We can increase motor unit recruitment using a heavy load by training with a light load and vice versa.
[[Behrens 2016]]

Though increases in motor unit recruitment can be achieved by plyometrics (which involve moving a light load quickly in an unfatigued state), it is a lot more difficult to get an increase in motor unit recruitment by performing strength training to failure with a light load.
[Jenkins 2017](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28611677/)

Light load strength training to failure does not allow us to reach as high levels of motor unit recruitment as heavy strength training because of [[Supraspinal CNS fatigue|supraspinal CNS fatigue]] mechanisms caused by the afferent feedback from the muscles and the cardiovascular system. This observation also tells us that the stimulus to increase motor unit recruitments getting a very high level of motor unit recruitment. This obviously can done at any velocity, simply moving as fast as possible, despite this, it is a lot harder to get during a very fatiguing contraction.
[Muddle 2018](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/323525420_Effects_of_fatiguing_submaximal_high-_versus_low-torque_isometric_exercise_on_motor_unit_recruitment_and_firing_behavior)

Increases in motor unit recruitment can be achieved in two-joint muscles (like the rectus femoris, semitendinosus, and the triceps long head) as well as single-joint muscles. The rectus femoris is a hip flexor as well as a knee extensor. When voluntary activation of the rectus femoris is increased by hip flexion strength training, this increases the voluntary activation recorded during knee extension efforts. So, the ability to recruit motor units is not a joint specific phenomenon in two-joint muscles.
[[Ema 2018]]

The ability to recruit motor units is known to differ between eccentric contractions and the other contraction modes (isometric and concentric). Voluntary activation levels are very often lower when measured in eccentric contractions than when are measured in isometric or concentric contractions,.
[Amiridis 1996](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8861684/)

There are signs that eccentric training causes greater increases in the ability to recruit moto units during eccentric contractions. and that concentric training casuistries increases in the ability to recruit motor units during concent5ric contractions. It seems to be that increases in the ability to recruit motor units are to a n extent contraction mode-specific.
[Tallent 2017](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28223941/)

This may be explained by referencing the presence of contraction mode-specific supraspinal [[Glossary#CNS|CNS]] fatigue from mental effort perceptions.

The ability to recruit motor units also differs to an extent between contractions performed at short muscle lengths (or through partial ROMs) and contractions performed at long muscle lengths. Increase in voluntary activation may be joint angle-specific, such that contractions performed at short muscle lengths preferentially increase the ability to recruit high threshold motor units at short muscle length, while contracts at long muscle lengths preferentially increase the ability to recruit motor units at long muscle lengths
[Noorkõiv 2014](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24504427/)

We can likely increase our ability to recruit motor units by maximizing the level of motor unit recruitment during maximal efforts that we use when training.
