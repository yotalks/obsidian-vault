> [!summary] Bottom line
> Muscle swelling (the pump) doesn't stimulate hypertrophy; if anything it reduces mechanical tension through calcium ion-related fatigue.

Because of bodybuilding anecdote, some researchers have proposed that muscle swelling can stimulate hypertrophy by by increasing [[Mechanical tension|mechanical tension]] through greater deformations because of the increased intracellular fluid.
[Schoenfeld 2013](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/285754036_The_Muscle_Pump_Potential_Mechanisms_and_Applications_for_Enhancing_Hypertrophic_Adaptations)

Research in [[Glossary#BFR|BFR]] training has shown that increased swelling through vasodilatory agents to increase blood flow to the trained muscle does not increase anabolic signaling pahtways. 
[Gundermann 2012](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22362401/)

Muscle swelling has shown to actually reduce mechanical tension. This appears to be because of [[Calcium ion-related fatigue|calcium ion related fatigue]] mechanisms (excitation contraction coupling failure and losses in myofibrillar calcium ion sensitivity have been implicated although exact mechanisms these are accelerated are still unclear)
[Watanabe 2019](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31175519/)
