> [!summary] Bottom line
> A wider muscle bulges further from the bone, which lengthens its internal moment arm and lets the same force produce more joint torque. Hypertrophy does this. So can anything else that makes a muscle bigger: swelling during a workout and in the days after, creatine, and glycogen from high carbohydrate intake. In a model, doubling the biceps' cross-sectional area lengthened its moment arm by 27%. Swelling, creatine and glycogen do enlarge muscles.

## Why width matters

Joint torque is muscle force multiplied by the [[Internal moment arm|internal moment arm]], so a longer moment arm means more torque, and more strength in an exercise, without any more force. Through [[Neuromechanical matching|neuromechanical matching]], a muscle's leverage relative to the other muscles at the same joint also decides how much neural drive it gets.
[Hudson 2019](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30985474/)

A muscle that gets wider pushes its line of pull further from the joint. In a model that treated the biceps and brachialis as cylinders, doubling their cross-sectional area lengthened the biceps' moment arm by 27% (2.17 to 2.76 cm) and the brachialis' by 37%. Halving it shortened them by about 25% and 28%. The authors think this is a small but real contributor to strength in slow, heavy lifts like powerlifting, but a longer moment arm may be a disadvantage in fast movements, because each bit of muscle shortening turns the joint less. The model assumed uniform growth and one forearm position, and hasn't been tested in living people.
[Vigotsky 2015](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4671169/)

## Size changes without growth

### Swelling during exercise

In 7 people doing progressive wrist flexion exercise, water inside the forearm muscle cells rose 12.6% and total water 10.1%, while water outside the cells didn't change. The rise tracked the fall in intracellular pH (r = −0.84), and changes in pH, phosphate and phosphocreatine together predicted it. That fits acidosis and phosphocreatine breakdown drawing water into the fibers by osmosis.
[Raja 2006](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16794019/)

This is the pump, which also lowers [[Mechanical tension|mechanical tension]] through calcium-related fatigue ([[Muscle swelling]]). So any leverage gain during a workout comes with fatigue that lowers force.

### Swelling after exercise

In rats, 4 hours of cut-off blood flow followed by reperfusion caused muscle damage and swelling, driven by free radicals and neutrophils.
[Hirose 2001](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11580130/)

After eccentric curls, the brachialis swelled over the following days ([[Elbow flexor training]]), and creatine reduced arm swelling in the week after eccentric curls ([[Creatine Supplementation]]). This swelling comes with muscle damage that lowers strength, so it's not expected to show up as a strength gain either.

### Creatine

In a randomized, double-blind crossover trial, 12 young men took creatine (20 g a day for 3 days, then 5 g a day for 7 days) or placebo. Creatine raised fat-free mass, total body water and body weight, and switched on genes involved in sensing cell volume and osmotic stress, which suggests the fibers swelled.
[Safdar 2008](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17957000/)

Unlike swelling from exercise, creatine swelling comes without fatigue or damage and lasts as long as you keep taking it. So it's the one kind of swelling whose leverage gain could show up as more strength. Moment arm wasn't measured, and creatine doesn't clearly raise maximum strength ([[Creatine Supplementation]]).

### Glycogen

In 5 people who ate very little carbohydrate for 4 days and then a lot for 4 days, muscle glycogen more than doubled (281 to 634 mmol/kg dry weight). Vastus cross-sectional area rose 3.5% (78 to 80 cm²) and thigh circumference 2.5%. MRI signals suggested the extra water was bound inside the fibers.
[Nygren 2001](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11903130/)

The source text cited a different study, which validated an ultrasound method (MuscleSound) for estimating glycogen. In 22 cyclists, its scores correlated closely with biopsy glycogen (r = 0.93 before and 0.94 after exercise).
[Hill 2014](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25295766/)

Later independent studies found MuscleSound scores often didn't match biopsy glycogen or change with diet and exercise as expected. A review called its validity "at best equivocal".
[Bone 2021](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34371881/)

### Intramuscular fat

The only source given is a measurement study. In 31 people, ultrasound echo intensity correlated closely with intramuscular fat measured by MRI (r = 0.76–0.91 across 4 leg muscles).
[Young 2015](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4575231/)

### Surplus and deficit

The idea is that glycogen and intramuscular fat rise when eating above maintenance and fall when eating below, so moment arms rise and fall with them, adding to the strength gains and losses of those phases. Nygren supports the glycogen part, but for carbohydrate intake rather than calories.
