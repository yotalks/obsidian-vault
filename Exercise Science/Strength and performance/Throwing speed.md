> [!summary] Bottom line
> Throwing speed depends on the impulse you can put into the object, which depends on both maximum strength and maximum speed. How much each matters changes down the proximal-to-distal chain: strength matters most for the hips and knees, both matter equally for the torso rotators, and speed matters most for the shoulder and elbow. So train the legs heavy, the torso both heavy and fast, and the arm by throwing.

## Strength and speed down the chain

Throwing speed is determined by the impulse we can produce, the product of force and time. The time available is set by the throw's range of motion. The force we can exert depends partly on our maximum strength, and partly on how much our force drops as movement speed rises, which depends on our maximum speed. So both maximum strength and maximum speed matter.

Throwing follows a proximal-to-distal sequence, from the legs through the torso to the arm, and the importance of each quality changes along it:
- **Hip and knee extensors:** maximum strength matters by far the most.
- **Torso rotators:** maximum strength and maximum speed matter about equally.
- **Shoulder and elbow:** maximum speed matters most.
→ [[Proximal-to-distal sequence]]

This fits how energy flows in a throw. In handball throws, 73% of the work on the ball was done in the last 50 ms, and high maximal segment speeds were key to getting energy into the ball.
[Jöris 1985](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/4030797/)
In baseball pitching, the trunk muscles were the main energy source, while the shoulder and elbow mostly transferred energy down the chain.
[Naito 2021](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34495332/)
[Naito 2011](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/19346182.2012.686502)
The trunk and shoulder motions were driven mainly by their own muscles, while elbow extension and wrist flexion were driven mainly by forces from the faster-moving segments above them.
[Hirashima 2008](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18678375/)
Cricket fast bowling shows a similar segment-by-segment sequence.
[Ferdinands 2012](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/19346182.2012.737800)

## Training for throwing speed

- **Hip and knee extensors:** since these depend mostly on strength, focus on heavy strength training: hip thrusts for the glute max, stiff-legged deadlifts for the adductor magnus, and back squats for the knee extensors (see [[Gluteus maximus training]]).
- **Torso rotators:** these need both heavy strength training and maximum speed work. Sideways medicine ball slams train their speed well. Heavy strength exercises are harder to find: an isolated torso rotation machine is ideal if your gym has one, otherwise cable rotations with as much external support as possible.
- **Shoulder and elbow:** it's doubtful these can be moved fast enough outside a throwing motion to raise their maximum speed, so they're likely best trained by throwing objects of different weights.

For the same proximal-to-distal approach applied to running, see [[Sprint speed]].
