f> [!summary] Bottom line
> Squat setup shifts which muscles do the work: a low bar, knees behind the toes and a wider stance emphasize the hip extensors (including the glutes), while a high bar, knees over the toes and a narrower stance emphasize the quads. Being more stable lets you recruit more of the target muscle.

## Stability

Increasing stability allows us to maximize motor unit recruitment to the target muscle and remove unintended muscles from being used.
[Ostrowski 2017](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27564994/)
→ [[Increasing motor unit recruitment]]

Stabilizing your body allows for more quad motor unit recruitment.
[Bampouras 2017](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28395183/)

## Bar position

Increasing hip extension moment arm by using a low bar position on squatting increases the relative involvement of the hip extensors and increasing the length of knee extension moment arm, increases the relative involvement of the knee extensors by using a high bar position.
[Murawa 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7289144/)
→ [[Internal moment arm]]

Low bar emphasizes the hip extensors, while high bar emphasizes the knee extensors.
[Glassbrook 2019](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28195975/)

## Knee travel

Knees over toes increases quad involvement while knees behind toes increases hip extensor involvement.
[Kernozek 2018](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28422563/)

## Stance width

Using a wider foot position increases the hip extensor involvement.
[Lahti 2019](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30230052/)

A narrow foot stance increases quad involvement while wider stance increases the involvement of hip extensors.
[Sinclair 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9503729/)

Using a wider foot stance increases glute involvement.
[Sinclair 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9503729/)

## Squat and leg press variations

Bilateral squats differ in technique (weightlifting or powerlifting style), bar position and type (front, back or safety bar), and range of motion and resistance type. Bilateral leg presses differ only in foot position and range of motion and resistance type. Besides being more stable, the leg press differs from the squat in hip range of motion: the squat extends the hip from about 90° of flexion to full extension, while the leg press goes from about 135–150° to about 60–75° of flexion. So the leg press likely uses relatively more adductor magnus and gives more stimulus for sarcomerogenesis in the hip extensors → [[Stretch-mediated hypertrophy]].

The adductor magnus' hip extension moment arm grew up to about 75° of hip flexion, while the gluteus maximus' shrank as the hip flexed.
[Németh 1985](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/3988782/)

### Technique and foot position

A weightlifting-style back squat (high bar, narrow stance, knees traveling forward) lengthens the bar's moment arm on the knee, making it more knee-dominant. A powerlifting-style squat (low bar, wide stance, hips traveling back) lengthens the moment arm on the hip, making it more hip-dominant. In the leg press, a low foot position makes it more knee-dominant and a high foot position more hip-dominant.

In 14 rugby players squatting to parallel, a wide stance gave a higher ratio of hip to knee extension moments than a narrow one, while the narrow stance had more knee flexion.
[Lahti 2019](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30230052/)

In 12 experienced powerlifters, low-bar squats produced more muscle activity in the lowering phase than high-bar squats at 60–65% of 1RM.
[Murawa 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7289144/)

### Range of motion and resistance profile

Lengthened partials or a conventional resistance profile emphasize stretch-mediated hypertrophy in the quadriceps, adductor magnus and gluteus maximus (especially in the leg press, which reaches a more flexed hip), and shift hip work toward the adductor magnus and away from the glutes. Contracted partials or an accommodating resistance profile, like elastic bands, reduce stretch-mediated hypertrophy and shift hip work toward the glutes → [[Partial vs full range of motion]].

In 29 people, squats with elastic bands produced substantially less quadriceps activity than with a barbell, mainly in the parts of the movement where the bands were slack.
[Iversen 2017](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28628370/)

### Bar position

A back squat allows relatively more hip extensor contribution and a front squat more knee extensor contribution. But all squats are fundamentally knee-dominant, so front and back squats don't differ hugely for the quadriceps. The front squat probably gives slightly more quadriceps activation, but its main effects are less hip extensor activation and much less weight on the bar.

In 12 people squatting maximal loads, the front squat produced more vastus medialis activity and the back squat more semitendinosus activity, with more trunk lean in the back squat.
[Yavuz 2015](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25630691/)

### Best choices for the quads

The best squats for the quadriceps are knee-dominant variations with peak force in the stretched position, like deep front squats and deep weightlifting-style back squats. Knee-dominant leg presses (with a low foot position) with peak force in the stretched position are likely similarly effective → [[Quadriceps training]].

## Lunges and split squats

Terminology isn't consistent, but here a lunge starts from standing and steps one foot forward, sideways or backward, while a split squat keeps both feet in place throughout. Lunges involve decelerating as the landing foot hits the ground, which makes them better suited to athletic preparation than bodybuilding. Changing torso lean or step length affects both exercises the same way.

### Torso inclination

Leaning forward increases the hip extensors' share (adductor magnus, gluteus maximus and hamstrings). That isn't always feasible, practical or safe depending on the load, but since lunges and split squats are already more hip-dominant than squats and leg presses, it's a useful way to shift the focus. In 10 adults, forward lunges with the trunk leaning forward increased hip flexion, hip extensor impulse and gluteus maximus and biceps femoris activity compared with an upright trunk.
[Farrokhi 2008](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18591759/)

### Step length

Longer steps with the knee going past the toes increase the hip extensors' share, while shorter steps keeping the knee behind the toes increase the knee extensors' share. This is probably a safer, more convenient way than torso lean to make a lunge or split squat more hip-dominant. In 11 people doing split squats, changing the front shin angle shifted range of motion and joint moments between the front knee, rear knee and hip.
[Schütz 2014](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24345718/)

### Step direction

Backward steps maximize the hip extensors' share, then forward steps, then lateral steps. So the popular forward lunge is probably not the best for either the hip extensors (better trained by backward lunges) or the knee extensors (better trained by lateral lunges).
- In 32 young adults, forward lunges had 45–65% more hip extensor impulse than lateral lunges, while lateral lunges had 17–48% more knee extensor impulse. [Riemann 2013](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23584319/)
- In 20 women, forward lunges produced more quadriceps force, knee flexion and patellofemoral joint loading than backward lunges, with less forward trunk tilt. [Goulette 2021](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33310585/)
- Forward lunges produced higher peak vastus medialis oblique activity and vertical ground reaction force than backward lunges. [Park 2021](https://koreascience.kr/article/JAKO202122162142679.page)

### Best choices

For the hip extensors in lunges, backward steps and long steps look best, and in split squats a forward-leaning torso and a long distance between the feet. For the knee extensors, an upright torso, lateral steps and short steps look best → [[Gluteus maximus training]].
