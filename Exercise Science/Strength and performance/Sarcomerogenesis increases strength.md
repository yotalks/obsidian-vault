> [!summary] Bottom line
> Increases in muscle volume count both fiber hypertrophy and added sarcomeres, and they track strength gains very closely. Sarcomeres in series may add force like myofibrils in parallel because force travels sideways through costameres into the endomysium, not just along the fiber. But in old rats, eccentric training that added sarcomeres without parallel growth raised peak power, not maximum isometric torque.

## Muscle volume and strength

There are multiple ways to measure muscle growth. Increases in muscle mass and volume include all of the fiber hypertrophy and [[Sarcomerogenesis|sarcomerogenesis]] across all regions of the muscle and are therefore the most important. Increases in muscle volume are statistically strongly associated with increases with maximum isometric strength in animal models and humans.
[Marques 2025](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40680785/)

In 39 untrained young men over 15 weeks of lower body training, the within-person correlation between gains in quadriceps volume and strength was 0.92 for isometric torque and 0.89 for 1RM. Muscle growth contributed more than 5 times as much as changes in neuromuscular activation.

## Contraction mode

This relationship between muscle mass increases and strength increases remains even when the training program has different contraction modes. This is very important as eccentric training mainly causes sarcomerogenesis while isometric and concentric training mainly causes fiber hypertrophy. If muscle fiber hypertrophy led to strength gains but sarcomerogenesis doesn't, then eccentric training would lead to a smaller increase in strength after training in animal models (which neural contributions to strength gains are removed by design).
[Ashida 2018](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29074713/)

In rodent models, the majority of the increase in muscle mass that occurs after eccentric training programs is from sarcomerogenesis. Very little fiber hypertrophy happens.
[Hinks 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11449626/)

In that study of old rats, 4 weeks of submaximal eccentric training raised sarcomere number by 8–11% and muscle mass by 13–14%, with no change in physiological CSA. Maximum isometric torque didn't change, while peak power rose 23%.

## Specific tension

Specific tension is the force produced by a single muscle fiber relative to its [[Glossary#CSA|CSA]]. Specific tension can either be estimated from whole muscles or measured in single muscle fibers. When it's estimated from whole muscles, muscle fiber force comes from physiological CSA and not anatomical CSA. Full ROM training leads to larger increases in specific tension than partial ROM strength training. This can be explained with full ROM training causes the addition of more sarcomeres (indicated by larger increases in fascicle length) that aren't reflected in the increased physiological CSA.
[Valamatos 2018](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29982844/)
→ [[Partial vs full range of motion]]

In that trial of 11 untrained men, the full ROM leg gained 4.9% in fascicle length and 25.8% in specific tension. The partial ROM leg gained 7.8% in physiological CSA.

## Lateral force transmission

Classical mechanics tells us that the tension at all points along a string must be equal, but a muscle fiber isn't a string and it does not transmit force longitudinally. Each sarcomere transmits force laterally into the outer endomysium and from there down to the tendon. This way, the addition of sarcomeres in series produces the same increase in maximum muscle fiber force as adding myofibrils in parallel.
[Erskine 2011](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20889606/)

In 42 young men, 9 weeks of leg extension training raised quadriceps specific tension by 17%, while power per unit of muscle volume didn't change. In the 17 who were biopsied, single-fiber specific tension didn't change either. That pattern fits more lateral attachments, which would raise force at the cost of contraction velocity.

Costameres (including dystrophin complexes) manage lateral force transmission. Animal models have shown that almost all the force produced by sarcomeres is transmitted laterally and dystrophin complexes help turn sarcomere forces into lateral forces.
[Ramaswamy 2011](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21224224/)

There's good evidence that strength training programs that involve high levels of passive mechanical tension (like eccentric only contractions) can cause the addition of new costameres and therefore the attachment of the new sarcomeres to the endomysium.
[Li 2013](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3916893/)
