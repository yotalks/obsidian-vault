> [!summary] Bottom line
> Jump height is set by the vertical impulse you produce relative to body weight. That depends on both maximum strength and maximum speed, and their importance shifts down the hip-knee-ankle chain: strength matters most at the hip, both matter at the knee, and speed matters most at the ankle. So train the hip extensors heavy, the knee extensors with both squats and assisted jumps, and the ankles with assisted jumps.

## Vertical impulse

How high we can jump is determined by the vertical impulse we produce relative to body weight. Across jumps from different depths, relative net vertical impulse correlated about 0.93 with jump height, and deeper squats increased both, even though peak force fell.
[Kirby 2011](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21844609/)

Impulse is force multiplied by time. The time available is the take-off phase, which starts at the bottom of the countermovement, which is why a bigger countermovement often helps. There is an optimal depth, though, and within a typical range of depths jump height changes only a little. Most people's preferred depth was shallower than their optimum.
[Mandic 2015](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25465982/)

The force we can produce depends on two things:
- **Maximum strength:** how much force we can exert at the start of the concentric phase.
- **Maximum speed:** how much our force drops as movement speed rises. With low maximum speed, force falls off quickly during the take-off, so total impulse stays modest even with high maximum strength.

## Hip, knee and ankle

The jump uses the hip extensors, knee extensors and ankle plantar flexors in a proximal-to-distal sequence, so each joint peaks at a faster speed than the one above it. The hip contributes while rotating slowest, closest to the strength end of the force-velocity curve, and the ankle while rotating fastest.
→ [[Proximal-to-distal sequence]]

So the quality that matters most differs by joint:
- **Hip:** maximum strength.
- **Knee:** both maximum strength and maximum speed.
- **Ankle:** maximum speed.

## Training for jump height

- **Hip extensors:** since the hip depends mostly on maximum strength, focus far more on heavy strength training: hip thrusts for the glute max and stiff-legged deadlifts for the adductor magnus (see [[Gluteus maximus training]]).
- **Knee extensors:** these need a balance of heavy strength training, like back squats, or knee extensions and maximum speed work, like assisted vertical jumps. Jumps can be assisted with a bungee system, or by holding elastic bands attached to a power rack to reduce body weight while jumping.
- **Ankle plantar flexors:** it's doubtful these can be trained fast enough outside a jump to raise their maximum speed, so they're likely best trained in assisted jumps along with the knee extensors.

For the same approach applied to other movements, see [[Sprint speed]] and [[Throwing speed]].
