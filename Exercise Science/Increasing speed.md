> [!summary] Bottom line
> Strength alone doesn't make you fast. Speed improves through coordination, firing rate, recruitment, less antagonist coactivation and faster fiber shortening, and all of these except recruitment require training at fast speeds.

Speed is our ability to move at very fast velocities Speed is different to strength. Strength allows us to accelerate but it does not mean we can actually move at a certain velocity. Having a lot of strength (ability to move a lot of force) without also having high maximum speed (ability to move very fast) means you accelerate fast up to a certain speed but stop accelerating there after when you hit your limit.

There is a common view that increases in maximum strength will automatically increase maximum speed we can move. This is not true. They argue more muscle force means more acceleration (F = ma) and more acceleration leads to greater top velocity, but they forget that force changes with speed. As you accelerate, and muscle fibers contract faster, the amount of force they can produce starts to dwindle. If maximum shortening velocity is slow, they will quickly stop producing force and the acceleration goes away.

## Mechanisms for speed

There are 5 adaptations for speed that can be affected by training: 1. improvements in coordination, 2. increases in motor firing rate, 3. increases in motor unit recruitment, 4. reductions in antagonist coactivation, and 5. increases in muscle fiber maximum shortening speed. These determinants are mostly very different to the adaptations of maximum strength.

Improvements in coordination contribute to increases in speed because it increases the efficiency of the movement happening. We improve coordination by performing a specific movement in a more well-coordinated way than the motor program that the brain already has. The brain notices that the movement was more efficient and updates the pattern for future use. Improvements in coordination are very specific to the task being practiced. This specificity 8includes the velocity of the movement. So, if we want to increase speed by increasing coordination, we have to practice the exercise as fast as we can.
[[Improvements in coordination traits]]

Data on coordination improvements by testing the effects of familiarization sessions has found it is incredibly velocity specific.
[[Coburn 2006]]

We can in practice maximize the improvements that we get in a practice session by maximizing the efficiency of the exercise we are doing. This gives the brain the best possible motor program to copy. Various techniques are available to help us maximize coordination in training.
[[Techniques to improve coordination]]

Improvements in the ability to recruit high-threshold motor units of a muscle contributes to increases in speed, because the newly-recruited high-threshold motor units will have to mainly control muscle fibers of the fastest fiber type (type IIX). Thus, increasing the ability to access high-threshold motor units increases the average muscle fiber shortening speed of the muscle fibers in a muscle.
[Enoka 2001](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11150961/)

Fast movement training with light loads increases the ability to recruit high-threshold motor units in any movement involving the same muscle group, including isometric contractions.
[[Behrens 2016]]

The stimulus that causes increases in the ability to access high-threshold motor units is just reaching a very high level of central motor command and thus reaching a very high level of motor unit recruitment for the muscle. This means producing a maximum effort and devoting all of that effort perception to the central motor command without losing any of it to the management of discomforting sensations like cardiovascular activity or metabolite--0related burning sensations. There are a lot of techniquess available for maximizing this stimulus.
[[Increasing motor unit recruitment]]

Increasing agonist muscle motor unit firing rate is another adaptation. Central motor command signals are sent repeatedly to a muscle in muscular contractions, many times each second. The rate at which signals are sent is called the motor unit firing rate, the motor unit firing frequency, or rate coding. In slow movements (even with maximal efforts), the number of signals per second is relatively low. While during fast movements, the number of signals per second is comparatively high.
[Harwood 2011](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21107544/)

Data has shown that fast movement training is able to increase motor unit firing rates as a long-term adaptation.
[Van Cutsem 1998](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9782179/)

Comparatively, heavy strength training is not able to produce the same adaptation. This tells us that the stimulus for increasing the motor unit firing rates is getting high motor unit firing rates when training.
[[Sterczala 2020]]

A reduction in the force produced by the antagonist muscle during an exercise reduces the braking force at the joint, which then increases the external force exerted during an exercise, even without the force of the agonist muscle increasing. The adaptation involves an increase in efficiency, like an improvement in coordination. Training studies have shown fast movement training causes much larger reductions in antagonist coactivation when strength is measured at fast speeds than when strength is measured at slow speeds. This tells us that reductions in antagonist coactivation are velocity-specific and are likely stimulated by practice at the speed needed for the movement.
[Janusevicius 2017](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5465986/)

This means we need to perform the movement we want very fast in order to get this adaptation. A lot like coordination increases.

This adaptation does reduce after other types of training but the time-course over which it happens is a bit different. Research has also shown that there are some techniques where we might maximize antagonist coactivation by training.

Increasing our muscle fibers maximum shortening velocity is another adaptation. Muscle fibers have a maximum shortening velocity that they can't go past, no matter how little external resistance they have going against them. Muscle fiber type is the key determinant of this maximum velocity, but even within muscle fiber types, the maximum shortening speed can aryl between muscles and individuals. For examples, elites sprinters tend to have very fast muscle fiber shortening velocities.
[Trappe 2015](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25749440/)

Maximum muscle fiber shortening velocity can be improved by fast movement training (even without changes in fiber type). This is a very important adaptation that is barely ever considered by coaches.
[Malisoux 2006](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16322375/)

Comparatively, heavy strength training is unable to give ethe same adaptation. This tells us that the stimulus for increasing the maximum fiber shortening velocity is getting a high fiber shortening velocity when training.
[Widrick 2002](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12121854/)

Since muscle fiber type is a key determinant of muscle fiber maximum shortening velocity, we want to stay away from muscle fiber type shifts to slower, more oxidative types if our goal to to be as fast as possible. Muscle fiber type shifts are caused by the same calcium ion accumulation that causes [[Calcium ion-related fatigue|calcium ion-related fatigue]], so this is simple to avoid.

All in all, speed can increase through multiple adaptations. All of these adaptations require a fast movement speed during training to achieve except increases in ability to recruit motor units.
