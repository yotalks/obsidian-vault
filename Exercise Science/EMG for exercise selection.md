> [!summary] Bottom line
> EMG amplitude can't predict how much a muscle will grow, but it may show where growth happens. In unfatigued isometric or heavy contractions, higher EMG tracks more motor units recruited and so more muscle fibers activated. So the muscle or region with the highest EMG in an exercise, or the exercise with the highest EMG for a muscle, likely has the most growth potential. This reasoning is contested, though, and the EMG-force link varies between muscles.

## The objection

EMG studies are often dismissed for choosing exercises because acute EMG amplitude doesn't predict how much a muscle grows long term. Critics argue the logic behind using EMG this way rests on weak premises, that it hasn't been validated against measured hypertrophy, and that greater EMG doesn't necessarily mean greater motor unit recruitment.
[Vigotsky 2022](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35006527/)
[Vigotsky 2017](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26670996/)

## The case for using EMG

The claim isn't that EMG predicts how much a muscle grows, but where it grows. The argument runs:
- **Single motor unit force depends on fiber number.** When individual motor units are stimulated, the force each produces in an unfatigued isometric contraction depends mainly on how many muscle fibers it has.
[Kanda 1992](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1176222/)
- **Bigger action potentials mean more recruitment.** High-threshold motor units produce larger motor unit action potentials (MUAPs) than low-threshold ones. In the quadriceps, average MUAP size rose linearly with force, while firing rates barely changed until about 30% of maximum, consistent with orderly recruitment. Since each unit's force depends on its fiber count, more recruitment means more activated fibers.
[Conwit 1999](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10423192/)
- **EMG amplitude tracks force.** In unfatigued isometric contractions, EMG amplitude rises with muscle force, so it reflects the level of recruitment and the number of fibers activated.
[Bilodeau 2003](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12488090/)
[Herzog 1998](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9678149/)

Since only activated fibers can grow, the number of fibers an exercise activates is a good indicator of its maximum growth potential. So in studies measuring several muscles in one exercise, the muscle or region with the highest EMG probably has the most fibers activated and the most growth potential. And in studies comparing exercises for the same muscle, the exercise with the highest EMG is probably best for training it.
→ [[Increasing motor unit recruitment]]

## Limits

The shape of the EMG-force relationship depends on the muscle: it was roughly linear in a small hand muscle but not in larger muscles like the biceps and deltoid. So EMG amplitudes are best compared within the same muscle, in unfatigued isometric or heavy contractions, rather than across different muscles or in fatiguing sets.
[Lawrence 1983](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/6874489/)
