> [!summary] Bottom line
> Rinsing carbohydrate without swallowing it increases reps to failure but not maximum strength, likely by reducing perceived effort and supraspinal CNS fatigue.

## Carb mouth rinsing on performance

Recent data has shown carbohydrate mouth rinsing increases the number of reps to failure in trained lifters. No carbohydrates were actually consumed so it must be attributed to a reduction in [[Glossary#CNS|CNS]] fatigue mechanisms.
[Decimoni 2018](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/1747954118755640)

We see this observed with heavy load performance too. It is likely that the brain detects glucose in the mouth and reduces effort perception cause of it.
[Yang 2024](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38674938/)

Across the literature, there is no noticeable effect on maximum strength, but a very clear effect on repetition strength whether with single or multiple sets.
[Rodrigues Oliveira-Silva 2023](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35373671/)

## Why does this happen?

We have a lot of data showing a reduction in body carbohydrate stores is rapidly detected by the brain which causes it to produce discomforting feelings of tiredness. These feelings reduce motor unit recruitment at maximum levels of effort which is [[Supraspinal CNS fatigue|supraspinal CNS fatigue]]

Discomforting feelings producing a reduction in motor unit recruitment at maximal efforts is explained by the [[Psychobiological model of fatigue|psychobiological model of fatigue]].
