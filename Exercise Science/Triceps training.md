> [!summary] Bottom line
> The triceps' long head crosses the shoulder and works best with the arm at the side, so cable pushdowns train it best, especially with a supinated grip. The medial and lateral heads only cross the elbow and work best with the arm raised to 90° or more and the elbow nearly straight, so narrow-grip bench press lockouts and overhead extensions train them best.

## Heads act separately

The triceps is three muscles. The long head crosses both the shoulder and elbow, while the medial and lateral heads cross only the elbow. Which head does the most work depends on shoulder position: with the arm at the side, the long head produced the most force and activation, while with the arm raised to 90°, 135° or 180°, the medial head produced the most force.
[Kholinne 2018](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29503079/)
→ [[Neuromechanical matching]]

The moment arms of elbow muscle segments, and so their leverage, change with both elbow angle and forearm position.
[Ettema 1998](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0167945797000304)

## Long head

The long head has its best relative leverage for elbow extension with the shoulder in the anatomical position (arm at the side) and the elbow bent to at least 90°. So the cable pushdown trains it well, since the cable's direction makes the exercise hardest in the middle of the range.

Using a supinated (palms-up) grip may increase long head activation further, almost certainly by changing its leverage:
- Elbow extensions with the arm at the side activated the long head more with the forearm supinated.
[Kim 2011](https://koreascience.kr/article/JAKO201116549676332.page)
- In single-arm pushdowns, a supinated grip on a handle activated the long head more than the other grips, though people managed fewer reps.
[Villalba 2024](https://journal.iusca.org/index.php/Journal/article/view/250)

The grip can be hard to hold, so cuffs may help.

## Medial and lateral heads

The medial and lateral heads have their best relative leverage for elbow extension with the shoulder flexed to at least 90° (arm parallel with the ground) and the elbow extended. So narrow-grip bench presses train them well, especially with a partial range of motion (lockouts), which make the end of the movement hardest. Overhead extensions are also great for these heads, since with the arm raised to 135° or 180° the medial head did the most work.
