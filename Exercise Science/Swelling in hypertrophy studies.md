> [!summary] Bottom line
> Muscle-damaging workouts make muscles swell for days afterward, and current methods can't tell that swelling apart from growth. One strenuous leg workout raised quadriceps cross-sectional area by 16% two days later, more than most studies record over 6–8 weeks of training. If size is measured a few days after the last session, programs that cause more damage, like higher volumes or training at long muscle lengths, may look better for hypertrophy than they are. One check is whether bigger size gains come with matching strength gains.

## Swelling after damaging exercise

This swelling comes from muscle damage and builds over days. It's different from the pump during a workout ([[Muscle swelling]]).

- In 10 untrained men, a strenuous quadriceps workout raised cross-sectional area near the knee by 13% after 4 hours and 16% after 52 hours, with muscle water content up 7–8%. The authors stress leaving enough time after the last workout before measuring muscle size. [Kristiansen 2014](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24330190/)
- After eccentric elbow flexor exercise in 13 people, arm circumference rose 3% right away, fell back, then rose by up to 9% and stayed up for as long as 9 days. Strength dropped almost 40% and had barely recovered after 10 days. [Howell 1993](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8229798/)
- After 120 maximal eccentric forearm flexor contractions in 8 men, muscle cross-sectional area was up at 48 hours, and it and other MRI markers of swelling peaked at 72 hours. [Rodenburg 1994](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8039516/)
- After downhill running, soleus fibers were 24% larger at 7–8 days than at 2–3 days, with fewer capillaries per fiber area, which the authors interpret as fiber swelling. [Yu 2013](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23614012/)

## More damage, more swelling

In rats, 70 or 150 stretch-shortening contractions caused force loss, degenerating fibers and inflammation 48 hours later, and 150 caused more degeneration than 70. Thirty contractions didn't.
[Baker 2007](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17762360/)

Maximal eccentrics at short muscle lengths caused smaller losses of strength and range of motion, and less arm swelling, soreness and creatine kinase, than the same eccentrics at long muscle lengths. They also cut the damage from a later long-length bout by 50–70%.
[Nosaka 2005](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15870627/)
→ [[Repeated bout effect]]

## How this could bias hypertrophy studies

Studies of training variables usually compare groups training for several weeks and measure muscle size before and after, often within a few days of the final workout. A group doing more damaging training, such as more sets or a larger range of motion, would carry more swelling at that measurement. Because the swelling can be as large as a whole program's growth, even a small difference in swelling between groups could make the more damaging program look much better for hypertrophy.
→ [[Training volume]]

## Checking against strength

If a training variable adds real muscle, strength should rise roughly in line with size. A bigger size response with no matching strength response is a sign that some of the extra size may be swelling.

In a meta-regression of 67 studies (2,058 people), more weekly sets increased both muscle size and strength, but the diminishing returns were much stronger for strength. Training frequency increased strength, while its effect on size was compatible with nothing. The authors coach and write in the fitness industry.
[Pelland 2026](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41343037/)
