> [!summary] Bottom line
> Creatine raises repetition strength through fatigue resistance, and its size gains are mostly water swelling rather than new muscle. It isn't a direct growth signal and can't work by adding ATP. It also speeds strength recovery after damaging workouts, but if that's through an anti-inflammatory effect, it could blunt adaptations. There's no need to cycle it, and it may improve memory in vegetarians.

## Creatine's affect on size are trivial and it does not stimulate hypertrophy directly.
Creatine's effect on muscle size are trivial and likely don't reach statistical significance when taken along with a resistance training program. The effect it had was much greater during short term programs vs. long term which can be explained by it having a one-time muscle size increase effect through muscle swelling, so creatine would have the same increase in absolute muscle size in both short term and long term training programs.
[Burke 2023](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37432300/)

Creatine does not help preserve muscle mass during periods of immobilization which means it does not directly stimulate hypertrophy. It also didn't affect the regain of muscle mass afterward. This makes sense as it's not an [[Glossary#AAS|AAS]]. 
[Backx 2017](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28054322/)
→ [[Anabolic Androgenic Steroids]]

## How creatine affects progressive overload

Creatine increases repetition strength, but not maximum strength. Since it does not increase maximum strength and hypertrophy requires an increase in maximum strength because an increase in myofibrils increases the amount of force muscle fibers can generate, this makes it likely the increases in size are just swelling. This can be explained by it increasing fatigue resistance through the creatine-phosphate system. 
[Furtado 2024](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38747788/)
→ [[Fatigue resistance]]

ATP is used for multiple important functions inside muscle fibers during exercise. It can be provided by the phosphagen system, glycolysis and glycogenolysis, and mitochondrial respiration. Some metabolic reactions involve the production of free hydrogen ions which causes metabolite related fatigue, but the phosphagen system actually consumes a hydrogen ion when it makes ATP, which buffers this fatiguing effect.
[Robergs 2004](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15308499/)

Creatine doesn't help by increasing ATP availability because we do not decrease ATP during exercise.
[[ATP does not deplete during exercise]]

## Creatine increases muscle size through muscle swelling

Without training, creatine increases total body mass, lean body mass, total body water, and muscle fiber cross sectional area. This can be explained by the increased creatine content inside the muscle fiber altering the osmotic balance causing water to be pulled into the muscle fiber via osmosis, thus increasing it's size through swelling. This would also mean that hopping off creatine would lose your "gains" you received from it.
[Safdar 2008](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17957000/)

Muscle swelling does not stimulate hypertrophy.
[[Muscle swelling]]

## Creatine increasing maximum strength

Even though the data doesn't find creatine increases maximum strength, it is still possible it can cause minor increases though the increased swelling of the muscle, increasing its size which causes the muscle to bulge away from the bone more, leading to a greater [[Glossary#IMAL|IMAL]], giving the muscle better leverage to generate a joint torque.
[Vigotsky 2015](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4671169/)

## Recovery from muscle damage

Creatine's buffering helps during a workout: the phosphagen system consumes a hydrogen ion each time it regenerates ATP, reducing acidosis. That shouldn't affect post-workout fatigue, which comes from calcium ion-related mechanisms instead.
→ [[Calcium ion-related fatigue]]

Even so, creatine does seem to speed recovery after a workout. After 28 days of creatine, men doing eccentric curls recovered strength faster, had better range of motion and less arm swelling over the following week, and had higher strength even immediately after the workout.
[Yamaguchi 2024](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38542807/)
A follow-up in 40 men and women again found faster strength recovery, plus less muscle stiffness and fatigue, with less swelling specifically in women.
[Yamaguchi 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12157024/)

Faster strength recovery and less swelling suggest creatine reduces secondary muscle damage, the delayed damage and swelling caused by the post-workout inflammatory process. How it does this matters, because some interventions that speed recovery by reducing secondary damage, like [[Post-workout cooling|post-workout cold water immersion]], also blunt adaptations. There are three possible mechanisms:
- **Less glycogen use:** creatine may reduce the initial calpain-driven damage from calcium ion influx by supplying ATP from another source, so muscle glycogen is used less. That would be purely positive.
- **An antioxidant effect:** part of secondary damage comes from oxidative stress produced by post-workout inflammation. In rats, creatine reduced markers of oxidative stress in blood and muscle after exercise. This is probably slightly negative for adaptations, but not by much, since oxidative stress mostly causes excess damage rather than essential repair.
[Deminice 2012](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22009139/)
- **An anti-inflammatory effect:** there's limited evidence creatine reduces inflammation directly. In cultured endothelial cells it reduced neutrophil adhesion and adhesion molecules, and in another study it reduced post-exercise fatigue through an anti-inflammatory action. This would probably be negative for adaptations, since inflammation drives essential repair.
[Nomura 2003](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1573908/)
[Yokota 2023](https://www.mdpi.com/1661-3821/3/2/19)

## No need to cycle it

About 95% of the body's creatine is in skeletal muscle. We get roughly 1 g a day from an omnivorous diet and make roughly another 1 g a day, mainly in the liver and kidneys, which matches the amount broken down into creatinine each day. Supplementing creatine does reduce the body's own production, but normal rates return once supplementation stops.
[Persky 2001](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11356982/)

So there's no need to cycle creatine to avoid suppressing your own production. As one review puts it, "Levels return to normal after a brief period of time when supplementation ceases."
[Cooper 2012](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC3407788/)

## Cognition

There's some evidence creatine improves memory and cognitive performance, mainly in vegetarians, who get little creatine from their diet:
- In 128 young women, 20 g a day for 5 days improved memory in vegetarians but not in meat eaters, and made reaction times more consistent in both.
[Benton 2011](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21118604/)
- In 45 young vegetarian adults, 5 g a day for 6 weeks improved working memory and intelligence test scores compared with placebo.
[Rae 2003](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/14561278/)
