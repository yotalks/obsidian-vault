> [!summary] Bottom line
> AAS use is associated with more tendon and ligament injuries. In HAARLEM, heart mass increased and pumping function fell during a cycle, then returned to baseline in those followed after stopping. Kidney injury has been reported in bodybuilders, but the case series cannot establish its frequency or isolate AAS as the cause.

## Tendons and ligaments

Tendons and ligaments fail to adapt properly to the training stimulus under AAS, so collagen strength lags while muscle force output climbs. 
[Kanayama 2015](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5206906/)
→ [[Tendon adaptation]]

### Proposed mechanisms

Steroids grow muscle even without training, and the effect adds to training. But by suppressing collagen breakdown they block tendon adaptation, so muscle force outpaces tendon strength and rupture risk rises, including on TRT.

Muscle and connective tissue get stronger by different mechanisms. Muscle adds myofibrillar protein. Tendon and ligament stiffen by reorganizing architecture and shifting collagen types, which requires breakdown as well as synthesis.
[Kanayama 2015](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5206906/)

AAS act the same direction in both tissues. AAS raise synthesis and suppress breakdown in muscle (↑[[Glossary#MPS|MPS]], ↓[[Glossary#MPB|MPB]]) and in connective tissue (↑collagen synthesis, ↓collagen breakdown). Since muscle adaptation is construction-limited, suppressed breakdown helps. Since tendon adaptation is breakdown-dependent, suppressed breakdown blocks it. 
[Pärssinen 2000](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10961515/)
[Kanayama 2015](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5206906/)

### Collagen deposition in wound healing

The collagen-synthesis half of that mechanism shows up clearly in wound healing, and it runs through the androgen receptor. In 50 Wistar rats (25 male, 25 female) given second-degree burns with a 1 cm metal disc heated to 100°C, groups of 5 per sex were treated twice daily with topical stanozolol 0.5%, stanozolol plus oral flutamide, flutamide alone, silver sulfadiazine 1% or the ointment base (Eucerin).

- **Closure.** Topical stanozolol closed wounds in about 20 days in both sexes, faster than every other group (24–27 days).
- **Collagen.** Hydroxyproline, a marker of collagen content, was higher with stanozolol than every other group at both day 7 and day 14 (p < 0.001).
- **Histology.** At day 7 the stanozolol group showed keratinocyte migration to the wound edges, granulation tissue and dense collagen bundles. Flutamide alone showed limited keratinocyte migration, abundant macrophages and neutrophils, and no granulation tissue.
- **Androgen receptor.** Flutamide blocks androgen receptors. Adding it to stanozolol pushed closure out to about 24 days, no different from the ointment base, so the effect is androgen-receptor-mediated rather than some property of the ointment.
- No differences between male and female rats.

The higher hydroxyproline finding applies to both sexes, with different significance levels across comparisons. The paper reports p < 0.001 across comparisons in males and reports p < 0.01 for several comparisons in females. 
[Asgharnejad 2025](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1155/adpp/6682255)

So more collagen laid down faster is exactly what closes a burn. Tendon stiffening is the opposite case: it needs remodeling, not just deposition, which is why the same androgen signal speeds wound healing and holds tendon adaptation back.

AAS-user tendons under the microscope show disorganization, fibril misalignment, damage, and fibrosis
[Kanayama 2015](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5206906/)

Rupture risk elevation applies to prescription [[Glossary#TRT|TRT]] as well as recreational bodybuilding doses. 
[Kanayama 2015](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5206906/)

Kanayama gives two possible explanations for the rupture association: muscle force could rise faster than tendon capacity, or AAS could directly change tendon structure. Their human cohort measured rupture histories. It did not establish the collagen mechanism or compare strategies for lowering rupture risk.

In Kanayama's bodybuilders, the excess was driven by upper-body tendons: 15 of 88 steroid users (17%) had an upper-body rupture versus none of 54 non-users, while lower-body ruptures didn't differ significantly (HR 3.1, 0.7–13.8). Only 6 of 31 ruptures happened while weightlifting.

The prescription studies are insurance-claims cohorts matched on age and sex. They show association, can't fully separate the drug from the reasons people are prescribed it, and the absolute risks are small (for quadriceps injuries, 0.06% vs under 0.01% in a year). But they point the same way across different tendons and ligaments.

Enhanced lifters should be cautious with heavier loads because if they're gaining a lot of strength from AAS-induced hypertrophy, that will expose the tendon to loading it's not equipped to handle. Off cycle (or actual physiological TRT blocks) where training is done to increase tendon stiffness is the only likely solution.

## Heart structure and function

If you make a skeletal muscle fiber grow, you're going to make a cardiac myocyte grow, it's the same process. Athlete's heart is completely different to the cardiac hypertrophy from AAS. Different parts of the heart are affected and the nature of myocyte growth (longitudinal vs. transverse) is also different. As far as we can tell, cardiac myocytes don't atrophy very much after AAS. Skeletal muscle hypertrophy from AAS is reversible but the addition of myonuclei is probably not, so the capacity for excessive growth probably stays and that can be taken advantage of with strength training. Cardiac myocyte growth is partially reversible after short AAS cycles, so that can be a management strategy but I doubt it would work long term because of the pathological nature of the growth. Everything starts with monitoring so one needs to set up a testing protocol to track heart mass over time to get an idea how big the problem is likely to get.

In the HAARLEM study, 31 men had echocardiograms before and after starting an AAS cycle. Over a median of 16 weeks, left ventricular mass rose by 28.3 g (95% CI 14.2 to 42.4) and ejection fraction fell by 4.9 percentage points (95% CI 2.5 to 7.2 lower). Measurements returned to baseline in the 25 men assessed after a median of eight months off AAS. The study measured ventricular mass by imaging.
[Smit 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9580689/)

## Kidney outcomes and lab tests

→ [[AAS mechanisms and blood testing#What standard testing doesn't measure|Limits of blood testing]]

In a case series of 10 bodybuilders with long-term AAS use and kidney abnormalities, mean serum creatinine was 3.0 mg/dL and mean urinary protein was 10.1 g/day. Blood and urine tests captured different features of kidney injury in those cases. The series cannot isolate the effects of AAS from other bodybuilding exposures or estimate how common the condition is. Nine of the ten had focal segmental glomerulosclerosis on biopsy, mean BMI was 34.7, and of the eight with follow-up, seven stabilized or improved after stopping while one who resumed developed progressive kidney failure.
[Herlitz 2010](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2799287/)
