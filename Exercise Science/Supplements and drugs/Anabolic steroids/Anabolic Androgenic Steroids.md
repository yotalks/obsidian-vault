> [!summary] Bottom line
> Testosterone increases muscle size with or without resistance training, and training adds to its effects. Trials up to 600 mg a week found greater fat-free mass gains at higher doses, but did not establish a ceiling or a dose giving half the maximum growth. Faster recovery of strength does not by itself show that muscle damage has repaired faster.

## Muscle

[[Glossary#AAS|AAS]] increase muscle mass without any training stimulus. Human subjects given AAS for several weeks gain substantial mass even with no strength training program running alongside.
[Bhasin 1996](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8637535/)
[Bhasin 2001](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11701431/)

AAS do this by binding to the androgen receptors and starting the hypertrophy process which happens to skeletal muscle, but also cardiac myocytes, kidney podocytes, and prostate cells which is why it has potentially dangerous side effects.
[Griggs 1989 - testosterone and muscle protein synthesis in men](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/2917954/)

AAS increase muscle size dose-dependently, and the increase is fiber hypertrophy starting as low as 50 mg per week.

The trial assigned 61 men to one of five testosterone enanthate doses for 20 weeks while suppressing their endogenous testosterone. Measured fiber area increased significantly from baseline at 300 and 600 mg per week for type I fibers and at 600 mg per week for type II fibers.
[Sinha-Hikim 2002](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12067856/)

Steroids may also let fibers grow past the size limit that oxidative capacity normally sets, and add new fibers through hyperplasia. Pushing past that limit can substantially raise the risk of rhabdomyolysis and compartment syndrome.
→ [[Individual differences in muscle growth]]

The two stimuli are additive, not overlapping. Trained subjects on AAS hypertrophy more than untrained subjects on AAS, so training adds on top rather than being substituted for.
[Bhasin 1996](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8637535/)

Animal limb-immobilization work is the cleanest separation of the two. AAS-treated animals grow in the free limbs and in the immobilized limb, but less in the immobilized one. So AAS don't fully rescue atrophy from absent [[Mechanical tension|mechanical tension]],. [[Glossary#MT|MT]] and androgens are independent inputs.
[Taylor 1999](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10569356/)

### Dose, binding and muscle growth

Muscle fibers will grow until they reach the size associated with that dose. We know this because those on TRT from early in life don't end up like Olympia level competitors decades down the line.

Binding affinity describes how tightly a compound binds. Receptor occupancy is the fraction of receptors bound at a given time, which also depends on the free drug concentration in muscle.
[Barley 1975 - rat brain and pituitary receptor binding](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/172193/)
[Roselli 1998 - rat brain receptor binding and aromatase activity](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0006899398001486)

The testosterone dose-response trial above found greater gains in muscle size at higher doses.

### Testosterone dose-response

Two trials ran the same design: 20 weeks of weekly testosterone enanthate at 25, 50, 125, 300 or 600 mg, endogenous production suppressed with a GnRH agonist, and no resistance training. One used 61 men aged 18–35, the other 60 men aged 60–75. Nadir total testosterone in the young men was 253, 306, 542, 1,345 and 2,370 ng/dL across the five doses. Change in fat-free mass, in kg:

| Weekly dose | Young, underwater | Young, DEXA | Older, DEXA | Older, underwater |
| ----------- | ----------------- | ----------- | ----------- | ----------------- |
| **25 mg**   | −1.0              | −0.4        | −0.3        | −0.8              |
| **50 mg**   | −0.6              | +1.1        | +1.7        | +1.4              |
| **125 mg**  | +3.4              | +2.9        | +4.2        | +4.5              |
| **300 mg**  | +5.2              | +5.5        | +5.6        | +4.8              |
| **600 mg**  | +7.9              | +8.9        | +7.3        | +6.7              |

[Bhasin 2001](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11701431/) · [Bhasin 2005](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15562020/)

The low arms were below replacement. The GnRH agonist shut down endogenous production, so at 25 and 50 mg the men sat at or under their own baseline and fat-free mass fell or barely moved. The bottom of this curve is a suppressed state rather than an untreated one, which is why a percentage of maximum has no natural zero to count from.

Older men responded as much as young men. After adjusting for testosterone levels there was no age effect on the change in fat-free mass, by DEXA or by underwater weighing. In those older men, 125 mg a week gave 4.2 kg of fat-free mass and 28 kg of leg press strength at high-normal testosterone levels with few adverse events, which the authors called the best trade-off between anabolic effects and adverse events. Three older men on 300 mg were discontinued: one for hematocrit above 54%, one for hematocrit above 54% with leg edema, and one for hematocrit above 54% with PSA above 4 ng/ml.
[Bhasin 2005](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15562020/)

#### Half of maximum

A common claim is that around 250 mg a week delivers about half the muscle available from any dose. A halfway point needs a ceiling, and no trial has gone above 600 mg. At 600 the curve is still climbing, with 300 to 600 adding another 2.5 kg in young men.

Fitting a saturating curve to all 20 published points leaves the ceiling nearly free, because curves with different ceilings only separate past 600 mg, where there is nothing to fit them against:

| Assumed ceiling | Half-maximal dose | Residual sum of squares |
| --------------- | ----------------- | ----------------------- |
| **10 kg**       | ~260 mg           | 22.3                    |
| **14 kg**       | ~480 mg           | 18.8                    |
| **20 kg**       | ~870 mg           | 21.0                    |

Fitting each of the four published series on its own gives half-maximal doses from about 50 to about 480 mg, and the two that disagree most are underwater weighing and DEXA applied to the same 61 men.

The trial authors tested a saturating model directly. They compared a linear model, a logarithmic model and a growth model, and reported that the logarithmic model fit the data best. A logarithmic curve has no ceiling, so on the authors' own preferred fit there is no maximum to take half of.
[Bhasin 2001](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11701431/)

So 250 mg follows from assuming a low ceiling rather than from a measurement. → [[Evidence hierarchy]]

### Energy intake and recovery

Higher muscle protein synthesis rates mean damage is repaired faster, which implies AAS users can tolerate higher workout volumes.
[Zhao 2008](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18436443/)

Severe caloric restriction and persistent sleep loss both raise systemic [[Glucocorticoids|glucocorticoids]], which drive oxidative stress inside the fiber and elevate muscle protein breakdown. AAS are glucocorticoid-antagonistic, so users can hold deeper deficits for longer without the atrophy, reduced motivation, and slow recovery a natural lifter would get.

A caloric surplus is likely unnecessary on AAS too, the benefits of AAS are bigger when in a deficit. The signaling for hypertrophy happens regardless, and some AAS side effects like those caused by aromatization can be worse at higher body fats.

The amount of dietary protein required to build muscle is small so I don't think it's going to help going above the same nutritional recommendations as for naturals. It's also a good idea to set a precautionary principle and do anything to protect the kidneys using AAS even with normally healthy things in naturals like eating a lot of protein.
→ [[Protein Intake]]

Piovesan studied nandrolone after a freezing injury to rat muscle. The experiment measured markers of muscle repair after injury.
[Piovesan 2013](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22791621/)

### Strength recovery versus damage repair

In exercise science, recovery usually means getting back to pre-workout strength. On AAS, muscle is growing in the background during the days after a workout, so strength can come back sooner because of the added muscle, not because damage was repaired any faster. Faster strength recovery on AAS doesn't show that damage repair is faster.
→ [[Recovery between workouts]]

Rat injury studies found faster repair with nandrolone:
- **Contusion.** After a drop-mass contusion, nandrolone decanoate at 20 mg/kg made no difference to force at day 7, but by day 14 the injured muscles were significantly stronger in twitch force than controls, with a similar trend for tetanic force.
  [Beiner 1999](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9934411/)
- **Cryoinjury.** With nandrolone decanoate at 5 mg/kg twice a week after a freezing injury, MyoD rose by day 7 and myogenin by day 21, and the injured muscle showed no edema or myonecrosis at day 7. [Piovesan 2013](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22791621/)

Converted to a human-equivalent dose by body surface area (rat dose × 6/37), those are about 3.2 mg/kg and about 0.8 mg/kg twice a week, or roughly 260 mg and 130 mg a week for an 80 kg man.
[FDA 2005](https://www.fda.gov/media/72309/download)

Muscle biopsies from long-term users look different from non-users:
- **Regeneration markers.** Powerlifters on long-term AAS had larger fibers of every type, more myonuclei per fiber and a higher proportion of central nuclei in the vastus lateralis than drug-free powerlifters. In the trapezius, more of their fibers also expressed developmental myosin. Central nuclei and developmental myosin are markers of fibers that have been regenerating. [Eriksson 2005](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16059740/)
- **No difference in internal nuclei.** In 10 athletes who had used AAS for 5–15 years and 7 who never had, internal nuclei per fiber were the same (median 0.07 in both). Creatine kinase was above the clinical range in 4 of the users. [Yu 2014](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4160183/)

## Testosterone replacement

TRT is a marketing term. There is only exogenous AAS and doses of that. Human male endogenous testosterone production is around 50mg per week. The average clinic will prescribe testosterone enanthate which is an ester so it needs a higher dose to give the same effect as pure testosterone, around 70mg. So when clinics have people going off 100mg per week and up, this is supraphysiological, even assuming an ester with a very long tail (and thus molecular weight)
