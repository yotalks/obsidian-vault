> [!summary] Bottom line
> Some exogenous AAS can aromatize, causing the accumulation of estradiol. In males, this is first and foremost and intracrine process, taking place in specific tissues in the body. The biggest generators of estradiol by aromatization are muscles, adipose tissue, bones, and vasculature. When it's generated by breast adipose tissue, estradiol creates gynecomastia, especially when tissue androgen levels are too low, such as immediately post-cycle. Breaking the rule that accumulation of estradiol is generally an intracrine process, the spillover of estradiol produced by the various tissues does affect the kidney when using aromatizable AAS. Estradiol causes the kidney to retain sodium which can then increase water retention. The water tends to accumulate in the interstitial spaces and creates a subcutaneous bloating effect that's associated with aromatizable AAS.

### What is aromatization

Aromatization is a biochemical process performed by the enzyme aromatase (CYP19A1), a cytochrome P450, on C19 androgens ([Thompson & Siiteri, 1974](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/4370479/)). It involves removing the C19 methyl group and creating a phenolic A-ring. This creates C18 estrogens, including estradiol. Estradiol (17β-estradiol or E2) is the strongest, most common form of estrogen in the human body.

To convert testosterone into estradiol, it needs 3 steps

Standard P450 hydroxylation of the C19 angular methyl, which releases water

Second hydroxylation of C19, which releases water

Oxidative C10-C19 bond cleavage and aromatization of the A-ring (loss of 1β- and 2β-hydrogens), which releases formic acid (including C19).

None of these steps inherently cause problems. The amount of formic acid produced is small and easily handled. But the cytochrome P450 monooxygenase pathway is inherently leaky (like the ETC) and therefore causes a reasonable amount of oxidative stress ([Bondy & Naderi, 1994](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8043018/); [Zangar et al. 2004](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15364547/)). But all in all, the amount of oxidative stress from aromatization from exogenous AAS is not that substantial and can probably be largely discounted WRT being a contributor to the negative side effects of aromatizing androgens. So we focus exclusively on estradiol.

#### Which AAS can be aromatized

Aromatization can only happen in certain AAS, according to their particular chemical format. So we can categorize exogenous AAS as either aromatizable or non-aromatizable.

Aromatizable AAS: The most obvious class of AAS that can be aromatized is testosterone and its esters (enanthate, cypionate, propionate). The amount of aromatization that happens scales with dose fairly linearly. Other common aromatizable AAS include boldenone (Equipoise), nandrolone, and metandione (Dianabol)  ([Nieschlag & Vorona, 2015](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26373946/); [Bond et al. 2022](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36644692/)).

Non-aromatizable AAS: The most obvious class of AAS that can't be aromatized is the DHT derivatives (stanozolol, oxandrolone, and mesterolone) because they're 5a-reduced.

### The intracrine system

#### Estradiol comes from the intracrine system

Aromatization has been reported taking place in multiple spots around the body, including skeletal muscle ([Larionov et al. 2003](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12732294/)), adipose tissue ([Zhao et al. 1995](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7608217/)), bone ([Schweikert et al. 1995](https://europepmc.org/article/med/7641410); [Sasano et al. 1997](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9286757/)), articular chondrocytes ([Sasano et al. 1997](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9286757/)), and endothelial and/or smooth muscle cells ([Sasano et al. 1999](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10460007/)). The specific estradiol could come from two different systems, the intracrine and the endocrine. The endocrine system is used when the estradiol circulates through the blood after producing one tissue and then arrives to another tissue to interact with the estrogen receptor. The intracrine system is used if the tissue itself aromatizes AAS into estradiol locally. We can see in the following examples, estradiol is nearly always produced by the intracrine system in male AAS users. 

Breast tissue: It's true that pre-menopausal women have high circulating estrogen levels, to the point that most of the estrogen arriving at the estrogen receptor comes from the endocrine system. But the intracrine production of estrogen inside the breast is an order of magnitude larger than the endocrine production in post-menopausal women ([Pasqualini et al. 1996](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8636351/)). It's very likely that male AAS users local levels of estradiol are similarly way higher than circulating levels because of intracrine production.

Bones: They have the abiility to convert weaker androgens into stronger androgens using 5a-reductasse ([Bruch et al. 1992](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/1618995/)). They also have the ability to convert androgens into estradiol ([Sasano et al. 1997](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9286757/)). All of the sex hormones that bones need are made locally, as demonstrated by the problems men bace with normal circulating sex hormones but aromatase mutations ([Morishima et al. 1995](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8530621/)). So again, we can be confident that any estradiol mediated effects in bone that are experienced by AAS users will be produced by estradiol that's made locally in the intracrine system.

Vasculature: This definitely has the ability to convert androgens into estradiol ([Sasano et al. 1999](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10460007/)). They're so good at it that they export estradiol into the endocrine system. So any estradiol-mediated effects in the vasculature that are experienced by AAS users will be made by estradiol locally produced by the intracrine system.

Brain: It's relatively predictable that locally-synthesized estrogen will be impossible for influencing neuronal function in everyone. including male AAS users. because the blood-brain barrier limits the endocrine access ([Naftolin et al. 1975](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/812160/); [Azcoitia et al. 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8744447/)).

Testes: While not that relevant for AAS users whom testicular function has ceased due to constant availability of exogenous testosterone, it's worth noting that the testes have their own capacity to make estradiol out of local testosterone ([Nitta et al. 1993](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8440194/)).

It's important to differentiate these two pathways.

It's only valid to use circulating estradiol levels in the bloodstream as a monitoring tool in your blood panel if the estradiol is predominately following the endocrine pathway to reach the tissue you're looking for side effects on. If the estradiol that generates the side effects is mainly following the intracrine pathway, then serum estradiol levels in the blood are telling you nothing useful. In AAS users, the level of circulating estradiol is just the spillover from what the muscle, adipose tissue, and vasculature don't need after they made it with local AAS.

If you're planning to use aromatase inhibitors as a precautionary measure to avoid the risk of gynecomastia, you must know that you're not just turning down a system level thermostat. You're reducing aromatization in every specific tissue individually, and that has tissue-specific effects everywhere, some you don't want. Estrogen has positive effects in the brain, vasculature, and bones. Reducing aromatization in those to very low levels for long durations could create side effects like muscle and joint pain. ([Khan et al. 2010](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20871846/)).

### Estradiol side effects

While estrogen in men does play key roles ([Cooke, 2017](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6151497/); [Hess & Cooke, 2018](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29438493/)), the excess estradiol created by the dose-responsive aromatization of AAS causes side effects. We can subdivide these effects in those mediated through the intracrine production of estradiol

Gynecomastia: AAS enter the breast tissue and then convert locally to estradiol by aromatase in stromal cells ([Sasano et al. 1994](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8200649/); [Zhao et al. 1995](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7608217/); [Majzoub & Canguven, 2026](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42026176/)). Estradiol then activates estrogen receptors, causing downstream signaling processes that cause an expansion of breast tissue. The presence of sufficient androgens can impede this process. The key factor is the local ratio of androgen-to-estrogen.

HPG-axis suppression and infertility: Locally produced estradiol has a strong suppressive effect on GnRH in the brain. This suppresses FSH and LH which leads to Leydig cells turning off intratesticular testosterone production, testicular atrophy, and either azoospermia or severe oligospermia. While estrogen-mediated suppression of the HPG axis isn't really a concern for AAS users while on-cycle (because the androgens are already maximally suppressing the same axis), it does have useful implications for post-cycle therapy if androgens are low and estrogen is still high in specific tissues.

Mood and behavioral effects: Aromatase in the brain converts androgens locally into estrogen through the intracrine system ([Azcoitia et al. 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8744447/)) Subsequent estrogen receptor signaling can then lead to changes in mood and behavior, including anxiety. Estrogen is also supportive of libido ([Schulster et al. 2016](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4854098/)), so trying to overcompensate for aromatizing AAS by using aromatase inhibitors in excess will reduce sex drive.

Side effects mediated through the endocrine production of estradiol are the following.

Water retention: Spillover estradiol from the muscle, adipose tissue, and vasculature enters the kidney and hits estrogen receptors in the nephron. Estradiol increases sodium reabsorption and retention. which then causes water retention by osmosis. That increases total body water, which is stored in the extracellular space (which is comprised of the intravascular and interstitial components). Estradiol also increases capillary hydrostatic pressure, which shifts fluid from the intravascular to the interstitial compartment, and this causes a subcutaneous bloating effect.

Blood pressure: Blood pressure is equal to cardiac output multiplied by peripheral resistance, and an increase in intravascular water leads to increased venous return which leads to increased cardiac output. So, contribution from estradiol's effects on water retention does contribute to increasing blood pressure. The water retention made by estradiol does tend to be more in the interstitial component compared to the intravascular, so its impact on blood pressure is smaller than you'd expect relative to the overall change in body water. Also estradiol stimulates the release of nitric oxide ([Hayashi et al. 1995](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7575554/)), which is a vasodilator and therefore reduces peripheral resistance, and acting to reduce blood pressure. The overall effect of estradiol on blood pressure isn't clear but it's very unlikely the major contributor to any increase caused by AAS even when water retention in severe.

### Other sources of estrogen

Aromatization may not be the only source of estrogens when using AAS. Oxymetholone (Anadrol) is widely claimed to have estrogenic effects when used, but it can't aromatize because its A-ring is saturated (5a-reduced) and aromatase needs the available A-ring geometry to carry out its oxidative process. There is no clear theory to explain this but there are some suggestions.

It acts directly on the androgen receptors: Researchers have suggested that oxymetholone has just enough similarity with an estrogen compound to stimulate the estrogen receptors directly. The proposal has managed to acquire primary status in the scientific community but there's just as little evidence to support it as the other suggestions.

It increases progesterone: Some clinicians argue the bloat and gynecomastia from oxymetholone is explained by its effect on progesterone. But the only data that exists point the opposite way. In vitro rodent data shows oxymetholone blocks the pregnenolone to progesterone conversion ([Ferrari & Arnold, 1963](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/14089410/)) and in vivo rodent work shows it reduces circulating progesterone levels ([Cox et al. 1975](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/1115110/)). Now female rodents obviously may not translate to male AAS users but this is the best evidence we have.

It's being blended with something else: Skeptics argue that the reports regarding estrogenic effects of oxymetholone are being confounded by either deliberate stacking with aromatizing AAS by the user or accidental/deliberate swapping with other AAS by the manufacturer or distributor. 

### Post-cycle gynecomastia

#### The risk of gynecomastia is highest immediately post-cycle

It's possible for AAS users to have strong estrogenic effects (especially gynecomastia) right post-cycle even when they were using non-aromatizable AAS while on-cycle. This is because breast tissue growth is caused by the ratio of estrogens to androgens locally in the tissue, as created by the intracrine system. Breast stroma can make estradiol from any circulating androgens, including weak ones that are sent by the adrenal cortex (androstenedione, DHEA, and DHEAS). Right after a cycle, the androgens that were being provided by the AAS have gone and the endogenous testosterone that should be produced by the testes isn't back yet. This changes the overall androgen to estrogen ratio towards the estrogen side in the breast tissue, regardless of what the circulating levels of testosterone and estradiol are doing.

### Medications

There are medications to help prevent these effects.

Aromatase inhibitors: These reduce the conversion of androgens to estradiol. The biggest problem with using these is that they can reduce estradiol below physiological levels in unwanted areas (like the brain, vasculature, and bones), which can then cause unexpected sides that AAS users misattribute to the androgens. All in all, aromatase inhibitors affect a large number of parts of the body to purely reduce risk of gynecomastia and bloating so it's not a very precise tool at all.

Selective estrogen receptor modulators (SERMs): These block estrogen receptors in breast tissue to avoid gynecomastia but they increase the effects of estrogen elsewhere. So the negative effects on other factors like water retention (and blood pressure) and prostate remodeling are enhanced.

### Bloodwork says nothing about tissue-specific estradiol

Usually estradiol levels are measured in blood panels ([Gibbons et al. 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10962511/)), but this tells you nothing about what's happening in specific tissues. It's often claimed to track your T:E2 ratio to determine your risk of gynecomastia because they hear breast tissue only grows if the ratio of estrogens to androgens is too high. But they have a false assumption that estrogen levels in the bloodstream reflect estrogen levels in the peripheral tissue. The only way that would be true is if the majority of estrogen in breast tissue was sent there by the endocrine system, which is not true. In males, most of it is from the intracrine system.

#### Practical implications

Don't obsess about circulating estradiol WRT gynecomastia: AAS are converted locally into estradiol in various parts of the body that are negatively affected, like the breast adipose tissue. So, what the tissues actually see looks nothing like what's circulating in the bloodstream.

Do look at circulating estrogen to monitor water retention: Spillover estradiol from aromatized AAS in the muscle, adipose tissue, and vasculature reaches the kidney and promotes sodium retention, which then causes water retention by osmosis. So, circulating estradiol levels are very useful for telling us about water retention.

Know that estrogen affects mood and libido: Generally AAS users think their mood issues is due to androgens, but aromatization causing estrogen production in the brain can also contribute. One examples is when aromatase inhibitors are used (based on circulating levels), the user then drops estrogen too far in the brain and losing libido cause of it.
