> [!summary] Bottom line
> AAS cause apoptosis (and other cell death mechanisms). This is important because cell death presents a risk of permanently losing organ tissue, especially when the cells of that organ are permanently differentiated and can't be regenerated (For the heart, brain, and part of the kidney). Signs of apoptosis are also not found in most blood panels.

### What is apoptosis

Apoptosis is programmed cell death, which requires energy in the form of ATP and is often caspase-dependent. In contrast, necrosis is not programmed and doesn't need energy and tends to come with a lot more inflammation (which you can see by the cell contents getting dumped into the bloodstream). While apoptosis and necrosis were once defined as distinct processes, researchers have now found other types of cell death that are in between them, including necroptosis, ferroptosis, and pyroptosis. This new terminology is just recognition that physiology is messy and operates on a gray scale or spectrum rather than clearly defined categories (which is why we see increased cell membrane permeability after apoptosis when that would normally imply the presence of necrosis). A small number of studies have found AAS provoke necrosis, so it's not valid to just say AAS uniquely cause apoptosis and not other cell death types.
[Abu-Shakra 1997](https://europepmc.org/article/med/9008149) · [Rocha 2000 - aldosterone and myocardial necrosis](https://europepmc.org/article/med/11014244) · [El-Halwagy 2016](https://www.academia.edu/download/93674895/ijcep0019825.pdf)

### How AAS cause apoptosis

Some reviewers view apoptosis caused by AAS as the natural downstream cause of oxidative stress, possibly helped by inflammation. And this is true, but we can't limit the impact to just that.

Oxidative stress: AAS are very well documented causes of mitochondria sourced oxidative stress and they deplete antioxidant defenses.
[Lopes 2014](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24658017/) · [Riezzo 2014](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25065671/)
Oxidative stress is a known cause for apoptosis (with or without inflammation).

Inflammation: AAS are also known to upregulate inflammatory processes, both through their actions on oxidative stress but also directly.
[Sellau 2020](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32651360/)
TNF-o elevation especially has been implicated in leading from the inflammatory processes to apoptosis.
[Riezzo 2014](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25065671/) · [Do Nascimento 2015](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25673179/)

Direct androgen effect on apoptosis: Data has shown that AAS can directly cause apoptosis through several mechanisms. This means we can have excessive apoptosis even without oxidative stress or inflammation. These are at least 3 well-defined pathways.

A) Apoptosis signaling pathway activation: Androgen receptor activation by AAS has been shown to cause the cellular signaling pathway that leads right to mitochondrial apoptosis, though the underlying reasons are unclear.
[Zaugg 2001](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11241353/) · [Verzola 2004](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15086464/)

B) Calcium ion overload: AAS directly increase intracellular calcium ions by acting on muscle cell membrane androgen receptors, which leads to mitochondrial overload and the usual calcium ion related cell damage processes.
[Vicencio 2006](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16339199/)
This might be an excessive version of the muscle fiber type shift program that normally occurs when training (Muscle fiber activation leads to calcium ion influx that shifts fiber to more oxidative phenotype). Data in spinal cord injury shows AAS cause both muscle growth and fiber type shifts.
[Ung 2010 - clenbuterol and testosterone in paraplegic mice](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20482256/) · [Wu 2012 - nandrolone after spinal cord injury](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5364642/)

C) Withdrawal of survival signaling: AAS turn off pro-survival ERK signaling, likely through an androgen receptor.
[Basile 2013](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3648690/)
This allows apoptosis to function with less resistance. This may be produced by a competition for energy within the cell, since AAS push everything toward growth signaling pathways.

Apoptosis accelerators: Data often shows AAS alone aren't strong signals for cells to have apoptosis. They more often show that AAS lead to apoptosis when combined with damage from another source (like exercise, ischemia, or growth factor withdrawal in in vitro experiments), which implies that they should likely be considered accelerators of apoptosis rather than instigators.
[Ling 2002](https://www.researchgate.net/profile/Rodney-Dilley/publication/11499597_Testosterone_T_Enhances_Apoptosis-Related_Damage_in_Human_Vascular_Endothelial_Cells/links/0c960527086122862a000000/Testosterone-T-Enhances-Apoptosis-Related-Damage-in-Human-Vascular-Endothelial-Cells.pdf) · [Verzola 2004](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15086464/) · [Crisostomo 2006](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16439666/) · [Riezzo 2014](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25065671/)
While it seems counterintuitive that AAS cause apoptosis after damage when they're supposed to be pro-growth, this might be explained by growth processes being energy-demanding and things like exercise, ischemia, and growth factor withdrawal leading to an energy deficient environment that is damaging even before the AAS show up and start demanding more ATP to build things with.

### Which organs are affected

The apoptosis process is basically the same across most organs we see sides form AAS with, including the heart and vasculature, brain, liver, and kidney. There are small differences in what happens but the main result is basically the same, though what happens after can be quite different.

Heart: Apoptosis of cardiomyocytes has been recorded after AAS use.
[Zaugg 2001](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11241353/) · [Hassan & Kamal 2013](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23559905/)
It appears to occur mainly downstream of oxidative stress, as indicated by its amelioration in the presence of antioxidant support.
[Amini 2025](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39894191/)
Cardiac apoptosis causes a permanent loss of contractile mass that's filled up with collagen.
[Papamitsou 2011](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3560513/)
The heart is an organ you don't want problems with when it comes to the oxidative stress-inflammation-apoptosis axis.

Brain: Neurons are also post-mitotic and display signs of apoptosis causing permanent loss of brain matter.
[Turillazzi 2016](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5125979/) · [Joukar 2017](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28144902/) · [Bjornebekk 2017 - structural brain imaging in long-term users](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27616036/) · [Karimooy 2019](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31026221/) · [Hauger 2019 - brain characteristics of AAS dependence](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6767448/) · [Tabor 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7288073/)
This seems largely downstream of oxidative stress because it can be ameliorated by targeted antioxidant support.
[Shirpoor 2026](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41616897/)
Even then, the reactive astrogliosis process that deals with neuronal damage (including apoptosis) is followed by a reconnecting process that involves trophic factors that support axonal sprouting and synaptogenesis around the damaged area and remaps brain function onto surviving circuits. So there is a functional improvement after apoptosis but no regeneration of neurons. This functional flexibility is why it's difficult to determine if there are brain problems on AAS.

Kidney: The tubular epithelium has very high regenerative capacity but the glomerulus acts like the heart because podocytes are terminally differentiated and can't be replaced. Losing them to apoptosis is what leads to glomerulosclerosis.
[Riezzo 2014](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25065671/)

Liver: Apoptosis of hepatocytes can be regenerated after, but the conditions of high oxidative stress and poisoning from oral compounds could slow this process a lot.
[El-Halwagy 2016](https://www.academia.edu/download/93674895/ijcep0019825.pdf)
But the apoptosis here is less problematic, despite it being what everyone cares about.

Testes: Apoptosis of testes happens because testicular atrophy has been widely recorded after AAS use in a ton of species, including humans. The apoptosis process has also been documented at the mechanistic level in the spermatogenic cells and Leydig cells and appears to be downstream of the oxidative stress-inflammation-apoptosis axis.
[Shokri 2010](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20002066/) · [Janjic 2012](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23085480/) · [Ahmed 2015](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25542992/)

### Apoptosis markers are weakly present in bloodwork

Medical testing practices don't often include apoptosis markers.
[Gibbons 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10962511/)
This is because apoptosis markers aren't available commercially, and are only used for research. There are some blood panel markers that tell us about increases in cell membrane permeability (like ALT, AST, and troponin), which indicate secondary necrosis is happening. Secondary necrosis is downstream of apoptosis, so this is a delayed marker compared to the apoptosis process. It's possible to scan for structural alterations (like testicular volume and reduced sperm count) and functional changes (like urinary ACR, and eGFR, and eGFRcr-cys) to see the upstream impact of apoptosis. But, nobody wants to wait till their kidneys stop working to know their renal cells were dying off for a while.

### Practical implications

Protect mitochondria: One of the main mechanisms upstream of apoptosis is oxidative stress. AAS also have a direct mechanism that cause apoptosis of mitochondria through androgen receptor activation. AAS should do their daily cardio and get good sleep to help and may want proper supplementation.

Blood panels don't say everything: This isn't picked up in bloodwork and the readout for it in bloods is late-stage already.

Protect your heart: Apoptosis causes permanent loss of cardiomyocytes which isn't picked up by blood panel. If you want to protect heart, look into an annual echocardiogram and adding a cardiac MRI every few years to see progression of fibrosis.

 Protect your brain: Apoptosis destroys neurons permanently which doesn't get picked up by bloodwork. You may want to expand blood panel to include metrics used for tracking recovery of traumatic brain injury or concussion for some useful longitudinal data if done yearly.

Protect your kidneys: Apoptosis destroys kidney cells which doesn't get picked up by bloodwork. Ideally you take regular blood pressure measurements at home and log in a spreadsheet with daily measurements in the same location, position, and state every day. IF numbers start going too high on-cycle, it might be an early sign of kidney damage. You can also get a urine test for protein to creatinine ratio or albumin to creatinine ratio to pick up early signs of glomerular permeability which is downstream damage of oxidative stress in kidney.

Exogenous AAS cause apoptosis (and other cell death mechanisms) through multiple pathways and not just through oxidative damage. They're especially good at stimulating this when damage is produced by other causes like exercise or ischemia. Apoptosis causes permanent loss of tissue in the heart, brain, and part of the kidney. The capacity of the liver to regenerate after apoptosis is high but can be impaired by AAS in other regards. At least two of the mechanisms where AAS cause apoptosis involve the mitochondria so it's important to sleep good and do cardio and use proper supplements.
