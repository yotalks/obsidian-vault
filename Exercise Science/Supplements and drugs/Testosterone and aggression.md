> [!summary] Bottom line
> Baseline testosterone correlates with aggression at about r = 0.05 in men, and pooling the experiments that actually raised it gives no significant effect. The best controlled trial, at 600 mg a week, did raise manic and aggression scores on average, but the response was very uneven: 84% of men showed minimal psychiatric effects while 4% became markedly hypomanic. Genetic evidence in 306,248 people finds little effect on risk-taking.

## Correlational evidence

The most comprehensive meta-analysis pooled three designs: baseline concentrations, naturally occurring changes, and experimental manipulation.

| Design                 | Correlation with aggression                  | Men                     | Women          |
| ---------------------- | -------------------------------------------- | ----------------------- | -------------- |
| Baseline testosterone  | r = 0.054 (0.028–0.080)                      | r = 0.071 (0.041–0.101) | r = 0.002 (ns) |
| Change in testosterone | r = 0.108 (0.041–0.174)                      | r = 0.162 (0.076–0.246) | r = 0.010 (ns) |
| Administration         | r = 0.046 (−0.015 to 0.108), not significant |                         |                |

So the causal design gives the weakest estimate of the three, and it's the only one that isn't statistically significant.
[Geniole 2020](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31785281/)

Earlier meta-analyses of the correlational literature reached similar magnitudes.
[Book 2001](https://doi.org/10.1016/S1359-1789(00)00032-X)
[Archer 2005](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.avb.2004.01.001)

A correlation of 0.05 means testosterone accounts for roughly a quarter of one percent of the variance in aggression, which is far too small to carry explanations of sex differences in violence or the age-crime curve.

## The supraphysiological trial

The key experiment for the "roid rage" question gave doses in the range recreational users take, not replacement doses.

In a randomized, placebo-controlled crossover trial, 56 men aged 20–50 received testosterone cypionate rising to 600 mg a week for 6 weeks and placebo for 6 weeks, separated by 6 weeks of no treatment. Testosterone raised manic scores on the Young Mania Rating Scale (p = 0.002), manic scores on daily diaries (p = 0.003), liking of the drug effect (p = 0.008) and aggressive responses on the Point Subtraction Aggression Paradigm (p = 0.03).

The averages hide the important result. Of the 50 men who received 600 mg a week:

| Response | Number | Share |
|---|---|---|
| Minimal psychiatric effects (YMRS < 10) | 42 | 84% |
| Mildly hypomanic (YMRS 10–19) | 6 | 12% |
| Markedly hypomanic (YMRS ≥ 20) | 2 | 4% |

The 8 responders and 42 non-responders didn't differ on any baseline demographic, psychological, laboratory or physiological measure, so there was no way to predict in advance who would react.
[Pope 2000](https://doi.org/10.1001/archpsyc.57.2.133)

This is the shape of the effect: real, dose-dependent, and concentrated in a small and unidentifiable minority rather than spread across users.
→ [[Anabolic Androgenic Steroids]]

## Genetic evidence

Mendelian randomization avoids the reverse-causation problem, since aggression and status can raise testosterone as well as the other way round. Using genetic variants for testosterone in 306,248 UK Biobank participants, there was little evidence that testosterone affected socioeconomic position, self-rated health or risk-taking behavior. The authors noted that the gap between the MR estimates and the ordinary adjusted estimates suggests the observed associations come from residual confounding or reverse causation.
[Harrison 2021](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34321204/)

## Why the observational picture is misleading

Three things inflate the apparent link:

1. **Reverse causation.** Winning, competition and dominance raise testosterone, so cross-sectional differences between violent and non-violent groups can run backwards. The "change in testosterone" row above is larger than the baseline row, which fits a response to challenge rather than a driver of it.
2. **Confounding in user populations.** Elevated offending among anabolic steroid users is heavily confounded by polysubstance use and by selection into use.
3. **Averaging over responders.** As Pope's trial shows, a real effect in 4% of men produces a significant group mean while telling you almost nothing about a given individual.
