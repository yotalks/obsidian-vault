> [!summary] Bottom line
> Steroids grow muscle even without training, and the effect adds to training. But by suppressing collagen breakdown they block tendon adaptation, so muscle force outpaces tendon strength and rupture risk rises, including on TRT.

## Muscle

[[Glossary#AAS|AAS]] increase muscle mass without any training stimulus. Human subjects given AAS for several weeks gain substantial mass even with no strength training program running alongside.
[Bhasin 1996](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8637535/)
[Bhasin 2001](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11701431/)

AAS increase muscle size dose-dependently, and the increase is fiber hypertrophy starting as low as 50 mg per week.
[Sinha-Hikim 2002](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12067856/)

Steroids may also let fibers grow past the size limit that oxidative capacity normally sets, and add new fibers through hyperplasia. Pushing past that limit may raise the risk of rhabdomyolysis.
→ [[Individual differences in muscle growth]]

The two stimuli are additive, not overlapping. Trained subjects on AAS hypertrophy more than untrained subjects on AAS, so training adds on top rather than being substituted for.
[Bhasin 1996](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8637535/)

Animal limb-immobilization work is the cleanest separation of the two. AAS-treated animals grow in the free limbs and in the immobilized limb, but less in the immobilized one. So AAS don't fully rescue atrophy from absent [[Mechanical tension|mechanical tension]],. [[Glossary#MT|MT]] and androgens are independent inputs.
[Taylor 1999](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10569356/)

Higher muscle protein synthesis rates mean damage is repaired faster, which implies AAS users can tolerate higher workout volumes.
[Zhao 2008](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18436443/)

Severe caloric restriction and persistent sleep loss both raise systemic [[Glucocorticoids|glucocorticoids]], which drive oxidative stress inside the fiber and elevate muscle protein breakdown. AAS are glucocorticoid-antagonistic, so users can hold deeper deficits for longer without the atrophy a natural lifter would get.
[Piovesan 2013](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22791621/)

## Tendons and ligaments

Tendons and ligaments fail to adapt properly to the training stimulus under AAS, so collagen strength lags while muscle force output climbs. 
[Kanayama 2015](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5206906/)
→ [[Tendon adaptation]]

Muscle and connective tissue get stronger by different mechanisms. Muscle adds myofibrillar protein. Tendon and ligament stiffen by reorganizing architecture and shifting collagen types, which requires breakdown as well as synthesis.
[Kanayama 2015](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5206906/)

AAS act the same direction in both tissues. AAS raise synthesis and suppress breakdown in muscle (↑[[Glossary#MPS|MPS]], ↓[[Glossary#MPB|MPB]]) and in connective tissue (↑collagen synthesis, ↓collagen breakdown). Since muscle adaptation is construction-limited, suppressed breakdown helps. Since tendon adaptation is breakdown-dependent, suppressed breakdown blocks it. 
[Pärssinen 2000](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10961515/)
[Kanayama 2015](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5206906/)

AAS-user tendons under the microscope show disorganization, fibril misalignment, damage, and fibrosis
[Kanayama 2015](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5206906/)

Rupture risk elevation applies to prescription [[Glossary#TRT|TRT]] as well as recreational bodybuilding doses. 
[Kanayama 2015](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5206906/)

## Connections
**Builds on**
- [[Mechanical tension]] — tension and androgens are separate growth signals
- [[Glucocorticoids]] — steroids let users diet harder because they oppose glucocorticoids
- [[Individual differences in muscle growth]] — fiber size is normally capped by oxidative capacity, which steroids may push past
- [[Tendon adaptation]] — tendons stiffen only with heavy loads, and stiffness protects against rupture, which steroids interfere with
