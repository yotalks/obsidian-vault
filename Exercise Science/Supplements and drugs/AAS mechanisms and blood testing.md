## AAS side effects

People usually focus on the presence of clearly detectable side effects (like acne, bad sleep, irritable mood, sexual dysfunction, infertility, testicular atrophy, and gynecomastia), and indications provided by blood markers. These are very useful sources of information, but looking at the physiology of AAS side effects, we can add a lot more information. We can find the drivers of all of these problems and arrive at a series of mechanisms responsible for each group of side effects. We can then target any given problem driver with the appropriate supplementation. 

Also neither side effects nor bloodwork capture everything so just utilizing those would miss things that could be going on.

We can think of it as a 3-part sequence of mechanism to structural effect to functional outcome. If we catch a problem at its functional or even structural stage, that isn't great. We want to prevent or at least monitor the mechanisms upstream of those stages.

First the AAS arrives.

It then stimulates side effect mechanisms: There are 8 different molecular/cellular processes (oxidative stress, inflammation, androgen receptor overactivation, endocrine disruption, aromatization, hepatoxicity, and impaired collagen remodeling). These aren't completely distinct from one another and they do overlap and connect. Each one of these pathways can generate a subsequent structural alteration.

The side effect mechanisms then create structural alterations in 3 categories.

Damage (including fibrosis): The oxidative stress, inflammation, and apoptosis (and necrosis) pathway typically lead to permanent, structural damage in many tissues when it's incorrectly repaired, especially when the damage is combined with a poor collagen remodeling response. This includes the brain, heart, kidney, and liver. Cholestatic injury has this same potential for lasting damage in the liver.

Atrophy and loss: Hormonal suppression causes testicular shrinkage; hair loss occurs, apoptosis can lead to atrophy when it doesn't lead to permanent damage. 

Hypertrophy: Androgen receptor driven growth in the heart, kidneys, prostate, and bone.

Structural alterations create functional problems: The structural alterations lead to functional problems that are observable like altered left ventricular ejection fraction and global longitudinal strain (GLS), blood pressure, estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR), spermatogenesis, mood, erectile function, and libido.

### 8 mechanisms AAS cause sides

Oxidative stress (damages stuff): AAS increase levels of reactive oxygen species (ROS) by disturbing mitochondrial respiratory chain function and by upregulating both NADPH oxidase and cytochrome P450 monooxygenase. Physiological androgens normally participate in these pathways, so supraphysiological levels disrupt the normal processes. Excessive ROS impact negatively on pretty much all organs in the body.

Inflammation (clears away damaged stuff): AAS promote the release of pro-inflammatory cytokines. Like oxidative stress, excessive levels of pro-inflammatory cytokines impact negatively on most organs in the body.

Apoptosis (makes things die): AAS increase the tendency for damaged cells to choose apoptosis rather than repair. Testosterone naturally shifts cells in this direction, so supraphysiological levels of the same hormone would increase this tendency. While many organs can be affected, the most problematic in this regard are the heart, brain, and kidney. You don't want these cells to be lost as they can't be replaced later.

Androgen receptor over-activation (changes things): AAS drives growth by their actions on androgen receptors in skeletal muscle, the heart, and the kidney. They also compel an increase in the production of red blood cells in the bone marrow by their actions on the androgen receptors in the liver and kidney. Even so, the actions on androgen receptors aren't limited to just growth, this is also the category of mechanisms that are responsible for negative skin reactions, hair loss, and fat loss. 

Endocrine disruption (causes hormonal feedback): AAS exert a strong negative feedback on the hypothalamic-pituitary-gonadal (HPG) axis. When exogenous AAS are present, this removes the natural signals to the testes, causing testicular atrophy and suppressing natural testosterone production. When this happens, the testes shrink and sperm count drops.

Aromatization (changes things differently): Aromatizable AAS can be converted in various tissues to estradiol. This estradiol can lead to gyno, water retention, and other things.

Hepatotoxicity (direct poisoning of the liver): Oral AAS can't be easily oxidized by the liver, so they sit there, causing toxic effects that can  include cholestasis (altered bile acid synthesis and transport). The liver actually has a really good regenerative capacity and other organs are far more worth worrying about (that don't get monitored as much).

Impaired collagen remodeling (stops tendons adapting): → [[AAS organ and tissue effects#Proposed mechanisms|Tendon adaptation mechanism]]

## What standard testing measures

### Oxidative stress

- Nothing is directly measured.
- Indirectly measured through [[#GGT (y-glutamyl transferase)|GGT]], [[#Bilirubin|bilirubin]], [[#Albumin|albumin]], [[#Uric acid|uric acid]], [[#Ferritin (and related measures)|ferritin]].
- Fibrosis is a structural/functional change currently tracked through [[#Liver elastography and liver ultrasound|liver elastography]].

### Inflammation

- Nothing directly measures this.
- It's indirectly measured through [[#hs-CRP|hs-CRP]], [[#Erythrocyte sedimentation rate (ESR)|ESR]], full blood count with neutrophil to lymphocyte and platelet to lymphocyte ratios, [[#Ferritin (and related measures)|ferritin]].
- Fibrosis is a structural/functional change currently tracked through [[#Liver elastography and liver ultrasound|liver elastography]].

### Apoptosis

- Nothing directly measures this.
- It's indirectly measured by [[#ALT (alanine aminotransferase)|ALT]]/[[#AST (aspartate aminotransferase)|AST]], [[#GGT (y-glutamyl transferase)|GGT]], [[#CK|CK]], [[#hstroponin (high sensitivity cardiac troponin)|hs-troponin]].
- Structural/functional changes currently tracked are testicular volume, reduced sperm count, [[#Urine albumin to creatinine ratio (uACR)|urinary ACR]], estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR), eGFRcr-cys (eGFR using creatinine and cystatin C)

### Androgen receptor over-activation

- This isn't directly measured by anything.
- It's indirectly measured by [[#Hematocrit and hemoglobin|hematocrit and hemoglobin]], [[#DHT (dihydrotestosterone)|DHT]], [[#PSA (prostate specific antigen) panel|PSA panel]], and [[#Left ventricular hypertrophy by echocardiogram|echocardiogram]].
- Structural/functional changes are currently tracked through [[#Hematocrit and hemoglobin|hematocrit and hemoglobin]], [[#Blood pressure|blood pressure]], left ventricular hypertrophy by [[#Left ventricular hypertrophy by echocardiogram|echocardiogram]], [[#Urine albumin to creatinine ratio (uACR)|urinary ACR]], eGFR, eGFRcr-cys, lipid panel, prostate enlargement, acne, hair loss, and mood changes

### Endocrine dysfunction

- This is directly measured by [[#Testosterone, luteinizing hormone (LH), and follicle-stimulating hormone (FSH)|total testosterone]], SHBG, LH, FSH, [[#Anterior pituitary lactotroph hormone (prolactin)|prolactin]], inhibin b, AMH, semen analysis, TSH/free T4/free T3, IGF-1, AM cortisol, fasting insulin.
- It's indirectly measured through HbA1c (GH/glucose axis), HOMA-IR, continuous glucose monitoring, ApoB, Lp(a), [[#Uric acid|uric acid]].
- Structural/functional changes are tracked by lipids, urea and electrolytes, testicular atrophy, sperm count, libido, erectile dysfunction, fatigue, hot flashes, night sweats, mood changes, water retention, and sleep quality.

### Aromatization

- This is directly measured by nothing.
- It's indirectly measured by [[#Estradiol (E2 or 17β-estradiol)|estradiol]], [[#Estradiol (E2 or 17β-estradiol)|estradiol]] by mass-spectrometry, estrone, [[#Estrone (E1) and estrone sulphate|estrone sulphate]], [[#T:E2 (total testosterone to estradiol ratio)|T:E2 ratio]].
- Structural/functional changes currently tracked are gyno and nipple sensitivity, water retention, [[#Blood pressure|blood pressure]], mood changes, reduced libido, ED, and increased fat mass.

### Hepatotoxicity

- This is directly measured through [[#ALT (alanine aminotransferase)|ALT]], [[#AST (aspartate aminotransferase)|AST]], [[#Bilirubin|bilirubin]], [[#ALP (alkaline phosphatase)|ALP]], [[#Albumin|albumin]], [[#GGT (y-glutamyl transferase)|GGT]], [[#AFP (alpha-fetoprotein)|AFP]].
- It's not indirectly measured by anything.
- Structural/functional changes are tracked by fibrosis (by [[#Liver elastography and liver ultrasound|liver elastography]]), steatosis (by [[#Liver elastography and liver ultrasound|liver ultrasound]]), jaundice, dark urine, pale stools, nausea, and fatigue.

### Impaired collagen remodeling

- This is directly measured by nothing.
- It's indirectly measured through tendon ruptures and tendinopathy.

## Blood markers and imaging

### GGT (y-glutamyl transferase)

GGT is a membrane-bound enzyme that catalyzes the first step of glutathione degradation, which is a necessary step for glutathione resynthesis. While it's usually used in a liver panel to detect cholestasis or biliary obstruction, it'll also indicate severe oxidative stress since intracellular glutathione is one of the body's principal antioxidants. Elevated GGT indicates that the cells are making more glutathione in order to deal with oxidative stress. 

For this reason, it correlates reasonably well with lipid peroxidation markers and has been proposed as a proxy marker.
[Lee 2004](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15346644/)
[Lim 2004](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15336318/)

Yet, GGT can also be upregulated due to cholestasis by 17a-alkylated oral AAS. Therefore, it's more likely to be a marker of liver toxicity.

Though, it doesn't discriminate between oxidative stress, inflammation, or liver toxicity.

### Bilirubin

This is a natural antioxidant. Depending on the context, very low levels imply a reduced level of protection against oxidative stress, while very high levels can mean that the antioxidant system is working hard to deal with oxidative stress or cholestasis by 17a-alkylated compounds. Therefore, it's more likely to be a marker of liver toxicity.

### Albumin

Falling albumin signals reduced antioxidant capacity, systemic inflammation, and liver function. So, it's a very indirect marker and usually taken to indicate liver health rather than the presence of oxidative stress. This could be generated by oxidative stress, inflammation, or liver toxicity, though.

### Uric acid

While a product of purine metabolism, uric acid is created by xanthine oxidase, which produces superoxide as a byproduct. Therefore, higher levels of uric acid implicitly indicate increased oxidative stress (although uric acid itself behaves differently within tissues and within the bloodstream). Even then, it can also be elevated by strength training, high protein diets, and kidney issues, which makes it an unreliable marker for oxidative stress in bodybuilders.

### Ferritin (and related measures)

Ferritin is the intracellular iron-storage protein and free iron catalyzes the conversion of hydrogen peroxide into hydroxyl radicals. Therefore, it's suggestive of oxidative stress, yet, it also increases with inflammation so it can be unreliable. Its levels rise and fall naturally in tandem with inflammatory responses.

### Liver elastography and liver ultrasound

Liver stiffness testing by elastography can be used to estimate fibrosis. Fibrosis is a common downstream effect of oxidative stress and inflammation (as well as liver toxicity due to 17a-alkylated oral AAS use). But common liver stiffness elastography measures can be confounded by heart pathologies, which can lead to incorrect diagnoses.

Liver ultrasound can be used to detect steatosis (lipid accumulation).

### hs-CRP

This is made by liver cells as part of an inflammatory response, so it's often used as the standard inflammation marker (though everyone has their unique hs-CRP level, so it's best to measure your personal baseline). It can also be affected by AAS themselves as well as by exercise, illness, and inflammatory responses to injections. That makes it a lot more unreliable than people think.

### Erythrocyte sedimentation rate (ESR)

This is the ratet erythrocytes fall through plasma, thereby measuring fibrinogen and immunoglobulin. Though, it is suppressed by an increase in RBC counrt, which is a very common side effect of AAS use. Normal ESR can be recorded in the presence of high hematocrit while there is still a high level of inflammation. This makes the marker unreliable.

### Neutrophil to lymphocyte and platelet to lymphocyte ratios

These measures are taken from full blood count tests and calculate the difference between innate (neutrophil) or platelet and adaptive (lymphocyte) immune activation. An elevated ratio indicates either increased neutrophils/platelets and/or decreased lymphocytes. This implies elevated inflammation, infection, or trauma. Though, lymphocyte counts fall with elevated cortisol as a result of stress, which can be a confounder.

### ALT (alanine aminotransferase)

This is a liver enzyme linked with membrane permeability or rupture. Though, it can easily be elevated by exercise.
[Pettersson 2008](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17764474/)

It can only really be interpreted either after a break from strength training or in combination with the more muscle specific creatine kinase (CK) marker.
[Gibbons 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10962511/)

It can also be elevated by 17a-alkylated oral AAS in which case it's more likely a marker of liver toxicity.

Even if it can be directly attributed to AAS, it's not clear which mechanism is responsible (liver toxicity or cellular apoptosis).

### AST (aspartate aminotransferase)

This is another liver enzyme linked with membrane permeability or rupture that's easily affected by exercise, cardiac damage, 17a-alkylated oral AAS, and alcohol consumption.
[Pettersson 2008 (exercise effects)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17764474/)

It's impossible to identify which mechanism is responsible (liver toxicity or cellular apoptosis).

### CK

This is a blood marker used to track the presence of skeletal muscle damage post-exercise (or rhabdomyolysis, in severe cases). If CK is normal but ALT/AST is high, then that is usually a sign of hepatic damage (which would likely lead to apoptosis).

### hstroponin (high sensitivity cardiac troponin)

These are cardiac specific isoforms that track ongoing leakage from myocytes and therefore cellular injury (which would likely lead to apoptosis). Like ALT/AST, they can be elevated by intense exercise.

### Urine albumin to creatinine ratio (uACR)

This detects the presence of albumin in urine as the primary method for detecting kidney damage.

### Hematocrit and hemoglobin

Hematocrit is the percentage of total blood volume comprised of red blood cells (relative to plasma, white blood cells, and platelets) while hemoglobin measures the oxygen-carrying capacity of the blood. Since AAS act on the androgen receptors to stimulate the creation of RBCs (erythropoiesis), elevated levels are very good indicators of localized androgen receptor activity in the vasculature. But, the measure itself can be elevated by diuretics, and reduced by both water retention (common side effect of some AAS) and blood donation (common treatment by AAS users). After taking these factors into account, it's a valuable androgen receptor-specific marker.

### Lipid panel components

Provide a functional metabolic output of androgen exposure.

### Blood pressure

An important reading, blood pressure is frequently elevated by sodium and water retention caused by aromatized estradiol. Though, it can also be increased by enhanced RBC production caused by androgen receptor activity and pathological renal hypertrophy by androgen receptors. So, it's elevated by at least 2 mechanistic causes.

### DHT (dihydrotestosterone)

This is a metabolite of testosterone, produced by 5a-reductase activity. The blood panel measure attempts to establish whole-body DHT exposure but most of the DHT that actually acts on the androgen receptor targets in the prostate, brain, skin, hair follicles through the intracrine mechanisms (i.e. the circulating AAS are converted into DHT inside the tissues and not beforehand). Also, any DHT-derived AAS will not be detected as DHT though it'll act similarly in those tissues. That makes the blood panel marker very different than the tissue-specific effects and not as informative.

### PSA (prostate specific antigen) panel

PSA expression is regulated by its androgen receptors and this metric captures the intracrine effects through which the prostate makes DHT locally out of circulating androgens. This makes it many times more informative than blood panel DHT levels. So it's a very valuable androgen receptor-specific marker.

### Left ventricular hypertrophy by echocardiogram

This is one of the most important tests.

### Testosterone, luteinizing hormone (LH), and follicle-stimulating hormone (FSH)

While testosterone is typically supraphysiologically elevated while on cycle and LH/FSH are droppsed, the opposite is the case when off-cycle. Only when AAS levels drop back to below the previous endogenous baseline will the brain be stimulated to produce LH/FSH to increase testicular function. Even TRT doses of AAS will push LH and FSH down to minimal levels which is why doctors don't even check it. They're only relevant when coming off cycle to see when the anterior pituitary gland has started produing LH again.

### Anterior pituitary lactotroph hormone (prolactin)

This is a peptide hormone primarily secreted by the anterior pituitary gland that stimulates breast tissue development and can have effects on mood, libido, and ED. It has a very wide range of inputs, which means it can be affected by many things, which makes it difficult to interpret.

### Estradiol (E2 or 17β-estradiol)

Estradiol is the strongest estrogen and in men it's normally converted from testosterone (and can also be converted from AAS). But, most of the estradiol that actually acts on the androgen receptor targets in breast tissue produced through the intracrine mechanisms. This is why aromatase inhibitor dosages can often underestimate or overshoot what is required at the tissue level when they're based off blood panels. Going for mass-spectrometry over immunoassay makes sense in males due to the very low levels of this hormone.

### Estrone (E1) and estrone sulphate

Estrone is the weaker estrogen. Breast tissue uses steroid sulfatase to convert estrone sulphate back into active estrone and estradiol. So, high levels of these estrogens are additional strong risk factors for gyno when AAS and/or testosterone levels are low (which is often the case post-cycle).

### T:E2 (total testosterone to estradiol ratio)

This is often taken as the principal measurement when assessing the risk for gyno. Though, the external estrogen input into breast tissue comprises of both E1 and E2, which means the test is underestimating the estrogenic input. Also, breast tissue converts tons of AAS into E2 locally through intracrine methods, which don't get detected anywhere in bloodwork. So this metric is wrong for these reasons and shouldn't be used.

### ALP (alkaline phosphatase)

This can indicate liver cell membrane damage but also bone osteoblast activity (which is typically elevated by AAS through androgen receptor activity). So, it can be a marker of liver toxicity or androgen receptor activity in bone.

### AFP (alpha-fetoprotein)

This is typically used for early detection of hepatic adenomas, which is a liver specific problem that doesn't reflect other sources of liver damage.

## What standard testing doesn't measure

### Oxidative stress, inflammation, apoptosis

These related mechanisms are completely ignored by most. They're also more difficult to monitor. 
Oxidative stress is the worst as there's nothing outside of research grade labs that will get you anywhere close to seeing this happening on a mechanistic level. 
Apoptosis is similarly difficult to identify until it reaches the secondary necrosis phase and cells start leaking enzymes.
inflammation has more capacity for detection and there may be novel tests from related medical areas that could be applied to AAS users.

### Structural and functional measures

Though oxidative stress, inflammation, and apoptosis is pretty much undetectable at the top level, there are various downstream structural measurements for the heart (like left ventricular hypertrophy by echocardiogram and cardiac MRI) as well as functional derivatives (like left ventricular ejection fraction and others) from those scanning methods. There are also downstream functional measures for the kidney like urinary ACR, eGFR, and eGFRcr-cys and liver (elastography and ultrasound). While those are in the territory of advanced user at the moment, not all are financially unobtainable and should definitely be considered for long term AAS users. There's still nothing structural or functional for the brain, though, which is just as badly affected, as indicated by changes in mood and behavior often observed in AAS users.

### Impaired connective tissue remodeling

There are also no current tests as the mechanistic, structural, or functional level that are being used to track this problem.
→ [[AAS organ and tissue effects#Proposed mechanisms|Tendon adaptation mechanism]]

→ [[#Hematocrit and hemoglobin]] · [[#PSA (prostate specific antigen) panel]] · [[#DHT (dihydrotestosterone)]]

### Tissue specific estrogen receptor activity

→ [[#Estradiol (E2 or 17β-estradiol)]] · [[#Estrone (E1) and estrone sulphate]] · [[#T:E2 (total testosterone to estradiol ratio)]]

Exogenous AAS produce side effects through a range of separate mechanisms, some of which are very hard to detect. Entry-level medical monitoring misses a vast proportion of them. Top level effects of oxidative stress, inflammation, and apoptosis are very poorly covered by common testing practices. This means early warning of severe damage isn't possible and the first time the AAS user realizes that there's a problem will be either when they get a structural imaging scan or a functional test or if they have negative symptoms, that causes them to visit a doctor. AAS testing practices also don't detect anything WRT connective tissues. The problems are basically only detected at the point of injury. Androgen and estrogen receptor effects are driven by intracrine function, which means they make the relevant hormone inside the tissue that's affected.

## Oxidative stress

AAS produce excessive amounts of oxidative stress that is very damaging to all cells. This often leads to inflammatory responses, secondary damage, and apoptosis (or necrosis).
[Basile 2013 - toxicity in neuron-like cells](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3648690/)

In many organs, it'll manifest in the loss of healthy tissue that's then often replaced by collagen (fibrosis).

Studies routinely show that AAS elevate the production of ROS or produce lipid peroxidation in a variety of different animal species. This appears to happen through three mechanisms, though mitochondrial dysfunction appears to be the most important which has important practical implications for management of this category of side effects with supplementation.

### Mitochondrial dysfunction

Mitochondria make ATP using the electron transport chain which comprises a series of protein complexes. Unsurprisingly, the electron transport chain (ETC) transports electrons towards oxygen. If electrons escape this process, they can react with any available oxygen to form superoxide, which then reacts with other molecules in the muscle cell to create other ROS. Anything that allows or damages the ETC increases the ability of electrons to escape the process and therefore increases the production of ROS. AAS decrease the activity of ETC protein complexes and damage the mitochondria leading to excess ROS production.
[Molano 1999 - rat liver mitochondrial enzymes](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10063813/)
[Pey 2003 - rat liver](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/14698208/)
[Dornelles 2017 - rat liver and kidneys](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27896593/)
[Carteri 2021 - mouse heart and liver mitochondria](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33984388/)

When ROS are produced in the vicinity of mitochondria, they attack the PUFA in the mitochondrial membranes, which further impairs the ETC, creating a positive feedback loop.
[Pey 2003 - rat liver](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/14698208/)
[Carteri 2021 - mouse heart and liver mitochondria](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33984388/)

### NADPH oxidase upregulation

Unlike mitochondria, which create superoxides unintentionally when the ETC stops working perfectly, NADPH oxidases are enzymes that create superoxides deliberately. They transfer electrons from NADPH acrosss a membrane to oxygen. AAS has been found to increase NADPH-dependent hydrogen peroxide production in the liver and heart as well as in deep fat tissue, which as a high density of androgen receptors.
[Frankenfeld 2014 - rat liver, heart and kidney](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4165578/)
[Magalhães 2020 - rat retroperitoneal fat](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32712213/)

Indeed, NADPH oxidase activity could be upregulated by AAS actions on androgen receptors in the various tissues, but this hasn't been demonstrated definitively. Even so, it's clear that the activity of this normal cellular function is increased when AAS are present, leading to greater ROS production.

### Cytochrome P450-mediated metabolism

Cytochrome P450 monoxygenasses are enzymes that carry out oxidative metabolism of anything bad that gets in our body. By inserting oxygen into the offending substanes, they make it water-soluble and make it easier to excrete.
[Saborido 1993 - rat hepatic drug metabolism](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8350704/)

Its electron transport process is inherently leaky even when working properly, so it tends to cause a lot of ROS whenever it's used.
[Bondy & Naderi 1994 - hepatic microsomal ROS](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8043018/)

17a-alkylated oral AAS (like stanozlol, methyltestosterone, oxymetholone) are modified versions of testosterone with an extra carbon group that helps them resist degradation and allows oral bioavailability. This same extra carbon grups means they pass through cytochrome P450-mediated metabolism multiple times until they're excreted, which causes a lot of oxidative stress.

## Antioxidant defenses

The literature also shows that AAS negatively affect antioxidant defenses in a variety of animal species and in several different organs. This appears to happen though 3 different mechanisms

### Inadequate upregulation

When faced with the oxidative stress caused by AAS, the antioxidant system either tries to respond but can't, attempts a response that is too weak to do anything meaningful, or attempts a response that is capable to begin but can't handle the sheer volume of oxidative stress from the AAS.
[Camiletti-Moirón 2015 - rat brain](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26252547/)
[Frankenfeld 2014 - rat liver, heart and kidney](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4165578/)
[Abed & Al-Azawi 2020 - rabbit brain](https://www.researchgate.net/profile/Sabea-Abed/publication/363872189_THE_PROTECTIVE_EFFECTS_OF_THE_ORGANIC_SELENIUM_HIGH-SELENIUM_YEAST_ON_THE_OXIDATIVE_STRESS_CAUSED_BY_METHANEDIENONE_ON_THE_BRAIN_OF_MALE_RABBITS/links/633303fa694dbe4bf4c64f3d/THE-PROTECTIVE-EFFECTS-OF-THE-ORGANIC-SELENIUM-HIGH-SELENIUM-YEAST-ON-THE-OXIDATIVE-STRESS-CAUSED-BY-METHANEDIENONE-ON-THE-BRAIN-OF-MALE-RABBITS.pdf)
[Patil & Inamdar 2017 - mouse brain and kidneys](https://www.academia.edu/download/56086052/Antioxidant_AJPHR-507010_5927.pdf)
[Joksimović 2017 - rat hippocampus and behavior](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11010-017-3014-0)
[Moraes 2026 - rat cardiac remodeling and function](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12782376/)

These findings imply that oxidative stress induced damage will build over time, both within cycles and over years of AAS use.

### Suppression

AAS can also actively suppress the antioxidant system to levels below seen in tissues not treated with AAS.
[Sadowska-Krępa 2020 - rat liver antioxidant defenses](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7680624/)
[Pey 2003 - rat liver](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/14698208/)
[Riezzo 2014 - mouse kidneys](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25065671/)
[Dornelles 2017 - rat liver and kidneys](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27896593/)
[De Lima 2020 - female rat kidneys](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31881226/)
[Mehrabi 2020 - reduced kidney GPx in trained rats](https://www.researchgate.net/profile/Ali-Gorzi-2/publication/353173704_The_Effect_of_Eight_Weeks_of_Testosterone_Enanthate_Consumption_on_Oxidative_Indicators_of_Kidney_Tissue_in_Resistance_Trained_Rats/links/61aa8630ca2d401f27c236eb/The-Effect-of-Eight-Weeks-of-Testosterone-Enanthate-Consumption-on-Oxidative-Indicators-of-Kidney-Tissue-in-Resistance-Trained-Rats.pdf)

Paradoxically, mitochondrial damage or suppression may partially underpin the problem, since antioxidant defenses usually rely on the mitochondrial functions.
[Pey 2003 - rat liver](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/14698208/)

This category of mechanisms probably underpins the prior reversibility of antioxidant defenses under certain circumstances.

### Non-enzymatic depletion

Small molecule antioxidants like glutathione (GSH) are depleted faster than they can be resynthesized in tissues exposed to AAS.
[Patil & Inamdar 2017 - mouse brain and kidneys](https://www.academia.edu/download/56086052/Antioxidant_AJPHR-507010_5927.pdf)
[Bueno 2017 - rat cortex, hippocampus and behavior](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5464548/)
[Abed & Al-Azawi 2020 - rabbit brain](https://www.researchgate.net/profile/Sabea-Abed/publication/363872189_THE_PROTECTIVE_EFFECTS_OF_THE_ORGANIC_SELENIUM_HIGH-SELENIUM_YEAST_ON_THE_OXIDATIVE_STRESS_CAUSED_BY_METHANEDIENONE_ON_THE_BRAIN_OF_MALE_RABBITS/links/633303fa694dbe4bf4c64f3d/THE-PROTECTIVE-EFFECTS-OF-THE-ORGANIC-SELENIUM-HIGH-SELENIUM-YEAST-ON-THE-OXIDATIVE-STRESS-CAUSED-BY-METHANEDIENONE-ON-THE-BRAIN-OF-MALE-RABBITS.pdf)
[Magalhães 2020 - rat retroperitoneal fat](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32712213/)
[Hassan 2023 - rat cardiac and skeletal muscle](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10015925/)
[Memudu & Dongo 2023 - rat kidneys](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9996376/)

This category of mechanisms is relatively reversible after short AAS blocks and will likely respond well to supplementation with exogenous N-acetyl cysteine (NAC), which can directly replenish the pool of GSH.
[Memudu & Dongo 2023 - rat kidneys](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9996376/)
[Kerksick & Willoughby 2005 - NAC and glutathione review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2129149/)

## Where the damage occurs

Across the data, the largest number of studies have tracked thhrough the chain of oxidative stres induced damage leading to structural pathology in the heart, brain, kidney, and liver. The heart and brain are most vulnerable to oxidative stresss because they have limited regeneration cppacity. The kidney has some ability to regenerate in certain parts and the liver has a very high regeneration capacity.

### Heart

Pretty much every study has recorded markers of oxidative stress in the heart after AS ussage has been declared at least one positive result.
[Frankenfeld 2014 - rat liver, heart and kidney](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4165578/)
[Carteri 2021 - mouse heart and liver mitochondria](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33984388/)
[Hassan 2023 - rat cardiac and skeletal muscle](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10015925/)
[Hussain 2024 - rat cardiac injury](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38529199/)
[Moraes 2026 - rat cardiac remodeling and function](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12782376/)

Such sings are frequently accompanied by signs of damage, inflammation, and necrosis and fibrosis in ventricular tissue, which can likely explain the reductions in heart function.
[Hassan 2023 - rat cardiac and skeletal muscle](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10015925/)

## Antioxidant supplements

AAS cause tons of oxidative stress at the mitochondria which means there are two aspects to the problem, the quantity of oxidative stress is large and it's surgically directed to a specific part of the body. These make conventional scavenger type antioxidants mostly useless.

Scavenger type antioxidants (like most polyphenols and vitamin C and E) neutralize oxidative stress by spending their own molecultes to neutralize individual free radicals. That means they need to be present in the right tissues in sufficient concentrations, constantly. But their standard means of distribution after consuming orally involves being dispersed across the entire body, so it gets spread very thin, which limits their effectiveness against AAS-induced oxidative stress which tends to be focused around the mitochondria.

And lower levels of oxidative stress have positive functions in smaller quantities in other tissues, which presents a problem. It's likely that if you had high dosages with these scavenger type antioxidants, you would end up suppressing these positive functions (because they can deal with the lower levels) while leaving the negative functions of the AAS-induced high levels of oxidative stress in the mitochondria.

That's before we talk about the fact that these antioxidants don't cross the blood-brain barrier to deal with the oxidative stress in the mitochondria of that organ.

Exceptions include exogenous NAC because it's directly fighting the 3rd mechanism where AAS negatively affect antioxidant defenses, selenium and/or sulforaphane because they target the 1st and 2nd mechanisms by which AAS negatively affect antioxidant defenses, and mitochondrial-targeted antioxidants (like CoQ10 and MitoQ) since they solve the location problem and deliver the antioxidant right to the problem site. But these are industrial strength options that you don't want to mess dosages up on.

### AAS actively damage mitochondria

While producing oxidative stress, AAS has been shown to damage mitochondria.
[Carteri 2021 - mouse heart and liver mitochondria](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33984388/)

This is a central element of the oxidative stress problem produced by AAS because of the multiple positive feedback loops that perpetuate the issue, it's therefore a major concern for those using AAS, who want to look carefully at ways to protect the mitochondria when they're on cycle (the two main ones being aerobic exercise and good sleep hygiene). Mitochondrial damage appears to happen through two interconnected mechanisms.

#### Calcium ion influx

AAS has been shown to directly influence the influx of calcium ions into various cells, which then triggers damage in various structures and organelles, including the mitochondria.
[Vicencio 2011 - intracellular calcium signaling review](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21443511/)

 This may be what suppresses activity in the mitochondrial ETC and causes the mitochondria to leak electrons, which produces oxidative stress.
[Agriesti 2020 - HepG2 mitochondrial respiration](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32041983/)

#### Oxidative stress

After AAS damage the mitochondria by calcium ion influx, causing them to leak electrons, this produces oxidative stress. This oxidative stress contributes to further mitochondrial damage because it attacks the lipid membranes of the organelles, which creates a positive feedback loop.
[Pey 2003 - rat liver](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/14698208/)

There is another positive feedback loop in the form of reduced antioxidant defenses because they rely heavily upon the mitochondria in order to properly function, so the more mitochondria are damaged, the more oxidative stress they create and the weaker the antioxidant defenses are.

### Oxidative stress isn't tested

Medical testing practices don't often include oxidative stress markers. This is because oxidative stress markers aren't commercially available and only used for research. There are some blood panel markers that give limited indirect insight into the presence of oxidative stress in the body (like GGT, bilirubin, albumin, uric acid, and ferritin). It's possible to scan for structural changes typically found downstream of oxidative stress (like cardiac and liver fibrosis, which can be seen with cardiac MRI scans and liver elastography). But obviously downstream structural changes (irreversible in this case) aren't a good method of discovering you've been having a ton of oxidative stress in your organs.

### Practical implications

We can derive the following suggestions for an AAS user

#### Protect your mitochondria

AAS cause oxidative stress primarily by producing mitochondrial dysfunction that damages multiple organs, including the heart, liver, kidney, and brain. AAS users need to ensure they're doing daily cardio as well as having proper sleep hygiene. Both are ways to boost mitochondrial strength and we might expect certain supplements that support mitochondrial function to help

#### Don't use useless antioxidants

AAS literally have mitochondria with oxidative stress 24/7 so a bit of vitamin C isn't doing anything to help and there are more robust options that make a difference.

#### Blood panels don't say everything

AAS-induced oxidative stress in not picked up in standard bloodwork, so AAS users aren't getting any warning of oxidative stress-induced damage to their organs. The measures currently being taken for liver indicate (primary or secondary) necrosis caused by cell membrane failure, and not oxidative stress. Getting a good blood panel report for your liver doesn't mean nothing bad is happening.

#### Protect your heart

AAS mediated oxidative stress damages the heart but none of the damage gets picked up in a blood panel. If you want to actually monitor your heart, you need regular directed testing of the heart. An annual echocardiogram gives a clear dataset that shows signs of pathological progression over time. Adding a cardiac MRI every couple of years also adds the ability to detect the progression of fibrosis.

#### Protect your brain

None of the oxidative stress damage by AAS on the brain is picked up by bloodwork and unlike there heart, there are no real testing methods. The obvious option is to expand the blood panel to include metrics that are currently used for tracking recovery after traumatic brain injury or concussion (not useful for benchmarking against other people but can give useful longitudinal data if done yearly).

#### Protect your kidneys

The kidney damage from oxidative stress isn't picked up by bloodwork. The best things you can do are to measure blood pressure regularly and create a spreadsheet with your daily measurements (ideally taken in the same spot, position, and state everyday). If the numbers start going up too high on cycle, that might be an early warning of kidney damage. You can also order a cheap urine test for protein to creatinine ratio or albumin to creatinine ratio along with your standard bloodwork. These will pick up early signs of glomerular permeability which is a downstream effect of damage caused by oxidative stress to the kidney.

Exogenous AAS stimulate the production of excessive oxidative stress (largely by damaging mitochondria) while also suppressing antioxidant defenses. This damages various organs and most classes of antioxidant supplementation isn't doing anything, though there are some exceptions. Supporting your mitochondria directly is likely to be helpful and can be done with cardio and good sleep with proper supplementation.

### Preventing AAS-induced oxidative stress

#### Mitochondrial dysfunction

AAS impair the mitochondrial ETC which allows electrons to escape and generate excessive ROS. Since this appears to be one of the main causes of AAS-induced oxidative stress, supporting mitochondrial function is likely quite important.

CoQ10: An essential component of the ETC that helps transport electrons and protect mitochondrial membranes. In rats exposed to nandrolone, CoQ10 reduced cardiac apoptosis and fibrosis and improved mitochondrial calcium-related measures.
[Ali 2018](https://doi.org/10.21608/ejfsat.2018.28292)

SS-31 (elamipretide): A peptide that binds to cardiolipin in mitochondrial membranes helping maintain their structure and function. Animal studies have shown protection against mitochondrial injury and subsequent kidney fibrosis.
[Szeto 2017](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5407729/)

Elamipretide is approved as Forzinity, where the label describes it as a mitochondrial cardiolipin binder that localizes to the inner mitochondrial membrane and improves mitochondrial morphology and function. Its only approved use is improving muscle strength in Barth syndrome at 40 mg subcutaneously once daily, under accelerated approval, dropping to 20 mg if eGFR is under 30. The randomized crossover trial missed its primary endpoints, and the strength gain came from the open-label extension.
[Forzinity label](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=146bf34c-76f2-48db-ac07-fb29cce2cd75)

Honokiol: This activates mitochondrial SIRT3, which regulates mitochondrial function and antioxidant defenses. In mice, honokiol improved mitochondrial respiration and reduced cardiac oxidative stress, hypertrophy, and fibrosis.
[Pillai 2015](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25871545/)

PQQ: This stimulates mitochondrial biogenesis, potentially increasing the production of new mitochondria.
[Chowanadisai 2010](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19861415/)

Urolithin A: This stimulates mitophagy, a process where cells remove damaged mitochondria. Human trials have shown improvements in some mitochondrial health markers.
[Singh 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9133463/)

Creatine: This helps cells maintain ATP availability which may be useful when mitochondrial energy production is impaired.

The AIS puts creatine monohydrate in Group A, its strongest evidence tier, for the phosphocreatine system rather than for mitochondrial function: creatine and phosphorylcreatine supply energy for brief intense exercise, at a very high rate but with very limited storage. Loading is about 5 g four times a day for 5 days, or 0.3 g/kg a day, then 3–5 g a day; the maintenance dose alone reaches saturation in about 4 weeks. The page reports no systematic serious adverse effects, and no reason to use any form other than monohydrate.
[AIS: Creatine](https://www.ausport.gov.au/ais/nutrition/supplements/group_a/performance-supplements2/creatine)
→ [[Creatine Supplementation]]

Taurine: This supports mitochondrial protein synthesis, membrane stability, and calcium regulation.

Ergothioneine: This is a naturally occurring antioxidant that accumulates in the mitochondria and can help against oxidative damage. Animal studies show improvements in mitochondrial function and reductions in kidney injury and fibrosis.
[Fong 2024](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38909533/) · [Peng 2026](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42335181/)

#### NADPH oxidase upregulation

AAS increase oxidative stress by upregulating NADPH oxdiase activity, which are enzymes that produce ROS.

Grape seed proanthocyanidins: In a rat study, boldenone increased cardiac NADPH oxidase activity, while grape seed extract reduced these changes and showed improvements in oxidative stress and cardiac injury markers.
[Tousson 2018](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30116498/)

Losartan / telmisartan: ARBs inhibit AT1 receptors which can stimulate NADPH oxidase activity. Losartan has also been shown to reduce cardiac fibrosis in nandrolone-exposed rats.
[Do Carmo 2011](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21407130/)

ARBs need monitoring. UK Kidney Association guidance for stable primary care patients is to measure urea, creatinine and electrolytes before starting or raising the dose and again within 2 weeks, with potassium rechecked within 1–2 weeks of either. Measured against the pre-treatment creatinine, a rise over 15% but under 30% means continuing with a repeat in 1–2 weeks and a review of fluid status and blood pressure. A rise over 30% means rechecking within 5–7 days, and stopping or dropping back to a tolerated dose if it persists.
[UK Kidney Association: ACEi/ARB monitoring](https://www.ukkidney.org/clinical-resources/aki-guide-resources/medication-considerations/changes-in-kidney-function-and-serum-potassium-during-acei-arb-diuretic-treatment-in-primary-care/)

Spironolactone: This blocks mineralocorticoid receptors, which contribute to oxidative stress, inflammation, and fibrosis. Spironolactone prevented the increase in cardiac collagen deposition in nandrolone exposed rats.
[Do Carmo 2011](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21407130/)

Honokiol: This has also been found to reduce NADPH oxidase activity and ROS production in endothelial cell studies.

Ergothioneine: In a study that involved human endothelial cells, this reduced NADPH oxidase 1 (NOX1) expression and ROS production, while also protecting cells against oxidative damage.
[Li 2014](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25022513/)

#### Cytochrome P450-mediated metabolism

Certain AAS especially 17a-alkylated oral compounds, go through extensive metabolism in the liver which can contribute to oxidative stress.

NAC: This supports glutathione production which helps liver cells manage oxidative stress. In a study involving nandrolone-exposed rats, NAC reduced liver damage and fibrosis related signaling.
[Mohammadpour-Asl 2026](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41242401/)

TUDCA: A bile acid derivative that reduces endoplasmic reticulum (ER) stresss, which can contribute to mitochondrial dysfunction, inflammation, and apoptosis. Animal studies have shown reductions in cellular injury and fibrosis, including in the heart.
[Rani 2017](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28426781/)

#### Inadequate upregulation

Normally when oxidative stress increases, the body responds by increasing antioxidant defenses, but AAS can produce more oxidative stress than they can handle.

Sulforaphane: This activates the Nrf2 pathway which regulates antioxidant enzymes and proteins involved in glutathione metabolism. A human study involving people with chronic kidney disease found that sulforaphane increased expression of NRF2 and NQQ1.
[Ribeiro 2024](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38772511/)

Honokiol: This activates SIRT3, which helps regulate mitochondrial antioxidant activity including the function of mitochondrial superoxide dismutasse (MnSOD).
[Pillai 2015](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25871545/)

Melatonin: This influences mitochondrial antioxidant function and endogenous antioxidant enzymes. It also has shown partial protection against cardiovascular abnormalities in stanozolol-exposed rats.
[Dos Santos 2013](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25610273/)

Black cumin (thymoquinone): This influences antioxidant and inflammatory pathways. Animal studies involving AAS have shown improvement in testicular oxidative stress and tissue damage.
[Sharoot 2025](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00580-025-03706-6)

Ergothioneine: This activates the Nrf2 pathway, which helps increase the body's natural antioxidant defenses. A study looking at human skin cells found that it increased several antioxidant proteins and protected cells against oxidative damage.
[Hseu 2015](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26021820/)

#### Suppression

AAS can suppress antioxidant enzymes, including (SOD), catalase, and glutathione peroxidase (GPx).

Taurine: In nandrolone-exposed rats, this improved testicular antioxidant defenses and reduced oxidative damage, inflammation, and apoptosis.
[Ahmed 2015](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25542992/)

Grape seed extract: This improved SOD and catalase activity in the hearts of boldenone-exposed rats.
[Tousson 2018](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30116498/)

Sulforaphane: This supports the regulation of endogenous antioxidant enzymes through Nrf2.

Honokiol: This supports mitochondrial antioxidant activity through SlRT3-dependent regulation of MnSOD.
[Pillai 2015](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25871545/)

Selenium: This is an essential mineral required for glutathione peroxidase, an antioxidant enzyme that helps break down harmful peroxides. A study involving methandienone-exposed rabbits found that selenium supplementation helped protect against oxidative stress.
[Abed & Al-Azawi 2020 - rabbit adrenal gland](https://www.bibliomed.org/?mno=121581)

Ergothioneine: In human endothelial cells, ergothioneine increased the expression of antioxidant enzymes including SOD, catalase, and glutathione reductase.
[Li 2014](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25022513/)

#### Non-enzymatic depletion

AAS contribute to the depletion of GSH

NAC: An important building block for GSH synthesis. In a 2025 study, NAC reduced cardiac oxidative DNA damage, inflammation, and mitochondrial apoptosis in nandrolone-exposed rats.
[Amini 2025](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39894191/)

Sulforaphane: This may provide support by stimulating enzymes involved in GSH synthesis and antioxidant recycling.

Ergothioneine: This has been shown to increase cellular GSH levels by stimulating pathways involved in production.
[Hseu 2015](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26021820/)

#### Calcium ion influx

AAS can also cause calcium movement into cells and mitochondria which can trigger additional oxidative damage. 

Taurine: This influences intracellular calcium handling and mitochondrial membrane stability.

CoQ10: This improved mitochondrial calcium related abnormalities in nandrolone-exposed rat hearts.
[Ali 2018](https://doi.org/10.21608/ejfsat.2018.28292)

TUDCA: This has shown protection against abnormal calcium handling and mitochondrial dysfunction in cardiac injury models.

SS-31: This helps maintain mitochondrial membrane integrity, potentially supporting animal mitochondrial calcium regulation

#### Oxidative stress

Excessive ROS produced by dysfunctional mitochondria can attack their own membranes causing further mitochondrial damage.

Several of the compounds may interrupt this process, particularly SS-31, CoQ10, honokiol, melatonin, PQQ, ergothioneine, and urolithin A.
