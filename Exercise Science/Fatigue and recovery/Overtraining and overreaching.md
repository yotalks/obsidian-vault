> [!summary] Bottom line
> Overreaching is a training-related drop in performance: functional overreaching rebounds above baseline after recovery, while nonfunctional overreaching takes longer to resolve without that benefit. Overtraining syndrome involves prolonged maladaptation and is a clinical diagnosis made after excluding other causes of underperformance. We distinguish these states by the recovery course, not just how tired a workout makes us.

## Functional, nonfunctional and overtraining

| State             | Performance after recovery | Typical recovery course                              |                 |
| ----------------- | -------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------- | --------------- |
| [[Glossary#FOR]]  | Functional overreaching    | Improves above baseline                              | Days to weeks   |
| [[Glossary#NFOR]] | Nonfunctional overreaching | Eventually recovers, without the performance benefit | Weeks to months |
| [[Glossary#OTS]]  | Overtraining syndrome      | Prolonged impairment with broader symptoms           | Months          |

Nonfunctional overreaching and overtraining syndrome can have similar symptoms. The time needed to recover helps distinguish them, often only after a period of rest.
[Kreher 2012](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3435910/)

## Clinical assessment

Overtraining syndrome is a diagnosis of exclusion. Other causes of fatigue and underperformance, including infection, inadequate energy or nutrient intake and iron deficiency, need to be considered. No single hormonal, biochemical, psychological or performance marker is sufficient on its own. Both nonfunctional overreaching and overtraining syndrome can involve clinical symptoms; they are not separated simply by whether a physician has given a diagnosis.
[Meeusen 2006](https://doi.org/10.1080/17461390600617717)

## Calcium sensitivity and force

Calcium-dependent force production can remain impaired after intensified training. Inflammation and oxidative stress can alter calcium handling and contractile proteins, linking repeated strenuous exercise to prolonged low-frequency force depression. These muscle-side changes are separate from a reduction in the brain's motor command. The proposed interaction between inflammation, oxidative stress and longer-lasting weakness is also relevant to [[Impaired muscle regrowth]]. The 2020 review received foundation and Swedish research support; its authors declared no conflicts of interest.
[Cheng 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7284919/)
→ [[Calcium ion-related fatigue]]

In 14 athletes (4 women and 10 men), 3 weeks of intensified training increased training hours to as much as 150% of usual, with three added high-intensity sessions each week. Knee-extensor force at low electrical stimulation frequencies (5–20 Hz) fell 6–27%, and single muscle fibers showed reduced calcium sensitivity. Maximal voluntary force, twitch measures and maximal single-fiber force did not significantly change. This shows how low-frequency force production can worsen while maximal-force tests stay unchanged. The study received Natural Sciences and Engineering Research Council of Canada support.
[Roussel 2024](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39121503/)

## What the labels mean for programming

Functional overreaching describes a successful performance rebound after recovery. Nonfunctional overreaching describes a longer period of reduced performance without that benefit. These outcomes concern performance; they do not measure how much muscle a training block built.

For hypertrophy programming, we still need to consider the stimulus, fatigue and recovery together. [[Training volume]] covers the dose response within workouts, while [[Training frequency and hypertrophy]] covers how sessions are spaced.
