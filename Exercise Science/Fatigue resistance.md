> [!summary] Bottom line
> Measures that include maximum strength overstate fatigue resistance; relative muscular endurance shows it improves mainly with light-load training. It comes from better tolerance of supraspinal fatigue, the repeated bout effect, and better hydrogen ion buffering, not from more capillaries.

Fatigue is a temporary reduction in exercise performance caused by fatigue mechanisms. When adaptations happen as a result of exercise help us resist these fatigue mechanisms, muscular endurance increases.

## How fatigue resistance is measured

There are many ways of measuring changes in fatigue resistance as a result of a training program. The measurements can be divided into those that control for increases in maximum strength and those that don't. Those that don't control for increases in maximum strength include changes in absolute muscular endurance (defined as an increase in the number of respite a given weight in kilograms or with a baseline percentage of [[Glossary#1RM|1RM]]),, changes in repetition strength such as 15RM or 20RM testing,  changes in average force across of the reps in a fixed number of isokinetic repetitions, and increases in volume load during a workout.

Fatigue resistance is conventionally measured by relative muscular endurance. This accounts for any changes in maximum strength, allowing us to look only at the effects of adaptations affecting fatigue.

For example, volume studies normally test increases in repetition strength (e.g. 15RM or 20RM). Of course, the measurements increase at least partly due to increases in maximum strength.
[Radaelli 2015](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25546444/)

Similarly, many studies test increases in absolute muscular endurance. We also see here these values increase after training at least partially due to increases to maximum strength.
[Kacin 2011](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21385216/)

Changes in workout volume load are far more commonly measured. Interestingly, training with light loads tends to cause greater increases in volume load than moderate loads. Given that gains in maximum strength are either similar or smaller when training with light loads, this tells us that the larger increases in muscular endurance must happen from increases in fatigue resistance.
[Schoenfeld 2016](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5003312/)

Relative increases in muscular endurance tends to not increase after heavy or moderate load strength training. It only meaningfully increases after light load strength training. This happening shows us that increases in fatigue resistance are necessarily occurring.
[Schoenfeld 2015a](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25853914/)

Changes in fatigue resistance can also be measured in isolation from changes in maximum strength by assessing percentage losses in strength from set to set over the workout. In this study, the researchers saw that the changes in fatigue resistance were limited to the first 4 weeks of a training program with moderate loads.
[Gacesa 2013](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3867092/)

Relative muscular endurance is a much more interesting outcome to look at than absolute muscular endurance because it excludes the effects of a separate quality (maximum strength) that we already know pretty well. When we look at the current literature, we see that changes in relative muscular endurance are very affected by the rep range used when training. Heavy loads give no beneficial effects and occasionally can give negative effects. As the load is reduced, the positive effects on fatigue resistance are apparent and display a logarithmic trend. This tells us that there is no stimulus for fatigue resistance adaptations with heavy loads, a rapidly increasing stimulus through the moderate and light load zone, and a slowly increasing stimulus after.
[Anderson 1982](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7079558/)
[Campos 2002](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12436270/)
[Van Roie 2013](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23999311/)
[Schoenfeld 2014](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24714538/)
[Su 2016](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27330916/)
[Schoenfeld 2017](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28834797/)
[Stone 1994](https://www.ovid.com/jnls/nsca-jscr/abstract/00124278-199411000-00005~strengthendurance-effects-from-three-resistance-training)

## Which fatigue mechanisms can we get resistance from?

Changes in fatigue resistance must happen from adaptations that affect fatigue mechanisms. These include [[Supraspinal CNS fatigue|supraspinal CNS fatigue]], spinal [[Glossary#CNS|CNS]] fatigue, [[Calcium ion-related fatigue|calcium ion-related fatigue]], and metabolite related fatigue.
→ [[ATP does not deplete during exercise]]

When muscular endurance training with very light loads is done, this causes an increase in the total magnitude of peripheral fatigue happening at muscular failure in a repetition strength test. The capacity to tolerate peripheral fatigue is indicative of the degree to which the brain is capable of withstanding discomforting sensations (which cause supraspinal CNS fatigue). Thus, when peripheral fatigue at muscular fatigue is increased, this tells us that supraspinal CNS fatigue resistance has developed. Whether spinal CNS fatigue can be changed over time is not known.
[Zghal 2015](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25681110/)

Improved resistance to calcium ion-related fatigue is the repeated bout effect. The [[Glossary#RBE|RBE]] describes how post-workout fatigue is reduced by sequential workouts, especially those that involve a lot of calcium ion accumulation, like eccentric-only contractions. Since all post workout fatigue is caused by calcium ion-related fatigue mechanisms, any change in calcium ion-related fatigue causes a change in post-workout fatigue.
[[Repeated bout effect]]

The effect of the RBE is incredibly rapid and the vast majority of the impact happens from workout one to workout two. Further changes are described by the multiple RBE and these changes and very small by comparison.
[[Repeated bout effect#Multiple repeated bout effect]]

Metabolites accumulate inside comparatively glycolytic muscle fibers when they use energy in the form of ATP rapidly during exhaustive exercise. While the metabolites can leave the muscle fiber, they are prevented from doing so when vascular occlusion happens, which happens whenever whole muscle force exceeds 25-30 percent of maximum levels.
[de Ruiter 2007](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17473770/)

Whenever the muscle relaxes, the metabolites can be carried away by the bloodstream. For this reason, it has been proposed that increasing the level of muscle capillarization could contribute to increases in fatigue resistance. However, looking at the capillarization literature shows that is almost 100% not happening.

More likely to contribute are increases in either intracellular or extracellular buffering capacity (hydrogen ion transport). Buffering capacity and hydrogen ion transport both involve the removal of hydrogen ions from the inside of the muscle fiber (acidosis). Acidosis is a metabolite-related fatigue mechanism. Reducing the build-up of hydrogen ions will reduce fatigue and increase relative muscular endurance. Data has shown that training above the lactate threshold such that muscle fibers create acidosis will stimulate an adaptation in intracellular buffering capacity.
[Edge 2006](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16283370/)

Data has also shown that training above the lactate threshold such that muscle fibers create sustained periods of acidosis stimulates multiple adaptations in extracellular buffering capacity. There are various processes that remove hydrogen ions from inside the muscle fiber, including lactate/H+ transport and sodium+/H+ ion exchange. This study showed that both of these processes are enhanced after speed-endurance training and that this was linked to improvements in fatigue resistance.
[Mohr 2007](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17194727/)
