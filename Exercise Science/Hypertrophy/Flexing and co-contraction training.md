> [!summary] Bottom line
> Flexing is a co-contraction: the muscles on both sides of a joint contract against each other, so both work isometrically. Isometric contractions give activated fibers high mechanical tension, so all flexing needs to build muscle is enough activation. Maximal co-contraction reaches about half to three quarters of maximum activation, more in experienced bodybuilders. In trials, 12 weeks of maximal elbow co-contractions grew the biceps and triceps by about 4%, and maximal contractions with no load grew muscle about as much as lifting with weights. It's a usable option when there's no equipment, such as when travelling.

## Why it can work

Muscle grows when many fibers are activated and those fibers experience high [[Mechanical tension|mechanical tension]]. How much tension an activated fiber produces depends mostly on how fast it shortens. In single muscle fibers, each myosin head pulls with about the same force at any speed. Fibers produce less force when shortening faster because fewer heads are attached at once, since they detach sooner.
[Piazzesi 2007](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18022371/)

An isometric contraction doesn't shorten at all, so every activated fiber produces high tension, and the only requirement is high activation ([[Increasing motor unit recruitment]]). Sustained isometric knee extensions held at 75% of maximum for 12 weeks increased quadriceps volume by 8.1%, compared with 2.6% for brief explosive isometric contractions.
[Balshaw 2016](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27055984/)
→ [[Isometric training and hypertrophy]]

## How much activation flexing reaches

Muscles on opposite sides of a joint can't both be fully activated at once. During a maximal elbow co-contraction, untrained men reached 46% of maximum biceps activation and 57% of maximum triceps activation. Bodybuilders, who practise co-contractions, reached 66% and 74%, and activation rose with years of bodybuilding experience.
[Maeo 2013](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3829833/)

Knee extensions done with no load but with maximal intent reached the same peak activation in the vastus lateralis and medialis as a set to failure with a 10-rep max, though average activation over the set was lower.
[Alves 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7459905/)

## Training studies

### Co-contraction

Young men did maximal co-contractions of the elbow flexors and extensors at 90° (5 sets of 10 four-second contractions, 3 times a week):
- After 12 weeks, biceps and triceps thickness both rose 4%, and isometric strength rose 15% for the flexors and 46% for the extensors. [Maeo 2014](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24368554/)
- After 4 weeks, strength rose but muscle thickness hadn't changed yet. [Maeo 2014b](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23868685/)

In 8 weeks of maximal abdominal bracing, a co-contraction of the trunk muscles, internal oblique thickness rose 22%, and trunk extension and hip extension strength rose 14% and 35%.
[Tayashiki 2016](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27377782/)

### Maximal contractions with no load

In 13 people, one arm did elbow flexions through a full range of motion with no load, contracting as hard as possible, and the other arm lifted 70% of its 1RM, for 18 sessions. Muscle thickness grew similarly in both arms, but 1RM rose more with the load (2.3 kg vs 1 kg).
[Counts 2016](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27329807/)

In 20 hospitalized women (average age 59), 5 weeks of maximal contractions with no load increased biceps, triceps and pec thickness by 14–38%, the same as training with elastic bands.
[Barbalho 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6926436/)
