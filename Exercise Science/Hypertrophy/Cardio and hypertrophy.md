> [!summary] Bottom line
> Aerobic exercise can interfere with muscle growth because it causes the same fatigue as strength training, which lowers fiber tension and motor unit recruitment. The interference is bigger when aerobic exercise comes right before lifting, and when it's more muscle-damaging: more activation, more volume and eccentric contractions, as in running. Short, low-intensity, concentric-only cardio using lots of muscle mass, like cycling or an elliptical with arms and legs, interferes least. The overall effect on fiber growth is small.

## Why cardio can interfere

Aerobic exercise done alongside a strength program can cause the same peri-workout and post-workout fatigue as strength training. Peri-workout fatigue happens during the workout, and post-workout fatigue in the days after. Both can reduce single fiber [[Mechanical tension|mechanical tension]] and [[Increasing motor unit recruitment|motor unit recruitment]], which weakens the hypertrophy stimulus → [[Calcium ion-related fatigue]].

Across 15 studies, concurrent training slightly reduced muscle fiber growth compared with strength training alone (standardized mean difference −0.23, −0.46 to −0.00), even though whole-muscle growth doesn't seem to be affected. Running reduced type I fiber growth (−0.81, −1.26 to −0.36) but cycling didn't. Frequency, training status and the order of exercises within a session made no difference.
[Lundberg 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9474354/)

## Timing

Aerobic exercise right before a strength workout leaves peri-workout fatigue in place, so the strength training is done with lower fiber tension and recruitment. So it's better to do cardio after strength training. When it has to come first, keep it short and low intensity, since higher intensity and higher volume cause more peri-workout fatigue.

In 21 trained men, running 5 km or 7 km at 90% of anaerobic threshold before leg presses cut total volume lifted by 12% and 22%, and the longer the run, the bigger the drop. Isometric strength, voluntary activation and muscle activity fell after every distance.
[Ribeiro 2019](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30096988/)

## Type of cardio

More muscle-damaging cardio causes more post-workout fatigue and more interference. Cardio causes more post-workout fatigue with higher muscle activation, higher volume and eccentric contractions. So the best choice is short, concentric-only exercise with low muscle activation. The way to keep activation low while keeping cardiovascular demand high is to use more total muscle mass: leg cycling is better than arm cycling, and an elliptical with both arms and legs is better than cycling.

In a meta-analysis of 21 studies, concurrent training with running, but not cycling, significantly reduced hypertrophy and strength, and more frequent and longer endurance sessions were linked to smaller gains.
[Wilson 2012](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22002517/)

## Conclusion

To minimize the effect of cardio on muscle growth, avoid doing it right before strength training, and avoid muscle-damaging forms, ideally choosing short, concentric-only exercise that works a lot of muscle mass at once, like an elliptical with both arms and legs.
