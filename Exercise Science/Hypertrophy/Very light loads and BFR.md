> [!summary] Bottom line
> Sets to failure grow muscle about equally across a wide range of loads, but very light loads (below about 25–30% of [[Glossary#1RM|1RM]]) grow less unless [[Glossary#BFR|blood flow restriction]] is added. Below about 25% of maximum force the muscle doesn't squeeze its blood vessels shut, so metabolites wash out and fatigue comes mainly from calcium ions, which lowers fiber tension. BFR traps the metabolites, and metabolite-related fatigue keeps fiber tension high. Metabolites alone, without contraction, don't stimulate growth.

## The light-load limit

A wide range of rep ranges stimulate about the same hypertrophy when the same number of sets are taken to failure, but an upper limit is usually given at around 30–40RM, or about 25–30% of 1RM. Below that, sets to failure produce less growth unless BFR is added.

In 30 men training each limb at different loads with volume matched for 12 weeks, 20% of 1RM grew the vastus lateralis 8.9% and the elbow flexors 11.4%, while 40–80% of 1RM grew them about 20% and 25%. 80% of 1RM grew both more than 20% did.
[Lasevicius 2018](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29564973/)

In 40 people doing knee extensions to failure (up to 90 reps a set) for 8 weeks, 15% of 1RM with no BFR, moderate BFR or high BFR was compared with 70% of 1RM. Muscle thickness increased in every condition, with no significant difference in how much it changed between conditions, though 70% of 1RM generally gave greater thickness. Only 70% of 1RM increased 1RM strength.
[Jessee 2018](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6198179/)

## Why: blood flow and the type of fatigue

During strength training, fatigue inside the muscle forces more [[Increasing motor unit recruitment|motor units]] to be recruited to keep doing reps. With heavy (1–5RM), moderate (6–15RM) or light (16–30RM) loads, metabolite-related fatigue predominates. It mainly slows muscle fiber shortening speed, so more motor units have to be recruited to keep the bar moving, but it doesn't reduce the [[Mechanical tension|mechanical tension]] on each fiber, so every activated fiber still gets a full hypertrophy stimulus.

Lower pH (acidosis) slows both the rate myosin attaches to actin and the rate it detaches, which reduces force and power.
[Jarvis 2018](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30382520/)

Below about 25% of maximum force, the muscle doesn't contract hard enough to close its blood vessels. In the knee extensors, blood inflow during isometric contractions stopped at about 25% of maximum torque in the vasti and 35% in the rectus femoris, measured with near-infrared spectroscopy.
[de Ruiter 2007](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17473770/)

So with very light loads, blood flows freely out of the muscle and carries the metabolites with it, and metabolite-related fatigue can't build up. The only fatigue that can build up is [[Calcium ion-related fatigue|calcium ion-related fatigue]], which does reduce single fiber tension by stopping actin-myosin crossbridges from forming, so the hypertrophy stimulus of each activated fiber is impaired.

Low-frequency fatigue, where force drops more at low activation frequencies and recovers slowly, seems to come from calcium uncoupling excitation from contraction.
[Balog 2010](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2932900/)

## Adding blood flow restriction

With BFR, the cuffs or wraps stop blood, and so metabolites, from leaving the muscle. Metabolite-related fatigue then predominates, preserving fiber tension at the end of the set when motor unit recruitment is high.

Some argue that the metabolites themselves stimulate growth, rather than their effect on fatigue. But metabolites accumulating in muscle that isn't contracting don't stimulate growth. In 20 young men, BFR at rest didn't change myofibrillar protein synthesis (0.044% vs 0.043% an hour), while BFR added to exercise at 20% of 1RM raised it 10% more than the same exercise without BFR.
[Nyakayiru 2019](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30694972/)

BFR during 2 weeks of bed rest didn't change muscle protein synthesis or prevent the loss of thigh muscle volume or strength.
[Fuchs 2025](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38411283/)

BFR at rest does increase muscle thickness briefly, but through a fluid shift rather than growth. Blood lactate didn't change.
[Loenneke 2012](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23238542/)

## Recovery after high-frequency BFR

In 17 untrained men, one leg performed 4 sets of knee extensions to failure at 20% of 1RM while the other used a fixed 30–15–15–15-repetition protocol. Fourteen sessions were spread across two 5-day blocks separated by 10 days of rest. Strength fell 5–10% after the first block, but was 6–11% above baseline 24 days after training ended. Muscle size increased 5–10%, with responses peaking 10–24 days after the intervention.
[Bjørnsen 2021](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33735465/)

## Conclusion

To use very light loads (under about 25% of 1RM) for hypertrophy, BFR cuffs or wraps are needed so the metabolites produced during exercise stay in the muscle.
