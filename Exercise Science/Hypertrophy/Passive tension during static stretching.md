> [!summary] Bottom line
> Passive resistance to stretch doesn't rise evenly across the range of motion. It stays low through the first two-thirds and then climbs sharply near end range, which rules out a single elastic structure. The tension comes from inside the sarcomere rather than the surrounding collagen, and titin's two-part structure explains the shape: the compliant Ig segment takes up the early stretch, then the stiff PEVK segment loads at long lengths.

## Measuring passive tension

When you static stretch, muscle fibers are stretched, and this produces resistance to stretch that can be detected by external measurement of joint torque.

When muscles are stretched through the entire physiological ROM, we can measure the corresponding resistance to the stretch with a dynamometer. Measuring the joint torque generated at all joint angles shows a non-linear change in passive resistance torque. Torque levels at short muscle lengths are pretty low and stay that way in the first two-thirds of the ROM then there is a sharp increase in passive resistance up to the max joint angle. This cannot be explained by a single elastic structure because that would increase torque linearly with length/joint angle.
[Klee 2002](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/17461391.2002.10142576)

## Where the tension comes from

A long time ago, researchers showed that passive mechanical tension inside whole muscles was the same as passive mechanical tension produced by single muscle fibers without the surrounding collagen layers. This tells us that we need to look at the myofibrils in the individual sarcomeres to see what's causing the passive tension.
[Magid 1985](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/4071053/)

### Titin

This is made up of two elements, one relatively compliant (Ig domains) and one relatively stiff (PEVK). When a muscle fiber is passively stretched without being activated, the compliant section of titin lengthens first, which provides a small increase in passive tension, then the stiff segment stretches after, which gives a lot greater increases in passive tension corresponding to the passive resistive torque increases at longer muscle lengths. We see that titin's behavior explains joint torques produced by resistance to muscle stretch the entire physiological ROM.
[Nishikawa 2020](https://journals.physiology.org/doi/full/10.1152/physiol.00036.2019)
