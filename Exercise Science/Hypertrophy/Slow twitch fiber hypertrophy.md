> [!summary] Bottom line
> Motor units are recruited from small and slow twitch to large and fast twitch as effort rises, and fibers grow whenever they're activated under high tension. So slow twitch fibers grow preferentially only when recruitment falls short of maximal, either because effort isn't maximal, as with slow lowering phases, or because of central fatigue, as might happen with light loads or short rests. The second effect seems too small to show up in growth data.

## Recruitment order

Muscles contain hundreds of thousands of fibers grouped into motor units, each with roughly 20 to 2,000 fibers. Henneman's size principle sets the order of recruitment: smaller motor units with fewer fibers are recruited at low effort and above, and larger ones with more fibers only at high effort.
[Mendell 2005](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15914463/)

How many fibers each motor neuron supplies (the innervation ratio) varies widely within a muscle, though data on its range and distribution are limited.
[Enoka 2001](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11150961/)

Smaller motor units recruited at low effort tend to have more oxidative, slow twitch fibers, while larger ones recruited only at high effort have more glycolytic, fast twitch fibers. A muscle's overall fiber type proportion sets the effort at which slow fibers give way to fast. In a muscle that's 50% slow twitch by volume, like the quadriceps, all its slow fibers are active by 50% of maximal effort, with few fast fibers active. In a muscle that's 80% slow twitch, like the soleus, slow fibers keep being added up to 80% of maximal effort → [[Individual differences in muscle growth]].

## Which fibers grow

Fibers grow when they're activated and shorten slowly enough to produce high [[Mechanical tension|mechanical tension]]. Over a set to failure, effort rises a lot, activating many more fibers, while movement speed, and so fiber tension, changes comparatively little. So hypertrophy is stimulated in whichever fibers are activated, from the more oxidative up to the more glycolytic → [[Increasing motor unit recruitment]].

## When slow twitch fibers grow more

Slow twitch fibers grow preferentially when recruitment doesn't reach maximal levels, either because effort isn't maximal or because of central nervous system fatigue ([[Supraspinal CNS fatigue]]).
→ [[Increasing motor unit recruitment]]

Slow lowering phases, where effort isn't maximal, typically favor slow twitch growth. In 28 trained women doing 9 weeks of lower body training at 6–8RM with matched time under tension, 6-second concentrics with 2-second eccentrics grew both type I and type IIA fibers, while 2-second concentrics with 6-second eccentrics grew only type I fibers.
[Gillies 2006](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16685548/)
→ [[Lifting tempo]]

Training methods with a lot of discomfort, like light loads to failure or short rest periods, might be expected to grow fast twitch fibers less because recruitment falls short. But there's no data supporting this yet, so the effect is likely too small to notice. Light and moderate loads usually give similar growth with the same number of sets to failure, so the number of fast twitch fibers left unrecruited with light loads is probably small → [[Very light loads and BFR]].
