> [!summary] Bottom line
> Research on frequency for hypertrophy is thin: most comparisons find no difference, with only a weak trend favoring 3× over 1× per week. In theory, frequency should matter: each extra set in a workout adds less than the one before, and the post-workout growth window lasts about 48 hours, so spreading sets across more sessions should give more growth. The limit is recovery, since training a muscle that's still damaged gives a weaker stimulus, and the best frequency varies between people and muscles.

There are very few training studies when assessing training frequency for hypertrophy. The only trend is for 3x per week being better than 1x per week.

Study on trained adults found no differences between 1 and 2 times frequency.
[Brigatto 2019](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29528962/)

Study on untrained adults comparing 1 and 2 times frequency found no differences.
[Gentil 2015](https://www.researchgate.net/profile/Martim-Bottaro-2/publication/261735493_Effects_of_equal-volume_resistance_training_performed_one_or_two_times_a_week_in_upper_body_muscle_size_and_strength_of_untrained_young_men/links/54da487b0cf261ce15cc9045/Effects-of-equal-volume-resistance-training-performed-one-or-two-times-a-week-in-upper-body-muscle-size-and-strength-of-untrained-young-men.pdf)

Study comparing 1x and 2x frequency on trained adults found no differences.
[Tavares 2017](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28316261/)

Study comparing 1 and 3 times frequency in trained adults found no differences but 3x was numerically better.
[Ochi 2018](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30013480/)

Study on trained adults comparing 1 and 3 times frequency found 3x is better than 1x.
[Schoenfeld 2015b](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25932981/)

Study comparing 1 and 3 times frequency on trained adults found 3x is better than 1x
[Neves 2022](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36228016/)

Study on untrained adults comparing 2 and 3 times frequency found no differences.
[Barcelos 2018](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29852092/)

Study on untrained elderly comparing 2 and 3 times frequency found no differences
[Grzyb 2022](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32028459/)

Study on trained adults comparing 2 and 3 times frequency found no differences.
[Lasevicius 2019](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31531139/)

## Why frequency could matter

### Diminishing returns per set

Each extra set in a workout adds less stimulus than the one before ([[Training volume]]). In a model where the first set is worth 1 arbitrary unit (AU), 3 sets in one workout total 1.61 AU, while 1 set in each of three workouts totals 3.0 AU. With the same weekly sets, spreading them out should give more stimulus.

### The post-workout window

After resistance exercise, [[Glossary#MyoPS|myofibrillar protein synthesis]] stays raised for up to about 48 hours. In 20 young men and women, the protein synthesis over those 48 hours correlated with muscle growth after 8 weeks of training, though not in the untrained state.
[Abou Sawan 2022](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35081094/)

Outside that window, muscle is slowly lost, which is what maintenance studies measure. After 16 weeks of training 3 days a week, young adults kept all their muscle growth over the next 32 weeks on either one third of the dose (one day a week) or one ninth of it. The one-third dose even added more fiber growth. Older adults lost fiber size on both maintenance doses.
[Bickel 2011](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21131862/)

A model built on this assumes 3 sets in one weekly workout is exactly enough to maintain muscle. Then the 1.61 AU from that workout is lost over the 5 days outside the 48-hour window, about 0.32 AU a day. On that model, 3 sets once a week gives no net gain, while 1 set three times a week gives 3.0 AU and loses only about 0.32 AU. → [[Detraining adaptations speed]]

## The limit: recovering from damage

Training a muscle that hasn't recovered from the last workout gives a weaker stimulus, for two reasons:
- **Less tension per fiber.** Most of the early strength loss after damaging exercise comes from excitation-contraction coupling failure, and a slow loss of contractile protein over the following days drags out recovery. [Warren 2001](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11337828/)
- **Less recruitment.** In mice, raising IL-1β in the brain worsened running performance after uphill running, and blocking it improved performance after muscle-damaging downhill running. So inflammation from damage may cause central fatigue. [Carmichael 2006](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16778069/)

Recovery varies between people. After 50 maximal elbow flexor eccentrics, 21 people lost 49% of their strength on average, 22 lost 23%, and 3 lost none. The bigger losses came from the muscle itself, with no difference in central measures 5 minutes after exercise.
[Hubal 2007](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17473772/)

It also varies between muscles. When sedentary men did eccentrics for 9 muscle groups, strength 1 day later had dropped by 16–57%, with more damage in the arm muscles than the leg muscles. A second session 2 weeks later caused much less damage in every muscle ([[Repeated bout effect]]).
[Chen 2019](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30663816/)

## Finding your own frequency

When 20 people trained one leg 5 times a week and the other 2–3 times a week for 8 weeks, average growth and strength gains were the same. Individually, about a third grew more with the higher frequency, about a third with the lower one, and the rest responded similarly to both.
[Damas 2019](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30289872/)

So the best frequency is the highest one a muscle can recover from.
