> [!summary] Bottom line
> Junk volume is reps, sets or workouts that give little or no hypertrophy stimulus, usually because they're done while fatigued. Fatigue from earlier exercise lowers both motor unit recruitment and fiber tension, so extra reps after failure (drop sets), extra sets late in a long workout, and extra workouts before recovering from the last one all add less than they seem to.

## What it is

Junk volume is the reps, sets or workouts that provide little or no stimulus for hypertrophy. Any rep gives minimal stimulus if [[Increasing motor unit recruitment|motor unit recruitment]] is too low or [[Mechanical tension|mechanical tension]] is too low. Fatigue from previous exercise causes both, often at the same time, so junk volume often means training in a fatigued state. It shows up in three ways: extra reps after a set to failure, extra sets at the end of a high-volume workout, and extra workouts later in a week before recovering from earlier ones.

## Extra reps after failure

Drop sets and similar techniques fit the definition of junk volume. At the end of a set to failure, several central nervous system fatigue mechanisms briefly limit how many motor units can be recruited, so reps done right after add little compared with the reps leading up to failure → [[Supraspinal CNS fatigue]].
→ [[Stimulating reps and failure]]

Over 9 bouts of 10 maximal plantar flexions, voluntary activation fell 12.6% and resting twitch force 16.2%, showing that both central and peripheral fatigue build up with repeated maximal efforts.
[Nordlund 2004](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12972439/)

## Extra sets in a workout

High-volume workouts also fit the definition. Meta-analyses show a non-linear dose-response, with later sets stimulating less growth than earlier ones, and at very high volumes each extra set adds very little → [[Training volume]]. That's at least partly from fatigue accumulating over the workout, both central fatigue and [[Calcium ion-related fatigue|calcium ion-related fatigue]].

In rats, the force deficit an hour after lengthening contractions grew with the number of contractions (33% after 3, 37% after 10, 68% after 60, 78% after 240), and low-frequency force depression, a sign of excitation-contraction coupling failure, developed mostly in the early repetitions.
[Willems 2009](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19053963/)

## Extra workouts in a week

Excessively high-volume, high-frequency programs, often called overreaching blocks, fit the definition too. When recovery between workouts is incomplete, post-workout fatigue from the last workout is still present during the next one and impairs its stimulus, making the extra workouts largely ineffective.
→ [[Overtraining and overreaching]]

In mice given three bouts of resistance exercise, muscle protein synthesis rose after the third bout when bouts were 72 or 24 hours apart, but not when they were 8 hours apart, even though mTOR signaling was highest in the 8-hour group.
[Takegaki 2017](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5704086/)

In 22 untrained people doing maximal eccentric biceps training every other day for 20 days, strength dropped after 8 days and stayed down while muscle activation rose, suggesting the recovery time wasn't enough to repair the damage. The early rise in muscle thickness likely reflected swelling from damage → [[Muscle swelling]].
[Krentz 2010](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20495928/)

## The common thread

The three scenarios look different, but the same physiology applies to each. Fatigue from prior exercise accumulates and interferes with stimulating hypertrophy in the reps, sets or workouts that follow.
→ [[Calcium ion-related fatigue]]
