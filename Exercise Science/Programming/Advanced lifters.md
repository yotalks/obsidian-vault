> [!summary] Bottom line
> Advanced lifters (over 2 years of training) likely only grow the fibers of their highest-threshold motor units, so they won't grow from a workout unless motor unit recruitment is extremely high. That makes maximizing recruitment their main focus: stable, easy-to-coordinate, single-joint and single-limb exercises, avoiding methods that cause breathlessness or burning, and a motivating environment with training partners, music and logbook targets. Multi-joint exercises are efficient and progress for a long time, but they limit recruitment, cause more CNS fatigue and mostly grow only the limiting muscle, which matters most for advanced lifters.

## The key difference

There's really only one key difference between intermediates (6 months to 2 years of training) and advanced lifters (over 2 years). Advanced lifters likely only grow the fibers of the highest group of high-threshold motor units, while intermediates still grow fibers further down the motor unit pool → [[Beginners vs intermediates]]. Going up the pool, fibers become less oxidative, and by the size principle of striated muscle (not Henneman's), slower fibers reach their maximum size at smaller diameters, so they stop growing earlier in a lifter's career → [[Individual differences in muscle growth]]. So an advanced lifter won't grow from a workout unless they reach extremely high motor unit recruitment → [[Increasing motor unit recruitment]].

## Raising motor unit recruitment

### 1. Stable exercises

More stable exercises allow higher recruitment for the same effort, so advanced lifters benefit from stable machine exercises over less stable free-weight ones → [[Squat patterns]]. In 8 men, leg extensions on a Swiss ball produced 70.5% less force and 44.3% less quadriceps activation than on a bench, with more antagonist activity.
[Behm 2002](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12173956/)
→ [[Increasing motor unit recruitment]]

### 2. Easier coordination

Exercises that are easier to coordinate allow higher recruitment for the same effort, so easier single-joint machine exercises beat harder multi-joint free-weight ones. Eccentric cycling, which is harder to coordinate than concentric cycling, was rated more mentally demanding and had larger errors in hitting a target torque.
[Kan 2019](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31025096/)

### 3. Less muscle mass

Exercises that use less total muscle mass allow higher recruitment for the same effort, so single-joint and even single-limb exercises help. Maximal force was about 7% lower in two-leg than one-leg knee extensions, with a 4% deficit in voluntary activation, and up to 20% lower in two-hand finger flexion.
[Van Dieën 2003](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12544644/)

In 8 men exercising to exhaustion, single-leg knee extensions caused much more quadriceps fatigue than cycling (−28% vs −16% maximal force), suggesting smaller muscle mass lets a muscle be pushed further before stopping.
[Rossman 2012](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3485418/)

### 4. Low cardiovascular demand

Methods that cause less CNS fatigue in the brain allow higher recruitment for the same effort. So advanced lifters should avoid cardiovascularly demanding exercises, like those using lots of muscle mass with high reps and large ranges of motion, since breathlessness causes this kind of fatigue → [[Stimulating reps and failure]]. In 11 people, traditional sets lowered voluntary activation while sets with short rests inside them didn't, and the cardiovascular strain correlated with the drop.
[Río-Rodríguez 2016](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4794235/)

### 5. Avoiding burning sensations

Burning in the muscle also causes CNS fatigue, so advanced lifters should avoid methods that cause it, like short rest periods or high reps. Injecting combinations of protons, lactate and ATP into muscle at low concentrations caused sensations of fatigue, and at higher concentrations pain, while single metabolites caused nothing.
[Pollak 2014](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3946674/)

In 18 trained men doing isometric knee extensions to failure, 70% of maximum recruited higher-threshold motor units with higher firing rates than 30% of maximum throughout.
[Muddle 2018](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5907942/)

### 6. A motivating environment

Training around other people, especially competitive lifters who also cheer us on, raises recruitment by raising the maximum effort we'll tolerate. Loud, upbeat music does much the same → [[Psychobiological model of fatigue]].
[Marcora 2008](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18618133/)

### 7. Targets and goals

A target or clear goal for a session is motivating too, which raises recruitment. So advanced lifters benefit from keeping careful logs and always trying to progress, however slightly. In 13 powerlifters, a target velocity of 1.0 m/s produced faster bench press reps than being told to move as fast as possible (0.84 vs 0.82 m/s).
[Hirsch 2021](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31268998/)

## Pros and cons of multi-joint exercises

### Advantages

Multi-joint exercises have three big advantages:
1. **Efficiency.** They train several muscle groups at once. A very concise program covering most of the body can use just four exercises, like an incline bench press, a wide-grip pull-down, a squat and a stiff-legged deadlift.
2. **Long-lasting progress.** They often allow progressive overload for much longer than single-joint exercises, so the program needs changing less often → [[Progressive overload]].
3. **Always possible.** Many advanced lifters can't use single-joint machines like the lateral raise, knee extension and leg curl because they're too strong even for moderate loads and single-limb variations.

Together, these mean a program built on multi-joint exercises could be followed for years with minimal changes.

### Disadvantages

1. **Lower recruitment.** Using more total muscle mass, they don't allow the same motor unit recruitment as single-joint exercises, as the bilateral force deficit shows. That's a problem for advanced lifters, who need maximal recruitment to grow at all.
2. **More CNS fatigue.** Using lots of muscle mass, they cause cardiovascular and inflammatory responses that create supraspinal CNS fatigue for the exercises that follow, which is why most people can do far more sets of single-joint exercises than multi-joint ones in a workout → [[Stimulating reps and failure]].
3. **Only the limiting muscle is fully trained.** One muscle usually limits the lift and reaches high recruitment, while the others aren't trained as hard. Beginners and intermediates can still grow those other muscles, but advanced lifters probably only grow the limiting muscle, and the rest are just maintained.

Bilateral deficits vary by muscle. Maximal two-leg knee extensions produced about 7% less force than one-leg ones, with a 4% voluntary activation deficit ([Van Dieën 2003](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12544644/)), but contracting both thumbs at once barely lowered activation of the right thumb adductors (88.6% vs 90.3%).
[Herbert 1996](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8926266/)

In 15 trained men, bilateral biceps curls raised heart rate and cardiovascular strain more than unilateral curls.
[Moreira 2017](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26382128/)

In 27 trained men, 6 weeks of targeted training for whichever bench press prime mover was least active (anterior deltoid, pecs or triceps) raised bench press 1RM and that muscle's activity during the lift.
[Stronska 2022](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32460639/)
