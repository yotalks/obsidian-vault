> [!summary] Bottom line
> Sarcomeres are added when stretched fibers feel passive tension and are lost when that stretch stops, about twice as fast as hypertrophy is lost.

Sarcomeres are added whenever they produce and experience passive [[Mechanical tension|mechanical tension]]. When sarcomeres are stretched past a threshold, titin molecules inside them produce enough passive mechanical tension to stimulate the addition of new sarcomeres on the end of the muscle fiber. This means we will lose sarcomeres with the absence of sarcomere stretch, and the subsequent lack of exposure to passive mechanical tension. We see this in many animal models.

When tendons are elongated which causes sarcomeres to sit at shorter resting lengths when a muscle is at a given length, this reduces passive [[Glossary#MT|MT]] magnitude.
[Hoeffner 2023](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37348011/)

When a limb is immobilized at a short muscle length, it causes sarcomeres to sit at shorter resting lengths when muscles are at a given length, which reduces the passive MT magnitude vs. if they were immobilized at a longer muscle length.
[Williams 1978](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/744744/)

When exposed to static stretching then detrained, it stops sarcomeres from experiencing regular exposure to passive tension.
[[Sparrow 1982]]

When exposed to uphill running or walking, it increases relative exposure to active muscle contractions without passive tension which led to a reduction in  sarcomeres in series.
[Butterfield 2005](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15947030/)

When limbs are exposed to eccentric-only training then detrained, it stops sarcomeres from experiencing regular exposure to passive tension.
[[Miura 2025]]
[[Behan 2022]]
[[Presland 2018]]
[[Kubo 2010]]

We see that the number of sarcomeres in a muscle fiber is rapidly lost in animal models and fascicle lengths in human models, with all previously made gains being lost in half the time it takes for muscle atrophy to happen after strength training programs that mainly produce hypertrophy.
