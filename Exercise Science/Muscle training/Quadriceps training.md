> [!summary] Bottom line
> The single-joint vasti are trained by any knee extension movement, like squats, leg extensions and leg presses. The two-joint rectus femoris barely works in squats and leg presses, so it needs its own exercises: leg extensions for its lower region, and hip flexion on a four-way hip machine for its upper region.

## Four muscles

There are four quadriceps. The rectus femoris crosses both the hip and knee, while the vastus lateralis, vastus medialis and vastus intermedius cross only the knee. Like most two-joint muscles, the rectus femoris can be divided further into upper and lower regions.

## As knee extensors

All four quadriceps have similar leverage for knee extension, so they're activated roughly in proportion to their size, with larger muscles getting more of the drive.
[Buford 1997](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9422462/)
Neural drive to the vastus lateralis and vastus medialis tracked their force-generating capacity: the bigger the vastus lateralis was relative to the vastus medialis, the more drive it got.
[Hug 2015](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC4685812/)
→ [[Neuromechanical matching]]

Even so, single-joint knee extension training grows the rectus femoris more than the other quads, which suggests it has better leverage there, especially its lower region.
[Maeo 2018](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1002/tsm2.38)

The rectus femoris is barely used in multi-joint lower body exercises like squats and leg presses, likely because it's also a hip flexor, which inhibits it while the hip extends. After squats, the vasti showed clear signs of having worked while the rectus femoris showed essentially none.
[Ploutz-Snyder 1995](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7573553/)

## Rectus femoris as a hip flexor

The upper region of the rectus femoris is used for hip flexion, and its lower region for knee extension.
[Watanabe 2012](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22153052/)

Its leverage relative to the other hip flexors is best from standing upright to 60° of hip flexion, where it provides about two-thirds of hip flexion torque; beyond 60° the iliopsoas takes over. So hip flexion on a standard four-way hip machine targets the upper rectus femoris.
[Kumazaki 2022](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/jmj/68/4/68_JMJ22-0009-OA/_article/-char/ja/)
