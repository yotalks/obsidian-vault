> [!summary] Bottom line
> The pecs have three regions with different leverage. The upper (clavicular) region is best trained with narrow-grip presses on a slight incline, up to 30°. The middle (sternal) and lower (costal) regions are best trained with wide-grip presses, flys or pec decks.

## Regions act separately

Each region of the pec is used according to its leverage. In EMG recordings, the upper fibers were used for shoulder flexion, the middle fibers for horizontal flexion whatever the arm angle, and the lower fibers for extension from a raised arm. Adduction activity spread from the lower fibers to all but the clavicular head as the arm came down from 90° to the side.
[Paton 1994](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20870556/)
→ [[Neuromechanical matching]]

In cadaver measurements, the upper (clavicular) pec was among the most effective shoulder flexors, while the middle (sternal) and lower (costal) pec were among the most effective adductors.
[Ackland 2008](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC2644775/)

## Three regions

- **Upper (clavicular) region:** excellent leverage for shoulder flexion in the sagittal plane (raising the arm in front), from the arms-at-sides position up to 90° (arms straight out in front). So dumbbell front raises and narrow-grip bench presses train it well, especially on a slight incline of up to 30°. It probably also has acceptable leverage through the whole range of horizontal flexion (bringing the arms together in front), so it likely contributes to wide-grip presses and flys, though less.
- **Middle (sternal) region:** likely excellent leverage through the whole range of horizontal flexion, so wide-grip presses, flys and pec decks train it well. It also has good leverage for adduction (pulling the arm down to the side) through the whole frontal plane, better than the lats when the arms are close to the body.
- **Lower (costal) region:** likely good leverage through the whole range of horizontal flexion, so wide-grip presses and flys train it fairly well. Like the middle region, it has good leverage for adduction, better than the lats near the body. It also helps with shoulder extension once the arms are raised above about 30°, more so the higher they go, so it contributes to the start of shoulder-width pull-ups, pulldowns and pullovers.

Pec activation also changes with shoulder angle: in pullovers held at five positions from 0° to 180°, pec EMG varied with position, and differently from pulldowns. In both, pec activation peaked with the arm raised to 90–135° and was lowest with the arm at the side.
[Teixeira 2022](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9362894/)

## Pressing variations

Pressing exercises can shift work between the anterior deltoid, middle deltoid and the pec regions through grip width, range of motion, bench incline, forearm position (supinated or pronated) and stability:
- **Anterior deltoid:** a seated, contracted-position partial overhead barbell press with a shoulder-width or wide grip. A supinated (reverse) grip may add more anterior deltoid if comfortable → [[Deltoid training]].
- **Middle deltoid:** a seated, lengthened-position partial overhead dumbbell press with a shoulder-width grip.
- **Clavicular pec:** a flat or shallow-incline machine chest press with a shoulder-width grip.
- **Sternal and costal pec:** a flat machine chest press with a wide grip.

For the two chest presses, lengthened partials can keep the emphasis on the pecs rather than the anterior deltoids and triceps → [[Partial vs full range of motion]].

Evidence on each variable:
- **Bench incline:** in 30 trained adults, upper pec activity peaked at a 30° incline, middle and lower pec activity was highest flat, anterior deltoid activity was highest at 60°, and triceps activity was similar at all angles. [Rodríguez-Ridao 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7579505/)
- **Grip width and forearm position:** in 12 men, a supinated grip raised clavicular pec and biceps activity, and narrowing the grip raised triceps activity and lowered sternal pec activity, unless the grip was supinated. [Lehman 2005](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16095407/)
- **Grip width and force:** in 14 trained men, 1RM was 109.8 kg with a wide grip, 108.9 kg medium and 103.7 kg narrow. [Larsen 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7862765/) In 12 Paralympic powerlifters, close grip was more triceps while wide grip was more pec  [Dos Santos 2020](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32916657/)
- **Forearm rotation:** in 30 men holding a dumbbell overhead, anterior deltoid activity was higher with the palm up than down, while posterior deltoid activity was higher with the palm down. [Ijiri 2020](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31989947/)
- **Range of motion:** elite Paralympic powerlifters, contracted position partial was more triceps and front delt while bottom half ROM was more pec. [Mendonça 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8513888/)
- **Stability:** in 15 trained men, pressing an unstable flexible bar (60% of 1RM) was compared with a standard bar (75% of 1RM) for primary and stabilizing muscle activity. [Ostrowski 2017](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27564994/)
- **Leverage:** a systematic review found the anterior and middle deltoid had the largest elevating moment arms of the shoulder muscles in abduction and flexion. [Hik 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6284439/)
