> [!summary] Bottom line
> The delts have three regions with different leverage. The front delt works best with the arm overhead, so overhead presses that are hardest at the top train it best. The side delt works best below shoulder height, so lateral raises and wide-grip presses that are hardest before the arm is horizontal train it best. The rear delt can be trained with rear delt flys, narrow-grip rows or narrow-grip pulldowns.

## Regions act separately

In EMG recordings of 19 segments across the pecs, delts and lats, each segment's timing and intensity of activation depended on its moment arm relative to the intended movement.
[Brown 2007](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16458022/)
→ [[Neuromechanical matching]]

In cadaver measurements, the middle and anterior deltoid were the most effective shoulder abductors, the anterior deltoid was among the most effective flexors, and the posterior deltoid had one of the largest extensor moment arms.
[Ackland 2008](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC2644775/)

## Three regions

- **Anterior (front) region:** good leverage for both shoulder flexion in the sagittal plane (raising the arm in front) and abduction in the frontal plane (raising the arm to the side), but in both cases its leverage is best once the arm is above 90° (horizontal). So it helps with front raises and narrow-grip bench presses, but contributes more to overhead presses, wide or narrow. The best options are hardest in the overhead part of the movement, for example by using a partial range of motion.
- **Middle (side) region:** excellent leverage for abduction in the frontal plane, but its best leverage is below 90°. So lateral raises and wide-grip overhead presses train it well, best when they're hardest before the arm reaches horizontal.
- **Posterior (rear) region:** excellent leverage for both horizontal extension in the transverse plane (pulling the arm back at shoulder height) and extension in the sagittal plane (pulling the arm down and back). So rear delt flys, narrow-grip rows and narrow-grip pulldowns all train it.
