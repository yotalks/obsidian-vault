> [!summary] Bottom line
> Straight-knee calf raises train both gastrocnemius heads, with toes turned in favoring the lateral head and toes turned out favoring the medial head. Seated calf raises reduce the gastrocnemius contribution and emphasize the soleus. Standing calf raises or straight-knee toe presses are therefore the main options for the gastrocnemius, with seated raises as a soleus-focused option.

## Three components

The main calf muscle group, the triceps surae, consists of the soleus and the medial and lateral heads of the gastrocnemius. The soleus crosses only the ankle; both gastrocnemius heads cross the knee and ankle.

[[Neuromechanical matching]] proposes that neural drive is distributed according to the mechanical advantages of motor units for the task. This provides a framework for interpreting how joint position changes which calf muscles contribute.
[Hudson 2019](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30985474/)

## Soleus

Bending the knee shortens the gastrocnemius without directly shortening the soleus. In a motor-unit study, knee flexion reduced medial gastrocnemius recruitment while soleus activity increased as participants maintained the same plantar-flexion torque. This supports seated calf raises as a way to shift the work toward the soleus.
[Lauber 2014](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC4208651/)

The soleus may also contribute strongly during squats, leg presses and similar movements, where the gastrocnemius's knee-flexion action opposes knee extension.

## Gastrocnemius: knee and ankle position

Keeping the knee extended allows the gastrocnemius to contribute more effectively than bending it. An electrical-stimulation study found that knee and ankle position changed gastrocnemius plantar-flexion torque, with knee extension and ankle dorsiflexion favoring torque production.
[Landin 2015](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC4471746/)

In the standing-versus-seated training study, standing raises produced much more gastrocnemius growth: 12.4% versus 1.7% in the lateral head and 9.2% versus 0.6% in the medial head. These results support straight-knee calf raises for gastrocnemius hypertrophy.
[Kinoshita 2023](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10753835/)

## Lateral and medial heads

Foot position can bias growth between the two heads:
- **Lateral head:** toes turned inward.
- **Medial head:** toes turned outward.

In 22 young men training for 9 weeks, inward foot positioning produced more lateral-head growth than outward positioning (9.1% versus 5.5%). Outward positioning produced more medial-head growth than inward positioning (8.4% versus 3.8%). Forward-facing feet gave intermediate results. The exercise was a calf raise on a horizontal leg-press machine.
[Nunes 2020](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32735428/)

## Exercise selection

- **Soleus emphasis:** seated calf raises with the knee bent.
- **Lateral gastrocnemius emphasis:** straight-knee calf raises or toe presses with toes turned in.
- **Medial gastrocnemius emphasis:** straight-knee calf raises or toe presses with toes turned out.
- **Both gastrocnemius heads:** straight-knee calf raises with feet facing forward.
