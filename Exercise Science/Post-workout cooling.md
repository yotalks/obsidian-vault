> [!summary] Bottom line
> Post-workout cooling, like cold water immersion, doesn't change fatigue right after a workout, but it speeds strength recovery by reducing secondary muscle damage from oxidative stress and inflammation. The catch is that it also lowers muscle protein synthesis, raises protein breakdown like a stressor, and reduces long-term muscle growth, especially in fast twitch fibers. So it's counterproductive if you want hypertrophy.

## Faster recovery

Cooling doesn't change fatigue immediately after a workout, which fits with it acting on biochemical fatigue mechanisms rather than on the central nervous system. But it does speed strength recovery afterward. After damaging hamstring exercise, five daily 14-minute cold water immersions at 14 °C sped recovery of strength, soreness, flexibility and blood myoglobin.
[Huang 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11829708/)

The most likely reason is a smaller secondary muscle damage effect: the extra damage that oxidative stress and inflammation cause in the days after a workout. In rats, cool water immersion after downhill running reduced the share of damaged muscle fibers and an oxidative stress marker over the following 3 days.
[Nonaka 2012](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/jpts/24/7/24_613/_article/-char/ja/)

## Less muscle building

Faster recovery can't come from faster repair, though, since cooling lowers the rate of muscle building. Cold water immersion after resistance exercise lowered myofibrillar protein synthesis both after the workout and over 2 weeks of training.
[Fuchs 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7028023/)

Cooling also seems to trigger a stress response that increases muscle protein breakdown, most likely through [[Glucocorticoids|glucocorticoids]]:
- Four hours of local cooling raised breakdown-related genes (FOXO3a, atrogin-1, MuRF-1) and lowered muscle-building genes.
[McGlynn 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10528672/)
- In rats, 24 hours of cold exposure lowered protein synthesis, raised protein breakdown, and raised stress hormones including corticosterone.
[Manfredi 2013](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23908317/)
- In chickens, colder temperatures increased muscle protein turnover along with the stress hormone corticosterone.
[Yunianto 1997](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9227187/)

## Less growth over time

Cooling after every workout reduces how much muscle a training program builds:
- After 6 weeks of wrist training, forearms cooled in 10 °C water after each session grew less, and didn't gain strength, while uncooled arms did.
[Yamane 2015](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25760154/)
- After 7 weeks of whole-body training, cold water immersion blunted the growth of type II (fast twitch) fibers and anabolic signaling and raised breakdown markers, though strength gains were similar.
[Fyfe 2019](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31513450/)

Glucocorticoids preferentially affect fast twitch fiber size, so the extra loss of fast twitch growth supports the idea that cooling triggers a stress response at the fiber level (see [[Exercise isn't a stressor]] for why that differs from calling it a stressor). The reduced growth may come from both impaired damage repair and stress-induced atrophy.
