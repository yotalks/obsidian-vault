> [!summary] Bottom line
> The two-joint hamstrings (semimembranosus, semitendinosus and biceps femoris long head) are best trained with the hip flexed: seated leg curls and 45° back extensions, with the feet turned in for the medial muscles and out for the biceps femoris. The single-joint biceps femoris short head is best trained with the hip extended, in lying leg curls with the feet turned out.

## Four muscles

There are four hamstrings, two on each side of the back of the thigh:
- **Medial:** the semimembranosus and semitendinosus, both two-joint muscles (crossing the hip and knee).
- **Lateral:** the biceps femoris long head (two-joint) and short head (single-joint, crossing only the knee).

Like most two-joint muscles, the two-joint hamstrings can be divided further into upper and lower regions.

Training them differently does grow them differently. Seated leg curls with the hip flexed grew the two-joint hamstrings about 2.2 times as much as Nordic curls, while Nordics grew the knee-only short head about 1.9 times as much as seated curls.
[Maeo 2024](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38857522/)
→ [[Neuromechanical matching]]

Turning the feet changes which side works harder, but only slightly. Turning the knee inward raised medial hamstring activity and turning it outward raised biceps femoris activity, by 2–13% of maximum.
[Mohamed 2003](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12893150/)

## Semimembranosus and semitendinosus

These have their best leverage for knee flexion with the hip flexed and the feet turned in, so the seated leg curl trains them well. Their best leverage for hip extension is with the hip flexed to about 45°, again with the feet turned in, so the 45° back extension trains them well. The upper part of these muscles likely contributes more to hip extension exercises, and the lower part more to knee flexion.

## Biceps femoris long head

This has its best leverage for knee flexion with the hip flexed and the feet turned out, so the seated leg curl trains it well. Its best leverage for hip extension is with the hip flexed to about 45°, again with the feet turned out, so the 45° back extension trains it well. As with the medial hamstrings, its upper part likely contributes more to hip extension and its lower part more to knee flexion.

The hamstrings' hip extension moment arm increases up to about 35° of hip flexion and then decreases.
[Németh 1985](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/3988782/)

The 45° back extension (Roman chair) activated the biceps femoris more than both the stiff-legged deadlift (SLDL; the paper labels it a Romanian deadlift, but describes starting upright with slightly bent knees and lowering the torso to parallel) and the seated back extension machine, especially in the upper part of the movement.
[Andersen 2021](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8057707/)

## Biceps femoris short head

The short head only crosses the knee, and has its best leverage for knee flexion with the hip extended and the feet turned out. So the lying leg curl is the best option.
