> [!summary] Bottom line
> Spreading protein evenly across meals, around 20–30 g or 0.5 g/kg each, raises muscle protein synthesis more than skewing it toward one large meal, and may slightly improve hypertrophy. But distribution isn't limiting: hitting the daily protein target matters most.

## Muscle protein synthesis

The best study we probably have on this topic looked at protein distribution effects on MPS by dividing people into three groups. One group had 2 meals with 40g protein 6 hours apart, the second group had 4 meals with 20g protein around 3 hours apart, and group 3 had 8 meals a day with 10g protein around 90 minutes apart.

The group with 20g protein every 3 hours had the highest increase in MPS. Though, all three diet conditions had a statistically significant increase in MPS. 
![[Pasted image 20261102004213.png]]
[Areta 2013](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23459753/)

The 24 participants were trained men who ate 80 g of whey over the 12 hours after a leg workout, and 20 g every 3 hours raised muscle protein synthesis 31–48% more than the other two patterns.

These findings are also supported in other research looking at protein distribution and MPS. One of the only other studies on this subject compared "even" protein intakes throughout the day to "skewed" intakes. In the even group, they had 3 meals with 30g of protein spaced evenly apart, in the skew group, they had 10g of protein for breakfast, 15g for lunch, and 65g for dinner.

![[Pasted image 20261102004427.png]]
[Mamerow 2014](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24477298/)

In 8 healthy adults eating the same total protein for a week, 24-hour muscle protein synthesis was 25% higher with even distribution, and the difference held after 7 days. Two authors had received speaking fees from the National Cattlemen's Beef Association.

## Muscle growth

But measuring 24 hour MPS isn't the same as actual hypertrophy over time. We have data on this as well. In this study where protein distribution was skewed heavily toward later in the day, both the skewed and evenly distributed group saw an increase in muscle mass overall. But, the evenly distributed group, did better.
![[Pasted image 20261102004633.png]]
[Yasuda 2020](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32321161/)

In 26 young men training 3 times a week for 12 weeks on the same daily protein (1.3 g/kg), those eating more at breakfast (0.33 vs 0.12 g/kg) gained 2.5 kg of lean mass versus 1.8 kg, a large effect size that fell just short of statistical significance (P = 0.06).

## Conclusion

Protein distribution isn't limiting for MPS or hypertrophy. Having 3 meals with around 0.5g/kg bw of protein is most ideal. But hitting your daily protein target is most important.
