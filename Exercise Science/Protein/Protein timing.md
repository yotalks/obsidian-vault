> [!summary] Bottom line
> Eating protein soon after a workout seems to boost hypertrophy in unadjusted analyses, but that's because the timing groups usually ate more protein. Once total protein is accounted for, the effect disappears, so hitting the daily protein target matters far more than when it's eaten.

## The post-workout window

This meta analysis on protein timing and hypertrophy found that the overall, unadjusted effect of protein consumption within post workout window showed a statistically significant effect of eating protein within that window on measures of hypertrophy. But the vast majority of these studies do not control for protein.
![[Pasted image 20261102003223.png]]
[Schoenfeld 2013](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24299050/)

The hypertrophy analysis covered 23 studies with 525 people, and total protein intake was the strongest predictor of how much muscle people gained.

## Adjusting for total protein

Limiting the analysis to the four studies that did control for protein could potentially create issues with regards to statistical power. A secondary analysis was done that looked to adjust for total protein between groups. This way we can estimate what the effect might be if they controlled for protein. 
![[Pasted image 20261102003421.png]]
When adjusting for total protein between groups, the effect seems to become null.

## The anabolic window

There is a period of time from around 3-4 hours before and after your workout where protein intake maximizes MPS. But eating outside this window doesn't limit hypertrophy, long as daily protein is met.
[Aragon 2013](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23360586/)

## Conclusion

All in all, it seems merely hitting total target for protein in a day is far more important than timing protein.
