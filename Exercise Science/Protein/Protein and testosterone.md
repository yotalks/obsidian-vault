> [!summary] Bottom line
> Protein intakes people normally eat, up to about 3.4 g/kg/day, don't consistently lower testosterone. Very high intakes above that do lower total testosterone, but binding proteins fall too, so free testosterone may change less. Energy balance matters more: a large calorie deficit lowers testosterone whether protein is high or not, and extreme weight cutting can push it below the normal range.

## Everyday and high protein intakes

A meta-analysis of 27 studies and 309 men found that low-carbohydrate diets with moderate protein (under 35% of calories) had no consistent effect on resting total testosterone. High-protein, low-carbohydrate diets (35% or more) lowered it substantially, by about 5.23 nmol/L (about 151 ng/dL).
[Whittaker 2022](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35254136/)

That result is specific to very high intakes of more than 3.4 g/kg/day. Average intake is about 1.3 g/kg/day, typical high-protein diets are about 1.8–3 g/kg/day, and very few people go past 3.4 g/kg/day. These diets also have little carbohydrate, so this can't separate high protein from low carbohydrate.
[Whittaker 2023](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36266956/)

## The 1987 crossover

In 7 men, testosterone was lower after 10 days on a high-protein diet than on a high-carbohydrate diet with the same calories and fat (371 vs 468 ng/dL). Sex hormone binding globulin (SHBG) was also lower (23.4 vs 32.5 nmol/L), and cortisol was higher (10.6 vs 7.74 µg/dL).
[Anderson 1987](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/3573976/)

This compared extreme diets. The high-protein diet was 44% protein and 35% carbohydrate, at about 3.9 g/kg/day, and the high-carbohydrate diet was 10% protein and 70% carbohydrate, at about 0.9 g/kg/day.
[Whittaker 2023](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36266956/)

Total testosterone isn't the whole picture, since most of it is bound to carrier proteins like SHBG. The testosterone to SHBG ratio from the mean values was about 55 on the high-protein diet and 50 on the high-carbohydrate diet, so the available fraction didn't fall with total testosterone. Both average values are within the normal range.

## A single meal

In 23 adolescent males, testosterone and luteinizing hormone fell within 20 minutes of drinking either whey protein or glucose (1 g/kg). The drop lasted longer after protein, continuing to 65 minutes. Since it happened with glucose too, this isn't specific to protein, and the authors said its physiological significance is unknown.
[Schwartz 2019](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31055857/)

## Energy balance

In 33 adults, protein at 0.8, 1.6 or 2.4 g/kg/day made no difference to the fall in total and free testosterone or IGF-I during a 40% energy deficit over 21 days. SHBG rose in all groups. So high protein didn't lower testosterone at these intakes, but it didn't protect it from the deficit either.
[Henning 2014](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24641883/)

Extreme cutting has a clearer effect. In 19 amateur wrestlers, testosterone was lower during the competitive season than two months after it, and low levels tracked with low body fat (r = 0.72), large fat loss and large weight loss. At peak season, some were well below the normal range of 3.5–10 ng/mL. This is a correlation in a group restricting their diets, so it can't separate low body fat from the dieting itself.
[Strauss 1985](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/4068168/)
